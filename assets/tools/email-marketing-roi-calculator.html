The email marketing ROI formula

Revenue is the clicks your emails got, times the share that bought, times what they paid. Cost is your email software plus the time you spent:

revenue = emails sent × click rate × conversion rate × average order value

revenue per email sent = revenue / emails sent

cost = software cost + hours × cost per hour

profit = revenue × gross margin - cost

ROI = profit / cost

Use one month for every number. An ROI of 0% means email exactly paid for itself, and below 0% it lost money. Average order value counts one order per buyer, so for a subscription it counts the first payment only.

A worked example

These are round example numbers, the same ones the calculator starts with, not data from a real business. You send 40,000 emails in a month. At a 2.5% click rate that is 1,000 clicks, and at a 3% conversion rate, 30 orders. At $60 each that is 30 × $60 = $1,800 of revenue, or $1,800 / 40,000 = $0.045 per email sent.

At a 60% gross margin, $1,800 leaves $1,080 of gross profit. Email software costs $100 and 8 hours at $40 cost $320, so the total cost is $420. Profit is $1,080 - $420 = $660, and ROI is $660 / $420 = 157.1%.

Why clicks need UTM tags to be counted

Your email tool counts clicks, but it does not take the payment. Your analytics sees the visit, but many mail apps send no referrer, so without tags it cannot tell the visit came from an email and usually files it under Direct. The order that follows is filed there too, and email looks worth less than it is.

Put utm_medium=email on every link, with a utm_campaign for each send or automated flow. The UTM builder makes these links. Then join each payment to the visit before it, and you can replace the estimated revenue above with the real figure. For a store, see email marketing revenue tracking for online stores. The channel decision this should change

Use it to decide where the next hours go, more sends or another channel, and look at the extra sends alone. Say you go from 40,000 emails to 50,000 with 4 more hours, and people click a little less, so the click rate for the month falls to 2.2%. Revenue goes from $1,800 to $1,980, and ROI is still 104.8%. But the extra $180 of revenue leaves $108 at a 60% margin, the 4 hours cost $160, and profit falls from $660 to $608.

In that case put the 4 hours into a flow or a list signup instead, or into another channel whose extra spend leaves more. Work out that channel with the same margin and with its hours counted, from the orders it really brought.

Three mistakes with email ROI

  • Using revenue as if it were profit. With gross margin set to 100%, the default example shows an ROI of 328.6% instead of 157.1%.
  • Entering clicks divided by opens. 1,000 clicks from 10,000 opens is 10%, and typing that in as the click rate turns $1,800 of revenue into $7,200. Divide clicks by emails sent.
  • Counting orders that would have come anyway. If 10 of the 30 orders came from people who were going to buy without the email, 20 orders are left, profit is $300 and ROI is 71.4%. To find out, leave a random part of the list out of a send and compare what both groups buy.

Email marketing ROI calculator FAQ

How do you calculate email marketing ROI?

Multiply emails sent by click rate, conversion rate and average order value to get revenue. Multiply that by your gross margin, subtract the cost of your email software and the hours you spent, and divide what is left by those costs. In the example on this page, $660 of profit on $420 of costs is an ROI of 157.1%.

Why does this calculator use gross margin?

Because an order costs you something to fulfil. Revenue minus email costs looks like profit, but product, delivery and payment fees come out of it first. Enter the share of each order left after those costs. If you want ROI on revenue alone, set gross margin to 100.

Should I count opens or clicks?

Clicks. An open does not bring anyone to your site, and some mail apps load every email on their own, which counts as an open with nobody reading. A purchase on your site starts with a visit, so click rate is the number that leads to revenue.

What is a good email marketing ROI?

Above 0% means your emails earned more than they cost. Beyond that it depends on your list, margin and prices, so compare your own ROI month to month and against your other channels, worked out with the same formula.

Should I count the time spent writing emails?

Yes. Software is often the smaller cost. Add the hours spent writing, designing and setting up emails at what an hour costs you, or the ROI will look better than it is.

Why is email revenue in my analytics lower than this estimate?

Often because some email clicks are not counted as email. Many mail apps send no referrer, so a click without UTM tags usually lands in Direct, and so does its order. Tag every link with utm_medium=email. The rates you typed in may also be higher than your real ones.

How many orders does email need to pay for itself?

Divide the email cost by the gross margin to get the revenue you need, then divide by the average order value. In the example on this page, $420 / 60% = $700, and $700 / $60 = 11.67, so email pays for itself at 12 orders a month. At a 3% conversion rate that takes 400 clicks.
