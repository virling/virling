Startup runway calculator

Runway is how many months your cash lasts: runway = cash / net burn, where net burn is monthly costs minus monthly revenue. If revenue is growing or shrinking, net burn changes every month, so this calculator works it out month by month. Everything runs in your browser.

The runway formula

With flat revenue, runway is one division:

net burn = monthly costs − monthly revenue

runway in months = cash / net burn

With growing or shrinking revenue, the calculator repeats two steps for each month m until the cash is gone or 10 years have passed:

revenue in month m = monthly revenue × (1 + growth)m − 1

cash after month m = cash before − (monthly costs − revenue in month m)

The business breaks even in the first month when revenue is at least as high as costs. If that happens before the cash is gone and revenue is not shrinking, the cash never runs out.

A worked example

This is an example with made-up numbers, the same as the defaults above. A startup has $300,000 in the bank, pays $50,000 a month and collects $20,000 a month, so net burn in month 1 is $30,000. With flat revenue, runway is $300,000 / $30,000 = 10 months.

With revenue growing 5% a month, month 2 brings in $21,000 and burns $29,000, month 3 brings in $22,050 and burns $27,950, and so on. After month 13 about $4,260 is left. Month 14 would burn about $12,287, so the cash runs out in that month and the runway is 13.3 months. Revenue would only pass costs in month 20.

Now change the cash to $500,000. The startup reaches month 20 with money left: revenue is then about $50,539 against $50,000 of costs, so it breaks even. The lowest cash balance on the way is about $160,780, after month 19. If growth holds until then and revenue does not shrink afterwards, the cash never runs out.

What moves the runway

There are three levers: more cash, lower costs and more revenue. Cutting costs works from the first month. Growth works slowly at first and then faster, because each month builds on the one before. The channel decision this should change

Use it to decide which channel to cut, and whether to pay for more growth. Say $5,000 of the $50,000 monthly costs goes to one channel, and revenue is flat, so runway is 10 months. If the channel brings $4,000 a month that would stop with the spend, cutting it leaves $45,000 of costs and $16,000 of revenue, and runway goes up to 10.3 months. If it brings $9,000, cutting it leaves $11,000 of revenue, and runway falls to 8.8 months. Only take off revenue that would really stop: subscribers a channel already won keep paying until they cancel.

Adding spend has to pass the same test. From the defaults, $5,000 more a month with growth up from 5% to 7% shortens runway from 13.3 months to 11.8. At 8% the startup breaks even in month 15, with as little as $14,298 left on the way. Raise a budget only if the channel's own payments show it can bring that growth before the cash is gone.

Three mistakes with runway

  • Counting a yearly payment as monthly revenue. With flat revenue, a customer who prepays $12,000 for a year makes one month look like $32,000, which gives 16.7 months. Added to cash instead, it gives $312,000 / $30,000 = 10.4 months.
  • Leaving out bills that come once a year. $24,000 of tax and insurance a year is $2,000 a month. With it, flat runway drops from 10 months to 9.4.
  • Trusting one good month of growth. At 10% growth the default startup never runs out of cash, and at 5% it does in month 14. Use an average over several months, then run a lower rate too.
questions

Startup runway calculator FAQ

How is startup runway calculated?

Divide the cash in the bank by the monthly net burn, which is monthly costs minus monthly revenue. If revenue is changing, work through it one month at a time instead: update revenue, subtract that month's net burn from cash, and stop when the cash is gone.

What is the difference between gross burn and net burn?

Gross burn is everything you spend in a month. Net burn is what you spend minus the revenue you collect, so it is the amount your cash falls by. Runway uses net burn.

What does it mean if cash never runs out?

If revenue reaches costs before the cash is gone and does not shrink after that, the business pays for itself and there is no month when cash runs out. The calculator then shows the month it breaks even and the lowest cash balance on the way.

Should I include money I expect to raise?

No. Count only cash you already have and revenue you expect to collect. A round that has not closed can be late or fall through, so run the numbers without it first.

Why does the calculator stop at 10 years?

It adds up one month at a time for 120 months. Costs and growth that stay the same for that long are unlikely, so beyond that point it only says that cash lasts more than 10 years.

Does the calculator store my numbers?

No. The calculation runs in your browser and nothing is uploaded or saved.

How much does cutting costs extend runway?

With flat revenue, divide cash by the new net burn. In the example on this page, cutting costs 10%, from $50,000 to $45,000 a month, lowers net burn from $30,000 to $25,000 and extends runway from 10 months to 12. With revenue growing 5% a month the same cut lets the startup break even in month 18 instead of running out of cash in month 14.
