The conversion rate formula

Conversion rate is the share of visitors who became paying customers:

conversion rate = paying customers / visitors × 100%

Two more numbers come from the same inputs. Average order value is what each paying customer spent, and revenue per visitor is what each visit was worth:

average order value = revenue / paying customers
revenue per visitor = revenue / visitors = conversion rate × average order value

Count each visitor once, use the same date range for every channel, and use revenue you actually collected after refunds. The total row adds up the visitors, customers and revenue of all channels and divides those sums, rather than averaging the rows, so a small channel does not count as much as a large one.

A worked example

These are made-up numbers, the defaults above. Organic search brought 4,000 visitors and 60 paying customers who spent $3,000: a 1.5% conversion rate, $0.75 per visitor and a $50 average order. Paid search brought 2,500 visitors and 25 customers for $1,500: 1.0%, $0.60 per visitor and $60 per order. The newsletter brought only 800 visitors, but 24 of them paid $1,920 in total: 3.0%, $2.40 per visitor and $80 per order.

Together that is 109 customers from 7,300 visitors, a 1.49% conversion rate, and $6,420 of revenue, or $0.88 per visitor and $58.90 per order. The newsletter is the best channel: each of its visitors is worth more than three times a visitor from organic search, even though organic search sends five times the traffic.

Why small samples mislead

Suppose a channel sent 80 visitors and 3 of them paid (made-up numbers). The calculator shows 3 / 80 = 3.75%, but one customer fewer makes it 2.50% and one more makes it 5.00%. The channel's true rate could sit well away from 3.75%. A common way to put a range on it is the Wilson score interval, with p as the conversion rate, n as the visitors and z as 1.96 for 95% confidence:

range = (p + z² / 2n ± z × √(p × (1 - p) / n + z² / 4n²)) / (1 + z² / n)

For 3 of 80 that is (0.0375 + 0.0240 ± 0.0481) / 1.0480, or about 1.3% to 10.5%. For 300 of 8,000, the same 3.75%, it is about 3.4% to 4.2%.

The same goes for a gap between two channels. If another channel turned 8 of 80 visitors into customers, its 10% looks far better than 3.75%. Put both in the A/B test calculator, with the revenue fields empty, and the p-value is about 0.118: not significant at 95%. Visitors to two channels are not split at random as in a test, so read it as a check for chance, not as proof of what caused the gap.

Which number to change first

  • A handful of customers per channel: change nothing yet, collect more visitors.
  • Few visitors start a trial or checkout: the landing page or the audience you send to it.
  • Many trials, few payments: onboarding, or who the channel brings.
  • Good conversion rate, low revenue per visitor: price or order value.
  • Best revenue per visitor, few visitors: send that channel more traffic.

Compare channels by revenue, not traffic

A single site-wide conversion rate hides which channels bring buyers. The hard part is not the division but the inputs: most web analytics count visitors per channel but stop before the payment, so paying customers and revenue per channel need each payment tied to the visit that started it. Conversion rate calculator FAQ

How do you calculate conversion rate?

Divide the number of visitors who became paying customers by the total number of visitors, then multiply by 100. 60 customers from 4,000 visitors is a 1.5% conversion rate.

Should I count visitors or sessions?

Unique visitors. One person can visit several times before paying, so dividing by sessions makes the conversion rate look lower than it is and penalises channels that bring people back.

Why compare revenue per visitor as well as conversion rate?

A channel can convert well but bring small orders, or convert rarely but bring large ones. Revenue per visitor is conversion rate times average order value, so it ranks channels by what each visit is actually worth.

Where do I get paying customers per channel?

From an analytics tool that links visits to payments. Most web analytics count visits per channel but not the payments that followed, so customers and revenue per channel need each payment joined to the visit that brought the buyer.

How many visitors do I need for a reliable conversion rate?

There is no fixed number: it depends on the rate and on how precise you need it to be. In a made-up example at 95% confidence, 3 customers from 80 visitors fits a true rate of about 1.3% to 10.5%, while 300 from 8,000 narrows it to about 3.4% to 4.2%.

Should a free trial count as a conversion?

Not on this page. Count paying customers, and track trials as a step in between. A channel can start many trials that never pay, so keep visitor-to-trial and trial-to-paid as separate rates and compare channels on visitor-to-paid.
