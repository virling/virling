The sales velocity formula

Sales velocity is how much revenue your open pipeline turns into per day, at the rate you win deals now:

sales velocity = open opportunities × average deal value × win rate / sales cycle in days

The top of the formula is the revenue you expect from the deals that are open today. The bottom spreads it over the days a deal takes. Multiply the result by 30 for a month:

revenue per month = sales velocity × 30

Where to get the four numbers

NumberHow to count itCommon mistake
Open opportunitiesDeals someone is working on, past your own qualifying step, on the day you measureCounting every lead, which makes the win rate from qualified deals far too high for the list
Deal valueAverage of the deals you won in the period, as annual or total contract valueUsing list price when most deals close with a discount
Win rateWon / (won + lost), for deals that closed in the periodDividing by all deals, including the ones still open
Sales cycleDays from first touch, or from opening the deal, to the closeStarting the clock at a different step from one deal to the next

The sales cycle length calculator works out the cycle from your dates, as an average and a median, and the ACV calculator works out deal value from contracts with different terms. Use the same period for deal value, win rate and cycle, long enough to hold more than a handful of closed deals.

A worked example

The numbers filled in above are made up to show the method. A team has 40 open opportunities worth $6,000 each, wins 25% of them and takes 60 days to close. The pipeline is worth 40 × $6,000 = $240,000, of which 25%, or $60,000, should be won. Spread over 60 days that is $60,000 / 60 = $1,000 per day, or $30,000 per 30-day month.

With 10 percent more opportunities (44), a 10 percent higher deal value ($6,600) or a 10 percent higher win rate (27.5%), the result is $1,100 per day. With a cycle 10 percent shorter (54 days) it is $60,000 / 54 = $1,111 per day.

Now split the same 40 opportunities by channel:

Channel (made up)FormulaPer day
Organic search20 × $5,000 × 25% / 50 days$500
Outbound email10 × $8,000 × 10% / 100 days$80
Referrals10 × $6,000 × 40% / 40 days$600
All channels$500 + $80 + $600$1,180

The averages across these three channels are the same $6,000, 25% and 60 days, yet the channels add up to $1,180 and not $1,000. Averages mix the short cycle and high win rate of referrals with the slow outbound deals. Outbound has the largest deals and the lowest velocity: a tenth of them close, and each takes 100 days.

Why a shorter cycle moves it most

The first three numbers multiply, so 10 percent more of any of them adds exactly 10 percent. The cycle divides, and dividing by 0.9 adds 11.1 percent. That is true for every team, whatever the inputs. The one thing that can break the tie between the others is a win rate already close to 100%, which has no room left to grow.

The gap is about one point, so treat it as a starting place, not an answer. The useful question is which 10 percent is cheapest to get. Replying to demo requests the same day may cost nothing, while 10 percent more opportunities may mean 10 percent more ad spend. Cutting the cycle with a discount lowers deal value and can cancel the gain: closing 10 percent faster for 10 percent less leaves velocity where it was.

What sales velocity misses

It is a rate, not a forecast. It assumes you replace each deal that closes with a new one and that the next deals behave like the last ones. It says nothing about when cash arrives, whether customers renew or what each channel costs. A few very large deals can also lift the average value above what most buyers pay.

It also depends on knowing where a deal came from, and deals with a call in the middle often lose that. B2B SaaS attribution for small teams covers trials, invoices and sales calls, and how to track revenue from demo bookings shows how to join a booked call to the invoice paid weeks later.

Sales velocity calculator FAQ

How do you calculate sales velocity?

Multiply open opportunities by average deal value and by win rate, then divide by the sales cycle in days. 40 opportunities at $6,000 with a 25% win rate and a 60-day cycle give 40 × $6,000 × 0.25 / 60 = $1,000 of revenue per day.

What is a good sales velocity?

There is no figure that fits every business, because it depends on your price, the size of your pipeline and how long buyers take. Compare your own number with the same number last quarter, and compare your channels with each other.

Which of the four numbers should I improve first?

For the same 10 percent change, a shorter sales cycle adds the most: 11.1% against 10% for each of the other three. The gap is small, so pick the number you can move with the least work and cost.

How do I turn sales velocity into a monthly number?

Multiply revenue per day by the days in the month. This calculator uses 30 days, so $1,000 per day is $30,000 per month. Use calendar days for both the cycle and the month, not working days for one of them.

Should I use annual or total contract value as the deal value?

Either works if every deal uses the same one. With annual contract value the result is new yearly contract value won per day, and with total contract value it is everything the customer commits to. Neither is cash received per day.

Why do my channels add up to a different number than the total?

The total multiplies and divides averages, and a channel with a high win rate or a short cycle gets diluted by the others. In the made-up example the averages give $1,000 per day and the three channels add up to $1,180. When channels differ a lot, trust the split.
