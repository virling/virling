The suggestion is a simple proportional model: next month's budget is split in proportion to the revenue each paid channel brought, which is the same as weighting each channel's spend by its ROAS. It assumes a channel keeps its ROAS at a new spend level, which real channels rarely do, and it is only as good as the revenue you credit to each channel. The formulas

For each channel, return on ad spend is the revenue it brought divided by what you spent on it:

ROAS = channel revenue / channel spend

Share of spend and share of revenue compare each channel with the total across all channels:

share of spend = channel spend / total spend
share of revenue = channel revenue / total revenue

A channel whose share of revenue is higher than its share of spend returns more than your average channel; one whose share of revenue is lower returns less. The suggested budget gives each paid channel the same share of next month's budget as its share of revenue among paid channels:

suggested spend = total budget × channel revenue / revenue of all paid channels

Because channel revenue is channel spend × ROAS, this moves budget toward channels with a higher ROAS and away from those with a lower one, in proportion to how far apart they are. A channel that brought no revenue gets no budget.

A worked example

With the defaults above, four channels cost $10,000 last month and brought $25,000 of revenue, a blended ROAS of 2.5x.

  • Paid search: $4,000 of spend, $12,000 of revenue, ROAS 3x. 40% of spend, 48% of revenue. Suggested: $4,800, up $800.
  • Paid social: $3,000 of spend, $6,000 of revenue, ROAS 2x. 30% of spend, 24% of revenue. Suggested: $2,400, down $600.
  • Display: $2,000 of spend, $2,000 of revenue, ROAS 1x. 20% of spend, 8% of revenue. Suggested: $800, down $1,200.
  • Newsletter sponsorships: $1,000 of spend, $5,000 of revenue, ROAS 5x. 10% of spend, 20% of revenue. Suggested: $2,000, up $1,000.

The total stays at the $10,000 you entered. If you enter a bigger or smaller budget, every suggested amount scales with it.

Where the simple model breaks

Most channels get less efficient as you spend more on them: the cheapest clicks and the most interested buyers come first. Doubling a sponsorship that returned 5x will rarely return 5x again. Treat the suggestion as the direction to move, shift part of the budget, and measure again the next month.

The bigger limit is the revenue column. Ad platforms report the conversions they can see within their own attribution windows, and a sale that touched two platforms can be counted by both, so the revenue per channel can add up to more than you collected. A split built on those numbers moves money toward whichever platform claims the most. Marketing budget calculator FAQ

How should I allocate my marketing budget across channels?

Start from what each channel returned: divide the revenue it brought by what it cost. Move budget from channels with a low return toward those with a high one, a step at a time, and keep some budget for testing new channels that have no history yet.

How does this calculator suggest a budget?

It splits the total budget you enter in proportion to the revenue each paid channel brought. That is the same as weighting each channel's current spend by its ROAS, so channels that return more get a bigger share.

Why should I not move the whole budget at once?

Channels usually get less efficient as spend grows, so a channel will not keep its ROAS when its budget doubles. Shifting part of the budget and measuring again shows how each channel responds.

What revenue should I enter for each channel?

The revenue from customers who came through that channel in the same period as the spend. Use numbers matched from your payments rather than each ad platform's own report, since several platforms can claim the same sale.
