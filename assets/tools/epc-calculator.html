The EPC formula

EPC is the total commission earned divided by the number of clicks that earned it:

EPC = total earnings ÷ clicks

If you know the sales instead of the earnings, the earnings are the sales times the average order value times the commission rate:

total earnings = sales × average order value × commission rate

EPC is the conversion rate times the commission per sale, so it moves when either one does:

conversion rate = sales ÷ clicks

Many networks quote EPC per 100 clicks rather than per click, which is the same number times 100. Check which one a dashboard shows before comparing offers.

A worked example

These are made-up numbers, the defaults above. A link gets 2,000 clicks and 40 sales with an $80 average order at a 25% commission. Each sale pays $80 × 0.25 = $20, so the earnings are 40 × $20 = $800. The EPC is $800 ÷ 2,000 = $0.40, the conversion rate is 40 ÷ 2,000 = 2%, and 100 clicks earn $40.

A second offer pays 40% on a $50 order but converts at 1%: each sale pays $20 again, but 2,000 clicks bring 20 sales and $400, an EPC of $0.20. The higher rate earns half as much per click.

EPC for affiliates

An affiliate has a fixed amount of traffic, so EPC is the fairest way to compare offers: it folds the commission rate, the order value and how well the merchant's page converts into one number. A high rate on a page that rarely converts can earn less per click than a modest rate on one that does. Compare EPC from your own traffic where you can, since a network average mixes audiences that are not yours. For recurring commissions, count the renewals you are paid on too, or EPC will undersell the offer.

EPC for merchants

A merchant can turn the same number around to see which partners send buyers rather than clicks. Two partners with the same click count can be worth very different amounts: work out EPC per partner and the gap shows. It is still a proxy. Commissions are counted under your affiliate software's cookie rules, and EPC says nothing about refunds, renewals or customers who came back later through another channel. EPC calculator FAQ

How do you calculate EPC?

Divide the total commission earned by the number of clicks. $800 earned from 2,000 clicks is an EPC of $0.40, or $40 per 100 clicks.

What is a good EPC?

It depends on the niche, the order value and the commission, so there is no single figure. Compare offers on your own traffic: the one that earns more per click is the better one for you, whatever its commission rate.

Is EPC per click or per 100 clicks?

EPC means earnings per click, but many affiliate networks show it per 100 clicks. The per-100 number is simply 100 times the per-click one, so check the label before comparing two dashboards.

What does this calculator leave out?

It counts only clicks, sales, order value and commission. Refunds, reversed commissions, recurring payouts and sales your affiliate software did not credit to the click are not included.
