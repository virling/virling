How Virling picks the channel

Every visit gets one channel from its first pageview. The rules run in this order and the first match wins:

  1. utm_medium. cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social or paidsocial is Paid. email, newsletter or e-mail is Email. Case does not matter.
  2. Referrer host. A known AI, search, community or social host gives that channel. Any other host is Referral.
  3. utm_source, or ref when there is no utm_source. Used only when there is no referrer. A known name such as google, hn or chatgpt gives that channel. Any other value is Referral.
  4. Any other utm_medium with no referrer and no source is Referral.
  5. In-app browsers. With no referrer and no source, a user agent that names an app such as Instagram or LinkedIn credits that app.
  6. Everything else is Direct.

Referrers from your own domain and its subdomains are ignored, and so are returns from checkout and sign-in pages, so moving between your pages never changes the channel. This checker cannot see a user agent, so it skips the in-app browser rule. The full host and source lists are in the channels docs.

A worked example

The default above is a LinkedIn ad tagged utm_source=LinkedIn&utm_medium=paid-social, clicked from linkedin.com. The team expected Paid, but paid-social with a hyphen is not in the Paid list, so the first rule does not match. The referrer rule does: linkedin.com is a social host, so the visit is credited to Social with linkedin.com as the source. Changing the medium to paid_social or cpc makes it Paid.

If the same link had arrived with no referrer at all, as happens in some apps, the source rule would have matched linkedin and the visit would still be Social. If you see most of your traffic as Direct, why Direct is your biggest traffic source covers the usual causes.

Traffic source checker FAQ

Why does a visit show as Direct?

Direct means the visit arrived with no referrer, no utm_source or ref, no utm_medium and no in-app browser Virling recognises. Typed URLs, bookmarks, links in apps and documents that send no referrer, and tags misspelled so they are not read all end up there.

What is a referrer?

The address of the page the visitor clicked a link on, which the browser sends with the visit. Many sites send only their domain, and some apps and privacy settings send nothing.

Which wins, UTM tags or the referrer?

A paid or email utm_medium wins over everything. Otherwise the referrer wins, and utm_source or ref is used only when there is no referrer.

Why is utm_medium=paid_search not counted as Paid?

The Paid list is exact: cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social and paidsocial, in any case. paid_search or paid-social fall through to the referrer or source. The checker flags mediums that look paid or email but are not in the lists.

Is anything I paste sent anywhere?

No. The check runs in your browser and nothing you paste leaves the page.

Why does my Google ad show as Organic search?

The visit came from google.com and the link had no paid utm_medium, so the referrer decided the channel. Tag the ad link with utm_source=google and utm_medium=cpc and it counts as Paid.

Does the checker follow redirects?

No. It reads the landing URL you paste and does not open it. If a redirect might be dropping your tags, open the link in a private window and paste the address the browser ends up on.
