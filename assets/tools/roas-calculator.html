The ROAS formula

Return on ad spend is the revenue your ads brought divided by what you spent on them:

ROAS = revenue from ads / ad spend

ROAS uses revenue, not profit, so it says nothing on its own about whether the ads paid off. For that you need your gross margin: the share of each sale left after the cost of the product, hosting, payment fees and delivery. The ROAS at which the ads exactly pay for themselves is:

break-even ROAS = 1 / gross margin

Above it the ads earn more gross profit than they cost; below it they lose money on every sale.

A worked example

These are made-up numbers, the defaults above. $2,000 of ad spend brought $6,000 of revenue. ROAS is $6,000 / $2,000 = 3x, or 300%. At a 60% gross margin the break-even ROAS is 1 / 0.60 = 1.67x, so these ads clear it. The $6,000 of revenue leaves $6,000 × 0.60 = $3,600 of gross profit, and after the $2,000 of spend the ads made $1,600. At a 30% margin the same ads would need a ROAS of 3.33x to break even, and the 3x they returned would mean a $200 loss.

A subscription example

These are made-up numbers for a subscription, where the first payment is less than the customer's total. $1,500 of ad spend brought 25 customers at $40 a month, with an 80% gross margin, so break-even ROAS is 1 / 0.80 = 1.25x. The first payments come to 25 × $40 = $1,000: a ROAS of $1,000 / $1,500 = 0.67x and, after $1,000 × 0.80 = $800 of gross profit, a $700 loss.

If those customers stay 10 months on average, they pay 25 × $40 × 10 = $10,000. ROAS is then $10,000 / $1,500 = 6.67x, and the $8,000 of gross profit less the $1,500 of spend leaves $6,500. If all 25 pay a second month, gross profit so far is 2 × $800 = $1,600, which already covers the spend. Both readings are right, but only the first is money you have collected: use the second once your own customers have stayed that long. The CAC payback calculator works out the months to recover the spend, and the LTV calculator the customer's total.

Which number to change first

  • Revenue you cannot tie to the ads: fix that figure before you act on the ROAS.
  • One campaign below break-even: move or cut its spend.
  • Every campaign below break-even: work on price or product costs to raise the margin.
  • Only the first payment below break-even: check how long customers stay.
  • Above break-even: raise spend in steps and check the ROAS of the added spend.

ROAS needs revenue matched to the channel

The hard part of ROAS is not the division, it is the revenue. Ad platforms report the conversions they can see within their own attribution windows, and a sale that touched two platforms can be counted by both. For one platform against one store, see why Meta Ads purchases do not match Shopify. A blended ROAS across all channels hides which ones lose money. To get a ROAS you can trust per channel, each payment has to be tied to the visit that started it. ROAS calculator FAQ

How do you calculate ROAS?

Divide the revenue that ads brought by what you spent on those ads. $6,000 of revenue from $2,000 of spend is a ROAS of 3x, also written as 300%.

What is break-even ROAS?

The ROAS at which the gross profit from ad revenue exactly covers the ad spend. It is 1 divided by your gross margin, so at a 60% margin you need a ROAS of about 1.67x before the ads make money.

What is a good ROAS?

There is no single number. A ROAS is good when it is above your break-even ROAS with room for the costs this calculator leaves out, such as salaries, agency fees and refunds. A low-margin business needs a much higher ROAS than a high-margin one.

Why does my ROAS differ from what the ad platform reports?

Ad platforms count the conversions they can see with their own attribution windows, and several platforms can claim the same sale. Matching each payment to the visit and channel that brought it gives revenue per channel that adds up to what you actually collected.

What ROAS do I need to break even at a 50% margin?

2x. Break-even ROAS is 1 divided by your gross margin, and 1 / 0.50 = 2, so each $1,000 of ad spend has to bring $2,000 of revenue before the ads make money. At a 20% margin it is 5x and at an 80% margin 1.25x.

Should I use first-payment or lifetime revenue for a subscription's ROAS?

Work out both and label them. First-payment ROAS shows what you have collected so far and can sit below break-even for ads that pay off later. Lifetime ROAS depends on how long customers stay, so base it on the retention you have measured, not on a guess.
