How the calculator works

Expected clicks are the people who open the issue and then click your link:

clicks = list size × open rate × click rate

Customers are the clicks that buy, and revenue is what those customers pay:

customers = clicks × conversion rate and revenue = customers × customer value

Cost per click is the price divided by clicks, cost per customer is the price divided by customers, and ROI compares revenue with the price:

ROI = (revenue - price) / price

The break-even conversion rate is the share of visitors who must buy for revenue to equal the price:

break-even conversion rate = price / (clicks × customer value)

A worked example

With the defaults above, a sponsorship costs $1,000 in a newsletter with 20,000 subscribers. 40% open it and 3% of those click, so 20,000 × 40% × 3% = 240 visitors reach your site. These are example numbers, not a benchmark.

If 2% of them buy, that is 4.8 customers. At $250 each, they bring $1,200. Cost per click is $1,000 / 240 = $4.17, cost per customer is $1,000 / 4.8 = $208.33, and the ROI is ($1,200 - $1,000) / $1,000 = 20% on revenue.

The break-even conversion rate is $1,000 / (240 × $250) = 1.67%. Above that, the sponsorship pays back on revenue; below it, it does not. To judge profit, enter the customer value after your gross margin: at a 60% margin, $150 per customer, the same sponsorship needs 2.78% to pay back.

Measure the real result with a tagged link

An estimate tells you whether a sponsorship could pay back. Only the payments tell you whether it did. Clicks from email apps often arrive with no referrer, some readers type your URL or search your name later, and promo codes miss buyers who forget them, so newsletter revenue tends to land in Direct or Organic search.

Give each sponsorship its own link with utm_source set to the newsletter and utm_campaign set to the issue; the UTM builder makes one in seconds. Then join payments to the visits from that link, as in how to track sponsorship ROI, and put the real conversion rate and revenue back into this calculator before you book the next issue.

Newsletter sponsorship calculator FAQ

How do you calculate the ROI of a newsletter sponsorship?

Estimate the clicks as list size times open rate times click rate, the customers as clicks times your conversion rate, and the revenue as customers times what a customer is worth. ROI is revenue minus the price, divided by the price. A $1,000 sponsorship that brings $1,200 has an ROI of 20%.

What conversion rate do I need for a newsletter ad to pay back?

Divide the price by the expected clicks times your customer value. With 240 expected clicks and a $250 customer, a $1,000 sponsorship needs 1,000 / (240 × 250) = 1.67% of visitors to buy to break even on revenue.

Should the click rate be a share of opens or of the whole list?

This calculator takes it as a share of opens, so clicks are list size times open rate times click rate. If the newsletter quotes clicks as a share of the whole list, set the open rate to 100% and enter that rate instead.

Which customer value should I use?

Use the average order value for one-off purchases, or the revenue a customer brings in their first year for subscriptions and repeat buyers. To judge profit instead of revenue, multiply the value by your gross margin first.

How do I measure the real result of a sponsorship?

Give the newsletter its own tagged link with utm_source and utm_campaign, then join the payments that follow to the visits from that link. Without it, clicks from email apps often arrive with no referrer and the revenue lands in Direct.
