The break-even point formula

Each sale leaves a margin after the costs that come with it. You break even when the margins add up to your fixed costs:

payment fee = price × fee percent + fixed fee

margin per sale = price − variable cost − payment fee

units to break even = fixed costs / margin per sale, rounded up

revenue to break even = units to break even × price

units for target profit = (fixed costs + target profit) / margin per sale, rounded up

If the margin per sale is zero or negative, there is no break-even point: no number of sales covers the fixed costs.

A worked example

These are round example numbers, the same ones the calculator starts with, not data from a real business. A shop pays $6,000 a month in fixed costs and sells a product for $50. Each sale costs $18 for the product and shipping, plus a payment fee of 2.9% + $0.30, which is $1.75. The margin per sale is $50 - $18 - $1.75 = $30.25.

The shop breaks even at $6,000 / $30.25 = 198.35, rounded up to 199 sales, or $9,950 of revenue a month. For a $3,000 profit it needs $9,000 / $30.25 = 297.52, rounded up to 298 sales, or $14,900.

Now raise the price to $60 and keep the costs the same. The fee becomes $2.04 and the margin $39.96, so the break-even point falls to 151 sales. A 20% higher price takes 48 sales off the break-even point, as long as buyers accept it.

Lower your break-even point

There are three ways down: cut fixed costs, raise the price or cut the cost of each sale. A discount does the opposite; check it with the discount break-even calculator. Break-even point calculator FAQ

How do you calculate the break-even point?

Divide fixed costs by the margin per sale, which is the price minus the variable cost and the payment fee of one sale. With $6,000 of fixed costs a month and a margin of $30.25 per sale, $6,000 / $30.25 = 198.35, so you break even at 199 sales a month.

What counts as a fixed cost and what counts as a variable cost?

Fixed costs stay the same whether you sell one more unit or not, such as rent, salaries and software. Variable costs come with each sale, such as the product, packaging, shipping and payment fees. If a cost has both parts, split it.

How do I find the break-even point in revenue?

Multiply the units to break even by the price. You can also divide fixed costs by the margin per sale as a share of the price. The two differ by less than the price of one unit, because this calculator rounds up to whole units.

What if the price is at or below the variable cost?

Then you never break even. Each sale adds nothing toward fixed costs or loses money, so selling more does not help. Raise the price or lower the variable cost and fees until each sale leaves a margin.

How many units do I need for a target profit?

Add the target profit to fixed costs and divide by the margin per sale. With $6,000 of fixed costs, a $3,000 target and a margin of $30.25, that is $9,000 / $30.25 = 297.52, so 298 units. The result is profit before income tax.

Should I include advertising in fixed or variable costs?

A set monthly budget belongs in fixed costs. If you pay an amount for each sale, such as a commission or a steady cost per order, add it to the variable cost. Do not count the same spend in both.
