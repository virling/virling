How the calculator works

Podcast ads are sold per thousand downloads, so the calculator multiplies downloads per episode by the episodes you buy and divides the price by that total:

downloads = downloads per episode × episodes and CPM = price / downloads × 1,000

Nobody can click an ad they hear, so the next input is the visit rate: the share of downloads that end up on your site by any route, typed, searched or from the show notes. From there:

visits = downloads × visit rate, customers = visits × conversion rate and revenue = customers × customer value

Cost per visit divides the price by those visits. The return and the conversion rate at which the ad exactly pays for itself are:

ROI = (revenue - price) / price and break-even conversion rate = price / (visits × customer value)

Cost per download

Shows quote a CPM, but it helps to think per download: a $25 CPM is 2.5 cents a download. A download is not a listen. Apps fetch episodes in the background, and some listeners never press play or skip the ad. Ask whether the show's numbers follow the IAB Tech Lab podcast measurement guidelines, which filter out some of that.

Ask which downloads the price covers, too. A rate for downloads in the first 30 days leaves out listeners who find an old episode later: if the ad is baked into the episode they are a free bonus, and if it was inserted for a set run they never hear it.

Host-read or programmatic ads

A host-read ad is read by the host in their own words, often about their own use of the product. Listeners trust the host, so these ads tend to bring more visits per download, and they usually cost more per thousand.

Programmatic ads are inserted into many shows at once through an ad network, targeted by category or audience, and often voiced by the brand rather than the host. They cost less per download and reach more listeners, but expect a lower visit rate, and you may not know every show the ad played on, so give each network its own code and URL.

Run the calculator once for each with the same budget: the programmatic option with more downloads and a lower visit rate, the host-read one with fewer downloads and a higher rate. Then compare cost per visit and break-even.

Why promo codes show more sales than links

Many people listen while driving, walking or doing chores, with the phone in a pocket, so very few type a URL the moment they hear it. Days later they search your name or type your homepage, and the code they remember is the only thing that still ties the sale to the show.

So a podcast code usually counts more sales than the tracked link, which only sees listeners who typed the exact URL. The code can overcount too: once it leaks to a coupon site, strangers use it. Treat the link as the floor and the code as the ceiling, and add “which podcast?” to your how did you hear about us question, since “a podcast” alone does not name the show.

Virling measures the link: it credits each payment to a visit and does not read discount codes, so take redemptions from your store or payment provider. What discount codes miss shows how to compare the two.

A vanity URL on your own domain

Give each show a short path on your own domain, such as example.com/showname, for the host to say on air. Your own domain is easier to remember and sounds more trustworthy read aloud than a link shortener, and it keeps working if you change tools. Pick one word per show, without dashes or numbers that are hard to say.

Have the path redirect to your landing page with utm_source set to the show and utm_campaign to the sponsorship; the UTM builder makes the target URL. Check that the tags survive every redirect to the final page, and put the same URL in the show notes.

A typed address with no tags counts as Direct. With the tagged redirect, Virling credits the visit to the show and joins it to the payment that follows; tracked links in the docs covers how tagged visits are counted. Podcast revenue tracking has the full setup.

A worked example with made-up numbers

Say a show with 15,000 downloads per episode offers four host-read ads for $1,800. That is 60,000 downloads at a $30 CPM, or 3 cents a download. If 0.25% of downloads lead to a visit, 150 listeners reach your site, at $12 a visit. These numbers are invented to show the method, not benchmarks.

If 8% of those visitors buy, that is 12 customers. At $200 each they bring $2,400: $150 per customer and an ROI of ($2,400 - $1,800) / $1,800 = 33.33%. Break-even is $1,800 / (150 × $200) = 6%.

Compare a programmatic buy with the same $1,800 at a $15 CPM: 120,000 downloads, but at a 0.1% visit rate only 120 visits, at $15 each. More listeners, fewer visitors, and a dearer visit. Enter both sets of numbers above to check them.

Podcast sponsorship calculator FAQ

How do you calculate the ROI of a podcast sponsorship?

Multiply downloads per episode by the episodes you bought to get total downloads, then by your visit rate and conversion rate to get customers. Customers times customer value is revenue. ROI is what revenue exceeds the price by, as a share of the price: at the defaults, $1,000 for 4 episodes returning $1,250 is 25%.

How do you calculate podcast ad cost per download?

Divide the price by the total downloads across the episodes you bought. Podcast ads are usually quoted as CPM, the cost per thousand downloads: $1,000 for 20,000 downloads is $0.05 per download, or a $50 CPM.

What conversion rate do I need for a podcast ad to pay back?

Take the price and divide it by the visits you expect times your customer value. At the defaults, 100 visits and a $250 customer, a $1,000 ad needs 4% of visitors to buy. Remember that visits from a podcast arrive over days or weeks, so judge the rate after the last episode has had time to be heard.

Which customer value should I use?

Use the average order value for one-off purchases, or the revenue a customer brings in their first year for subscriptions and repeat buyers. To judge profit instead of revenue, multiply the value by your gross margin first.

How do I measure the real result of a podcast sponsorship?

Have the host say a vanity URL on your own domain that redirects to a tagged link, put the same link in the show notes, and give each show its own promo code. Join payments to the visits from the link, and compare them with code redemptions. A listener who types your plain address is counted as Direct.

Why does my podcast promo code show more sales than the tracked link?

Usually because listeners reached you another way: they typed your plain URL or searched your name, then used the code they heard. The code can also have leaked to a coupon site, where strangers use it. Search for the code online, and ask new customers which podcast they heard.

Is a host-read podcast ad worth more than a programmatic one?

Often per download, since listeners trust the host, but it also costs more. Compare cost per visit, not CPM: enter each offer in the calculator with its own downloads and visit rate, then measure each with its own code and URL to see which one actually paid back.
