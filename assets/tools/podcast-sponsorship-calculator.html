How the calculator works

Podcast ads are usually priced per thousand downloads, so the calculator starts with total downloads and the CPM:

downloads = downloads per episode × episodes and CPM = price / downloads × 1,000

Visits are the listeners who come to your site, customers are the visits that buy, and revenue is what those customers pay:

visits = downloads × visit rate, customers = visits × conversion rate and revenue = customers × customer value

Cost per visit is the price divided by visits, cost per customer is the price divided by customers, and ROI compares revenue with the price:

ROI = (revenue - price) / price

The break-even conversion rate is the share of visitors who must buy for revenue to equal the price:

break-even conversion rate = price / (visits × customer value)

A worked example

With the defaults above, a sponsorship costs $1,000 for 4 episodes of a show with 5,000 downloads each, so 20,000 downloads and a CPM of $1,000 / 20,000 × 1,000 = $50. If 0.5% of downloads lead to a visit, 100 listeners reach your site and each visit costs $10. These are example numbers, not a benchmark.

If 5% of them buy, that is 5 customers. At $250 each, they bring $1,250. Cost per customer is $1,000 / 5 = $200, and the ROI is ($1,250 - $1,000) / $1,000 = 25% on revenue.

The break-even conversion rate is $1,000 / (100 × $250) = 4%. Above that, the sponsorship pays back on revenue; below it, it does not. To judge profit, enter the customer value after your gross margin: at a 60% margin, $150 per customer, the same sponsorship needs 6.67% to pay back.

Track the real result with a unique link or code

The visit rate is the hardest number to guess, because a podcast ad is heard, not clicked. Listeners type the URL they heard days later, open show notes links that arrive with no referrer, or search your name, so podcast revenue tends to land in Direct or Organic search.

Give each show its own UTM link, with utm_source set to the show and utm_campaign set to the episode or sponsorship, and say a short vanity URL on air that redirects to it; the UTM builder makes the link in seconds. A unique promo code per show catches buyers who never use the link, and your payment provider counts its redemptions. Then put the real visit rate, conversion rate and revenue back into this calculator before you renew.

What the link, the code and the answer each miss

A podcast ad is the case where no single method is enough, so run all three for each show:

  • The tagged link or vanity URL. It counts visits, buyers and revenue per show. It misses listeners who type your plain address or search your name, and those who visit on a phone and pay on another device.
  • The discount code. It catches listeners however they reached checkout. It misses those who forget it or pay without it, cannot show how many came and did not buy, and credits the show for strangers if it leaks to a coupon site.
  • The how-did-you-hear answer. It can name a show that left no click and no code. It misses everyone who skips the question, and “a podcast” does not say which one, so ask for the name. The how did you hear about us template has a list to copy.

One listener can appear in all three, so never add the counts. Use the link as the figure you can check, and the code and the answers to judge how much it missed. Podcast sponsorship calculator FAQ

How do you calculate the ROI of a podcast sponsorship?

Multiply downloads per episode by the number of episodes, then by the share of downloads that visit your site and the share of visitors who buy, to get customers. Multiply customers by what a customer is worth to get revenue. ROI is revenue minus the price, divided by the price. A $1,000 sponsorship that brings $1,250 has an ROI of 25%.

How do you calculate podcast ad cost per download?

Divide the price by the total downloads across the episodes you bought. Podcast ads are usually quoted as CPM, the cost per thousand downloads: $1,000 for 20,000 downloads is $0.05 per download, or a $50 CPM.

What conversion rate do I need for a podcast ad to pay back?

Divide the price by the expected visits times your customer value. With 100 expected visits and a $250 customer, a $1,000 sponsorship needs 1,000 / (100 × 250) = 4% of visitors to buy to break even on revenue.

Which customer value should I use?

Use the average order value for one-off purchases, or the revenue a customer brings in their first year for subscriptions and repeat buyers. To judge profit instead of revenue, multiply the value by your gross margin first.

How do I measure the real result of a podcast sponsorship?

Give each show its own UTM link, say a vanity URL on air that redirects to it, and use a unique promo code as a cross-check. Then join the payments that follow to the visits from that link. Without it, listeners who type your URL arrive with no referrer and the revenue lands in Direct.

Why does my podcast promo code show more sales than the tracked link?

Usually because listeners reached you another way: they typed your plain URL or searched your name, then used the code they heard. The code can also have leaked to a coupon site, where strangers use it. Search for the code online, and ask new customers which podcast they heard.
