The churn rate formula

Customer churn rate is the share of the customers you had at the start of a period who cancelled during it:

customer churn = customers lost / customers at start

Revenue churn uses monthly recurring revenue instead of headcount, and counts downgrades as well as cancellations:

revenue churn = MRR lost / MRR at start

To state it as MRR kept instead, use the GRR calculator; to count upgrades as well, use the net revenue retention calculator.

If churn stays roughly steady, the average customer lifetime follows from it:

average lifetime = 1 / customer churn

Leave out customers who signed up during the month from both numbers. Counting them makes the base larger and the churn rate look lower than it is.

A worked example

With the defaults above, you start the month with 400 customers and 12 cancel. 12 / 400 = 3% customer churn, so 97% of customers stayed. At 3% a month the average customer stays about 1 / 0.03 = 33.3 months.

Those 400 customers paid $20,000 MRR at the start of the month, and cancellations and downgrades removed $500 of it. $500 / $20,000 = 2.5% revenue churn. Revenue churn is lower than customer churn here, which means the customers who left paid less than average.

Edge cases in churn

What counts as lost changes the rate more than most fixes do. Pick one treatment for each case and use it for every month and every channel:

CaseWhat to do
Failed cardsNot lost while your provider still retries the charge. When the retries run out and the subscription ends, count the customer as lost in that month. Keep these apart from people who chose to cancel, since the fix is a card update; the failed payment recovery calculator shows what it is worth.
DowngradesNot customer churn, because they still pay. They do count in revenue churn: a move from $50 to $20 adds $30 to MRR lost.
RefundsA refund alone is not churn. If the customer left with it, count them once, in the month they left. If they joined and left within the same month, they are in neither number, so track them as refund rate instead.
Cancelled, still in the paid periodCount them in the month the paid period ends, not the month they clicked cancel.
Paused subscriptionsCount them as lost in the month billing stops, and leave them out of the start of each month until they pay again.
Annual plansKeep them apart from monthly plans. They can only leave when their year ends, so mixing them in makes monthly churn look low. Of those whose year ended, count how many did not renew.
Customers who come backSomeone who returns within the month is not lost. A later return adds a customer in that month and does not undo the earlier loss.
UpgradesDo not take them off MRR lost. They belong in net revenue retention.

What to do with your churn rate

  • Turn it into customers. At 3% of 400 customers you need 12 new ones a month to stand still. If you bring fewer than that, the business is shrinking whatever the signup count says.
  • Split off failed cards. If they are a large part of the customers lost, fix retries and card reminders before the product.
  • One channel loses most of them. Read what the posts or ads there promise, and hold back spend on it until its customers stay longer.
  • Every channel loses them at the same rate. The cause is more likely the product, the price or the first weeks than the marketing.
  • Put the lifetime to use. Enter it in the LTV calculator to see what a customer is worth, per channel when the counts allow it.

Churn differs by channel

A single churn rate hides where it comes from. Customers from a launch day or a discount listing often leave after the first month, while customers from search or a referral stay for years, and the blended rate sits somewhere in between. Churn rate calculator FAQ

How is churn rate calculated?

Customers lost during a period divided by customers at the start of that period. Customers who signed up during the period are left out of both numbers, so new growth does not hide cancellations.

What is the difference between customer churn and revenue churn?

Customer churn counts people who left. Revenue churn counts the monthly recurring revenue they took with them, plus any downgrades. If larger customers stay and smaller ones leave, revenue churn is lower than customer churn.

How does churn relate to customer lifetime?

When churn is steady, the average customer stays about one divided by the churn rate. A 3% monthly churn means roughly 33 months. It is a simple estimate, since churn is usually higher in the first months.

Should I use monthly or annual churn?

Use the period that matches your billing. Monthly churn suits monthly plans. For annual churn, enter the customers at the start of a year and those lost during it, and read the lifetime as years instead of months.

Why does churn differ by channel?

Customers from different channels arrive with different intent. A launch or a discount site can bring many signups who leave after a month, while search or referrals bring customers who stay. Comparing churn per channel shows which ones are worth the spend.
