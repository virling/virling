The burn multiple formula

The burn multiple divides the cash a company burned in a period by the annual recurring revenue it added in that same period:

burn multiple = net burn / net new ARR

Net burn is cash going out minus cash coming in, not counting money raised. Net new ARR is ARR at the end of the period minus ARR at the start, so it already includes new customers, upgrades, downgrades and cancellations. The metric was introduced by David Sacks in The Burn Multiple (2020). The higher the multiple, the more the company burns for each unit of growth.

A worked example

This is an example with made-up numbers, the same as the defaults above. A company burned $600,000 over a quarter, and its ARR went from $2,000,000 to $2,400,000, so net new ARR is $400,000. The burn multiple is $600,000 / $400,000 = 1.5: it burned $1.50 of cash for each dollar of new ARR. Put the other way, each dollar burned added about 67 cents of ARR.

How to read the result

Lower is better. In the original article, Sacks calls a burn multiple of 2 reasonable for an early-stage startup and says that needing 3x burn or more to deliver growth suggests product-market fit is not what it appears to be. A result of zero or below means the company was cash flow positive in the period. If net new ARR is zero or negative, the multiple has no meaning: cash went out and ARR did not grow.

Why the burn multiple is more useful per channel

The burn multiple covers the whole company, so it cannot say which part of the spend is working. Marketing is usually the part that is easiest to change: paid search, sponsorships and content cost very different amounts and bring very different revenue. Comparing them needs each payment tied to the visit that started it. See MRR by acquisition channel for how to find which channels bring ARR for less spend. Burn multiple calculator FAQ

How is the burn multiple calculated?

Divide net burn by net new ARR for the same period. Net burn is the cash the company lost in the period; net new ARR is the change in annual recurring revenue over that period, after churn and downgrades.

What is a good burn multiple?

Lower is better: a lower multiple means less cash burned for each dollar of new ARR. David Sacks, who introduced the metric in The Burn Multiple (2020), calls 2x reasonable for an early-stage startup and treats 3x or more as a sign that growth is costing too much.

What if net new ARR is zero or negative?

Then the burn multiple has no meaningful value: the company spent cash and its ARR did not grow. The calculator shows no result in that case instead of a negative number.

What if the company is not burning cash?

If net burn is zero or negative, the company is cash flow positive for the period and the burn multiple is zero or below. Growth is not costing outside capital, which is the best case.

Should I use a month, a quarter or a year?

Any period works as long as net burn and net new ARR cover the same one. A quarter or a trailing twelve months smooths out a single large deal or a one-off expense.
