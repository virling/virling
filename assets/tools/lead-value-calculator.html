The lead value formula

A lead is worth the revenue it brings on average, which is the chance it becomes a customer times what a customer pays:

value per lead = lead to paid rate × first-year revenue per customer

new customers per month = leads per month × lead to paid rate

The most you can pay per lead and still break even on first-year revenue is the value per lead itself. Paying more loses money in the first year; paying less leaves room for the cost of serving the customer and for profit.

A worked example

These are round illustrative numbers, the same ones the calculator starts with, not data from a real company or a benchmark. A product gets 200 leads a month and 5% of them become paying customers, so 200 × 0.05 = 10 new customers. Each pays $1,200 in the first year, so a month of leads brings 10 × $1,200 = $12,000 of first-year revenue.

Each lead is worth 0.05 × $1,200 = $60, the same as $12,000 / 200. So $60 is the most this product could pay for a lead and break even on first-year revenue.

What to do with the number: B2B SaaS

  1. Turn it into a ceiling you can keep. Multiply the value per lead by your gross margin, then take off the sales time a lead uses. In a made-up case, $60 × 80% = $48, less $10 of sales time, leaves $38 as the most to pay.
  2. Split signups from demo requests. Run the calculator once for each kind and give each its own ceiling.
  3. Run it per channel. Put each result next to what the lead cost, from the cost per lead calculator. Hold spend where the cost is above the ceiling, and raise it in small steps where it is well below.
  4. Pick the weaker half to fix. Doubling the rate and doubling the revenue per customer raise the value equally. If many leads never take a call, work on the follow-up. If they buy the smallest plan, work on the offer.
  5. Check the rate against paid invoices. A booked call is not a sale. Track revenue from demo bookings shows how to join each invoice to the booking and the channel that brought it.

Why lead value differs by channel

One average hides big differences. A lead who found you by searching for the problem you solve, and a lead from a broad giveaway, can both fill in the same form and convert to paid at very different rates. To know which campaign each lead came from, capture UTM parameters in hidden form fields. Channels also bring different plan sizes, so the first-year revenue per customer moves too. Work out the lead to paid rate for each channel with the conversion rate calculator, and see trial-to-paid conversion by traffic source for how to credit each first charge to the visit that brought it.

Once you know the value of a lead from each channel, compare it with what each channel costs per lead. Spend more where a lead is worth more than it costs, and less where it is not.

Lead value calculator FAQ

How do you calculate the value of a lead?

Multiply your lead to paid rate by the revenue a new customer brings. If 5% of leads become customers and each customer pays $1,200 in the first year, a lead is worth 0.05 × $1,200 = $60 in first-year revenue.

How much should I pay per lead?

To break even on first-year revenue, pay no more than the value per lead. That figure ignores the cost of serving the customer and of sales time, so if you want to keep a margin, multiply it by your gross margin and pay less than that.

Why use first-year revenue instead of lifetime value?

First-year revenue is known sooner and needs no guess about churn, so it is a cautious ceiling. If you trust your retention figures, enter customer lifetime value instead and the result becomes the most a lead is worth over the whole relationship.

Should a signup and a demo request count as the same lead?

Usually not. A demo request often converts at a different rate than a free signup, so work out the value of each kind separately rather than averaging them together.

Why is the same lead worth more from one channel than another?

Leads from different channels convert to paid at different rates and buy different plans. Measure the lead to paid rate and the revenue per customer for each channel from real payments to see what a lead from each one is really worth.
