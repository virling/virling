The revenue per visitor formula

Revenue per visitor (RPV) is the revenue a group of visitors brought, divided by how many visitors there were:

revenue per visitor = revenue / unique visitors

With the defaults above, $1,800 from 1,200 visitors is $1.50 per visitor. Use the same date range for both numbers, count each person once, and use money you actually collected, after refunds. Dividing by sessions or page views instead makes the number look smaller than it is.

If you know your conversion rate and average order value, they give the same result: revenue per visitor = conversion rate × average order value. A 2% conversion rate and a $50 average order is 0.02 × $50 = $1.00 per visitor.

What the other results mean

  • Return on spend is revenue divided by cost. $1,800 from $600 of spend is 3.0x. For an ad channel this is return on ad spend (ROAS).
  • Profit is revenue minus cost, before product costs and payment fees.
  • Break-even revenue per visitor is cost divided by visitors: $600 over 1,200 visitors is $0.50. A channel whose revenue per visitor is below this costs more than it brings.
  • Against your site average divides the channel's revenue per visitor by your whole site's. 2.0x means each visitor from the channel is worth twice a typical visitor.

How to use it to compare channels

Run the calculator once per channel with the same date range, then rank channels by revenue per visitor rather than by traffic. A channel with few visitors and a high figure often deserves more time than a loud one. Count payments before trusting a result, since one payment moves a small channel a lot. For a step by step method and ways to raise the number, read what revenue per visitor is and how to calculate it. Example: revenue per visitor for four channels

These numbers are made up, not data from a real store. One store in one month, with each payment credited to one channel:

ChannelVisitorsRevenueRevenue per visitor
Organic search2,000$1,600$0.80
Paid search1,200$1,800$1.50
Email750$900$1.20
Referral50$300$6.00
All4,000$4,600$1.15

Paid search is the row the calculator starts with. The last row divides the sums, $4,600 / 4,000, so it is the site average to put in the optional field. Paid search is then 1.3x the average and referral 5.2x.

A high figure from a few buyers

Referral looks like the best channel, but its $300 came from two payments, one of $50 and one of $250. Take the larger one out and it is $50 / 50 = $1.00, below the site average. The money is real. It does not tell you what the next 50 visitors will bring.

So count payments, not only visitors, before you act. The number of payments a channel brings goes up and down by chance, typically by about its square root: 4 payments by 2, which is half, 16 by 4, which is a quarter, and 64 by 8, which is an eighth. To be fairly sure of a gap, let it be at least twice that swing:

  • A channel at double your average would have to lose half its payments to be ordinary. Wait for about 16.
  • A channel a third above your average would have to lose only a quarter. Wait for about 64.
  • Payments of very different sizes need more than that. Take the largest out and divide again: if the ranking changes, use a longer date range.

This is arithmetic for payments of similar size, not a benchmark. Until a channel gets there, repeat what brought it at a small cost and leave the other channels as they are.

When revenue per visitor and average order value disagree

Revenue per visitor is the conversion rate times the average order value, so a channel can be ahead on one and behind on the other. In the made-up store, email brought 30 orders from 750 visitors and organic search 40 orders from 2,000:

  • Email: 4% × $30 = $1.20 per visitor.
  • Organic search: 2% × $40 = $0.80 per visitor.

Organic search has the larger orders and email the more valuable visitors. Use revenue per visitor to decide where to look for more traffic, and average order value to decide what to change for the people who already buy. Revenue per visitor calculator FAQ

How is revenue per visitor calculated?

Revenue from the channel's visitors divided by the number of visitors. It is the fairest way to compare a channel that sends many cheap visits with one that sends a few that buy.

What is a good return on spend?

Above 1x the channel earns back what it costs. Revenue also has to pay for the product and fees, so the return you need depends on your margin.

What is break-even revenue per visitor?

What each visitor must bring for the channel to pay for itself: cost divided by visitors. If your site's revenue per visitor is below it, the channel loses money at that price.

Where do I get revenue per channel?

From an analytics tool that links visits to payments. Most web analytics stop at visits, so revenue per channel needs payment data joined to the visit that brought each customer.
