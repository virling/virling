The MER formula

The marketing efficiency ratio is all the revenue you took in divided by everything you spent on marketing in the same period:

MER = total revenue / total marketing cost

Marketing cost means every marketing line, not only ad spend: ads on every platform, agency and freelancer fees, influencer payments, affiliate commissions and marketing tools. Like ROAS, MER uses revenue, not profit. To know whether marketing paid for itself you need your gross margin: the share of each sale left after the cost of the product, hosting, payment fees and delivery. The MER at which marketing exactly pays for itself is:

break-even MER = 1 / gross margin

Above it, the gross profit on your revenue covers your marketing cost; below it, marketing costs more than the gross profit it sits against.

MER versus ROAS

ROAS divides the revenue a channel or ad platform claims by the spend on that channel. Each platform counts the conversions it can see in its own attribution window, so a sale that touched two platforms can be claimed by both and the platform totals can add up to more than you collected. MER sidesteps that: it takes the revenue in your bank and the cost in your books, so nothing is counted twice. That is why store owners who stopped trusting platform ROAS look at MER instead.

The trade-off is that MER is blended. It includes revenue from organic search, direct visits and repeat customers that no ad paid for, and it cannot tell you which channel earned what. A healthy MER can hide a channel that loses money, and a falling MER does not say which channel to cut.

A worked example

With the defaults above, a store took in $100,000 in a month and spent $20,000 on marketing. MER is $100,000 / $20,000 = 5x, so marketing cost was 20% of revenue. At a 50% gross margin the break-even MER is 1 / 0.50 = 2x, so this store clears it. The $100,000 of revenue leaves $50,000 of gross profit, and after the $20,000 of marketing that is $30,000 to pay for everything else. At a 20% margin the same store would need an MER of 5x just to break even, so the same month would leave nothing after marketing.

MER calculator FAQ

What is MER in marketing?

MER, the marketing efficiency ratio, is your total revenue divided by your total marketing cost over the same period. It is sometimes called blended ROAS because it does not try to split revenue between channels.

How do you calculate MER?

Divide total revenue by total marketing cost for the same period. $100,000 of revenue in a month with $20,000 of marketing cost is an MER of 5x, which means marketing cost was 20% of revenue.

What is the difference between MER and ROAS?

ROAS divides the revenue an ad platform or channel claims by the spend on that channel. MER divides all revenue by all marketing cost, including revenue from organic, direct and repeat customers. MER cannot be double counted across platforms, but it cannot tell you which channel earned the revenue.

What is break-even MER?

The MER at which the gross profit on your revenue exactly covers your marketing cost. It is 1 divided by your gross margin, so at a 50% margin you need an MER of 2x before marketing pays for itself.

What is a good MER?

There is no single number. An MER is good when it sits above your break-even MER with room for the costs this calculator leaves out, such as salaries, rent and refunds. A low-margin store needs a much higher MER than a high-margin one.
