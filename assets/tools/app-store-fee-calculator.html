App Store fees: a worked example

A $9.99 subscription with 100 sales a month is $999.00 of revenue. With a web checkout at 2.9% + 30 cents:

ChannelFee per saleFees a monthPayout a month
App Store, standard (30%)$3.00$299.70$699.30
App Store, reduced (15%)$1.50$149.85$849.15
Web checkout (2.9% + $0.30)$0.59$58.97$940.03

The fixed web fee per sale weighs more on cheap products: at $1.99, 30 cents is 15% of the price before the percentage fee, so the web fee comes to about 18% and costs more than a reduced store rate.

Web paywalls: the fee is the smaller question

A web paywall sells the subscription on a web page, and tools like RevenueCat then unlock it in the app. The fee is lower, as the table shows, but the bigger difference is what you learn. A web buyer is a visitor first, so the purchase can be tied to the ad, post or search that sent them. An in-app purchase usually starts in the store, where that trail is lost.

That changes where you spend. If one channel brings buyers who pay on the web and another brings installs that never convert, the fee you save matters less than knowing which channel to grow. Apple's App Review Guidelines let apps on the United States storefront link to other ways to buy, and Google Play lists separate rates for external web links; the rules differ by country, so check them before you move a purchase out of the app.

App Store fee FAQ

How much does Apple take from app sales?

The standard commission is 30%. It drops to 15% for members of the App Store Small Business Program, open to developers with up to $1 million in proceeds in the prior calendar year, and for auto-renewable subscriptions once a subscriber has a year of paid service.

How much does Google Play take?

Developers in the 15% tier pay 15% on their first $1 million of earnings each year, and auto-renewing subscriptions are 15% at any revenue. Above that, other purchases are up to 30%. In the US, EEA and UK, purchases from installs made after June 30, 2026 are 25% (20% plus a 5% billing fee), and Australia and Japan followed on September 30, 2026.

How does this calculator work out the fees?

The store fee is the price times the store commission. The web fee is the price times the web checkout percentage, plus the fee per sale. Payout is the price minus the fee, and the monthly figures multiply both by sales a month.

What does this calculator leave out?

Sales tax and VAT, so enter the price before tax. It also leaves out Apple's alternative terms in the EU, Google's program and external link rates, currency conversion, refunds and, on the web side, the cost of handling sales tax yourself or through a merchant of record.

Should I sell through a web paywall instead?

The fee is lower, but in-app purchase is easier for the buyer and the rules on linking out differ by store and country. Many apps offer both. Knowing which channel brings the buyers who pay matters more than the fee on each sale.
