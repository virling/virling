Marketing channel scorecard template

One row per marketing channel, filled in once a month. At the start of each month, enter last month's visitors, signups or trials, paying customers, revenue and spend for every channel. The template works out revenue per visitor and CAC, then marks each channel keep, test or cut by the rule written under the table. Copy it as CSV into the same sheet each month to see the trend. No signup, and everything stays in your browser.

The keep, test or cut rule

  • Cut a channel you pay for that brought no paying customers, or whose CAC is more than twice the CAC of all paid channels together (the total row).
  • Keep a channel that brought paying customers, earns at least the total revenue per visitor and, if you pay for it, has a CAC no higher than the total.
  • Test everything else: change one thing in it next month and score it again.

The rule compares each channel with your own totals, not with an industry figure. Read it over two or three months before you cut a channel, since one month can be noise.

How to use it each month

  • Pick the same dates every time. Score the last full calendar month, so each scorecard compares like with like.
  • Count paying customers, not only revenue. One large order can make a channel look strong when it brought a single buyer.
  • Enter spend per channel. Ad budget, sponsorships and tools paid for one channel. Leave it empty for channels that only cost time; they get no CAC.
  • Act on one row. Pick one channel marked test, change one thing in it, and see where it lands next month.
  • Keep a history. Copy as CSV puts the month in the first column, so each month's rows stack under the last in one sheet.

What each column means

  • Visitors: people who arrived from the channel in the month.
  • Signups or trials: accounts, trials or leads they started. Use whichever step comes before payment for you.
  • Paying customers: new customers whose first payment is credited to the channel.
  • Revenue: money those payments brought, after refunds.
  • Revenue per visitor: revenue divided by visitors. It lets a small channel that pays well stand out from a big one that does not.
  • CAC: spend divided by paying customers. The total row uses only the channels with spend, so free channels do not make paid ones look cheaper.

Marketing channel scorecard FAQ

What is a marketing channel scorecard?

A table with one row per channel that puts traffic, signups, paying customers, revenue and cost side by side, so you can compare channels on the same terms and decide where to spend next month.

How often should I update the scorecard?

Once a month, for the last full calendar month. Weekly numbers swing too much for most sites, and a channel needs a few months of rows before a cut is safe.

Should I score channels by revenue or by signups?

By revenue and paying customers where you can. Signups show interest, but a channel that brings many signups and few payments costs more than it looks. Keep signups in the row to see where people drop off.

How is CAC worked out for free channels?

It is not. CAC here is spend divided by paying customers, so a channel with no spend shows no CAC and is judged on revenue per visitor alone. If you want to count time, enter it as spend.

Why does the rule compare channels with my own totals?

Benchmarks from other businesses rarely match your prices and buyers. Comparing each channel with your own average tells you which channels do better or worse than the rest of your marketing.

Is this template free?

Yes. It runs in your browser with no signup. Nothing you type is sent anywhere; copy it as CSV or download the file.
