How to calculate retention rate

Customer retention rate is the share of the customers you had at the start of a period who are still customers at the end of it. New customers are taken out of the end count, so growth does not hide the ones who left:

retention rate = (customers at end − new customers) / customers at start × 100

Churn is the other side of the same number: churn rate = 100% − retention rate.

A subscription example

These are made-up numbers, the defaults above, for a subscription. You start the quarter with 400 customers and end it with 430, of which 60 are new. 430 − 60 = 370 of the original customers stayed, so 370 / 400 = 92.5% retention and 7.5% churn. The customer count grew by 30, yet 30 existing customers left.

A store example

A store has no cancellations, so you decide what counts as a customer first. Here it is anyone who ordered in the last 12 months, and the numbers are made up too. 1,000 people ordered in the 12 months before this year and 1,150 ordered this year. Of those 1,150, 870 were not among the 1,000: they ordered for the first time, or came back after a longer gap. Both groups count as new. That leaves 1,150 − 870 = 280 of the original customers, so 280 / 1,000 = 28.0% retention and 72.0% churn.

Monthly and yearly retention compared

A monthly rate compounds. If the same share of customers stays every month, the share of them left after a year is:

yearly retention = monthly retention ^ 12

At 95% a month that is 0.95 ^ 12 = 0.540, or 54.0% a year. The table is this formula worked out, not a benchmark:

Why retention differs by acquisition channel

A single retention rate hides where it comes from. Customers arrive with different intent depending on how they found you: a launch day or a discount listing can bring many buyers who leave after the first month, while search or a referral can bring customers who stay, and the blended rate sits somewhere in between. A channel that looks best on first-month revenue can be worth less than a smaller one whose customers keep paying.

To compare, run the calculator once per channel with the customers each channel brought. For subscriptions, the churn rate calculator gives the monthly loss and the implied customer lifetime, and the NRR calculator adds expansion and downgrades in revenue. For a store with one-off orders, the repeat purchase rate calculator measures how many buyers come back.

Customer retention rate calculator FAQ

How do you calculate customer retention rate?

Subtract the new customers gained during the period from the customers at the end, then divide by the customers at the start. 400 at the start, 430 at the end and 60 new gives (430 - 60) / 400 = 92.5%.

Why are new customers subtracted?

Retention asks how many of the customers you already had stayed. New customers were not there at the start, so counting them would let growth hide cancellations and could push the rate above 100%.

What is the difference between retention rate and churn rate?

They add up to 100%. Retention is the share of starting customers who stayed, churn is the share who left. A 92.5% retention rate is a 7.5% churn rate for the same period.

What period should I use?

Use a period that matches how often customers pay or buy: a month for monthly plans, a quarter or a year for annual plans or occasional purchases. Compare rates only across periods of the same length.

How do I turn monthly retention into yearly retention?

Raise the monthly rate to the power of 12. 95% a month is 0.95 ^ 12, or 54.0% a year, if the same share stays every month. Multiplying monthly churn by 12 overstates the loss: 5% times 12 says 60%, but the loss is 46.0%.

How do I calculate retention for a store without subscriptions?

Pick a window that fits how often people buy, such as 12 months, and count everyone who ordered in it as a customer. Then compare two windows in a row and count anyone in the second who was not in the first as new. In a made-up example, 1,000 buyers last year, 1,150 this year and 870 of them new gives (1,150 - 870) / 1,000 = 28.0%.
