What the fee covers

Paddle is a merchant of record: it sells your product on your behalf, so it is the seller on the receipt. Its pricing page lists the pay-as-you-go fee as covering payments, checkout and billing in one platform; global tax and regulatory compliance; fraud protection, chargebacks and buyer support; and no monthly fees, migration fees or hidden extras. Paddle's FAQ says it collects the correct rate of tax, tracks filing deadlines and submits sales tax returns, and that there is no additional cost for extra payment methods or payment-related buyer support. Paddle also offers international invoicing as an additional feature.

Paddle fee FAQ

How much does Paddle charge per sale?

On Paddle's pricing page, pay-as-you-go is 5% + 50 cents per checkout transaction, with no monthly fee. On a $29 sale that is $1.95, so you keep $27.05.

Does Paddle charge a monthly fee?

Not on pay-as-you-go. Paddle's pricing page says there are no monthly fees, migration fees or hidden extras. High-volume businesses can ask Paddle for custom pricing.

Does Paddle charge less on cheap products?

Paddle's FAQ says that if you sell products under $10 you can contact it for bespoke pricing. This calculator uses the standard 5% + 50 cents, which on a $5 sale is 75 cents, or 15% of the price.

What does this calculator leave out?

Bespoke and custom pricing, refunds, payout costs from your bank or payout provider, and currency conversion. Paddle's help centre says it adds no fees beyond the Paddle fee on payouts, but a wire in a different currency can carry a fee of 15 dollars, euros or pounds and converting your balance can carry a margin of up to 1.5%.

Why does Paddle cost more than a card processor?

Paddle is the merchant of record, so its fee covers sales tax and VAT compliance, fraud protection, chargebacks and buyer support as well as card processing. Compare it with a card processor in the Stripe fee calculator.
