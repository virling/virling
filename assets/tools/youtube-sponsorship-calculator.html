How the calculator works

YouTube sponsorships are often sold as a flat fee per video, so the calculator turns the price into an effective CPM, the cost per thousand views:

CPM = price / views × 1,000

Clicks are the viewers who follow the link to your site, customers are the clicks that buy, and revenue is what those customers pay:

clicks = views × click-through rate, customers = clicks × conversion rate and revenue = customers × revenue per customer

Cost per click is the price divided by clicks, and ROI compares revenue with the price:

ROI = (revenue - price) / price

The break-even conversion rate is the share of clicks that must buy for revenue to equal the price:

break-even conversion rate = price / (clicks × revenue per customer)

A worked example

With the defaults above, a sponsored video costs $2,000 and is expected to get 40,000 views, so the effective CPM is $2,000 / 40,000 × 1,000 = $50. If 0.5% of views click the link, 200 viewers reach your site and each click costs $10. These are example numbers, not a benchmark.

If 5% of them buy, that is 10 customers. At $250 each, they bring $2,500. Cost per customer is $2,000 / 10 = $200, and the ROI is ($2,500 - $2,000) / $2,000 = 25% on revenue.

The break-even conversion rate is $2,000 / (200 × $250) = 4%. Above that, the sponsorship pays back on revenue; below it, it does not. To judge profit, enter the revenue per customer after your gross margin: at a 60% margin, $150 per customer, the same sponsorship needs 6.67% to pay back.

Real results need tracked clicks joined to payments

The click-through rate is the hardest number to guess, and YouTube makes the real one hard to see. Most viewing happens in the mobile app, and links opened there often arrive with no referrer, so without a tag the click counts as Direct. Viewers who search your name or type your URL later leave no trace of the video at all.

Give each sponsored video its own UTM link, with utm_source=youtube and utm_campaign set to the creator and video, and ask the creator to put it in the description and pinned comment; the UTM builder makes the link in seconds. A unique promo code per creator catches buyers who never use the link. YouTube revenue tracking covers the setup step by step, and how to track sponsorship ROI covers joining payments to those visits. Then put the real click-through rate, conversion rate and revenue back into this calculator before you book the next video.

YouTube sponsorship calculator FAQ

How do you calculate the ROI of a YouTube sponsorship?

Multiply the expected views by the share of viewers who click the link and the share of clicks that buy, to get customers. Multiply customers by the revenue per customer to get revenue. ROI is revenue minus the price, divided by the price. A $2,000 sponsorship that brings $2,500 has an ROI of 25%.

How do you calculate the CPM of a YouTube sponsorship?

Divide the price by the views and multiply by 1,000. A $2,000 video that gets 40,000 views has an effective CPM of $50. Use the views the video actually gets, not the channel's subscriber count, since many subscribers never see a given video.

What conversion rate do I need for a YouTube sponsorship to pay back?

Divide the price by the expected clicks times your revenue per customer. With 200 expected clicks and a $250 customer, a $2,000 sponsorship needs 2,000 / (200 × 250) = 4% of clicks to buy to break even on revenue.

Which revenue per customer should I use?

Use the average order value for one-off purchases, or the revenue a customer brings in their first year for subscriptions and repeat buyers. To judge profit instead of revenue, multiply the value by your gross margin first.

How do I measure the real result of a YouTube sponsorship?

Give each video its own UTM link in the description and pinned comment, and use a unique promo code as a cross-check. Then join the payments that follow to the visits from that link. Without a tag, clicks from the YouTube app often arrive with no referrer and the revenue lands in Direct.
