How the calculator works

Creators usually quote a flat fee per video, so the calculator first turns that fee into an effective CPM, what you pay per thousand views:

CPM = price / views × 1,000

Then it follows the viewer from the video to the checkout:

clicks = views × click-through rate, customers = clicks × conversion rate and revenue = customers × revenue per customer

The fee divided by the clicks is the cost per click. ROI and the break-even conversion rate, the share of clicks that must buy for revenue to match the fee, follow from the same numbers:

ROI = (revenue - price) / price

break-even conversion rate = price / (clicks × revenue per customer)

Flat fee or price per view

A flat fee buys one video whatever views it ends up with, so you carry the risk that it flops. Some creators and agencies will price on views instead: a rate per thousand views, counted on a set day such as day 30, sometimes with a cap. That moves the risk to the creator.

To compare the two offers, enter the flat fee and the views you expect by the cut-off day. If the effective CPM comes out above the per-view rate you were offered, the per-view deal is cheaper at that view count. If you think the video will beat your estimate, the flat fee is the better bet.

Integrated or dedicated video

An integrated sponsorship is a short segment inside the creator's normal video. A dedicated video is about your product from the title onwards. Dedicated videos usually cost more and can get fewer views than the channel's normal uploads, since viewers can tell from the thumbnail that it is an ad, but those who watch are already interested, so more of them click.

To weigh them, keep the channel's usual views and click-through rate for the integration, lower the views and raise the rate for the dedicated video, and see which gives the cheaper click and the lower break-even. For an integration, ask where the segment sits too: a read in the first minutes is seen by more of the audience than one near the end, when many viewers have left.

The first 30 days and the long tail

A video's views do not arrive at once. Most come in the first weeks, while YouTube shows the upload to subscribers and on the home page; after that, views depend on search and suggested videos.

A tutorial or review that ranks in YouTube search can keep bringing views for months or years, long after a news or trend video has stopped. The link stays in the description the whole time, so the sponsorship keeps sending visitors after the report the creator sends at day 30, at no extra cost.

Judge the deal on the views you expect at 30 days, since that is what you can check soon. Then look at sales from the same link again at three and six months: every sale after day 30 lowers the real cost per customer.

A worked example with made-up numbers

Say a creator offers an integrated read for a flat $3,000, and their recent videos had about 75,000 views at 30 days. The effective CPM is $3,000 / 75,000 × 1,000 = $40. If 0.4% of viewers click, that is 300 clicks at $10 each. None of these figures come from a real deal; they only show the arithmetic.

At a 3% conversion rate, 9 of those clicks buy. At $400 each they bring $3,600, so each customer costs $3,000 / 9 = $333.33 and the ROI is ($3,600 - $3,000) / $3,000 = 20%. Break-even is $3,000 / (300 × $400) = 2.5%, so the video pays back if more than 2.5% of clicks buy.

If the same creator offered $35 per thousand views instead, 75,000 views would cost $2,625 and the ROI would be 37.14%. Now add the long tail: if the video reaches 120,000 views by month six at the same rates, the flat fee brings 480 clicks, 14.4 customers and $5,760, a 92% ROI on the original $3,000. Type them into the calculator to follow along.

Track it: a description link and a pinned comment

Ask the creator to put the same tagged link in two places: the first lines of the description, above the cut where YouTube hides the rest, and a pinned comment, which phone viewers see under the video without opening the description. Tag it with utm_source=youtube and utm_campaign set to the creator and video; the UTM builder makes it in seconds.

A link cannot be clicked on a TV, so give the creator a short promo code to say aloud as well, and ask new customers which channel they watched; the how did you hear about us template has a question to copy.

Without the tag, a click from the YouTube app usually arrives with no referrer and is counted as Direct. Tagged, the visit counts under YouTube and the creator, and Virling connects it to whatever that visitor pays later; channels in the docs lists how each source is classified. YouTube sponsorship calculator FAQ

How do you calculate the ROI of a YouTube sponsorship?

Start from the views the video will get, not the subscriber count. Views times click-through rate gives clicks, clicks times your conversion rate gives customers, and customers times revenue per customer gives revenue. Subtract the fee and divide by the fee: a $2,000 video that brings in $2,500 returns 25%.

How do you calculate the CPM of a YouTube sponsorship?

Divide the price by the views and multiply by 1,000. A $2,000 video that gets 40,000 views has an effective CPM of $50. Use the views the video actually gets, not the channel's subscriber count, since many subscribers never see a given video.

What share of clicks has to buy for a YouTube sponsorship to pay back?

Divide the fee by the clicks times your revenue per customer. At the defaults, $2,000 / (200 clicks × $250) is 4%. If your site converts below that, ask for a lower fee, a price per view, or a creator whose viewers click more.

Should I enter the order value or what a customer is worth over a year?

For a one-time product, your average order. For a subscription, what a customer pays in the first year, since viewers who sign up keep paying long after the video's views slow down. For profit rather than revenue, enter only the margin you keep on that amount.

How do I see which customers a sponsored video brought?

Put a tagged link in the description and a pinned comment, and give the creator a code to say aloud for viewers watching on a TV. Then join payments to the visits from that link. An untagged click from the YouTube app usually arrives with no referrer, so its revenue shows as Direct.

How many sales does a YouTube sponsorship need to break even?

Divide the price by your revenue per customer and round up. A $2,000 video needs 8 sales at $250 each, 20 at $100 or 40 at $50. Then divide the sales by the expected clicks to see the share that must buy: 8 sales from 200 clicks is 4%.

Should I pay a flat fee or a price per view for a YouTube sponsorship?

A flat fee is cheaper if the video beats the views you expected, and a price per view is cheaper if it falls short. Divide the flat fee by the views you expect by the cut-off day and multiply by 1,000: if that is above the per-view rate offered, the per-view deal costs less at that view count.
