The CPC formula

Cost per click is what you paid for each visit an ad sent to your site:

CPC = ad spend / clicks

Revenue per click is what each of those visits brought back in payments:

revenue per click = revenue / clicks

A click pays for itself when the gross profit it brings covers its cost, so the break-even CPC is:

break-even CPC = revenue per click × gross margin

With paying customers, the calculator also shows the share of clicks that became customers and the cost per customer, ad spend / paying customers.

A worked example

These are made-up numbers, the defaults above, not a benchmark. A campaign spent $2,000 and got 1,600 clicks, so the CPC is $2,000 / 1,600 = $1.25. Forty of those visitors paid, 2.5% of clicks, at a cost of $2,000 / 40 = $50 per customer.

They paid $4,800 in total, so revenue per click is $4,800 / 1,600 = $3.00. At a 60% gross margin, each click brings $3.00 × 0.60 = $1.80 of gross profit, and that is the break-even CPC. At $1.25 a click, the campaign earns $0.55 of gross profit per click; if its CPC rose above $1.80 it would lose money even though revenue per click still beat it.

Why CPC only matters next to revenue per click

A CPC on its own says what a visit cost, not what it was worth. A $0.40 click from people who never buy loses money, and a $4 click from buyers who spend $300 can be the best campaign you run. Cutting CPC often means bidding on cheaper, broader traffic that converts less, so the CPC falls and revenue per click falls faster.

Compare each campaign's CPC with its own revenue per click, the same idea as revenue per visitor applied to paid clicks. The gap between them is what the campaign earns per visit. Per customer, the same check is cost per customer against the break-even CPA, and across a whole channel it is CAC by channel. To see the return as a ratio, use the ROAS calculator; for placements priced per thousand impressions, the CPM calculator.

The revenue side is where it goes wrong. Ad platforms count the conversions they can see within their own windows, buyers often come back days later through search or a typed URL, and the same customer can be claimed by two platforms. Revenue per click is only as good as the join between your payments and the visits that started them.

The most you can pay per click, from revenue per visitor

Before a campaign has sales of its own, you can set a ceiling from the revenue per visitor of a similar one:

highest CPC = revenue per visitor × gross margin × share of clicks that arrive

The last part is there because revenue per visitor divides by visitors your site counted, and some clicks never load the page. Say 1,440 of the 1,600 example clicks arrived, which is 90%. Revenue per visitor is $4,800 / 1,440 = $3.33, a visit can cost $3.33 × 0.60 = $2.00, and a click $2.00 × 0.90 = $1.80. That is the break-even CPC the calculator shows, reached from the visitor side.

Break-even leaves nothing over. To keep a quarter of the gross profit, pay three quarters of the ceiling: $1.80 × 0.75 = $1.35. The example's $1.25 is under that.

Three mistakes with CPC

  • Dividing by a different kind of click. Some ad reports have a column for all clicks, including ones on a profile or a like, and another for clicks to your site. Use clicks to your site, and the same column for every campaign.
  • Judging it on a handful of sales. Revenue per click from 4 customers can halve or double with the next two. Keep the dates long enough for more payments before you change a bid.
  • Using one ceiling for the whole account. People who search your name and people who have never heard of you are worth different amounts per click. An average ceiling overpays for one group and underbids for the other.

CPC calculator FAQ

How do you calculate cost per click?

Divide what you spent by the clicks it bought. $2,000 of spend that brought 1,600 clicks is a CPC of $2,000 / 1,600 = $1.25.

What is revenue per click?

It is the revenue your paying customers brought divided by the clicks that brought them. If 1,600 clicks led to $4,800 of payments, revenue per click is $3.00. It is the number CPC has to be compared with.

What is a break-even CPC?

It is the most you can pay per click before the ads lose money: revenue per click times your gross margin. At $3.00 of revenue per click and a 60% margin, the break-even CPC is $1.80. Leave the margin blank to see the CPC where spend equals revenue.

Is a low CPC always better?

No. A low CPC on visitors who never buy loses money, and a high CPC can pay if those visitors buy often or spend a lot. Judge a CPC against the revenue per click of the same campaign, not against other campaigns' CPCs.

Where does the revenue per click number come from?

From the payments made by people who arrived through the clicks you paid for. Ad platforms report the conversions they can see in their own windows, so the reliable source is your payments joined to the visits that started them, per campaign.

How do I set a maximum CPC for a new campaign?

Take the revenue per visitor of the most similar campaign you have measured, multiply by your gross margin and by the share of clicks that arrive on your site. At $3.33 per visitor, a 60% margin and 90% arriving, that is about $1.80. Bid below it, and replace the estimate once the campaign has payments of its own.
