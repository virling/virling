The chargeback rate formula

Chargeback rate is the share of a month's transactions that buyers disputed with their card issuer:

chargeback rate = chargebacks / transactions

The monthly cost counts the order amount you lose on each chargeback plus the fee your processor charges for each dispute:

monthly cost = chargebacks × (average order value + dispute fee)

That is the cost if you lose every dispute. Winning one returns the order amount; whether the fee comes back depends on your processor. Goods already shipped and the time spent answering disputes are not in the number.

The distance to a threshold is the threshold minus your rate, and the room left is how many more chargebacks the same number of transactions can take before reaching it:

chargebacks to threshold = transactions × threshold - chargebacks

A worked example

With the defaults above, the month has 2,000 transactions and 8 chargebacks. 8 / 2,000 = 0.4% chargeback rate. At a $60 average order and a $15 dispute fee, each chargeback costs $75, so the month costs 8 × $75 = $600.

The rate is 0.1 points under Visa's 0.5% non-compliant VAMP ratio: 2,000 × 0.005 = 10 chargebacks would reach it, so 2 more would. It is 1.1 points under the 1.5% Mastercard ECM rate, which 30 chargebacks would reach, and 2.6 points under the 3% HECM rate, which 60 would reach. Mastercard also needs at least 100 chargebacks in the month, so at this volume the rate alone does not enter its program.

Find the traffic sources that bring high-dispute buyers

A blended chargeback rate hides where disputes come from. Buyers from some sources are more likely to dispute: a deal site, a coupon shared far beyond its audience, or an ad that promises more than the product does can bring orders that come back as chargebacks, while buyers from search or a referral who knew what they wanted rarely do. Friendly fraud, where a buyer does not recognise the charge, also clusters around sources that bring impulse buyers.

Chargeback rate calculator FAQ

How is chargeback rate calculated?

Divide the chargebacks in a month by the transactions in that month. The card networks each use their own version: Mastercard divides this month's chargebacks by last month's transactions, and Visa's VAMP ratio counts early fraud warnings as well as disputes.

What chargeback rate is too high?

According to Stripe's guide to card network monitoring programs, Mastercard places a business in its Excessive Chargeback Merchant level at 1.5% and 100 chargebacks in a month, and the High level at 3% and 300. Visa's VAMP is non-compliant at 0.5% and 5 disputes and fraud warnings, and excessive at 1.5% and 1,500 in most regions. Your processor may act before you reach them.

Is a chargeback the same as a refund?

No. You choose to give a refund. A chargeback is a buyer asking their card issuer to reverse the payment, which pulls the money back, usually adds a dispute fee, and counts toward the network thresholds. A refund given before a dispute is opened avoids the chargeback.

What does a chargeback cost?

If you lose the dispute, the order amount plus the dispute fee, and any goods already shipped. The calculator counts the order amount and the fee. Winning a dispute returns the order amount; whether the fee comes back depends on your processor.

Why do some traffic sources bring more chargebacks?

Buyers arrive with different intent. Deal sites, widely shared coupons and ads that overpromise can bring buyers who dispute, while search and referrals bring buyers who knew what they wanted. Joining each payment to the visit that brought it shows which sources those are.
