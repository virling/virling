The influencer ROI formula

The cost of a post is the creator fee plus what the product you sent cost you. The revenue it brought is the visits it sent, times the share of those visitors who paid, times what they paid:

revenue per post = visits × conversion rate × average order value

ROI = (revenue per post - cost of the post) / cost of the post

The conversion rate at which the post exactly pays for itself is:

break-even conversion rate = cost of the post / (visits × average order value)

This ROI uses revenue, not profit. To see whether a post paid off after product and delivery costs, put its cost and revenue with your gross margin into the ROAS calculator.

A worked example

These are made-up numbers, the defaults above. A creator charged $500 and you sent product that cost $50, so the post cost $550. It sent 1,200 visits and 2% of them paid, which is 24 orders. At $60 each that is $1,440 of revenue. ROI is ($1,440 - $550) / $550 = 162%. The break-even conversion rate is $550 / (1,200 × $60) = 0.76%, so the post would still have paid for itself if fewer than 1 in 100 visitors had bought.

Creator A sent five times the clicks, at $950 / 3,000 = $0.32 each against $350 / 600 = $0.58 for creator B, and brought more revenue. But 15 × $60 = $900 does not cover $950: ROI is ($900 - $950) / $950 = -5.26%. Creator B brought 12 × $60 = $720 for $350: ($720 - $350) / $350 = 106%. Revenue per click shows why: $900 / 3,000 = $0.30 for A and $720 / 600 = $1.20 for B.

12 to 15 orders each is a small sample, so run more than one post before you drop a creator.

Give each creator their own link or code

Creator reports show views and clicks, not payments. To fill in this calculator with real numbers, each creator needs something that only they share:

  • A tagged link. Put the platform in utm_source, influencer in utm_medium and the creator in utm_campaign. The UTM builder makes these links, and the influencer UTM link generator makes one per creator from a list of names. A link counts every visit and which of those visitors paid, but misses people who never click it.
  • A discount code. Your store counts it at checkout, even when the buyer typed your plain URL. It misses buyers who do not enter it, and a code that leaks credits the creator for sales they did not bring. Add the discount to your costs, or enter the order value after it.

Use both and compare them: discount code attribution explains what each gap means. The full setup, including sharing results with creators, is in how to track influencer sales.

Influencer ROI calculator FAQ

How do you calculate influencer ROI?

Add up what the post cost you: the creator fee plus the cost of any product you sent. Work out the revenue it brought, which is visits times conversion rate times average order value. ROI is revenue minus cost, divided by cost. A $550 post that brings $1,440 of revenue has an ROI of about 162%.

Should gifted product count as a cost?

Yes. Product you send a creator costs you what it took to make and ship, even if no fee changes hands. Enter that cost so a gifted post is compared fairly with a paid one.

What is the break-even conversion rate?

The conversion rate at which the post brings in exactly as much revenue as it cost. It is the cost of the post divided by visits times average order value. If the visitors a creator sends convert below it, the post lost money on a revenue basis.

Why compare creators by revenue instead of clicks?

Clicks do not pay for the post. Two creators with the same clicks can bring very different revenue, so compare them by the payments their visitors made. That needs one tagged link per creator and each payment joined to the visit that led to it.

Can a creator with fewer clicks have a better ROI?

Yes. In a made-up example, a creator who cost $950 sent 3,000 clicks and $900 of revenue, an ROI of -5.26%. Another cost $350 and sent 600 clicks and $720 of revenue, an ROI of 106%. The second audience was more likely to buy, and the post cost less.

Should I give each creator a link or a discount code?

Both, and never share either between creators. A tagged link counts the visits and which of those visitors paid. A code catches buyers who never clicked the link, but misses those who do not enter it and can leak to people the creator never reached.
