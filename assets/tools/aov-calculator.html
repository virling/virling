The average order value formula

Average order value (AOV) is what a single order is worth on average:

average order value = revenue / number of orders

Use the same date range for revenue and orders, and the same definition of revenue for every channel. For the overall figure, add up the revenue and the orders of all channels and divide those sums, rather than averaging the channel AOVs, so a channel with a few orders does not count as much as one with hundreds.

A worked example

These are round illustrative numbers, the same ones the calculator starts with, not data from a real store. A store takes $30,000 from 500 orders in a month, an average order value of $60. By channel: organic search brought $12,000 from 200 orders ($60), paid social $6,000 from 150 orders ($40), email $7,500 from 100 orders ($75), referral $3,000 from 30 orders ($100) and other sources $1,500 from 20 orders ($75).

Referral has the highest average order value at $100, but only 30 orders, so a handful of large orders can move it a lot. Paid social has the lowest at $40, which matters when you set what an ad click can cost: a channel with small orders needs a lower cost per order to break even.

Look at orders and revenue together

A high average order value on its own does not make a channel the best one. All96$4,600$47.92

The last row divides the sums, $4,600 / 96. Averaging the four channel figures would give $73.75, because two referral orders would count as much as 40 from search.

A high average from a few buyers

Referral's two orders were $50 and $250. Without the larger one its average is $50, close to the store's $47.92. How many orders to wait for depends on how large one unusual order can be. Say it is five times a usual one:

  • With 2 orders, it makes the average 3 times the usual.
  • With 10 orders, it adds 40%: nine usual orders and one worth five are 14 / 10.
  • With 40 orders, it adds 10%: 44 / 40.

Wait until one such order could not make the gap you see. Paid search is 57% above the store average with 24 orders, where an order of five times the usual adds about 17%, so a single order does not explain it. If your largest orders are ten times the usual, or if there are several, wait for more. Then take the largest order out and divide again: if the ranking changes, use a longer date range. This is arithmetic, not a benchmark, and average order value by traffic source shows the median as another check.

When average order value and revenue per visitor disagree

Average order value leaves out everyone who did not buy. In the made-up store, organic search orders average $40 and email orders $30, but the 40 search orders took 2,000 visitors and the 30 email orders took 750:

  • Organic search: $1,600 / 2,000 = $0.80 per visitor.
  • Email: $900 / 750 = $1.20 per visitor.

Each email visitor is worth more although each email order is smaller. Use average order value to decide what to offer buyers, such as a bundle for email readers, and revenue per visitor to decide which channel deserves more traffic. Average order value calculator FAQ

How do you calculate average order value?

Divide revenue by the number of orders in the same period. $30,000 of revenue from 500 orders is an average order value of $60.

Should average order value include tax, shipping and refunds?

Pick one definition and use it for every channel. Sales tax is not your money, so most stores leave it out, and refunded orders should come off the revenue so a channel full of returns does not look better than it is.

Is average order value per order or per customer?

Per order. A customer who buys three times counts as three orders, so a channel that brings back repeat buyers can have a modest average order value and still earn a lot per customer.

Why is average order value different for each channel?

Each channel brings different buyers at a different moment: people from an email may already know the products and buy more, while a first visit from a social ad may be a single small purchase. Discount codes shared on one channel also lower its average order.

Where do I get revenue and orders per channel?

From a tool that links each order to the visit that brought the buyer. Store reports often file many orders under direct or unknown, so revenue per channel needs every order joined to its traffic source.
