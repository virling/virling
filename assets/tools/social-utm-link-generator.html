UTM links for social media

Enter the page you want people to land on and a campaign name to get one tagged link for each platform: X, LinkedIn, Instagram bio, TikTok bio, YouTube description, Reddit, Threads, Bluesky, Discord and your newsletter. Copy one link or all of them. The links are built in your browser as you type.

One link per platform

How each link is built: your page URL, plus utm_source set to the platform name in lowercase, utm_medium set to social (or email for the newsletter), and utm_campaign set to your campaign name in lowercase with spaces turned into hyphens. The bio and description links also get utm_content, so you can tell them apart from links in posts.

These links say where a click came from. Virling shows what it was worth: it links every visit to the payment it led to and credits each payment to the source, campaign and landing page of the visit that brought it. See how channels are decided in the docs. 14 days free.

The sources are lowercase platform names because that is how Virling recognises them. A utm_medium of email is always Email. For the rest, the referrer decides when the browser sends one, and the utm_source decides when it does not, so reddit lands in Communities and the other platforms in Social either way. A worked example

With the defaults above, the landing page is https://example.com/pricing and the campaign is spring-launch. If you type Spring Launch instead, it becomes spring-launch, so every platform groups under one campaign.

The X link is https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=x&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=spring-launch. The Instagram bio link adds where it sits: https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=spring-launch&utm_content=bio. The newsletter link uses the email medium: https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=spring-launch.

If your URL already has a query string, the tags are added after it, and any UTM tag already in the URL is replaced.

What these links miss

A tagged link tells you which platform a visit came from. It does not tell you whether that visit paid. A platform can send the most clicks and the fewest buyers, and another can send a handful of visits that turn into your largest customers. Counting clicks or visits per source ranks platforms by attention, not by money.

To compare platforms you need revenue per channel: each payment joined to the visit that led to it, then added up by source and campaign. Virling does that from your real payments, so the same utm_source and utm_campaign values you tag here show up next to the revenue they brought. Social media UTM link FAQ

What utm_source should I use for each social platform?

The platform name in lowercase: x, linkedin, instagram, tiktok, youtube, reddit, threads, bluesky and discord. Use the same value every time, so one platform never splits into several rows.

What utm_medium should social links use?

Use social for organic posts and profile links. Keep paid ads on a paid medium such as paidsocial, so ad clicks and organic clicks from the same platform stay apart.

Should I tag the link in my Instagram or TikTok bio?

Yes. Links opened inside apps often arrive with no referrer, so without tags many tools count those visits as Direct. The utm_content=bio tag also tells bio clicks apart from links you share elsewhere on the same platform.

Why is Reddit grouped as Communities and not Social?

Virling puts discussion sites such as Reddit and Hacker News in their own Communities channel, because visits from a thread behave differently from visits from a feed. The utm_source is still reddit.

Do UTM tags show which platform brings revenue?

Not on their own. They name the source of a visit. To see revenue per platform, a tool has to join each payment to the tagged visit that led to it, which is what Virling does.
