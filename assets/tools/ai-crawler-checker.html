How to detect ClaudeBot, GPTBot and other AI crawlers

robots.txt says who may come. Your server logs say who did. AI crawlers send their token in the User-Agent header, so search an access log for it:

grep -iE "GPTBot|OAI-SearchBot|ChatGPT-User|ClaudeBot|Claude-(SearchBot|User)|Perplexity" access.log

A user agent can be faked, so treat a match as a claim. OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity and DuckDuckGo publish the IP addresses their bots use, and you can compare against those. Google-Extended and Applebot-Extended should not appear in logs, because they are robots.txt tokens and not bots that visit. The AI crawlers docs have snippets that count crawler hits by page. To count the hits in a log you already have, paste its lines into the AI crawler log analyzer.

How to read the result

  • Allowed, no rule names it. No group in the file names the bot and there is no * group. Crawlers read that as permission.
  • Allowed or Blocked, in the * group. The bot has no group of its own, so your general rules decide. Give it a group to treat it differently.
  • Allowed or Blocked by its own group. The bot follows only the group that names it and ignores the * group. A bot with its own group needs every Disallow you still want repeated there.

Blocked means your robots.txt asks the bot to stay away. It does not prove the bot will: the table marks the ones whose operators say a user's request can override the file. A firewall or CDN can also block a bot that robots.txt allows, and this check cannot see that.

Allow every AI crawler

Nothing is needed if the file has no rule against them. To state it, or to let them past a strict * group, name them and repeat any private paths below the Allow line:

User-agent: GPTBot
User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
User-agent: ChatGPT-User
User-agent: ClaudeBot
User-agent: Claude-SearchBot
User-agent: Claude-User
User-agent: PerplexityBot
User-agent: Perplexity-User
User-agent: Google-Extended
User-agent: Applebot-Extended
Allow: /

Block training, keep search and answers

This names the tokens whose operators document training use, plus CCBot. The search and answer bots keep following your other rules:

User-agent: GPTBot
User-agent: ClaudeBot
User-agent: Google-Extended
User-agent: Applebot-Extended
User-agent: Amazonbot
User-agent: meta-externalagent
User-agent: CCBot
Disallow: /

Google-Extended also covers grounding in some Gemini products, and meta-externalagent also indexes content for Meta's products, so those two lines can cost you more than training.

Block one bot from one folder

User-agent: ClaudeBot
Disallow: /members/

Paste the new file above before you publish it. Changes are not instant: OpenAI says its search results can take about 24 hours to adjust, Perplexity and Meta say up to 24 hours, and DuckDuckGo says 72 hours. Blocking Googlebot is a different decision, because it controls Google Search itself.

After crawling: do AI visits pay?

A crawl is not a visit. A bot that reads a page may lead to a cited link, the link may bring a visitor, and the visitor may buy. Only the last two show up in your revenue, so decide what to block with those numbers next to the crawl counts.

  • Count the crawls. See which pages each company's bots read, and whether they are the pages you want quoted: AI crawlers docs.
  • Measure the visits by what they pay. AI assistants usually send fewer people than search, so compare revenue per visitor and not totals: is AI search traffic worth anything?
  • Tie one assistant to payments. Follow a ChatGPT referral from the visit to the sale: ChatGPT revenue tracking.
  • Compare before and after a block. If you block a bot, watch that company's visits and revenue in the following weeks.

questions

AI crawler checker FAQ

Should I block AI crawlers?

It depends on what you want. Blocking training crawlers such as GPTBot or ClaudeBot tells their operators not to train on your pages. Blocking search and answer bots such as OAI-SearchBot or Claude-User can also keep you out of AI answers that could send you visitors.

How does this checker decide?

It follows the robots.txt rules crawlers use: a crawler obeys the group that names it, else the * group, and the longest matching Allow or Disallow path wins. An empty Disallow allows everything.

Does robots.txt stop every bot?

No. It is a request, not a lock. Bots that open a page because a user asked may skip it: Perplexity says Perplexity-User generally ignores it, and OpenAI says the rules may not apply to ChatGPT-User.

How do I detect ClaudeBot?

Search your access logs for ClaudeBot in the User-Agent header, ignoring case. A user agent can be faked, so compare the address with the IP list Anthropic publishes.

Does blocking Google-Extended remove me from Google Search?

No. Google says Google-Extended does not affect inclusion in Google Search and is not a ranking signal. It controls use for Gemini training and grounding.

Is my robots.txt sent anywhere?

No. The check runs in your browser and nothing you paste leaves the page.
