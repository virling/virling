The revenue growth rate formulas

Total growth compares the ending revenue with the starting revenue over the whole span:

growth = (ending revenue − starting revenue) / starting revenue

The compound monthly growth rate (CMGR) is the steady monthly rate that gets you from the start to the end in the same number of months. Annualized growth compounds that monthly rate over 12 months:

CMGR = (ending revenue / starting revenue) ^ (1 / months) − 1
annualized growth = (1 + CMGR) ^ 12 − 1

With 1 month, CMGR equals month over month growth. With 12 months, total growth equals year over year growth.

A worked example

These are round illustrative numbers, the same as the defaults above, not data from a real business. Revenue grows from $10,000 a month to $16,000 a month over 6 months. Total growth is ($16,000 − $10,000) / $10,000 = 60%.

CMGR is 1.6 ^ (1 / 6) − 1, about 8.15% a month. Dividing 60% by 6 gives 10%, but 10% a month compounded for 6 months would be about 77%, so the simple average overstates the monthly pace. Annualized growth is 1.0815 ^ 12 − 1, which is 1.6 squared minus 1, or 156%: the yearly growth if the same pace held for 12 months.

Revenue growth rate calculator FAQ

How do you calculate revenue growth rate?

Subtract the starting revenue from the ending revenue and divide by the starting revenue. Going from $10,000 to $16,000 is ($16,000 − $10,000) / $10,000 = 60% growth over the whole span. Divide by the number of months only for a simple average; use the compound monthly growth rate for a per month figure that adds back up to the total.

What is CMGR?

CMGR is the compound monthly growth rate: the steady monthly rate that would take revenue from the starting value to the ending value over the same number of months. The formula is (ending revenue / starting revenue) ^ (1 / months) − 1.

Why is CMGR lower than the average monthly growth?

Because growth compounds. Each month grows from a larger base, so a smaller monthly rate is enough to reach the same total. Growing 60% over 6 months averages 10% a month, but the compound rate is about 8.15%, and 10% a month for 6 months would give about 77% growth.

How do you annualize a monthly growth rate?

Compound it over 12 months: annualized growth = (1 + monthly rate) ^ 12 − 1. Multiplying the monthly rate by 12 understates growth when the rate is positive, since it ignores compounding.

How do you calculate month over month and year over year growth?

For month over month growth, enter last month's revenue as the start, this month's as the end, and 1 month. For year over year growth, enter the revenue of the same month a year earlier as the start, this month's as the end, and 12 months; total growth is then the year over year rate.

Which revenue should I use?

Use the same measure at both ends, for example MRR for a subscription business or net revenue after refunds for a store. Monthly revenue is noisy, so for longer spans compare months that are not distorted by one-off sales or annual renewals.
