The CAC payback formula

CAC payback is the number of months a new customer takes to pay back what it cost to acquire them, counting only the gross profit they bring:

payback months = acquisition cost / (monthly revenue per customer × gross margin)

With the defaults above, a customer who costs $600 to acquire and pays $50 a month at an 80% gross margin brings $50 × 0.80 = $40 of gross profit a month. $600 / $40 = 15 months. Until month 15 that customer has cost more than they returned.

Acquisition cost is everything spent to win customers in a period (ads, sponsorships, tools, and the share of salaries spent on sales and marketing) divided by the new customers won in that period. Use revenue you actually collect after discounts and refunds, and the same period for both numbers.

What the other results mean

  • Monthly gross profit per customer is monthly revenue times gross margin: what is left each month after hosting, payment fees and support.
  • Payback without margin divides acquisition cost by revenue alone. It is shorter and flattering, which is why the margin-adjusted figure is the one to plan with.
  • Gross profit in the first year is twelve months of gross profit per customer, assuming they stay. Above the acquisition cost, the customer pays back within the year.

Payback differs by channel

A single blended payback hides the real picture. Customers from paid search, a sponsorship and an organic blog post cost very different amounts to win, and often pay for different plans. A channel with a 4 month payback and one with a 30 month payback can average out to a figure that looks fine. CAC payback calculator FAQ

How is CAC payback calculated?

Customer acquisition cost divided by monthly revenue per customer times gross margin. The result is the number of months until the customer's gross profit covers what it cost to win them.

Why include gross margin?

Revenue also pays for hosting, payment fees and support. Only the gross profit is left to recover the acquisition cost, so leaving margin out makes payback look shorter than it is.

Does churn change payback?

This calculator assumes the customer stays. If many customers cancel before the payback month, the average customer never pays back, so compare payback with how long customers usually stay.

Where do I get acquisition cost per channel?

Spend per channel comes from your ad accounts and invoices. New customers per channel need an analytics tool that links visits to payments, so each paying customer is counted against the channel that brought them.
