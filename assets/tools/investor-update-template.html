What goes in a monthly investor update

  • Highlights. Three lines an investor can read in ten seconds: the number that moved most, the biggest event of the month and next month's focus.
  • Key metrics. The same few numbers every month, each with its change from last month, so a reader can compare updates. Keep a metric in the table when it gets worse; dropping it is what readers notice.
  • Revenue by acquisition channel. Where this month's new customers and revenue came from. It shows whether growth is coming from one channel you can keep feeding or from a one-off spike.
  • What worked and what did not. One or two lines each, with the number behind them. A clear failure with what you learned is more useful to investors than a list of wins.
  • Asks. Specific requests: an introduction to a named person or type of company, a hire, or advice on one decision. Specific asks get answers.

Where the channel numbers come from in Virling

Set the date picker to last month with Custom range. The Channels card lists revenue for every channel, using the same channel names as the table above. Click a channel to filter the whole page to it: the New customers number gives that channel's new customers, and Revenue shows its change against the previous period. Compare to other dates sets that period to the month before if you want exact calendar months. Renewals keep the channel of the customer's first payment, so a channel's revenue includes subscribers it brought in earlier months.

To let investors check the numbers themselves, turn on the public revenue dashboard and put its link under the table. The live demo shows the same cards with sample data.

Sending it

Send it in the first week of the month, for the last full month, on the same day each time. Copy as Markdown keeps the headings and tables for notes apps and email tools that accept Markdown; Copy as plain text gives one line per number for any email. Keep each update short enough to read on a phone, and link to the previous one rather than repeating it.

Investor update template FAQ

What should a monthly investor update include?

Highlights, a few key metrics with their change from last month, where new customers and revenue came from, what worked, what did not and your asks. Keep the same sections and metrics every month so updates can be compared.

How often should I send an investor update?

Monthly is common for early-stage companies, sent in the first week for the last full month. What matters most is sending it on a regular schedule, including in bad months.

Which metrics belong in an investor update?

The few that show the health of the business: for a subscription business usually MRR, new and lost customers, cash in bank and runway. Pick them once and keep them, with the change from last month next to each.

Why include revenue by acquisition channel?

It shows where growth comes from. Investors can see whether new customers come from a channel you can repeat and grow, and your asks can point at that channel, such as introductions to partners in it.

Should I share bad news in an investor update?

Yes. Put what did not work in its own section, with the number and what you will change. Investors can only help with problems they know about.

Is this template free?

Yes. It runs in your browser with no signup. Nothing you type is sent anywhere; copy it as Markdown or plain text.
