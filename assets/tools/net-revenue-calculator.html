Gross vs net revenue

Gross revenue is the full value of what you sold at list price. Net revenue is what you keep after everything that comes off it before the money reaches your bank:

net revenue = gross sales - discounts - refunds - chargebacks - fees

Payment and platform fees are usually a percent of what the buyer paid plus a fixed amount per order. Many providers keep their fee when you refund, so fees here are charged on gross sales after discounts, before refunds:

fees = (gross sales - discounts) × fee % + orders × fixed fee

The share of gross you keep is the headline:

share kept = net revenue / gross sales

Net revenue is not profit. Product cost, shipping, ad spend, salaries and tax all still come out of it.

A worked example

The defaults above are made-up sample numbers. A store sells $50,000 at list price in a month across 800 orders. It gives $2,500 in discount codes, refunds $1,500, and loses $300 to chargebacks and dispute fees. Its payment provider charges 2.9% + $0.30.

Buyers paid $50,000 - $2,500 = $47,500, so fees are $47,500 × 0.029 = $1,377.50 plus 800 × $0.30 = $240, or $1,617.50 in all. Net revenue is $50,000 - $2,500 - $1,500 - $300 - $1,617.50 = $44,082.50.

The store keeps 88.17% of gross. Discounts are the biggest deduction at 5% of gross, then fees at 3.24%, refunds at 3% and chargebacks at 0.6%. Together $5,917.50, or 11.83% of gross, never reaches the bank.

Why net revenue by channel matters

A blended net revenue hides where the deductions come from. A deal site or a discount code shared far beyond its audience can bring orders that arrive discounted and come back as refunds, while buyers from search or a referral who knew what they wanted keep what they bought. A channel can lead on gross sales and fall behind once its refunds come off. Net revenue calculator FAQ

What is the difference between gross and net revenue?

Gross revenue is the full value of what you sold, at list price, before anything comes off. Net revenue is what is left after discounts, refunds, chargebacks and payment or platform fees. Net revenue is the money you actually keep to pay for products, staff and marketing.

How do you calculate net revenue?

Start with gross sales and subtract discounts, refunds, chargebacks and fees: net revenue = gross sales - discounts - refunds - chargebacks - fees. Fees are usually a percent of the amount charged plus a fixed amount per order.

Are payment fees refunded when I refund an order?

Often not. Many payment providers keep their fee on a refunded payment, so this calculator charges fees on everything you charged, after discounts and before refunds. Check your provider's terms and set the fee inputs to match.

Is net revenue the same as profit?

No. Net revenue comes before product cost, shipping, ad spend, salaries and tax. For what is left after product cost, use a gross margin or contribution margin calculation on top of net revenue.

Why does net revenue differ by channel?

Buyers from different channels refund and dispute at different rates, and some channels lean on discount codes. Two channels with the same gross sales can keep very different amounts once those come off.
