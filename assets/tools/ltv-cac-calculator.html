The LTV to CAC formulas

Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is what a channel cost divided by the new paying customers it brought:

CAC = channel spend / new customers

Lifetime value (LTV) is the gross profit a customer brings over the months they stay:

LTV = monthly revenue per customer × gross margin × months a customer stays

The ratio divides one by the other, and payback is how many months of gross profit it takes to earn the acquisition cost back:

LTV to CAC = LTV / CAC
payback months = CAC / (monthly revenue per customer × gross margin)

With the first default row, $3,000 of paid search that brought 10 customers is a CAC of $300. Each customer pays $50 a month at an 80% margin, so $40 of gross profit a month, and stays 24 months on average: an LTV of $960. $960 / $300 is a ratio of 3.2, and $300 / $40 is a payback of 7.5 months.

What the results mean

  • A ratio above 1 means the average customer from that channel returns more gross profit than it cost to win them. Below 1, the channel loses money on every customer it brings.
  • Payback tells you how long your cash is tied up. Two channels with the same ratio can have very different payback if one brings customers who pay more each month but stay less long.
  • All channels adds up spend and customers across the rows, and weights LTV and payback by how many customers each channel brought. It is the blended figure most dashboards show, and the reason per channel numbers matter: a strong channel can hide a weak one.

Where the inputs come from

Spend per channel comes from your ad accounts and invoices. Months a customer stays is roughly 1 divided by monthly churn: 4% of customers cancelling each month is about 25 months. Use revenue you actually collect after discounts and refunds, and the same date range for every number. LTV to CAC calculator FAQ

How is the LTV to CAC ratio calculated?

Lifetime value divided by customer acquisition cost. Lifetime value is monthly revenue per customer times gross margin times the months a customer stays; acquisition cost is channel spend divided by the new customers it brought.

Why calculate it per channel?

Channels bring customers at different costs who pay for different plans and stay for different lengths of time. A blended ratio averages them together and hides the channel that loses money.

Why use gross margin and not revenue?

Revenue also pays for hosting, payment fees and support. Only the gross profit is left to cover what it cost to win the customer, so LTV on revenue alone makes every channel look better than it is.

How do I estimate how long customers stay?

Divide 1 by your monthly churn rate. If 5% of customers cancel each month, the average customer stays about 20 months. Customers from different channels often churn at different rates, so measure it per channel when you can.
