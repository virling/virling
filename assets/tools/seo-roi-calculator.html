The SEO ROI formula

The calculator looks at one month of SEO: what it cost, the customers that month's organic visits brought, and the revenue those customers pay you.

customers = organic visits × visit to customer rate
revenue = customers × revenue per customer × months a customer pays
ROI = (revenue - SEO cost) / SEO cost
months to break even = SEO cost / (customers × monthly revenue per customer)

If customers pay once, months a customer pays is 1, and a month of SEO either pays for itself within the month or does not pay back at these numbers. If customers pay every month, the customers won in a month pay back that month's SEO cost over time, and months to break even is how long that takes. If it is longer than customers usually stay, that month of SEO never pays back. ROI here uses revenue, not profit: to see profit, multiply revenue by your gross margin before comparing it with the cost.

A worked example

These are example numbers, the defaults above, not data from a real business. A site gets 8,000 organic visits a month, and 0.5% of them become customers: 40 customers. Each pays $30 a month and stays for 12 months, so the month's customers bring 40 × $30 × 12 = $14,400 of revenue. SEO costs $6,000 a month, so the ROI is ($14,400 - $6,000) / $6,000 = 140%. Those 40 customers pay $1,200 a month between them, so they cover the $6,000 in $6,000 / $1,200 = 5 months.

If the same customers paid $120 once instead, the month would bring 40 × $120 = $4,800, less than the $6,000 it cost: an ROI of -20%, and the month never breaks even at these numbers.

The inputs are guesses until revenue is tied to organic visits

Organic visits and SEO cost are easy to find: one is in your analytics, the other on your invoices and timesheets. The visit to customer rate and the revenue per customer are not. Most analytics tools count visits from search but cannot say which of those visitors later paid, and a customer who found you through a blog post often pays days later after coming back directly, so the sale is credited elsewhere. Until each payment is joined to the visit that brought the customer, the result above is an estimate built on a guessed rate.

SEO also takes time: content written this month may not rank for months, so the cost of one month and the visits of the same month are not a clean pair. Compare several months of cost with the revenue organic search brought over the same stretch. Track the real revenue: link, code and answer

Ads and sponsorships can be measured with a tagged link, a discount code and a question to the buyer. For organic search each of them works differently, and each misses something:

  • The tagged link. You cannot tag a normal search result; the referrer and the landing page do that job, and the search engine does not pass on what was typed. You can tag the links you control, such as the website link on your Google Business Profile. Both miss buyers who switch devices and those whose browser blocks the script.
  • The discount code. A code shown only on one article tells you its readers used it, including the ones who came from a newsletter or a shared link. It misses readers who buy without it, and once it leaks to a coupon site it counts people who never read the page.
  • The how-did-you-hear answer. It can reveal a search that left no visit you could match. But “Google” often means the buyer searched your name after hearing it elsewhere, it does not say which page, and people who skip the question are missing. The how did you hear about us template has a list to copy.

Use the visit joined to the payment as the main figure, and the other two to see what it misses, without adding them together. SEO ROI calculator FAQ

How do you calculate SEO ROI?

Multiply organic visits by the share that become customers, multiply that by the revenue each customer pays you, subtract what SEO cost in the same period and divide by that cost. 40 customers paying $30 a month for 12 months is $14,400; against $6,000 of SEO cost that is an ROI of 140%.

What costs should I include in SEO cost?

Everything organic search needs: writers, editors and designers, SEO and keyword tools, freelancers or an agency, link building, and the time your own team spends on content and technical fixes. Leaving out people's time makes SEO look nearly free.

How long does SEO take to break even?

It depends on your numbers. For subscriptions, divide a month's SEO cost by the monthly revenue the customers it won pay you. Content can also take months to start ranking, so early months often cost more than they bring before later months make it back.

How do I find my real visit to customer rate from organic search?

Count the customers whose first or last visit came from a search engine and divide by organic visits in the same period. That needs each payment joined to the visit that brought the customer, which analytics tools that only count visits cannot do.

How many organic visits do I need for SEO to break even?

Divide the SEO cost by the visit to customer rate times what a customer pays you in total. In the example on this page, a customer pays $30 × 12 = $360, so $6,000 / (0.5% × $360) = 3,333.3, or 3,334 visits a month. Below that, the month's customers never pay back its cost on revenue.
