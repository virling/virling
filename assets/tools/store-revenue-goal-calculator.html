The formulas in plain words

Orders needed is the revenue goal divided by your average order value:

orders needed = revenue goal / average order value

Some of those orders come from customers who have bought before. The rest have to come from new buyers:

new buyers needed = orders needed × (1 − repeat purchase share)

Your conversion rate is the share of visitors who place an order, so the visitors needed is the orders divided by that rate:

visitors needed = orders needed / conversion rate

Each result is rounded up to a whole number, since part of an order does not count toward the goal. Use the same month for every input: average order value and conversion rate from last month predict next month better than an all-time figure.

A worked example

These are round illustrative numbers, the same ones the calculator starts with, not data from a real store. A store wants $50,000 of revenue a month. Its average order value is $80, so it needs $50,000 / $80 = 625 orders. A quarter of its orders come from returning customers, so 625 × 0.75 = 468.75, rounded up to 469 orders have to come from new buyers. It converts 2% of visitors, so it needs 625 / 0.02 = 31,250 visitors a month.

Each lever moves the answer. Raising the average order value to $100 cuts the orders needed to 500 and the visitors to 25,000. Raising the conversion rate to 2.5% at the same $80 order cuts the visitors to 25,000 too, without changing the orders.

Visitors needed depends on which channels convert

The single conversion rate above is an average across every channel. In practice a visitor from email or from a search for your brand is often far more likely to buy than one from a first social post or a broad ad, so the same goal can need very different traffic depending on where it comes from. Before you plan how to get 31,250 visitors, find out which channels turn visitors into orders: work out the rate per channel with the conversion rate calculator, see how often customers come back with the repeat purchase rate calculator, and read how to track Shopify revenue by traffic source to get orders per channel from your own store.

Store revenue goal calculator FAQ

How many orders do I need to hit my revenue goal?

Divide the revenue goal by your average order value. A $50,000 monthly goal at an $80 average order needs 625 orders a month.

How much traffic do I need to make a sale?

Divide one by your conversion rate. At a 2% conversion rate you need about 50 visitors for each order, so 625 orders need 31,250 visitors.

What is the repeat purchase share?

The share of your orders placed by customers who have bought before. Those orders come from people you already have, so only the rest need new buyers. Leave it at 0 if you do not know it.

Why does the calculator round up?

You cannot take part of an order or part of a visit, and rounding down would leave you just short of the goal, so orders, buyers and visitors are rounded up to whole numbers.

Does every visitor need to come from a new channel?

No. Visitors needed counts all visits, including returning customers who come back by email or by typing your address. Each channel converts at its own rate, so the same goal can need far fewer visitors from a channel that converts well.
