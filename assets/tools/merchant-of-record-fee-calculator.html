Why a fixed fee hurts small prices

The percentage grows with the price; the fixed fee does not. On a $5 sale 50 cents is 10% of the price and 40 cents is 8%, before the percentage is added. On a $99 sale they are 0.5% and 0.4%. Billing less often helps: twelve $5 monthly charges pay a 50 cent fee twelve times, $6.00 a year, while one $60 annual charge pays it once.

To find the price that leaves a target amount, add the fixed fee to the target and divide by 1 minus the rate: price = (target + fixed fee) / (1 - rate). To keep $25.00 from a US subscription, charge $26.84 on Paddle, Lemon Squeezy or Polar (25.50 / 0.95), $26.60 on Dodo Payments (25.40 / 0.955) and $26.43 on Creem (25.40 / 0.961).

Merchant of record fee FAQ

What is a merchant of record?

A company that sells your product on your behalf. It is the seller on the receipt, so it collects and pays sales tax and VAT, and handles chargebacks. Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Creem and Dodo Payments work this way, and their fee covers that work as well as card processing.

How does this calculator work out the fees?

It divides monthly revenue by average order value to get orders a month. For each order it takes the provider's percentage rate, adds the international card extra for your share of international buyers and the subscription extra if you sell subscriptions, then adds the fixed fee per order. Net payout is revenue minus those fees.

Why does average order value matter so much?

Every provider charges a fixed fee per order, from 40 to 50 cents at the default rates. On a $10 order, 50 cents is 5% of the price before the percentage fee; on a $100 order it is 0.5%.

What does this calculator leave out?

Payout and bank fees, currency conversion, refunds, disputes, PayPal and other payment method surcharges, monthly plan prices and custom or high-volume pricing. These depend on your setup, so check each provider's pricing page.

Which merchant of record should I choose?

Fees are one part of the choice. Payout schedule, supported countries and payment methods, checkout and the integrations you need also matter. Enter your own numbers and rates here, then read each provider's terms.
