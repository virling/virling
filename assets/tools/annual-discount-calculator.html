The annual discount formulas

The annual price is twelve months of the monthly price, less the discount. Spread over the year, it gives the effective monthly price:

annual price = monthly price × 12 × (1 − discount)
effective monthly price = annual price / 12

The annual payment arrives on day one. Divided by the monthly price, it says how many months of monthly revenue you collect up front, which is also the break-even lifetime: a monthly subscriber who stays exactly that long pays the same as one annual payment.

months collected up front = break-even lifetime = annual price / monthly price

Churn decides which side of that line the average monthly customer lands on. With a constant monthly churn rate, the average monthly subscriber stays 1 / churn months, and the expected revenue from one monthly subscriber over the first twelve months is:

expected first-year monthly revenue = monthly price × (1 − (1 − churn)12) / churn

If the annual price is higher than that, an annual customer earns you more in the first year than the average monthly customer would. The comparison assumes the customer would have bought monthly at the same churn rate otherwise, and ignores refunds and renewals after the first year.

A worked example

These are round illustrative numbers, the same as the defaults above, not data from a real business. A plan costs $50 a month and the annual plan is 20% off, so the annual price is $50 × 12 × 0.80 = $480 and the effective monthly price is $40.

The $480 payment equals $480 / $50 = 9.6 months of the monthly price, collected up front. A monthly subscriber who stays longer than 9.6 months pays more than an annual one; one who leaves sooner pays less.

At 5% monthly churn the average monthly subscriber stays 1 / 0.05 = 20 months, but about a fifth have cancelled after 5 months and almost half after 12. Expected first-year revenue from a monthly subscriber is $50 × (1 − 0.9512) / 0.05, about $459.64. The annual customer pays $480, about $20.36 more in the first year.

Annual discount calculator FAQ

How do you calculate an annual plan discount?

Multiply the monthly price by 12, then subtract the discount. A $50 monthly plan with a 20% annual discount costs $50 × 12 × 0.80 = $480 a year, an effective $40 a month.

What is the break-even lifetime?

The number of months a monthly subscriber has to stay to pay as much as one annual payment: the annual price divided by the monthly price. Customers who stay longer than that pay more on the monthly plan.

How does churn change whether the discount pays off?

The higher your monthly churn, the sooner the average monthly subscriber leaves, so the less they pay in the first year. With high churn an annual payment, even at a discount, often brings more than the average monthly customer would.

How do annual plans count in MRR?

As one twelfth of the annual price each month, so a $480 annual plan adds $40 of MRR. The cash arrives up front, but MRR spreads it over the twelve months it covers.
