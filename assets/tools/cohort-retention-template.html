Cohort retention template by acquisition channel

One row per cohort and channel: the month customers first paid, the channel that brought them, how many there were, and how many were still paying in each month after. The template works out retention percentages for every channel, so you can see whether the channel that brings the most customers also brings the ones who stay. No signup, and everything stays in your browser.

The cohorts and numbers below are made up, not real data. Replace them with your own. Month 1 is the first month after the first payment; leave a month empty when the cohort has not reached it yet. Rows with no channel name are left out.

How to read it

  • Compare share of customers with month 3 or later. A channel with the biggest share of customers and the lowest later months brings volume, not customers who stay.
  • Look at the drop after month 1. A steep first drop often means the channel's promise does not match the product, or buyers came for a discount.
  • Read down a column in the top table. Newer cohorts from the same channel at the same month show whether a change you made helped.
  • Weigh retention against cost. A channel that keeps fewer customers can still pay off if it is cheap; put CAC next to it with the channel scorecard.

How to fill it in

  • Cohort: the month of each customer's first payment.
  • Channel: the channel credited for that first payment. Keep it fixed, even if the customer later comes back from somewhere else.
  • Customers: new paying customers from that channel in the cohort month.
  • Month 1 to month 6: how many of them were still paying that many months later. Use the same definition of still paying for every row.

How to fill this from Virling

Open the Retention page and set the date range to one cohort month. It groups the customers whose first payment fell in that month by channel, with customers acquired and how many still pay today. Note those counts in this month's column for that cohort, and repeat each month to build the rows. The retention by channel docs explain who counts as still paying.

Cohort retention template FAQ

What is a cohort retention table?

A table that groups customers by the month they first paid and shows how many of each group are still paying one, two, three or more months later. Reading down a column compares cohorts at the same age; reading along a row shows how one cohort fades.

Why split cohorts by acquisition channel?

Because channels bring different customers. The channel that brings the most customers can also bring the ones who leave first, and a total retention curve hides that. Splitting by the channel of the first payment shows which channels bring customers who stay.

What counts as still paying?

A customer with a live subscription, or for one-off or usage billing, a payment in that month that was not refunded. Pick one definition and use it for every cohort and channel.

Why are the later months empty for recent cohorts?

A cohort that started three months ago has no month 4 yet. Leave those cells empty: the channel average for each month only uses cohorts old enough to have that month, so young cohorts do not drag it down.

How is the channel average worked out?

For each month, it adds up the customers still paying across that channel's cohorts that have the month filled in, and divides by those cohorts' starting customers. Large cohorts count for more than small ones.

Is this template free?

Yes. It runs in your browser with no signup. Nothing you type is sent anywhere; copy it as CSV or download the file.
