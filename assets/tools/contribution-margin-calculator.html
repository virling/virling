The contribution margin formula

Contribution margin per order is what is left of one order after the costs that come with it. It is the amount that order adds toward rent, salaries, software and profit:

payment fee = order value × fee percent + fixed fee

contribution margin = order value - product cost - shipping - payment fee - acquisition cost

contribution margin % = contribution margin / order value

The calculator also shows the margin before acquisition cost. That is the most you could pay to win this order and still break even on it; see the break-even CPA calculator if customers come back for more orders.

A worked example

These are round illustrative numbers, the same ones the calculator starts with, not data from a real store. An order is worth $80. The products in it cost $30, shipping and packaging cost $8, and the card payment costs 2.9% + $0.30, which is $2.62. Before marketing, the order leaves $80 - $30 - $8 - $2.62 = $39.38. It came from an ad, at an acquisition cost of $15 per order, so the contribution margin is $24.38, or 30.5% of the order.

Now take a $40 order from another channel with the same product margin ($15 of product), the same $8 shipping and the same $15 acquisition cost. The fee is $1.46, and the order leaves $0.54, or 1.4% of its value. Shipping, the fixed part of the fee and the acquisition cost do not shrink with the order, so a channel with smaller orders keeps far less per order.

Margin per order differs by channel

Each channel brings its own average order value and its own cost per order, so one store-wide margin hides channels that lose money on every sale. To compare them you need the revenue and orders of each channel; see the average order value calculator and how to find which marketing channel pays.

Contribution margin calculator FAQ

How do you calculate contribution margin per order?

Take the order value and subtract every cost that comes with that one order: product cost, shipping and packaging, the payment fee and what you paid to acquire the order. An $80 order with $30 of product, $8 of shipping, a $2.62 payment fee and $15 of acquisition cost leaves $24.38, or 30.5% of the order.

What is the difference between contribution margin and gross margin?

Gross margin usually takes off only the cost of the product. Contribution margin also takes off the other costs that grow with each order, such as shipping, payment fees and the marketing cost of winning the order, so it is lower and closer to what the order adds to your profit.

Which costs belong in contribution margin?

Costs that come with each extra order: product cost, packaging, shipping, payment fees, marketplace or app fees charged per order, and the acquisition cost of that order. Rent, salaries and software subscriptions stay the same whether you sell one more order or not, so they are left out and paid from the total contribution.

Should the order value include shipping and tax?

Leave out sales tax, because it is not your money. If the buyer pays for shipping, include it in the order value and keep your real shipping cost as a cost, so the two cancel out as they do in your bank account.

Why is contribution margin different for each channel?

Channels bring different order values and cost different amounts per order. Shipping and the fixed part of the payment fee stay the same on a small order, so a channel with a lower average order value keeps a smaller share, and a paid channel carries an acquisition cost that organic search or email may not.
