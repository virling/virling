The discount break-even formula

A discount comes out of the profit on each unit, so the units you sell have to rise to make up for it. With the gross margin and discount as shares of the full price:

gross margin = (price - unit cost) / price

profit per unit after discount = price × (1 - discount) - unit cost

break-even sales lift = discount / (gross margin - discount)

units to break even = units at full price × gross margin / (gross margin - discount)

If the discount is as large as the gross margin or larger, each discounted unit earns nothing or loses money, and no sales lift brings the gross profit back.

A worked example

These are round illustrative numbers, the same ones the calculator starts with, not data from a real store. A product sells for $50 and costs $20, a 60% gross margin, and the store sells 100 a month at full price for $3,000 of gross profit. A 20% discount drops the price to $40 and the profit per unit from $30 to $20. To make $3,000 again the store must sell 150 units, a 50% lift. If the sale brings a 30% lift, 130 units at $20 make $2,600, which is $400 less than selling at full price.

The lower the margin, the harder the climb. The same 20% discount on a product with a 40% margin halves the profit per unit, so the store must sell twice as many units just to stand still.

Discount break-even calculator FAQ

How do you calculate the break-even sales lift for a discount?

Divide the discount by the gross margin left after the discount: break-even lift = discount / (gross margin - discount), with both as percentages of the full price. At a 60% gross margin, a 20% discount needs 20 / (60 - 20) = 50% more units to keep the same gross profit.

Why does a small discount need so many more sales?

The discount comes straight out of the profit on each unit, not out of the price as a whole. A 20% discount on a product with a 60% margin cuts the profit per unit from 60% of the price to 40%, a third less, so you must sell half as many units again to make up the difference.

What if the discount is bigger than my gross margin?

Then every discounted unit sells at or below its cost, and no number of extra sales brings the gross profit back. The calculator shows this as not reachable. A sale like that only pays if it brings customers who come back at full price, or clears stock you would otherwise write off.

Should I use unit cost or gross margin?

Either one. Gross margin is (price - unit cost) / price, so a $50 product that costs $20 has a 60% margin. Use the dropdown to switch; the calculator converts the number you entered so the two stay in step.

Does this include shipping, payment fees and ad spend?

No. It compares gross profit, price minus the cost of the product. If shipping or payment fees come with each order, add them to the unit cost. For the full margin on an order, including what you paid to win it, use the contribution margin calculator.
