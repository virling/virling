The trial to paid conversion rate formula

Trial to paid conversion rate is the share of free trials that became paying customers:

trial to paid rate = trials that paid / trials started × 100%

Use the same group of trials on both sides: count the payers among the trials that started in the period, not every payment in the period. Leave out trials that have not ended yet, and keep trials that ask for a card apart from trials that do not, since they convert differently.

A worked example

The defaults above are made-up numbers to show the method. Organic search started 120 trials and 30 paid: 25%. Communities started 200 trials and 16 paid: 8%. AI assistants started 25 trials and 9 paid: 36%. Together that is 55 payers from 345 trials, a 15.94% trial to paid rate.

Communities sends the most trials and converts the fewest; AI assistants send the fewest and convert the best. With only 25 trials, though, two more or fewer payers move that rate by eight points, so wait for a few dozen trials per channel before moving effort.

Edge cases in trial to paid

Most wrong trial to paid rates come from which trials were counted. Use the same treatment for every channel:

CaseWhat to do
Trials still runningLeave them out of both numbers. On 30 June, with a 14-day trial, count the trials that started on or before 15 June.
Paid before the trial endedCount them as paid once every trial that started in the same period has ended. Adding early payers while the rest still run makes the rate too high.
Extended trialsOne trial, in the period it first started. Leave it out until the new end date has passed, then count it as paid or not paid. The extension is not a second trial.
No card askedKeep these apart from trials that ask for a card. Count a trial as paid at its first real payment. Some upgrade after the end, so give every trial the same window, such as 30 days from the start, and leave out those whose window is still open.
First charge failedNot paid until a charge succeeds. Wait for your provider's retries to finish before you close the period.
Cancelled during the trialStill a trial that started, and not paid. Taking it out makes the rate too high.
Second trial by the same personCount one trial per customer, the first one.

What to do with your trial to paid rate

  • Turn it into trials. In the made-up example, ten more customers take 10 / 0.25 = 40 more search trials or 10 / 0.08 = 125 more from communities, if the rates hold. Compare that with what each trial costs you.
  • One channel is far below the rest. Read what it promises and who it reaches before you change the trial.
  • Every channel is low. Look at the trial itself: the first day, the price, the reminder before it ends.
  • A channel is high. Check that its customers stay with the churn rate calculator before you spend more on it.

Why trial conversion differs by acquisition channel

A single trial to paid rate hides which channels bring people ready to buy. Someone who searched for a tool like yours, or asked an AI assistant for one, arrives closer to buying than someone who clicked a thread out of curiosity. A post that oversold the product brings trials that leave on day two. Some channels convert on day one, others need the whole trial and a reminder email.

Measuring it needs the channel of each trial and each payer credited back to it. See trial-to-paid conversion by traffic source, step by step. Trial to paid calculator FAQ

How do you calculate trial to paid conversion rate?

Divide the number of trials that became paying customers by the number of trials started in the same period, then multiply by 100. 30 paid from 120 trials is a 25% trial to paid conversion rate.

Which trials should I count?

Only trials that have had the chance to finish. If your trial lasts 14 days, leave out the trials that started in the last 14 days, or the most recent weeks will look worse than they are because those trials have not ended yet.

What is a good free trial conversion rate?

It depends on whether the trial asks for a card, your price and your market, so a figure from another company says little. Compare your own channels with each other and with your overall rate, within one trial type.

Why is the first payment after a free trial often unattributed?

With a card up front trial the payment provider charges the card when the trial ends, so that charge never passed through your site and carries no visitor ID. It has to be linked back to the trial start, which did.
