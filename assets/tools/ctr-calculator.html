The CTR formula

Click-through rate is clicks divided by impressions, as a percentage:

CTR = clicks / impressions × 100

The same formula turned around gives the other two:

clicks = impressions × CTR / 100 and impressions = clicks / CTR × 100

With a cost, orders and an average order value, the calculator also shows what a click cost and what a thousand impressions earned:

cost per click = cost / clicks and revenue per 1,000 impressions = orders × average order value / impressions × 1,000

For an ad, use the impressions and clicks in the ad report. For search, use the impressions and clicks of one page or query. For email, say whether you divide by emails delivered or by opens, because dividing by opens gives a higher rate, and keep to one.

A worked example

These are made-up numbers, the ones the calculator starts with, not a benchmark. An ad was shown 40,000 times and clicked 600 times, so its CTR is 600 / 40,000 × 100 = 1.5%. It cost $900, so each click cost $900 / 600 = $1.50.

The clicks led to 12 orders at an average of $80, which is $960 of revenue. Revenue per 1,000 impressions is $960 / 40,000 × 1,000 = $24.00.

To plan instead, empty the clicks field: 100,000 impressions at the same 1.5% would give 1,500 clicks.

Why a high CTR can still bring no customers

CTR stops at the click. It says the headline, picture or offer made people curious, not that they wanted to buy. A vague promise, a free giveaway or a surprising headline can raise CTR by bringing people who were never going to pay. If the page then shows something else, or a price they did not expect, they leave. Some reports also count accidental taps and bots as clicks.

Take another made-up ad next to the one above, with the same 40,000 impressions and the same $80 orders. It gets 1,200 clicks, a CTR of 3%, but only 3 orders. Its revenue per 1,000 impressions is $240 / 40,000 × 1,000 = $6.00, a quarter of the first ad's $24.00, with twice the CTR.

CTR calculator FAQ

How do you calculate CTR?

Divide clicks by impressions and multiply by 100. 600 clicks from 40,000 impressions is 600 / 40,000 × 100 = 1.5%. Count both for the same ad, page or email over the same dates.

How do I find clicks or impressions from CTR?

Clicks are impressions times CTR: 40,000 impressions at 1.5% is 600 clicks. Impressions are clicks divided by CTR: 600 clicks at 1.5% is 40,000 impressions. Fill in any two in the calculator and leave the third empty.

Can CTR be over 100%?

Not when each impression is counted once and can be clicked once. Some reports count every click, so one person clicking twice on a single view can push it over. If clicks are above impressions, check that both cover the same dates, the same placement and the same way of counting.

What is a good CTR?

There is no figure that fits every channel, because a search result, a banner and an email are seen in different ways. Compare an ad, page or email with your own earlier ones in the same place, and then with the orders each one brought.

Is CTR the same as conversion rate?

No. CTR is the share of impressions that became clicks, before anyone reaches your site. Conversion rate is the share of visitors who then did what you wanted, such as paying. A page can have a high CTR and a low conversion rate.

Why does my high-CTR ad bring no sales?

Usually the people who click are curious, not buyers, or the page does not deliver what the ad promised. Accidental taps and bots also count as clicks in some reports. Payments joined to visits show which clicks turned into customers.
