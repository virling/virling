The refund rate formula

Refund rate by count is the share of orders in a period that were refunded, in whole or part:

refund rate by count = orders refunded / orders

Refund rate by revenue is the share of the money taken that went back to buyers:

refund rate by revenue = amount refunded / revenue

Net revenue is what you keep:

net revenue = revenue - amount refunded

And a target rate turns into a budget of refunds for the same period:

refunds allowed = orders × target rate and amount allowed = revenue × target rate

Count refunds against the orders they belong to, not the day the refund happened. A refund this month for an order placed last month makes this month look worse and last month look better than they were.

A worked example

With the defaults above, the period has 500 orders worth $30,000. 15 of them were refunded, for $1,200 in total. 15 / 500 = 3% refund rate by count, and $1,200 / $30,000 = 4% by revenue. Net revenue is $30,000 - $1,200 = $28,800.

The rate by revenue is higher than the rate by count, so the refunded orders were larger than average: $80 per refund against a $60 average order. If the refunds were mostly partial, the rate by revenue would be the lower one.

A 2% target would allow 500 × 0.02 = 10 refunded orders and $30,000 × 0.02 = $600 refunded, so this period is 5 refunds and $600 over it.

Why refund rate by channel matters

A single refund rate hides where refunds come from. Some channels bring buyers who refund: a deal site or a discount code shared far beyond its audience can bring orders that come back, while buyers from search or a referral who knew what they wanted keep what they bought. A channel can look like your best by gross revenue and fall behind once its refunds come off. Refund rate calculator FAQ

How is refund rate calculated?

By count, it is orders refunded in a period divided by orders in that period. By revenue, it is the amount refunded divided by the revenue from those orders. Count refunds against the period of the original order.

Should I use refund rate by count or by revenue?

Use both. The rate by count says how many buyers were unhappy, and the rate by revenue says how much money it cost. When they differ, refunds are landing on larger or smaller orders than average, or many refunds are partial.

Do partial refunds count?

In the rate by count, an order refunded in part counts as a refunded order. In the rate by revenue, only the amount actually refunded counts, so partial refunds weigh less there.

What does a target refund rate tell me?

It turns a percentage into a number of refunds and an amount for the period. If you are over it, the gap shows how many refunds you would need to prevent to get back under, which is easier to plan around than a percentage.

Why does refund rate differ by channel?

Buyers from different channels arrive with different intent. A deal site or a widely shared discount code can bring buyers who refund, while search or referrals bring buyers who keep what they bought. Revenue per channel after refunds shows which channels are worth the spend.
