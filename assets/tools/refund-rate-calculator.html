The refund rate formula

Refund rate by count is the share of orders in a period that were refunded, in whole or part:

refund rate by count = orders refunded / orders

Refund rate by revenue is the share of the money taken that went back to buyers:

refund rate by revenue = amount refunded / revenue

Net revenue is what you keep:

net revenue = revenue - amount refunded

And a target rate turns into a budget of refunds for the same period:

refunds allowed = orders × target rate and amount allowed = revenue × target rate

Count refunds against the orders they belong to, not the day the refund happened. A refund this month for an order placed last month makes this month look worse and last month look better than they were.

A worked example

With the defaults above, the period has 500 orders worth $30,000. 15 of them were refunded, for $1,200 in total. 15 / 500 = 3% refund rate by count, and $1,200 / $30,000 = 4% by revenue. Net revenue is $30,000 - $1,200 = $28,800.

The rate by revenue is higher than the rate by count, so the refunded orders were larger than average: $80 per refund against a $60 average order. If the refunds were mostly partial, the rate by revenue would be the lower one.

A 2% target would allow 500 × 0.02 = 10 refunded orders and $30,000 × 0.02 = $600 refunded, so this period is 5 refunds and $600 over it.

Where to find the inputs in Stripe, Paddle and Shopify

  • Stripe. Export the itemized CSV of Balance change from activity. Rows are grouped by reporting category: count and add up charge for orders and revenue, and refund for the amount refunded. A payment can have more than one refund, so count different charge_id values for orders refunded. The report dates a refund by when it changed your balance. The column list has charge_created_utc, the time of the original charge, so you can put it back in the right period (all read on 2026-11-25).
  • Paddle. Create two reports: transactions with the status completed, and adjustments with the action refund and the status approved. Both cover only the last month by last update unless you set the updated_at filter. A transaction can have several adjustments, so count transactions, not adjustments. Paddle's refund metric leaves out tax, fees and chargebacks, so do not divide it by revenue that includes tax (all read on 2026-11-25).
  • Shopify. Export orders placed in the period as CSV. Each order has a Total and a Refunded Amount, and orders with several items take more than one line, so count order names once. Orders refunded are those with a Refunded Amount above zero, the same ones that show as Refunded or Partially refunded. The Sales reversals column in the finance reports is different: it is the value of goods returned, on the day the return was processed (all read on 2026-11-25).

Why refund rate by channel matters

A single refund rate hides where refunds come from. Some channels bring buyers who refund: a deal site or a discount code shared far beyond its audience can bring orders that come back, while buyers from search or a referral who knew what they wanted keep what they bought. A channel can look like your best by gross revenue and fall behind once its refunds come off. Run the calculator per channel with the orders and refunds each one brought, or let Virling do it from your payments: it credits each payment, after refunds, to the channel, referrer, campaign and landing page of the visit that brought it, so channel revenue is already net of refunds. The formula per channel, with a worked example, is in refund rate by traffic source. For why discount codes alone do not tell you the channel, see discount code attribution.

What Virling shows per channel

The Channels card on the dashboard shows revenue net of refunds for each channel, and Refunded, the share of its payments refunded in whole or part, which is the rate by count. A refund counts on the date of the payment it belongs to, the way this page recommends. Renewals are credited to the channel of the customer's first payment, so a refunded renewal lowers the channel that brought the customer, not the one they used last. Which visit gets a first payment depends on the attribution model and lookback window. The Retention page adds the dollars refunded and the share of customers with any refund.

Virling stores no disputes apart from refunds. A lost dispute on Stripe and a chargeback on Paddle count as refunds, and Shopify chargebacks are not read. A refund can take up to 6 hours to show on Paddle, and one on a transaction older than 60 days is not picked up there automatically.

Refund rate calculator FAQ

How is refund rate calculated?

By count, it is orders refunded in a period divided by orders in that period. By revenue, it is the amount refunded divided by the revenue from those orders. Count refunds against the period of the original order.

Should I use refund rate by count or by revenue?

Use both. The rate by count says how many buyers were unhappy, and the rate by revenue says how much money it cost. When they differ, refunds are landing on larger or smaller orders than average, or many refunds are partial.

Do partial refunds count?

In the rate by count, an order refunded in part counts as a refunded order. In the rate by revenue, only the amount actually refunded counts, so partial refunds weigh less there.

What does a target refund rate tell me?

It turns a percentage into a number of refunds and an amount for the period. If you are over it, the gap shows how many refunds you would need to prevent to get back under, which is easier to plan around than a percentage.

Why does refund rate differ by channel?

Buyers from different channels arrive with different intent. A deal site or a widely shared discount code can bring buyers who refund, while search or referrals bring buyers who keep what they bought. Revenue per channel after refunds shows which channels are worth the spend.

Does Virling show refund rate by channel?

Yes. The Channels card shows each channel's revenue net of refunds and the share of its payments refunded in whole or part. A refund counts on the date of the original payment, and a refunded renewal counts for the channel of the customer's first payment.
