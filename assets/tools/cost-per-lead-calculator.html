The cost per lead formula

Cost per lead is what you spent on a channel divided by the leads it brought, over the same period:

cost per lead = channel spend / channel leads
blended cost per lead = total spend / total leads

With a lead to customer rate and a deal value, the same numbers give two more figures:

cost per customer = cost per lead / lead to customer rate
revenue per lead = lead to customer rate × deal value

A channel pays for itself on that deal value when revenue per lead is above its cost per lead.

A worked example

These are made-up sample numbers, the same as the defaults above, not data from a real company or a benchmark. In one month, paid search cost $3,000 and brought 50 leads: $60 per lead. Paid social cost $2,000 for 100 leads: $20 per lead. Content cost $1,200 in freelance writing for 50 leads: $24 per lead.

Together that is $6,200 for 200 leads, a blended cost per lead of $31. If 10% of leads become customers, a customer costs $31 / 0.10 = $310 blended, and $600, $200 and $240 by channel. Paid search leads cost three times as much as paid social leads. At a $2,000 deal value each lead brings 0.10 × $2,000 = $200 of revenue, well above the cost per lead on every channel.

What to do with the number: agencies

  1. Agree the count before you report it. Write down with the client what a lead is, which system counts it and whether your fee is in the spend.
  2. Set a ceiling for each client. Revenue per lead is the break-even. Multiply it by the client's gross margin and stay below that.
  3. Get a rate for each channel. Ask the client which leads paid, or join payments to visits. How to calculate CAC by channel has the steps. If the leads come from a Typeform, Typeform hidden fields for UTM tracking shows how to keep the campaign with each response.
  4. Report two costs side by side. Show cost per lead and cost per customer for each channel, so a cheap form fill is not read as a win.
  5. Move budget in small steps. Wait out the sales cycle before you judge a change: leads from last week may not have had time to buy. The sales cycle length calculator gives the wait.

Cost per lead is not cost per paying customer

A cheap lead is only cheap if it buys. Channels convert leads to customers at very different rates: a lead who searched for the problem you solve and a lead from a broad giveaway fill in the same form, but one is far more likely to pay. This calculator applies one rate to every channel, so its cost per customer is only a first guess. Work out what a lead from each channel is worth with the lead value calculator, then compare channels by what they cost per paying customer with the CAC calculator. Cost per lead calculator FAQ

How do you calculate cost per lead?

Divide what you spent on a channel in a period by the number of leads it brought in that period. $3,000 of ad spend that brought 50 leads is a cost per lead of $60.

What is blended cost per lead?

Total spend across all channels divided by total leads. It describes your lead generation as a whole, while per-channel cost per lead shows which channel brings leads most cheaply.

What is the difference between cost per lead and cost per customer?

Cost per lead counts everyone who fills in a form; cost per customer counts only the ones who pay. Divide cost per lead by your lead to customer rate to get cost per customer: a $30 lead with a 10% rate is a $300 customer.

Should I always pick the channel with the lowest cost per lead?

No. A channel with cheap leads can still be the most expensive per customer if few of its leads pay. Compare channels by cost per paying customer, measured from real payments, before moving budget.

What counts as a lead?

Whatever action starts your sales process: a free signup, a demo request or a contact form. Count each kind separately if they convert to paid at different rates, and use the same definition for every channel.
