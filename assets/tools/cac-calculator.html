The CAC formula

Customer acquisition cost is what you spent to win customers divided by the new paying customers you won, over the same period:

CAC = acquisition costs / new paying customers

Per channel, use that channel’s spend and the customers it brought. Blended CAC uses the totals, and adding shared costs gives a fully loaded figure. This calculator spreads shared costs evenly over every new customer:

channel CAC = channel spend / channel customers
blended CAC = (total spend + shared costs) / total customers
channel CAC with shared costs = channel CAC + shared costs / total customers

Count only new paying customers, not trials or signups, and not renewals from customers you already had.

A worked example

These are round illustrative numbers, the same as the defaults above. In one month, paid search cost $3,000 and brought 20 new customers: a CAC of $150. Paid social cost $2,000 for 8 customers: $250. Content cost $1,200 in freelance writing for 12 customers: $100.

Together that is $6,200 of channel spend for 40 customers, a blended CAC of $155 before shared costs. Add $4,000 of shared salaries and tools and the blended CAC is $10,200 / 40 = $255. Split evenly, the shared costs add $100 to every customer, so the channels come to $250, $350 and $200. Content is the cheapest channel per customer, and on spend alone paid social costs more than twice as much.

Compare channels by paying customers, not clicks

Customer acquisition cost calculator FAQ

How do you calculate customer acquisition cost?

Divide what you spent to win customers in a period by the number of new paying customers in that period. $3,000 of ad spend that brought 20 new customers is a CAC of $150.

What costs should go into CAC?

At least the direct spend on each channel: ads, sponsorships, affiliate payouts, freelance content. A fully loaded CAC also adds shared sales and marketing costs such as salaries, agencies and tools. Leave out costs that serve existing customers, like support and hosting.

What is the difference between blended and per-channel CAC?

Blended CAC divides all acquisition costs by all new customers, so it describes the business as a whole. Per-channel CAC divides each channel’s spend by the customers that channel brought, so it shows where the next dollar is best spent.

How are shared costs split across channels here?

Evenly per customer. The calculator divides shared costs by all new customers and adds that amount to each channel’s CAC. It is a simple, neutral split; if you know a cost serves one channel only, add it to that channel’s spend instead.

Where do I get new paying customers per channel?

From a tool that links each payment to the visit that brought the buyer. Ad platforms report their own conversions and web analytics count visits, but neither sees which channel each paying customer in your billing system came from.
