The quick ratio formula

The SaaS quick ratio compares the MRR you added in a month with the MRR you lost in the same month:

Quick ratio = (new MRR + expansion MRR) / (contraction MRR + churned MRR)

New MRR is what customers who started paying this month pay per month. Expansion MRR is extra MRR from existing customers who upgraded, added seats or used more. Contraction MRR is what existing customers stopped paying when they downgraded but stayed. Churned MRR is what customers who cancelled were paying. Net new MRR is the difference between the two sides: new plus expansion, minus contraction and churned MRR.

A worked example

With the defaults above, new customers add $4,000 MRR and upgrades add $1,000, so you gained $5,000. Downgrades remove $500 and cancellations remove $1,500, so you lost $2,000.

The quick ratio is ($4,000 + $1,000) / ($500 + $1,500) = $5,000 / $2,000 = 2.5. You added $2.50 of MRR for every $1 you lost, and net new MRR is $5,000 - $2,000 = $3,000.

If cancellations doubled to $3,000 with everything else the same, the quick ratio would fall to $5,000 / $3,500 = 1.43 and net new MRR to $1,500. Growth would still be positive, but more of each month's new revenue would go to replacing what was lost.

Quick ratio calculator FAQ

How is the SaaS quick ratio calculated?

Add the MRR you gained in a month from new customers and from upgrades, then divide it by the MRR you lost in the same month to downgrades and cancellations. Quick ratio = (new MRR + expansion MRR) / (contraction MRR + churned MRR).

What does the quick ratio tell me?

It shows how much MRR you add for every dollar you lose. Above 1, MRR grew during the month; exactly 1, it stayed flat; below 1, it shrank. Two companies with the same net new MRR can have very different quick ratios: one may add a lot and lose a lot, the other may add a little and lose almost nothing.

What is a good SaaS quick ratio?

Anything above 1 means MRR grew, and a higher number means growth depends less on replacing lost revenue. What is realistic depends on your stage, pricing and how many customers you have, so compare against your own past months first and watch the trend.

What if I had no churn or contraction?

Then the quick ratio has no finite value, because you divide by zero. Every dollar of new and expansion MRR went straight into growth. Track net new MRR for that month instead, and the quick ratio again once you lose some revenue.

Why split new MRR by channel?

The quick ratio is one number for the whole business. Splitting new MRR by the channel that brought each customer shows which channels feed growth and how much of your lost MRR each one makes up for, so you know where to put more time and budget.
