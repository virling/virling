The CPM, CPC and CTR formulas

CPM, cost per mille, is what you paid to reach a thousand people:

CPM = cost / impressions × 1,000

CPC is what you paid for each visit to your site:

CPC = cost / clicks

CTR is the share of people reached who clicked:

CTR = clicks / impressions

With the revenue the placement brought, revenue per thousand impressions sits on the same scale as CPM, and the return compares revenue with cost:

revenue per thousand = revenue / impressions × 1,000 and return = (revenue - cost) / cost

For a newsletter, use the count the price was quoted on, usually sends or opens. For a podcast, use downloads of the episode; for a video, views. Compare placements on the same basis.

A worked example

With the defaults above, a newsletter sponsorship cost $1,500 and went to 50,000 subscribers. It sent 600 clicks, and the buyers from those clicks paid $2,400. These are example numbers, not a benchmark.

CPM is $1,500 / 50,000 × 1,000 = $30. CPC is $1,500 / 600 = $2.50. CTR is 600 / 50,000 = 1.2%.

Revenue per thousand is $2,400 / 50,000 × 1,000 = $48, above the $30 CPM, so the placement brought in more revenue than it cost. The return is ($2,400 - $1,500) / $1,500 = 60% on revenue. Whether it made money depends on your margin: at a 50% gross margin, $2,400 of revenue leaves $1,200, less than the $1,500 fee.

What this number misses

CPM, CPC and CTR describe what you bought, not what it earned. A cheap CPM on an audience that never buys loses money, and a high CPC can be worth it if those visitors pay. Two placements with the same CTR can bring very different revenue.

The revenue field is where most sponsorship maths goes wrong. Clicks from email apps often arrive with no referrer, podcast listeners type your URL days later, and some people search your name instead, so sponsor revenue lands in Direct or Organic search. Promo code counts miss buyers who forgot the code. You need revenue per channel and per placement from your payments, not a guess. Give each placement its own tagged link and join payments to visits, as in how to track sponsorship ROI, then check the return against your margin with the ROAS calculator.

CPM calculator FAQ

How do you calculate CPM?

CPM is cost per thousand impressions. Divide what the placement cost by its impressions, then multiply by 1,000. A $1,500 newsletter sponsorship sent to 50,000 subscribers has a CPM of $1,500 / 50,000 × 1,000 = $30.

What counts as an impression for a newsletter or podcast?

Use the number the seller quotes the price on: sends or opens for a newsletter, downloads for a podcast episode, views for a video. Compare placements on the same basis, because CPM on opens is higher than CPM on sends for the same price.

What is the difference between CPM and CPC?

CPM is the cost of reaching a thousand people, whether they click or not. CPC is the cost of each click to your site. Two placements with the same CPM can have very different CPCs if one audience clicks more than the other.

What does revenue per thousand impressions tell me?

It is the revenue the placement brought for every thousand impressions. When it is higher than the CPM, the placement brought more revenue than it cost. To make money, revenue times your gross margin has to be more than the cost.

Why is the revenue from a sponsorship hard to know?

Clicks from email apps often carry no referrer, podcast listeners type your URL later, and some people search your name. Without a tagged link per placement and payments joined to visits, that revenue lands in Direct or Organic search instead of the sponsor.
