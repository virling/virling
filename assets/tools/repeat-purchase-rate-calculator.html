The repeat purchase rate formula

Repeat purchase rate is the share of customers who ordered more than once in a period:

repeat purchase rate = customers with 2 or more orders / all customers × 100

Two numbers go with it. Purchase frequency is how many orders an average customer placed, and the average days between orders turns that into time:

purchase frequency = total orders / all customers
average days between orders = days in the period / purchase frequency

Use the same period for all three inputs and count each customer once, by email or customer ID, not by order. Guest checkouts with a new email each time look like new customers, so the real rate can be higher than the one your store reports.

A worked example

These are round illustrative numbers, the same ones the calculator starts with, not data from a real store. In a year, 1,000 customers placed 1,400 orders, and 250 of those customers ordered at least twice. The repeat purchase rate is 250 / 1,000 = 25%. The purchase frequency is 1,400 / 1,000 = 1.4 orders per customer, and the average days between orders is 365 / 1.4, about 261 days.

By first channel, organic search brought 400 customers and 100 came back (25%), paid social 300 with 45 (15%), email 150 with 60 (40%), referral 100 with 30 (30%) and other sources 50 with 15 (30%).

Why the first channel of repeat buyers matters

A channel is often judged on its first order alone. If one channel brings customers who come back and another brings one-time buyers, the first order makes them look alike while the revenue over a year does not. Grouping customers by the channel of their first order shows which channels are worth paying more to acquire a customer from, and which ones only bring a discount hunter. Read every later order back to that first channel, not to the email or bookmark that brought the repeat order, or the repeat revenue piles up under email and direct. New vs returning customer revenue by channel works through both ways of counting.

Repeat purchase rate calculator FAQ

How do you calculate repeat purchase rate?

Divide the number of customers who placed 2 or more orders by the number of all customers in the same period, and multiply by 100. 250 repeat customers out of 1,000 is a repeat purchase rate of 25%.

Is repeat purchase rate the same as purchase frequency?

No. Repeat purchase rate counts customers: what share ordered more than once. Purchase frequency counts orders: total orders divided by customers. A store where a few customers order very often can have a high frequency and a low repeat rate.

What period should I use?

One long enough for a customer to need your product again. For things bought every few weeks, 90 days can work; for most stores a full year is safer, and a short period will understate the rate.

How is average days between orders calculated here?

The days in the period divided by the purchase frequency. It is an average over all customers, including those who ordered once, so it is a rough guide. For the gap between orders of repeat buyers alone, measure the dates of their orders directly.

Where do I get repeat customers per first channel?

From a tool that links each customer's first order to the visit that brought them. Store reports often file many orders under direct or unknown, so the first channel needs every order joined to its traffic source.
