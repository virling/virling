How GA4 picks the default channel group

Google publishes the rules in Default channel group. They read the session source, medium and campaign name, ignore case and cannot be edited. The checker tests them in the order Google lists them and reports the first that matches:

  1. Direct. Source is exactly (direct) and medium is (not set) or (none).
  2. Cross-network. Campaign name contains cross-network.
  3. Paid Shopping. Source is on the shopping list or the campaign name matches ^(.*(([^a-df-z]|^)shop|shopping).*)$, and the medium matches the paid pattern ^(.*cp.*|ppc|retargeting|paid.*)$.
  4. Paid Search, Paid Social, Paid Video. Source is on the search, social or video list, and the medium matches the paid pattern.
  5. Display. Medium is display, banner, expandable, interstitial or cpm.
  6. Paid Other. Medium matches the paid pattern, whatever the source.
  7. Organic Shopping. Source is on the shopping list or the campaign name matches the shop pattern.
  8. Organic Social. Source is on the social list, or medium is social, social-network, social-media, sm, social network or social media.
  9. Organic Video. Source is on the video list, or the medium contains video.
  10. Organic Search. Source is on the search list, or medium is exactly organic.
  11. AI Assistant. Medium is exactly ai-assistant.
  12. Referral. Medium is referral, app or link.
  13. Email. Source or medium is email, e-mail, e_mail or e mail.
  14. Affiliates, Audio, SMS. Medium is affiliate or audio; source or medium is sms.
  15. Mobile Push Notifications. Medium ends with push or contains mobile or notification, or source is exactly firebase.
  16. Unassigned. Nothing above matched.

The search, social, video and shopping lists are Google’s own, from the sources and categories spreadsheet linked on that page, and the checker holds all of them. The checker counts a source only when it is on a list as written, ignoring case: google is on the search list, google.com is not. Google also has rules for clicks it reads from its own ad platforms, such as auto-tagged Google Ads clicks with gclid, which use the ad network and campaign type instead of UTM tags. A URL does not carry that data, so the checker does not run those rules.

Why traffic is Unassigned in GA4

Unassigned is not a source of traffic. It is the group GA4 uses when the source and medium arrived but no rule accepts them. The usual reasons are:

  • A misspelled medium. emial, refferal or afiliate matches nothing. Case does not matter, spelling does.
  • A medium of your own. newsletter, partner or print are reasonable names, but no default rule knows them. Unless the source is on one of Google’s lists, the visit is Unassigned.
  • A source with no medium. A link with utm_source=newsletter and nothing else leaves the medium (not set), and only a listed source can rescue it.
  • No session start. If the tag loads late, a session can have no source at all. Tags cannot fix that; see GA4 unassigned traffic and (not set) for the setup causes and what those visits paid.

To stop new links from landing in Unassigned, pick the medium first and the source second. utm_source vs utm_medium explains the split, the UTM builder makes links with a consistent medium, and the UTM checker finds missing tags, capitals and spaces in a whole list before it goes out.

Why Virling’s channel can differ

Virling has fewer, broader channels: Paid, Email, AI, Organic search, Communities, Social, Referral and Direct. Its rules are in src/channel.rs and the channels docs. Three differences explain most rows above. Virling’s Paid list is exact (cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social, paidsocial), where GA4 accepts any medium matching its pattern. Virling never leaves a tagged visit unassigned: a source it does not know is Referral. And it reads the source before the medium, so chatgpt.com is AI and youtube is Social whatever the medium says, unless the medium is paid or email.

This checker assumes the visit had no referrer, as with a link in an email or an app. With a referrer, both tools can credit the referring site instead; the traffic source checker takes a referrer and shows how Virling handles it.

GA4 channel group checker FAQ

What is the GA4 default channel grouping?

A fixed set of rules that sorts every session into a channel such as Organic Search, Paid Social or Email, using its source, medium and campaign name. Google publishes the rules and they cannot be edited, but you can build a custom channel group next to them.

Why is my traffic Unassigned in GA4?

No default rule matched the source and medium. The usual causes are a misspelled medium, a medium of your own such as newsletter or partner, or a source with no medium, on a source that is not on one of Google’s lists.

Why is utm_source=google.com with utm_medium=cpc not Paid Search?

Paid Search needs a source on Google’s search list, and the list has google but not google.com. The medium still matches the paid pattern, so the visit is Paid Other. Use utm_source=google.

Does the checker cover auto-tagged Google Ads clicks?

No. Clicks with gclid are sorted by the ad network and campaign type GA4 reads from Google Ads, which a URL does not show. The checker runs the rules for manual UTM tags.

Is anything I paste sent anywhere?

No. The rules and Google’s source lists are in the page, and nothing you paste leaves your browser.
