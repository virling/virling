The ARR formula

ARR is your monthly recurring revenue times 12:

ARR = MRR × 12

If you enter plans, MRR is the subscribers on each plan times its monthly price, added up across plans. Convert yearly plans to a monthly amount first by dividing the price by 12, or use the MRR calculator, which does it for you.

Net new ARR is the change in ARR from one month of new and churned revenue:

net new ARR = (new MRR - churned MRR) × 12

The net rate is that change as a share of current MRR, and ARR in 12 months applies it once a month for a year:

net rate = (new MRR - churned MRR) / MRR and ARR in 12 months = ARR × (1 + net rate)12

Count only recurring subscription revenue. Leave out one-time fees, setup charges, usage overages you do not bill every month, and taxes.

A worked example

With the defaults above, MRR is $10,000, so ARR is $10,000 × 12 = $120,000. In plans mode the defaults reach the same MRR: 100 subscribers at $20, 80 at $50 and 20 at $200 add up to $2,000 + $4,000 + $4,000 = $10,000.

This month brought $600 of new MRR and lost $300 to churn, so net new MRR is $300 and net new ARR is $300 × 12 = $3,600. The net rate is $300 / $10,000 = 3% a month.

If every month for a year grows by the same 3%, ARR in 12 months is $120,000 × 1.0312, about $171,091. If churn had been $900 instead, the net rate would be -3% and ARR would shrink to about $83,261.

What this number misses

ARR is one total. It says how big recurring revenue is, not which marketing brought it or which customers are about to leave. The 12 month figure also assumes this month repeats, and a single good or bad month can move it a lot. Two businesses with the same ARR and the same net rate can be in very different places: one adding most of its new MRR from a channel that keeps customers, the other from a channel whose customers churn within a few months. To see that you need revenue per channel, with each subscription payment joined to the visit that brought the customer. ARR calculator FAQ

How do you calculate ARR?

Multiply your monthly recurring revenue by 12. If you only have plans, first add up subscribers times monthly price across plans to get MRR, then multiply by 12.

What is the difference between ARR and MRR?

They measure the same recurring revenue over different periods. MRR is one month of it and ARR is a year of it at the current rate. Businesses that sell mostly yearly contracts often report ARR; those that sell mostly monthly plans usually report MRR.

What is net new ARR?

The change in ARR from one month of movements: new MRR minus churned MRR, times 12. If churn is larger than new revenue, net new ARR is negative and the business is shrinking.

How is ARR in 12 months calculated here?

The calculator takes this month's net new MRR as a share of current MRR and applies that rate once a month for 12 months. It is a projection that assumes every month looks like this one, not a forecast.

Should one-time fees count in ARR?

No. ARR counts only revenue that recurs. Leave out setup fees, one-time purchases, services and taxes, and use the price customers pay after discounts.
