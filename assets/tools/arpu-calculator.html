The ARPU formula

Average revenue per user is recurring revenue divided by the users who generate it. Average revenue per account uses paying accounts instead:

ARPU = MRR / users
ARPA = MRR / paying accounts

For the annual figures, use ARR, or multiply the monthly figures by 12:

annual ARPU = ARR / users = monthly ARPU × 12
ARR = MRR × 12

Use the same period for revenue and counts. If you take MRR at the end of the month, count the users and paying accounts at the end of the same month. To get MRR from your plans, use the MRR calculator.

ARPU versus ARPA

ARPA divides revenue by paying accounts: one company on a team plan is one account. ARPU divides by users, so the same company with 5 seats counts 5 times. If every account has a single user, the two are equal. For a B2B product sold per team or per seat, ARPA tells you what a customer is worth and ARPU tells you what a seat is worth; many SaaS teams say ARPU when they mean ARPA, so check which one a number uses before you compare.

A worked example

These are round illustrative numbers, the same as the defaults above, not data from a real business. A product has $5,000 of MRR from 165 paying accounts with 400 users between them. ARPA is $5,000 / 165 = $30.30 a month, or about $364 a year. ARPU is $5,000 / 400 = $12.50 a month, or $150 a year. Each account has 400 / 165 = 2.4 users on average.

Split by channel, organic search brought 70 accounts and $2,000 of MRR, an ARPA of $28.57, while referrals brought 25 accounts and $900, an ARPA of $36. Referral brings fewer customers, but each one pays more.

Why ARPU per channel matters

A single ARPU hides that channels bring different customers. One that brings many small accounts can look best on signups and worst on revenue. ARPA per channel, multiplied by how long customers stay, is the lifetime value you can set against what each channel costs; the LTV to CAC ratio calculator does that step. Getting the real numbers per channel means joining each subscription to the visit that started it; see revenue attribution for SaaS and trial to paid conversion by traffic source.

ARPU calculator FAQ

How do you calculate ARPU?

Divide your recurring revenue for a period by the number of users in the same period. $5,000 of MRR across 400 users is a monthly ARPU of $12.50. Multiply by 12, or divide ARR by users, for annual ARPU.

What is the difference between ARPU and ARPA?

ARPU divides revenue by users; ARPA divides it by paying accounts. An account with 5 seats counts once in ARPA and 5 times in ARPU. When every account has one user, they are the same number.

Should free users count in ARPU?

It depends on the question. Counting only paying users shows what a paying user is worth; counting free users too shows what an average signup is worth. Pick one, say which, and keep it the same each month so the trend means something.

How do yearly plans count in ARPU?

Convert them to MRR first: a yearly plan adds one twelfth of its price each month. Counting the full yearly payment in the month it was paid would make ARPU jump that month and drop for the next eleven.

Should I use monthly or annual ARPU?

Use the period you report revenue in. Products sold mostly on monthly plans usually track monthly ARPU against MRR; those sold mostly on yearly contracts often use annual ARPU against ARR. Annual ARPU is monthly ARPU times 12.
