The MRR formula

Monthly recurring revenue is the subscription revenue you can expect every month. For each plan, multiply the paying subscribers by the monthly price, then add the plans together:

MRR = sum of (subscribers × monthly price)
monthly price of a yearly plan = yearly price / 12
ARR = MRR × 12

Net new MRR is how much MRR grew during the month, after what you lost:

net new MRR = new + expansion − contraction − churned

Count only recurring subscription charges. Leave out one-time fees, setup charges, taxes and refunds, use the price customers actually pay after discounts, and do not count free trials until they convert.

A worked example

These are round illustrative numbers, the same as the defaults above, not data from a real business. A product has 100 subscribers on a $20 monthly plan ($2,000 of MRR), 40 on a $50 monthly plan ($2,000) and 25 on a $480 yearly plan. Each yearly subscriber adds $480 / 12 = $40 a month, so that plan adds $1,000. MRR is $5,000 and ARR is $60,000.

During the month the product gained $600 of MRR from new customers and $200 from upgrades, and lost $100 to downgrades and $300 to cancellations. Net new MRR is $600 + $200 − $100 − $300 = $400, so MRR was $4,600 at the start of the month.

Edge cases in MRR

Two people can get different MRR from the same billing page. Pick one treatment for each case and keep it every month:

CaseWhat to do
Annual plansDivide the yearly price by 12 and count that for each month the plan covers, not the whole charge in the month it was paid. Divide a quarterly price by 3 and enter it as a monthly price.
DiscountsCount the price after the discount. A $50 plan at 20% off is $40 of MRR while the discount runs, and $50 after it. The $10 that comes back is expansion, not new MRR.
Paused subscriptionsLeave them out while nothing is billed, as churned MRR in the month the pause starts. When billing starts again, put the amount in new MRR and note it as a return, so it does not pass for a new customer. Check the status in your billing provider: a paused subscription can still be listed as active.
Free trialsLeave them out until the first charge succeeds.
Failed cardsKeep the subscription in while your provider retries the charge. Once it ends for good, the amount is churned MRR in that month.
Cancelled, still in the paid periodKeep it in until the period they paid for ends, then count it as churned.
Usage chargesLeave out usage that changes from month to month, and count the fixed base fee.

What to do with your MRR

  • Lost MRR is close to new plus expansion. More signups will not help much until fewer customers leave. Start with the churn rate calculator, once per channel.
  • Little is lost and new MRR is small. Put more into the channel with the highest net new MRR, not the one with the most signups, and check what its customers cost with the CAC payback calculator.
  • Expansion is the larger part. Find out which channel brought the customers who upgrade, and look for more of them there.
  • One customer moves the number. At this size, read three or more months together before you move any budget.

MRR calculator FAQ

How do you calculate MRR?

Multiply the paying subscribers on each plan by the plan's monthly price and add the plans together. Yearly and quarterly plans are converted to a monthly amount first, by dividing the price by 12 or by 3.

How do yearly plans count in MRR?

As one twelfth of the yearly price every month, even though the customer pays once a year. A $480 yearly subscription adds $40 of MRR for each of the 12 months it covers, not $480 in the month it was paid.

What is the difference between MRR and ARR?

They measure the same recurring revenue over different periods. ARR is MRR times 12. Businesses that sell mostly monthly plans usually report MRR; those that sell mostly yearly contracts often report ARR.

What is net new MRR?

The change in MRR over a month: new MRR plus expansion MRR, minus contraction MRR and churned MRR. If it is negative, the business lost more recurring revenue than it added.

Should discounts, taxes and one-time fees count in MRR?

Use the price after discounts, since that is what customers pay. Leave out taxes, one-time fees and setup charges, because they are not recurring revenue you keep each month.
