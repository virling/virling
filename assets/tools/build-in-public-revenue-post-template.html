Build in public revenue update template

A monthly revenue update post tells your readers the same few things every month: your MRR or revenue, how many new customers you got and which channels the money came from. What goes in a build in public revenue update

  • The headline number. MRR for a subscription product, revenue for one-time sales. Use the same metric every month so readers can compare posts.
  • New customers. How many people paid for the first time this month. It shows growth even in a month where churn kept MRR flat.
  • Revenue by channel. Where the money came from: search, AI answers, communities, social posts or referrals. This is the part other founders can act on, and the part most updates leave out.
  • What worked and what is next. One line each. A channel that sent traffic but no revenue is as useful to share as the one that paid.

What is safe to share in public

A public post is read by customers, competitors and future hires as well as other founders, and it stays up. Post totals and shares, never anything that points at one customer.

  • Fine to post. MRR or revenue, rounded if you like, the count of new customers, channel names with their share or amount, and what you tried.
  • Keep out. Customer names, emails and logos without permission, single payments or deal sizes, which customer came from which post, and cash in bank. A small month with one big payment can give that customer away even without a name.
  • Stay consistent. Pick what you share once. Readers notice when a metric quietly disappears in a weak month.

Sharing growth without revenue

Tick Show channel shares in percent instead of amounts and the post drops your MRR or revenue and every amount, keeping new customers and each channel as a share, such as Organic search: 42%. To show growth without a figure, add your month over month rate from the revenue growth rate calculator to the What worked line.

Short or long post: X, LinkedIn and Indie Hackers

  • X. The short post is one headline line, the top channel, an optional link and #buildinpublic, a few lines for a single post. For a thread, post the short version first and reply with the channel lines from the long post.
  • LinkedIn. Use the long post. It is plain text with dashes for lists, which LinkedIn shows as typed, and the What worked and Next month lines give the post a story.
  • Indie Hackers. Use the long post as the body of your monthly update post, with the product name and month as the title. The channel list is what other founders ask about in the comments.

Build in public revenue update FAQ

What should a build in public revenue update include?

Your MRR or revenue for the month, new customers, revenue by channel and one line on what worked. Keep the same numbers every month so readers can follow along.

Can I post a revenue update without sharing revenue?

Yes. Turn on percent mode: the post leaves out your MRR or revenue and shows each channel as a share of channel revenue instead of an amount.

What is the difference between the short and long post?

The short post is a few lines for a single update. The long post lists every channel and adds what worked, next month and your dashboard link, for a thread, a forum post or a newsletter.

Where do I get revenue by channel?

Your payment provider shows revenue but not which visit brought each payment. Virling joins each visit to the payment that followed, so its Channels card shows revenue by channel for any date range.

Is it safe to post my revenue by channel publicly?

Channel names with shares or totals do not point at any customer, so most founders can post them. Leave out customer names, single payments and cash in bank; the Virling Share card never includes customers or emails.

Is this template free?

Yes, with no account. The posts are written in your browser and nothing goes out until you copy the text and post it yourself.
