The email subscriber value formula

Revenue per subscriber is the revenue your emails bring in a month, divided by the people on the list:

monthly revenue = subscribers × emails per month × click rate × conversion rate × order value

revenue per subscriber per month = monthly revenue / subscribers

Revenue per subscriber per year is the monthly figure times 12. What a new subscriber is worth is the monthly figure times the number of months a subscriber stays on the list before unsubscribing or going quiet. Use rates from the same period and the same kind of emails; a launch email and a weekly digest rarely click or convert alike.

A worked example

These are round illustrative numbers, the same ones the calculator starts with, not data from a real list or a benchmark. A list has 5,000 subscribers and gets 4 emails a month, so 5,000 × 4 = 20,000 emails are delivered. At a 2% click rate that is 20,000 × 0.02 = 400 clicks. At a 3% conversion rate, 400 × 0.03 = 12 orders, and at $50 each that is 12 × $50 = $600 a month.

Revenue per subscriber per month is $600 / 5,000 = $0.12. Per year it is $0.12 × 12 = $1.44. If a subscriber stays on the list for 12 months, a new subscriber is worth $0.12 × 12 = $1.44, which is the most you could spend to win one and still break even on email revenue alone.

Measure it from real payments with tagged links

Every rate above is a guess until you measure it. Email tools often count opens and clicks well but credit revenue by their own rules, and most email clients send no referrer, so without UTM tags a click from your newsletter lands as Direct and so does the payment that follows. Tag every link with the same source and medium, and one campaign per issue:

https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=issue-42

In Virling, utm_medium=email puts the visit in the Email channel, and each payment is credited to the visit that led to it. Click Email in the Channels card, take the revenue for a month, net of refunds, and divide it by the subscribers you had in that month. That is your column and row in the table, with no estimated rates in between. If other links use the same medium, such as trial reminders or paid placements in other newsletters, add up only the campaigns of your issues.

Use several months, because readers often pay days after the click and one large payment can move a small list. For subscriptions, a renewal is credited like the customer's first payment, so the month's revenue also includes renewals from readers who bought earlier.

Email subscriber value calculator FAQ

How do you calculate revenue per subscriber?

Divide the revenue your emails brought in a period by the number of subscribers on the list. $600 of email revenue in a month from a list of 5,000 is $0.12 per subscriber per month.

How much is an email subscriber worth?

Multiply revenue per subscriber per month by the number of months a subscriber stays on the list. At $0.12 a month for 12 months, a new subscriber is worth $1.44. It depends entirely on your own list, so measure it rather than borrow someone else's figure.

Should I use average order value or plan price?

Use average order value for a store and the first payment for a subscription. A subscriber who buys a plan may keep paying for months, so for subscriptions this calculator undercounts; enter customer lifetime value instead of the plan price if you know it.

Should I count opens or clicks?

Clicks. An open does not take anyone to your site, and many email apps load images automatically, so open counts are unreliable. Revenue follows clicks that lead to a visit and then a payment.

Why does my email tool show different revenue?

Email tools often credit a sale to any email that was opened or clicked within a window before it, so the same order can be counted by several emails and by other channels too. Click-based attribution credits each payment once, which lets you compare email fairly against search, social and ads.

How much can I spend to get a subscriber?

To break even on revenue, no more than a new subscriber is worth. To break even on profit, multiply that by your gross margin first: $1.44 at a 60% margin is $0.86. Use the value of subscribers from the same source if you can, because readers who came for a giveaway may not buy like readers who came from a product page.

Does removing inactive subscribers raise subscriber value?

It raises the average, but not your revenue. If 1,000 of 5,000 subscribers never buy and you remove them, $600 a month is now split between 4,000 people, so the figure goes from $0.12 to $0.15 while your emails still bring $600. Compare total email revenue as well as revenue per subscriber when you clean a list.
