The LTV formula

For a subscription business, a customer brings ARPU times gross margin in profit each month and stays, on average, one divided by the monthly churn rate months:

average lifetime in months = 1 / monthly churn
LTV = ARPU × gross margin / monthly churn

For an ecommerce business, multiply what a customer spends per order by how often they order and for how long, then keep the margin:

LTV = average order value × purchases per year × years retained × gross margin

Leave out the margin and you get lifetime revenue, which the calculator also shows. Use the same definitions each time you calculate it, so the trend means something. To measure churn, use the churn rate calculator; to see how many customers order again, use the repeat purchase rate calculator.

A worked example

These are round illustrative numbers, the same as the defaults above, not data from a real business.

Subscription: ARPU is $50 a month at an 80% gross margin, so each customer brings $50 × 0.8 = $40 of gross profit a month. Monthly churn is 4%, so the average customer stays 1 / 0.04 = 25 months. LTV is $40 × 25 = $1,000, and lifetime revenue is $50 × 25 = $1,250.

Ecommerce: the average order is $60, customers order 2.5 times a year and keep buying for 3 years, so each places 2.5 × 3 = 7.5 orders and spends $60 × 7.5 = $450. At a 40% margin, LTV is $450 × 0.4 = $180.

By channel, with the subscription defaults: organic search customers pay $45 a month and churn 3%, an LTV of $45 × 0.8 / 0.03 = $1,200; paid search customers pay $55 but churn 6%, an LTV of $55 × 0.8 / 0.06 = $733.33. The channel with the higher price brings the lower lifetime value.

LTV versus the LTV to CAC ratio

LTV tells you what a customer is worth. It does not tell you whether a channel pays for itself; for that, set LTV against what it cost to win the customer. The LTV to CAC ratio calculator does that step per channel, with payback months. Getting real LTV per channel means joining each payment to the visit that started it; see revenue attribution for SaaS.

LTV calculator FAQ

How do you calculate customer lifetime value?

For a subscription, multiply ARPU by gross margin and divide by the monthly churn rate: $50 a month at 80% margin and 4% churn is $40 / 0.04 = $1,000. For ecommerce, multiply average order value by purchases per year, years retained and margin.

Should LTV use revenue or gross margin?

Gross margin, if you will compare it with what you spend to win customers. Revenue also pays for hosting, payment fees, goods and support, so LTV on revenue alone makes every customer look more valuable than they are. This calculator shows both.

What if my churn rate is very low?

One divided by churn grows fast as churn falls: 1% monthly churn implies customers stay 100 months on average, longer than most businesses have data for. If your product is young, cap the lifetime at a period you can defend and say that you did.

How do I find purchases per year and years retained?

Purchases per year is orders in the last 12 months divided by the customers who ordered in that time. Years retained is harder to measure early on; start from how long past customers kept ordering and update it as your order history grows.

What is the difference between LTV and the LTV to CAC ratio?

LTV is what a customer is worth. The LTV to CAC ratio divides that by customer acquisition cost, to show whether the customers a channel brings are worth what the channel costs.
