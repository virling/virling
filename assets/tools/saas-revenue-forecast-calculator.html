The formulas in plain words

The forecast walks forward one month at a time. Each month starts with the MRR the previous month ended with. Expansion and churn are shares of that starting MRR:

expansion MRR = starting MRR × expansion rate

churned MRR = starting MRR × churn rate

New MRR starts at the amount you enter and grows by the monthly growth rate, so month 2 adds a little more than month 1, month 3 a little more again:

new MRR in month m = new MRR per month × (1 + growth)m − 1

Add them up to get the month's ending MRR, which becomes the next month's starting MRR:

ending MRR = starting MRR + new MRR + expansion MRR − churned MRR

ARR at the end is the ending MRR of the last month times 12. New MRR added in a month starts churning the month after. Set growth to 0 if you expect to add the same new MRR every month, or use a negative number if new MRR is falling.

A worked example

These are round illustrative numbers, the same as the defaults above, not data from a real business. A product has $10,000 of MRR, adds $2,000 of new MRR in month 1, and expects that to grow 5% a month. It loses 3% of MRR to churn and gains 1% from upgrades each month.

Month 1: $10,000 + $2,000 new + $100 expansion − $300 churned = $11,800. Month 2 starts at $11,800 and adds $2,100 of new MRR ($2,000 × 1.05), $118 of expansion and loses $354 to churn, ending at $13,664. After 12 months the product ends at about $36,737 of MRR, or $440,842 of ARR, having added about $31,834 of new MRR along the way. In month 12 it loses about $1,020 to churn, against $340 of expansion: churn grows with the base, so new MRR has to keep growing to keep the curve going up.

A forecast by channel needs real data

The calculator treats new MRR as one number. In practice it comes from several channels, each with its own volume, growth and churn, and a forecast is only as good as your read on which of them will keep delivering. Guessing that split is how forecasts go wrong. SaaS revenue forecast calculator FAQ

How do I forecast SaaS revenue?

Start from your current MRR and step forward one month at a time. Each month, add new MRR from new customers and expansion MRR from upgrades, and subtract the MRR lost to churn. The result is next month's starting MRR. Repeat for as many months as you want to forecast.

What is the difference between MRR and ARR in a forecast?

MRR is the recurring revenue you bill in a month. ARR is that figure times 12. The calculator shows ARR as ending MRR times 12, which is the annual run rate at the end of the forecast, not the revenue you collect during it.

Why does churn matter more as MRR grows?

Churn is a percentage of the MRR you already have, so the dollars lost each month grow with your base. New MRR does not grow on its own. At some point the churned MRR catches up with the new MRR and growth flattens unless you add more new MRR or lower churn.

How accurate is a revenue forecast like this?

It is only as good as the inputs. It uses one churn rate, one expansion rate and a steady growth rate in new MRR, while real months vary. Use your own measured rates from recent months and treat the result as a range to plan around, not a promise.

Does the calculator store my numbers?

No. The calculation runs in your browser and nothing is uploaded or saved.
