The ACV formula

ACV is the recurring value of one contract, spread evenly over its term and scaled to a year:

ACV = contract value / term in months × 12

Contract value here means the recurring charges over the whole term, after discounts and without one-time fees or taxes. Total contract value adds the one-time fees back, and the monthly equivalent is the recurring value per month:

TCV = contract value + one-time fees and monthly equivalent = contract value / term in months

The average of several contracts is the sum of their ACVs divided by how many there are:

average ACV = sum of ACVs / number of contracts

ACV, TCV and ARR compared

MetricWhat it coversFormula
ACVOne contract, per year, without one-time feesContract value / term in months × 12
TCVOne contract, whole term, with one-time feesContract value + one-time fees
ARRAll active customers, per year, recurring revenue onlyMRR × 12

Use ACV to compare deals with different terms, TCV for what a customer has committed to in total, and ARR for the size of the recurring business. The ARR calculator works out the last one from MRR or plans.

A worked example

These are the example numbers filled in above. A customer signs for $36,000 over 24 months and pays a $3,000 setup fee. ACV is $36,000 / 24 × 12 = $18,000, TCV is $36,000 + $3,000 = $39,000 and the monthly equivalent is $36,000 / 24 = $1,500.

Add two more contracts: $12,000 over 12 months, with an ACV of $12,000, and $90,000 over 36 months with a $3,000 fee, with an ACV of $30,000. Average ACV is ($18,000 + $12,000 + $30,000) / 3 = $20,000. Average TCV is ($39,000 + $12,000 + $93,000) / 3 = $48,000, higher because two of the contracts run for more than a year and carry fees.

What this number misses

ACV says how large a deal is per year, not when the cash arrives, whether the customer renews or which marketing brought them. A few large contracts can also lift the average above what most customers pay. ACV calculator FAQ

How do you calculate ACV?

Divide the recurring value of the contract by its term in months, then multiply by 12. Leave one-time fees out. A contract worth $36,000 over 24 months has an ACV of $18,000.

What is the difference between ACV and TCV?

ACV is the recurring value of one contract per year. TCV is everything the customer commits to over the whole term, one-time fees included. For a 12-month contract with no one-time fees the two are the same.

What is the difference between ACV and ARR?

ACV describes one contract, or the average of several. ARR adds up the yearly recurring revenue of every active customer. If every active contract is counted, with no one-time fees in either number, the sum of their ACVs usually matches ARR.

Should one-time fees count in ACV?

This calculator leaves them out, because setup, training and other one-time fees do not repeat each year. They count in TCV. Some teams include them in the first year's ACV, so check which definition is used before comparing numbers.

How do you calculate ACV for a contract shorter than a year?

The same formula scales it up to 12 months: $3,000 over 6 months gives an ACV of $6,000. That assumes the contract renews at the same price. If it will not renew, TCV is the safer number to report.

How do you calculate average ACV?

Work out the ACV of each contract, add them up and divide by the number of contracts. Each contract counts once, whatever its term, so a three-year deal does not weigh more than a one-year deal.
