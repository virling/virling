The cart abandonment rate formula

Cart abandonment rate is the share of carts that never became an order:

cart abandonment rate = (carts created - orders completed) / carts created × 100

The revenue left in abandoned carts is an estimate from your average order value:

revenue left in carts = (carts created - orders completed) × average order value

Count carts and orders over the same period. A cart counts when a shopper adds at least one item; some stores count started checkouts instead, which gives a lower rate because it leaves out shoppers who never reached checkout. Pick one and keep it, so this month compares with the last.

A worked example

These are round illustrative numbers, the same ones the calculator starts with, not data from a real store. In a month, shoppers create 1,000 carts and complete 300 orders. 700 carts were abandoned, so the abandonment rate is 700 / 1,000 = 70%, and 30% of carts became orders.

With an average order value of $60, the abandoned carts held about 700 × $60 = $42,000. That is an upper bound, not money you can win back: abandoned carts are often smaller or larger than completed orders, some shoppers come back and order later, and many were only comparing prices or checking shipping costs.

Abandonment by channel

One store-wide rate hides big differences between traffic sources. Shoppers from an email to past customers often know what they want and finish checkout, while a first visit from a social ad may add an item to see the total and leave. A store-wide rate can rise just because a campaign sent more of the second kind, with nothing wrong in the checkout. To read it per source, run this calculator once for each channel with that channel's carts and orders.

Virling counts orders and revenue per channel: every visit gets one channel from its first pageview, and each Shopify or WooCommerce order is joined to the visit that brought the buyer. The Almost paid card on the dashboard lists visitors who reached pricing or checkout and did not pay, by channel or by the last page they saw. To count carts, send a goal from your store when an item is added, such as virling("goal", "add_to_cart"), then click a channel on the dashboard to filter the Goals card to it. Cart abandonment rate calculator FAQ

How do you calculate cart abandonment rate?

Subtract completed orders from carts created, divide by carts created and multiply by 100. 1,000 carts and 300 orders is an abandonment rate of 70%.

What is the difference between cart abandonment and checkout abandonment?

Cart abandonment starts counting when an item is added to the cart. Checkout abandonment only counts shoppers who started checkout, so it leaves out everyone who left from the cart page and is usually lower. Say which one you report.

Is the revenue left in carts money I can recover?

Not all of it. It is the number of abandoned carts times your average order value, an upper bound. Some shoppers come back and order later, and many carts were never meant to become orders.

Why is cart abandonment different for each traffic source?

Each source brings shoppers at a different moment. Returning customers from an email are often ready to buy, while first-time visitors from an ad may be browsing or checking shipping costs. A shift in your traffic mix can move the store-wide rate on its own.

Where do I get carts and orders per channel?

From a tool that links each cart and order to the visit that brought the shopper. Virling joins each Shopify or WooCommerce order to its visit, and a goal sent when an item is added to the cart can be split by channel the same way.
