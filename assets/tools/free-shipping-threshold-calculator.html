Free shipping threshold calculator

Before you offer free shipping over a set order value, check whether the bigger orders pay for the shipping you take on. Enter your average order value, gross margin, average shipping cost and the threshold you have in mind. Everything runs in your browser.

Gross margin is the share of the order value left after the cost of the products. Average shipping cost is what you pay the carrier per order, the cost you take on when an order ships free.

How the calculator works

It compares the gross profit on your average order today, with the customer paying for shipping, against an order that grows to the threshold and ships free. With the gross margin as a share of the order value:

extra order value needed = threshold - average order value

margin per order before = average order value × gross margin

margin per order after = threshold × gross margin - shipping cost

share of orders that must move up = shipping cost / (extra order value × gross margin)

The share is a cautious figure: it assumes you pay shipping on every order once the offer is live, and asks how many orders must grow to the threshold for the extra gross profit to cover it. Orders that stay below the threshold and still pay for shipping make the real cost lower. If an order that reaches the threshold earns less than your average order does today, no share of orders moving up makes the offer pay, and the calculator shows it as not reachable.

The lowest threshold that covers shipping on an order that moves up is the average order value plus the shipping cost divided by the gross margin.

A worked example

These are round hypothetical numbers, the same ones the calculator starts with, not data from a real store. A store has a $60 average order value and a 50% gross margin, so the average order makes $30 of gross profit, and shipping costs it $8 an order. A free shipping threshold of $80 asks each customer to add $20. An order that reaches $80 makes $40 of gross profit, less $8 of shipping, which is $32, or $2 more than today. If the store ends up paying shipping on every order, 8 / (20 × 50%) = 80% of orders must move up to $80 to break even.

At $76, the lowest threshold that covers shipping in this example, an order that moves up earns exactly what the average order does today. Set the threshold lower and every order that moves up earns less than before.

Check which orders really moved

After you launch, compare your average order value and the number of orders by channel against a normal period. A higher average can come from one channel, such as email to past buyers, while new visitors from ads leave at the shipping step. Free shipping threshold calculator FAQ

How do you calculate a free shipping threshold?

Start from your average order value and add the order value it takes to pay for shipping: the lowest threshold that covers it is average order value + shipping cost / gross margin. With a $60 average order, a 50% margin and $8 shipping, that is 60 + 8 / 0.5 = $76. Set the threshold above that so the orders that move up earn more than they do today.

Why does the gross margin matter so much?

You pay shipping in full, but you only keep your gross margin on the extra items a customer adds. At a 50% margin, a customer must add $16 of products to cover $8 of shipping. At a 25% margin, they must add $32.

What does the share of orders that must move up mean?

It is the share of orders that must grow from your average order value to the threshold for the extra gross profit to cover shipping, if you paid shipping on every order. It is a cautious figure, because orders below the threshold still pay for their own shipping. Above 100%, the offer cannot pay for itself at that threshold.

Does this include payment fees, returns and ad spend?

No. It compares gross profit, the order value minus the cost of the products, less shipping. If payment fees or packaging come with each order, add them to the shipping cost. For the full margin on an order, including what you paid to win it, use the contribution margin calculator.
