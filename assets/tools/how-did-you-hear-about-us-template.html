How to write the options

  • Keep it to about ten options. A long list gets skimmed, and people pick the first one that is close enough.
  • Name examples in brackets. “AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity)” is clearer than “AI”, and you can split it later if one name dominates.
  • Always include “AI assistant” and “Friend or colleague”. Both usually land in Direct in analytics, so they are where the survey adds the most.
  • End with “Other (please specify)” and a text box. Read those answers every month. When one keeps coming up, such as a podcast or a newsletter by name, give it its own option.
  • Shuffle the order if your form allows it, but keep “Other” last. People pick the top options more often.
  • Ask one question, single choice. Multiple choice makes every answer count, so nothing stands out.

Where to ask it

  • At signup, for SaaS. One optional field on the signup form reaches every new account. The first onboarding screen works too and keeps the signup form short.
  • At checkout or on the thank-you page, for stores. The thank-you page does not slow the purchase down. Ask only first-time buyers, since a repeat buyer remembers your shop, not how they found it.
  • In onboarding, for sales-led products. Add it to the first call or the onboarding form, and write the answer into your CRM so it stays with the customer.

Make the question optional. A required question fills up with whatever option is first.

Comparing survey answers with tracked sources

Survey answers and analytics disagree, and both can be right. Analytics records the last click it can see. The survey records what the buyer remembers, which is often the first time they heard your name. The table shows where each answer usually lands in tracked sources, using the channels Virling counts.

How did you hear about us FAQ

What are good “How did you hear about us” options?

About ten options that match where your buyers really are: search engine, AI assistant, friend or colleague, social media, communities, podcast, YouTube, blog post, newsletter and events for SaaS, plus influencers, ads and in-person for stores. End with “Other (please specify)” and a text box.

Should the question be required?

No. A required question collects rushed answers, usually the first option in the list. Optional answers are fewer but more honest.

Where should I ask how customers heard about us?

For SaaS, on the signup form or the first onboarding screen. For stores, on the checkout or thank-you page, and only for first-time buyers.

Why do survey answers not match my analytics?

Analytics sees the last click it can trace, and a survey records what the buyer remembers. Word of mouth, podcasts and links shared in chat apps leave little or no trace, so they show up in the survey and land as Direct in analytics.
