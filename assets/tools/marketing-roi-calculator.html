The marketing ROI formula

Marketing ROI is the profit a channel made after paying for itself, divided by what it cost. Revenue alone overstates it, so the calculator first takes your gross margin: the share of each sale left after the cost of the product, hosting, payment fees and delivery.

gross profit = revenue × gross margin
profit = gross profit - spend
ROI = profit / spend

An ROI of 0% means the channel exactly paid for itself. Above 0% it earned more than it cost; below 0% it lost money. Count every cost of the channel in spend, not only the ad bill: a blog that costs nothing in ads still costs writers, tools and hours, and leaving those out makes it look free.

A worked example

With the defaults above, four channels cost $13,500 and brought $30,000 of revenue at a 70% gross margin. That is $21,000 of gross profit, $7,500 of profit after spend and an overall ROI of $7,500 / $13,500 = 55.6%. Ranked by ROI:

  • Content and SEO: $3,000 of spend, $9,000 of revenue. Gross profit $6,300, profit $3,300, ROI 110%.
  • Paid search: $5,000 of spend, $12,000 of revenue. Gross profit $8,400, profit $3,400, ROI 68%.
  • Paid social: $4,000 of spend, $7,000 of revenue. Gross profit $4,900, profit $900, ROI 22.5%.
  • Newsletter sponsorships: $1,500 of spend, $2,000 of revenue. Gross profit $1,400, a loss of $100, ROI -6.7%.

Paid search made the most profit, but content and SEO returned more for each dollar it cost. The sponsorships brought revenue yet lost money once margin is counted, which a revenue-only view would hide.

ROI by channel needs revenue joined to the visit that brought it

The spend column is the easy part: it is on your invoices. The revenue column is where ROI by channel goes wrong. Ad platforms report the conversions they can see within their own attribution windows, and a sale that touched two platforms can be counted by both, so the revenue per channel can add up to more than you collected. Organic channels such as content, communities and AI answers have no platform report at all, so their revenue is often a guess.

To get an ROI you can trust per channel, each payment has to be joined to the visit that brought the customer, and that visit to its channel. Then every dollar of revenue is counted once, under one channel, and the totals match what you were paid. Marketing ROI calculator FAQ

How do you calculate marketing ROI?

Multiply the revenue a channel brought by your gross margin to get gross profit, subtract what the channel cost, and divide the result by that cost. $12,000 of revenue at a 70% margin from $5,000 of spend is $8,400 of gross profit, $3,400 of profit and an ROI of 68%.

What is the difference between marketing ROI and ROAS?

ROAS divides revenue by ad spend and ignores what the sales cost you to deliver. ROI uses profit after margin and after the channel's full cost, so it shows whether the channel actually made money. A ROAS above 1x can still be a negative ROI.

What costs should I include in marketing spend?

Everything the channel needed in the period: ad spend or sponsorship fees, tools and software, freelancers or agencies, and the time your team spent on it. Leaving out tools and time makes organic channels look free and their ROI look infinite.

What is a good marketing ROI?

There is no single number. Any ROI above 0% means the channel paid for itself in the period, and a higher ROI means each dollar returned more. Compare channels with each other over the same period rather than against an outside benchmark, and allow for revenue that arrives after the period ends.
