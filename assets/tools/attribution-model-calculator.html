How each attribution model splits the revenue

Every model starts from the same journey: the visits a customer made, oldest first, ending with the payment. They differ only in which visits get credit and how much.

  • Last non-direct gives all the revenue to the latest touchpoint that was not Direct. If every touchpoint was Direct, the latest Direct one gets it.
  • First touch gives all the revenue to the first touchpoint, Direct or not.
  • Last touch gives all the revenue to the touchpoint right before the payment, Direct included.
  • Linear splits the revenue equally: credit per touchpoint = payment / number of touchpoints.
  • Position-based (40/20/40) gives 40% to the first touchpoint, 40% to the last, and splits the remaining 20% equally between the ones in the middle. With only two touchpoints, each gets 50%.

Linear and position-based count Direct like any other touchpoint. When a channel appears more than once in a journey, the calculator adds its shares together.

A worked example

The defaults above describe a customer who found you on Hacker News, came back from a Google search, clicked a link in your newsletter, then typed your URL and paid $100.

  • Last non-direct skips the final Direct visit and gives $100 to Newsletter.
  • First touch gives $100 to Hacker News.
  • Last touch gives $100 to Direct.
  • Linear gives $25 to each of the four touchpoints.
  • Position-based gives $40 to Hacker News, $40 to Direct, and $10 each to Google and Newsletter.

Three channels can each claim the whole payment depending on the model. That is why a report should always say which model it uses, and why Direct almost never deserves full credit: people type a URL they already learned about somewhere else.

Attribution model calculator FAQ

What is the difference between last touch and last non-direct?

Last touch credits the visit right before the payment, even when it was Direct. Last non-direct skips Direct visits and credits the latest visit that came from a real source, falling back to Direct only when every visit was Direct.

Why is Direct handled differently?

A Direct visit has no referrer: a typed URL, a bookmark, or a link from an app that hides where it came from. People rarely type a URL they have never seen, so crediting Direct usually hides the channel that actually introduced them.

How does position-based attribution work?

It gives 40% of the revenue to the first touchpoint, 40% to the last, and splits the other 20% equally between the touchpoints in between. With two touchpoints, each gets half.

Which attribution model should I use?

Last non-direct suits most products, since it credits the channel that brought the customer back to buy. First touch shows which channels introduce people who later pay and fits longer buying cycles. Compare both before moving budget.
