How to calculate sales cycle length

The sales cycle of one deal is the number of days from the first touch to the close:

sales cycle = close date − first touch date

The first touch is the first time the buyer reached you: a first website visit, a demo request or a first reply. Pick one definition and use it for every deal. Count only closed deals, and use the date the deal was won or the first payment arrived, again the same for every deal.

A worked example

The defaults above are made-up dates to show the method. Five deals took 42, 21, 79, 25 and 103 days. Sorted, that is 21, 25, 42, 79 and 103, so the median sales cycle is 42 days. The average is 270 days / 5 = 54 days, pulled up by the two slow deals. The longest took 103 days.

Why the attribution window should be longer than your sales cycle

An attribution window is how many days before a payment a visit can earn credit for it. Visits older than the window are ignored. If your median deal takes 42 days and the window is 30, the visit that started at least half of your deals has already dropped out when the payment arrives, and the credit goes to a later visit or to nobody.

So set the window longer than most of your deals take, not just the typical one, and add any wait between the signed deal and the paid invoice. In the example a 90-day window covers four of the five deals, and only a 365-day window covers the 103-day one. Sales cycle calculator FAQ

How do you calculate sales cycle length?

For each closed deal, count the days from the first touch, such as the first website visit or first contact, to the close date. The average sales cycle is the sum of those days divided by the number of deals; the median is the middle value once the deals are sorted.

Should I use the average or the median sales cycle?

Look at both. One deal that took a year pulls the average up, while the median shows what a typical deal takes. If the average is far above the median, a few slow deals are stretching it.

What is the average sales cycle for B2B SaaS?

It depends on your price, the size of the buyer and whether a sales call is involved, so a figure from another company says little about yours. Measure your own closed deals and compare channels and deal sizes with each other.

How long should my attribution window be?

Longer than most of your deals take from first touch to payment, including any wait between the signed deal and the paid invoice. If the window is shorter than the cycle, the visit that started a slow deal falls outside it and another visit, or nothing, gets the credit.
