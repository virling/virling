The SaaS magic number formula

The magic number compares the growth in revenue this quarter with what was spent on sales and marketing the quarter before, because spend takes time to turn into paying customers. With quarterly revenue, the growth is multiplied by four to make it annual:

magic number = (revenue this quarter - revenue last quarter) × 4 / sales and marketing spend last quarter

With ARR, the figure is already annual, so the change in ARR is used as it is:

magic number = (ARR at end of this quarter - ARR at end of last quarter) / sales and marketing spend last quarter

A worked example

With the defaults above, revenue grew from $250,000 last quarter to $280,000 this quarter, a gain of $30,000. Times four, that is $120,000 of new annual revenue. Last quarter's sales and marketing cost $160,000, so the magic number is $120,000 / $160,000 = 0.75: each dollar spent added 75 cents of annual revenue. The same company entered by ARR, going from $1,000,000 to $1,120,000, gives $120,000 / $160,000 = 0.75 too.

What the other results mean

  • New annual revenue is the quarter's growth in annual terms: the revenue change times four, or the ARR change.
  • Spend per $1 of new annual revenue is the magic number turned around: what one dollar of new annual revenue cost to win.
  • Months of new revenue to repay spend is twelve divided by the magic number: how long the new revenue takes to equal the spend, before gross margin and churn.

The number is net growth: customers who cancelled or downgraded during the quarter pull it down, and upgrades push it up. A quarter with heavy churn can show a low magic number even when sales and marketing worked well, so read it next to your churn rate.

SaaS magic number calculator FAQ

How is the SaaS magic number calculated?

Take the change in revenue from last quarter to this quarter, multiply it by four to make it annual, and divide by last quarter's sales and marketing spend. If you track ARR, divide the change in ARR by last quarter's spend instead.

Why use last quarter's spend?

Sales and marketing spend takes time to turn into paying customers. Comparing this quarter's growth with the previous quarter's spend lines up the money with the revenue it most likely produced.

Should I use revenue or ARR?

Either works if you use the same one for both quarters. Quarterly revenue includes one-off payments and is multiplied by four; ARR counts only recurring revenue and is already annual, so it is often the cleaner input for subscription businesses.

Does churn affect the magic number?

Yes. The formula uses the net change in revenue, so cancellations and downgrades in the quarter reduce it and upgrades increase it. A low result can come from churn rather than from weak sales and marketing.

What counts as sales and marketing spend?

Everything spent to win customers in the quarter: ads, sponsorships, events, sales and marketing tools, and the salaries and commissions of the people doing sales and marketing.
