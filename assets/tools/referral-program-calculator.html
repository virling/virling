The referral program formula

Start from the customers you already win each month and follow them through the program:

referred customers = customers × share who refer × friends per referrer × referral conversion rate

This calculator assumes both rewards are paid only when a referred friend becomes a customer, the usual double-sided "give and get" setup. So each referred customer costs both rewards:

cost per referred customer = reward to referrer + reward to new customer

reward cost = referred customers × cost per referred customer

ROI = (referred customers × customer value - reward cost) ÷ reward cost

The program breaks even when the rewards for one referred customer equal what that customer is worth. So the highest combined reward you can pay is the customer value itself, and anything below it is profit.

A worked example

These are made-up numbers, the defaults above. A business wins 200 customers a month. 10% of them, 20 people, refer friends, 2 friends each, so 40 friends hear about it. 25% of those buy: 10 referred customers a month. The referrer gets $20 and the friend gets $20, so each referred customer costs $40 and the month's rewards cost $400. Each customer brings $300 of gross profit, $3,000 in total, so the ROI is ($3,000 - $400) ÷ $400 = 650%. The rewards could go as high as $300 per referred customer, for example $280 to the referrer while the friend keeps $20, before the program stopped paying back.

When a referral program loses money

Four things turn a program that pays back on paper into one that does not. The figures continue the two made-up programs:

LeakWhat it costs, and how to see it in payments by source
RefundsIf 3 of the store's 16 orders are refunded, the goods can be sold again and the rewards are kept, 13 orders bring $364 against $320 of rewards: the profit falls from $128 to $44 and the ROI to 13.8%. At 5 refunds it is a $12 loss. Filter payments to refunded ones and check which referral links they came from. Pay rewards after the refund window.
Coupon leakageA friend code posted in public stops being a referral: each use costs the store $20, whoever typed it. One referrer with far more payments than friends, or codes redeemed without a visit through any referral link, is the sign. See how to check coupon site orders.
Renewals that still paySuppose the subscription gives the referrer $5 for every month the friend pays, instead of $30 once. Over 12 months that is $60, the two rewards come to $75 and $165 is left. A friend who moves down to a $5 plan brings $4 of gross profit a month and still costs $5. Check that the renewals on the friend's timeline still cover the credit, and end it after a set number of months.
Would have bought anywaySuch a friend would have brought the store $28 without a reward, so it gives up $20 for nothing. Open the payment: a visit from search, email or an ad before the referral click is the sign. A friend who paid you before keeps the source of that first payment, so a reward for a friend missing from the referrer's payments is worth a check too. It is a hint and not proof, so pause the rewards for a few weeks and compare total new customers.

How many friends who would have bought anyway a program can carry is a break-even too:

break-even share = (customer value - cost per referred customer) ÷ customer value

For the subscription that is $195 ÷ $240 = 81.25%. For the store it is $8 ÷ $28 = 28.6%: with 4 of the 16 orders it still makes 12 × $8 - 4 × $20 = $16, and with 5 it loses 5 × $20 - 11 × $8 = $12.

It does not read codes or pay rewards, so match those against your store and your referral software.

Check which referred customers really paid

The math only holds if the referred customers you count are the ones who actually paid and stayed. Signups and codes entered at checkout say little about refunds, cancellations or renewals, and a friend who would have bought anyway still costs a reward. Give each referrer their own link, tie each payment to the visit before it, and compare the revenue per referrer with what you paid out. Referral program calculator FAQ

How do you calculate the ROI of a referral program?

Multiply the referred customers by what a customer is worth, subtract the rewards you paid, and divide by the rewards. 10 referred customers worth $300 each, with $400 in rewards, give an ROI of ($3,000 - $400) ÷ $400 = 650%.

How much should I pay for a referral?

Never more than a referred customer is worth in gross profit, counting both the referrer's and the friend's reward. Stay well below that to leave room for refunds, friends who would have bought anyway and the cost of running the program.

Which customer value should I use?

Use gross profit, not revenue: the order value times your gross margin for one-off purchases, or the lifetime value times your margin for subscriptions. Referred customers may stay longer or shorter than others, so measure them separately once you have the data.

What does this calculator leave out?

It assumes rewards are paid only on a successful referral and that every referred customer is new business. Rewards paid on signup, fraud, refunds, the referral software and customers who would have bought without the referral are not included.
