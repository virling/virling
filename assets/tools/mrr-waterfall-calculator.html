MRR waterfall calculator

Enter the MRR you started a month with, then the MRR you gained from new customers, upgrades and returning customers, and the MRR you lost to downgrades and cancellations. You get ending MRR, net new MRR and the net MRR growth rate, with a waterfall chart, sometimes called an MRR bridge, ready for a board or investor update. Everything runs in your browser.

Use the same month for every number. Starting MRR is what customers paid per month at the start of the month. New MRR comes from first-time customers, expansion from upgrades and added seats, reactivated MRR from customers who had cancelled and came back, contraction from downgrades by customers who stayed, churned MRR from customers who cancelled.

The MRR waterfall formula

An MRR waterfall starts from the MRR at the beginning of a month, adds each source of gained MRR, subtracts each source of lost MRR and lands on the MRR at the end of the month:

Ending MRR = starting MRR + new + expansion + reactivated - contraction - churned MRR

Net new MRR is the change in the middle, and the net MRR growth rate compares it with where you started:

Net new MRR = new + expansion + reactivated - contraction - churned MRR

Net MRR growth rate = net new MRR / starting MRR

Keep each customer in one bucket per month. A customer who signs up for the first time is new MRR, even if they upgrade a week later. A customer who cancelled in an earlier month and pays again is reactivated MRR, not new MRR, so returning customers do not make acquisition look better than it is.

A worked example

The defaults above are made-up sample numbers, not data from a real company. The month starts at $20,000 MRR. New customers add $2,000, upgrades add $800 and returning customers add $200, so you gained $3,000. Downgrades remove $400 and cancellations remove $600, so you lost $1,000.

Net new MRR is $3,000 - $1,000 = $2,000. Ending MRR is $20,000 + $2,000 = $22,000. The net MRR growth rate is $2,000 / $20,000 = 10% for the month.

If cancellations tripled to $1,800 with everything else the same, net new MRR would fall to $800, ending MRR to $20,800 and the growth rate to 4%. The new MRR bar would look the same, but the waterfall would show where the growth went.

Split new MRR by acquisition channel

The new MRR bar is one number, but it is made of customers from search, referrals, a launch, a newsletter or paid ads. A board or investor will often ask which of those drove the month. Split new MRR by the channel that brought each customer to answer that, as described in MRR by acquisition channel, and put the split in your investor update. MRR waterfall calculator FAQ

What is an MRR waterfall?

It is a chart that walks from starting MRR to ending MRR for a period. Bars for new, expansion and reactivated MRR step up, bars for contraction and churned MRR step down, and the last bar is the MRR you ended with. It shows not only how much MRR changed but why.

Is an MRR bridge the same as an MRR waterfall?

Yes. Both names describe the same breakdown from starting MRR to ending MRR. Some teams show it as a chart and others as a table with one row per movement; the numbers are the same.

What is the difference between new and reactivated MRR?

New MRR comes from customers paying you for the first time. Reactivated MRR comes from customers who cancelled before and started paying again. Keeping them apart stops returning customers from inflating what your acquisition channels brought.

How do I calculate net MRR growth rate?

Add new, expansion and reactivated MRR, subtract contraction and churned MRR to get net new MRR, then divide by the MRR you started the month with. Net MRR growth rate = net new MRR / starting MRR.

Why split new MRR by channel?

The waterfall shows how much new MRR you added but not where it came from. Splitting new MRR by the channel that brought each customer shows which channels feed growth, so you know where to put more time and budget and can answer the question in a board meeting.
