Press coverage revenue tracking: do mentions bring customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-21.

To see whether press coverage brings customers, join each payment to the visit that came before it, then look at which visits came from the article. Where you control the link, hand the writer a tagged link with the outlet as utm_source and the story as utm_campaign, so each mention gets its own row. Where you do not, the click usually carries the outlet's host as the referrer, so it still shows under that host. Readers who type your URL after reading print or hearing your name show as Direct. Compare paying customers and revenue per visitor for each mention over a few months, not page views or reach.

A feature in a trade magazine, a line in a roundup, a mention on a podcast or a news story about your launch can each bring a wave of readers. Most of them leave. The question for each mention is whether any of the people it sent became paying customers, and how that compares with your other mentions and your site average.

How a visit from a mention is credited

Virling gives every session one channel, decided by its first pageview. The rules run in order and the first match wins:

  1. utm_medium. email or newsletter is Email, and cpc or paid is Paid, before anything else is checked.
  2. The referrer host. A click from a web page usually arrives with the outlet's host as the referrer. Most news sites, magazines and blogs land in Referral under their own host. Known hosts land in their own channel: news.ycombinator.com, reddit.com and producthunt.com are Communities, and linkedin.com, x.com and youtube.com are Social.
  3. utm_source, or ref. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, and ref when that is missing. An outlet's name is not a known source, so it lands in Referral.
  4. Everything else is Direct. No referrer, no source and no in-app browser it can name.

The referrer is a host only, never the article's path. Two stories on the same outlet share one row unless their links are tagged. The full rule order and host lists are in channels, and the guest post revenue tracking guide covers the same rules for articles you write yourself.

Step by step

1. Ask for tagged links

Journalists and roundup editors copy whatever link you hand them. Make that link the tagged one:

  • Put it in the pitch email, the press kit, the company boilerplate and any form that asks for a product URL.
  • Use one utm_source per outlet and keep it for every story there, utm_medium=referral on all of them, and one utm_campaign per story.
  • Do not use email or newsletter as the medium, even when the story also goes out in the outlet's newsletter, or every click from the web page counts as Email.
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=examplenews&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=examplenews-launch-story
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=exampletrade&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=exampletrade-tools-roundup

The UTM builder makes each link. If an editor wants a clean URL or the site strips query strings, use a tracked link instead: in Virling, open Links, set the source to the outlet's name, the channel to Referral and the page it should open. The short link redirects with utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign added, its id becomes the campaign, and clicks from bots are not counted. Make one link per story. See tracked links.

2. Read referrers when you cannot tag

Much coverage happens without you: a writer adds you to a list, or a news story links your homepage. You cannot tag those links, but a click from a web page still carries the outlet's host. On the Channels card, open Referral or use the Source tab, and each host is a row with visitors and revenue. Check Communities and Social too, since a story shared on Hacker News, Reddit or LinkedIn lands there under that host, not under the outlet.

An untagged story is credited to the outlet as a whole. If the outlet covered you twice, both stories share one row.

3. Know what shows up as Direct

A visit with no referrer, no utm_source and no ref is Direct. For press, that is:

  • Readers who type your URL after reading a print article, hearing you on a podcast or radio, or seeing your name in a screenshot. Nothing in the visit points at the mention.
  • Links on sites that hide the referrer, with rel="noreferrer" or a strict referrer policy, when the link carries no tags.
  • Links opened from a PDF, an email client or an app that sends no referrer. Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, LinkedIn, Telegram, WhatsApp, Slack and Discord in-app browsers are credited to that app instead, but only when the app names itself in the user agent.

A rise in Direct on the day a story runs is a hint, not proof. Why Direct is your biggest traffic source covers how to read it, and dark social revenue tracking covers links shared in private messages. For spoken mentions, a short vanity URL that redirects to a tagged page turns typed visits into tagged ones; podcast revenue tracking explains how.

4. Pick the model that fits press

A reader often hears of you in a story, leaves, and comes back days later before paying. Which visit gets the payment depends on the site's model:

  • Last non-direct, the default, credits the latest session in the lookback window that was not Direct. A reader who clicked the story and later typed your URL is credited to the story. One who came back through a search is credited to Organic search.
  • First touch credits the earliest session in the window. It shows which mentions introduce people who later pay.
  • Last touch credits the latest session, Direct included.

The lookback window is 30, 60, 90 or 365 days, 90 by default. Change either in site settings; attribution for every payment is rebuilt right away, history included. See model and lookback window.

Compare revenue per mention

  1. Pick a date range that starts the day the story ran and runs for a few months, so late buyers are included.
  2. On the Channels card, open the Campaign tab. Each tagged story is a row with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  3. Open the Source tab for untagged mentions, by host, under Referral, Communities and Social.
  4. Note visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor for each mention next to your site's revenue per visitor for the same range.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see every visit before it, with the credited one marked.

A mention with paying customers and revenue per visitor near or above your average tells you which outlets and angles to pitch again. One with many visitors and no customers reached the wrong readers. If you paid for a placement, the revenue per visitor calculator shows its return.

Frequently asked questions

Does press coverage bring customers?

Only your own data can say. Give each mention a tagged link where you can, read the referrer host where you cannot, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and look at paying customers and revenue per visitor for each mention over a few months. Readers who never clicked a link are not counted per mention.

How do I track revenue from a press mention?

Hand the journalist a link with utm_source set to the outlet, utm_medium=referral and a utm_campaign for the story, or a tracked link from Virling, which adds those tags on the click. Each mention is then its own row in the Campaign tab with visitors and revenue.

What if the article links to me without UTM tags?

A click from a web page usually carries the outlet's host as the referrer, so the visit shows under that host in Referral, or in Communities or Social for known hosts such as reddit.com or linkedin.com. All untagged stories on one outlet share that host's row.

Why does press traffic show up as Direct?

A visit with no referrer, no UTM source and no ref is Direct. That covers readers who type your URL after reading print or hearing your name, links on sites that hide the referrer, and links opened from PDFs or apps that do not send one.
