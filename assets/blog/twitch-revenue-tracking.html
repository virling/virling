Twitch revenue tracking: which streams bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-20.

To see which Twitch visits turn into paying customers, put a UTM-tagged link in your panels, chat commands, sponsor segments and VOD descriptions, with utm_source=twitch and a campaign for each stream or sponsor. Carry the visitor ID into checkout, then join each payment to the visit that came before it. You then see the revenue and revenue per visitor each stream brings, and a brand sees which creator sent its paying customers. Viewer counts, follows and link clicks do not tell you this.

This is written for two readers: streamers who sell their own product, merch or course and want to know which streams pay, and brands that sponsor streamers and want to know which creators sent buyers, not only viewers.

Where Twitch clicks come from

A stream sends people to your site through a few kinds of links, and each one should be tagged:

  • Panel links. The panels under the stream stay the same from one stream to the next. They deserve a fixed campaign such as twitch-panel, so you can tell them from links tied to one stream.
  • Chat commands. A bot command such as !shop or !sponsor posts a link in chat whenever someone asks for it. Change the link behind the command when you want to credit a new stream or a new sponsor.
  • Sponsor segments. When a sponsor's read is on stream, the link in chat, on screen or in the panel for that sponsor should be the sponsor's own, so its buyers are kept apart from your other traffic.
  • VOD descriptions. Highlights and uploads keep sending visitors long after the stream ends. Tag their links with the same campaign as the stream, or with one of their own.

Why Twitch clicks show up as Direct or Referral

There are three ways a visit can be credited, and for Twitch only one of them names the stream.

  • The referrer. A click in a desktop browser can arrive with twitch.tv as the referrer. Twitch is not on Virling's list of known social hosts, so that visit lands in Referral under twitch.tv. A referrer is a host only, so it cannot tell you which stream or which sponsor the click came from.
  • The in-app browser. Many viewers watch in the Twitch mobile app, and links opened there often arrive with no referrer. Virling credits some apps' in-app browsers, such as Instagram and Discord, when the app names itself in the user agent, but Twitch is not on that list. With no referrer and no tag, a click from the Twitch app counts as Direct, in Virling and in most other tools.
  • A UTM tag. The tag travels in the link itself, so it survives the app, the system browser and a missing referrer. Because twitch is not a known social source yet, a tagged Twitch visit counts as Referral, not Social, even with utm_medium=social. It is still out of Direct, and its campaign is still stored.

If your Direct traffic rises while you are live, this is usually why. The full rule order is in channels, and why Direct is your biggest traffic source covers the other causes.

How to set up Twitch revenue tracking with Virling

1. Tag every Twitch link

Give each stream or sponsor its own campaign, start every campaign with twitch-, and use utm_content to say where the link sat:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=twitch&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=twitch-launch-stream&utm_content=chat-command
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=twitch&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=twitch-panel&utm_content=panel
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=twitch&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=twitch-launch-stream&utm_content=vod-description
  • utm_source=twitch on every Twitch link, so they share one source and stay out of Direct.
  • utm_medium=social for organic streams. Do not use cpc or email, since those move the visit into the Paid or Email channel; keep cpc for ads you pay Twitch to run.
  • utm_campaign names the stream or the sponsor: one value per stream, lowercase, with hyphens, starting with twitch-. The Campaign tab groups visitors and revenue by this value, so this is the tag that gives you revenue per stream, and the prefix keeps your Twitch rows together among other Referral campaigns.
  • utm_content names the spot: panel, chat-command, sponsor-segment or vod-description. Virling stores it with each visit; keep what you want to compare in the campaign so it shows up in reports.

Brands that sponsor several streamers put the streamer in the campaign instead, such as twitch-jane-doe, and never let two streamers share one value. The UTM builder makes the link for you, and which UTM parameters to use has more naming rules. To announce a stream on X, Discord and other platforms, make one tagged link per platform; for Twitch, set the source to twitch by hand.

A long tagged URL is hard to read out on stream and clutters chat. Use a tracked link instead: in Virling, open the Campaign tab on the Channels card, click + Tracked link, pick Other, type twitch as the source and pick the page it should open. The short link adds the tags on the click, and the Campaign tab lists it with its visitors and revenue. Make one link per stream or sponsor. See tracked links.

2. Add the script

Paste the Virling script before </head> on every page of your site. It reads the UTM tags on the landing page and sets one first-party cookie, virling_vid, the visitor ID that checkout will carry. Leave cookieless mode off for this site: without the visitor ID, payments cannot be linked to visits. See add the script.

3. Connect a payment provider and pass the visitor ID

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs a connected payment provider and the visitor ID in checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Stores on Shopify or WooCommerce add a small theme snippet that stores the ID on each order. The full list is on the integrations page, and the setup for each provider is in link checkout to visits.

Make a test payment through a tagged Twitch link, then check that it appears on the Payments page with your twitch- campaign in its journey.

4. For brands: one partner link per streamer

If you sponsor streamers, open Partners in Virling, add each streamer by name and the page their link should open. Virling makes a short link that adds the streamer's name as utm_source, referral as the medium and its own id as the campaign, so these visits count in Referral. Put that link in the streamer's chat command, panel and sponsor segment. Partners are ranked by revenue, with visitors, paying customers and refund rate for the range, credited under your attribution model. Copy report link gives a streamer a read-only page with only their own numbers for the last 7, 30 or 90 days, without signing in. Virling does not pay streamers or calculate commissions. See revenue per stream and per creator

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more, so VOD viewers and slower buyers are both in it.
  2. In the Channels card, open Referral. Tagged Twitch visits are here, together with any visit that arrived with a twitch.tv referrer.
  3. Click the Referral row to filter the whole page, then switch to the Campaign tab. Each twitch- campaign is a row with its own visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  4. Brands using partner links open Partners instead and compare streamers by revenue, not by viewers.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

Viewers often hear about a product on stream, leave, and come back later by typing your address. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the payment goes to the tagged Twitch visit, as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. A later search or other tagged visit does take it, so switch the model to first touch in Site settings to see which streams start journeys; history is recomputed, and you can switch back. What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • No Twitch campaigns, but Direct jumped while you were live. Some links are untagged. Check the chat commands and panels first.
  • Twitch visits are not under Social. That is expected: Twitch is not a known social host or source, so its visits land in Referral. Find them by their twitch- campaigns.
  • Two streams or two streamers share one row. They use the same campaign, often because a chat command was not updated. Give each a value of its own.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A buyer says they found you on a stream but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have watched without clicking and searched for you later. No analytics tool can count a view that never reached your site.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so Twitch's real visitor number can be higher than what any analytics tool shows. Revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from Twitch?

Put a UTM-tagged link in your panels, chat commands, sponsor segments and VOD descriptions, with utm_source=twitch and a utm_campaign for that stream. Pass the visitor ID into checkout and use a tool that joins payments to visits. The Campaign tab then shows visitors and revenue per stream.

Why do Twitch visits show up as Direct?

Links opened in the Twitch mobile app often arrive with no referrer, and Twitch is not one of the in-app browsers Virling recognizes. With no referrer and no tag, the visit is Direct. A utm_source tag in the link keeps it out of Direct.

Which channel do Twitch visits land in?

Referral. Neither twitch.tv as a referrer nor twitch as a utm_source is on Virling's list of known social hosts and sources yet, so Twitch visits count as Referral, even with utm_medium=social. Start every Twitch campaign with twitch- so you can find them together in the Campaign tab.

Can I see which stream sent a paying customer?

Yes, if each stream or sponsor segment has its own utm_campaign or its own tracked link. A panel link stays the same across streams, so give it a fixed campaign such as twitch-panel; it then shows what the panel brings in total, not per stream.

How can a brand see which streamer sent paying customers?

Give each streamer their own link, either a tagged link with the streamer in utm_campaign or a partner link from the Partners page in Virling. Partners are ranked by revenue, and Copy report link gives each streamer a read-only page with only their own visitors, paying customers and revenue.
