Affiliate revenue tracking: which partners bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

Affiliate revenue tracking means giving every affiliate and partner their own tagged link, so you can see which affiliate and partner visits turn into paying customers. Use one utm_source or ref value per partner, plus their own campaign. Carry the visitor ID into checkout, then join each payment to the visit that came before it. You then compare partners by the revenue their visitors paid, not by how many clicks they sent.

Affiliates, resellers, newsletter sponsors and integration partners all send traffic with the same promise: their audience will buy. Some of them are right. The only way to know which is to follow each partner's visitors all the way to a payment, and then to the renewals after it.

One source value per partner

A partner visit can reach your site in three ways, and only a tag keeps the partner's name attached to it.

  • The referrer. A click from a partner's blog or site usually arrives with their host as the referrer, and counts in the Referral channel. That works for partners with one site, but not for partners who post in apps, emails or videos, where the referrer is often missing or belongs to someone else.
  • No referrer. A link in an email, a PDF, a chat or a mobile app often arrives with nothing. With no referrer and no tag, the visit is Direct, and the partner gets no credit in Virling or in most other tools.
  • A tag in the link. Virling reads utm_source, and ref when utm_source is missing, so a link like ?ref=jane-doe keeps working when the referrer does not. With no referrer, a partner name is not a known source, so the visit counts in the Referral channel.

Pick one lowercase value per partner, with hyphens, and never reuse it. If your affiliate program already adds ?ref= values, keep them; do not give the same partner a different utm_source on other links. The full rule order is in channels.

Compare partners by paid revenue, not clicks

Clicks, signups and trials are easy to count and easy to inflate. A partner who sends a large audience that never pays looks great on a click report and costs you support time. A partner who sends a few visitors who buy the yearly plan and renew looks small on the same report.

  • Revenue from paying customers the partner sent, net of refunds.
  • Revenue per visitor, so a small partner and a large one can be compared on the same scale. See what revenue per visitor means. The EPC calculator gives the affiliate's side of it, earnings per click.
  • Refund rate, since a partner whose buyers ask for their money back is worth less than the first payment suggests.
  • Renewals, since a subscription credited to a partner keeps counting toward them every month it renews.

Where payout software fits

Affiliate payout software and Virling answer different questions, so keep both. Payout software decides what each partner is owed: commission rules, its own cookie window, approvals, payouts and tax forms. Virling does not pay partners, calculate commissions or handle payouts.

What Virling adds is the view from your own site and your own payment provider: every visit a partner sent, which of those visitors paid, their full journey before the payment, and how a partner compares with your other channels on revenue per visitor. Use it to check what your payout software reports, to spot partners whose buyers refund, and to decide whom to give a better rate.

Expect the two to disagree. Virling credits each payment under your attribution model, last non-direct by default, inside a lookback window of 90 days by default. Payout software uses its own rules, so a sale it credits to a partner may be credited to search or another partner in Virling, and the other way round. See model and lookback window.

Set it up with Virling

  1. Tag each partner's link. Give every partner a link with their own source and campaign, and referral or affiliate as the medium. Do not use cpc or email as the medium, since those move the visit into the Paid or Email channel. The UTM builder makes the link for you, and which UTM parameters to use has more naming rules.
    https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=jane-doe&utm_medium=affiliate&utm_campaign=jane-doe
https://yourdomain.com/pricing?utm_source=acme-agency&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=acme-agency&utm_content=resources-page
    Virling stores the source with each visit, but its reports group revenue by campaign, so put the partner in utm_campaign too. Use utm_content for where the link sat, such as a review post or a resources page.
  2. Or create partner links. In Virling, open Partners, add the partner by name and the page their link should open. Virling makes a short link that adds the partner's name in dashed words as utm_source, such as jane-doe, referral as the medium and its own id as the campaign. Partners are ranked by revenue, with visitors, paying customers and refund rate for the range. See the dashboard guide and tracked links.
  3. Add the script. Put the Virling script in the head of every page, with your domain in data-site. It reads the tags on the landing page and sets the visitor ID. See add the script.
  4. Connect a payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. The setup is in link checkout to visits, and each provider has its own page: Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce. All of them are listed under integrations.
  5. Read the numbers. Pick a range of a few weeks or more. Open Partners for partner links, or the Campaign tab in the Channels card for links you tagged yourself, and compare visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey, with the credited visit marked.

Share results with partners

Partners ask what they sent you. On the Partners page, Copy report link gives a partner a read-only page with only their own visitors, paying customers and revenue for the last 7, 30 or 90 days, without signing in. They never see your other partners or channels. Removing the partner stops both their short link and their report link.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • A partner has visits but no revenue. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If much of your revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A partner's visits land in Direct. The link has no tag, or a redirect on the way drops the query string. Open the link in a private window and check the address bar.
  • Two partners share one row. They use the same campaign. Give each partner a value of their own.
  • Payout software credits a sale Virling gives to another channel. The buyer probably came back another way before paying, such as from a search, and the two tools credit that differently. Switch the model to first touch in Site settings to see which partners start journeys; first-touch or last-touch attribution explains the difference.

For partners paid a flat fee to post, see which creators bring paying customers. If affiliates promote a sale, tagging a Black Friday sale shows how to give each one its own row. For partners that list codes, see whether coupon sites bring new customers.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track affiliate revenue?

Give each affiliate or partner a link with their own value in utm_source or ref and their own utm_campaign, or create a partner link in Virling. Pass the visitor ID into checkout and use a tool that joins payments to visits. Then compare partners by the revenue their visitors paid, not by clicks.

Should affiliates use utm_source or ref?

Either works for the channel: Virling reads utm_source, and ref when utm_source is missing. Use one lowercase value per partner, and add utm_campaign with the same value, since the Campaign tab is where Virling groups visitors and revenue per partner.

Why compare partners by revenue and not clicks?

Clicks and signups say nothing about who paid. A partner who sends few visitors who buy and renew is worth more than one who sends many who never pay. Revenue per visitor and refund rate show the difference.

Does Virling replace affiliate payout software?

No. Virling does not pay partners, calculate commissions or handle payouts. It shows which partner visits turned into paying customers, with the full journey, so you can check the numbers your payout software reports and decide which partners to invest in.

Why do Virling's partner numbers differ from my affiliate dashboard?

They credit sales differently. Virling credits each payment under your attribution model and lookback window, 90 days by default, and counts revenue net of refunds. Affiliate software usually uses its own cookie and commission rules, so the two rarely match exactly.

Can a partner see the revenue they sent?

Yes. On the Partners page, Copy report link gives the partner a read-only page with only their own visitors, paying customers and revenue for the last 7, 30 or 90 days.
