X Ads revenue tracking: see which campaign brings paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-24.

X Ads revenue tracking means joining each payment on your site to the X ad click that brought the buyer. Add utm_source=x, utm_medium=paid_social and a utm_campaign to the website URL of your ads, add the Virling script to your site, and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Virling counts the click as Paid and credits the payment to it, so the Campaign tab shows payments and revenue per campaign. Without the tag, an ad click looks like a click on any other post: the referrer is t.co at best, and the visit is Social or Direct.

Step 1: tag the ad

Put the tags in the website URL of the ad, with your own campaign name:

https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=x&utm_medium=paid_social&utm_campaign=spring-sale

  • Tag the full URL. In its announcement of a third-party click tag, X lists Google Analytics UTM parameters as an example of parameters added to the destination URL for tracking (source: X Developers, CM360 Click Tag).
  • Type the campaign name. We found no list of placeholder values for campaign names or IDs in X’s Ads API docs, so do not copy the braces from another platform. A typed name needs no placeholder.
  • Link straight to the page. X says not to use redirects in your URLs, because they strip its click ID (source: X, Conversion tracking for websites). A redirect can drop your UTM tags too.
  • Get it right before launch. X’s policy says you may not change the end destination or landing page of a URL after you have promoted it (source: X, URL requirements for advertising). Open the tagged URL yourself first.
  • Keep one name. A campaign tagged two ways becomes two rows. You can add utm_content for each ad, which Virling stores with the pageview, but reports group by campaign.

The free UTM builder makes tagged URLs for you.

What a t.co referrer tells you without tags

X says that links shared on X are shortened to a t.co link, and that you cannot opt out (source: X, About X’s link service). A click in a browser can therefore arrive with t.co as the referrer. That names X and nothing more: not the post, not the campaign, and not whether you paid for it. Your tags are part of the destination URL, so they are still there when the short link opens your page.

Links opened inside an app often arrive with no referrer at all. Virling credits some apps from the name in the user agent, but X is not one of them. An untagged click with no referrer is Direct, shown there as X app when the user agent names it. The visit stays Direct either way.

Telling paid posts from organic ones

X has two kinds of post you can promote. Its docs say a promoted-only post does not appear in your public timeline, though it is public, while an organic post is served to your followers and does appear there. Either kind may be promoted, and a published post cannot be changed from one kind to the other (source: X Ads API, Creatives).

So use a promoted-only post for an ad you want to measure, and give it a link of its own. The split is not perfect: a promoted-only post is public, so someone who shares it can send you an unpaid click with the paid tag. For your profile and unpaid posts, see X (Twitter) revenue tracking.

How Virling sorts an X ad visit

Every session gets one channel from its first pageview, and the first matching rule wins:

  • A paid medium. cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social or paidsocial, in any case, is Paid. It is checked first, so a tagged click from t.co is Paid, not Social.
  • No paid medium, with a referrer. t.co, x.com and twitter.com are Social and share one row, x.com, so the ad is mixed in with your posts.
  • No referrer, with a source tag. x and twitter are Social. A value that is not a known source is Referral.
  • No referrer and no tag. The visit is Direct.

Spelling counts. paid-social with a hyphen, promoted and cpm are not on the list, so those clicks are not Paid. An email medium is not Paid either: it is Email. See how channels are decided.

What about the twclid?

X’s docs say that website click-throughs from X ads append twclid to the landing page URL (source: X Ads API, GTM server side). It is the X Click ID, for X’s own conversion measurement. Virling does not store it or use it to pick the channel. It only checks whether it is there, so a Direct visit can be labelled a shared link. It does not name your campaign, so leave it on and add UTM tags. See click IDs and UTM tags.

Step 2: add the script

Add the Virling script to the <head> of every page the ad can land on. It reads the tags on the landing page and sets the first-party visitor ID. Redirects on your site must keep the query string; see UTM parameters lost after redirect. The setup is in the script docs.

Step 3: link checkout

Connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout, so each payment can be matched to a visit. Payment links from Stripe, Polar, Dodo Payments and LemonSqueezy get the ID from the script when they are on a page that runs it; a checkout you create on your server needs the virling_vid cookie. See link checkout to visits.

Under the default model, the whole payment, after refunds, is credited to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct. Someone who clicks your ad on Monday and types your address on Thursday is still credited to the ad. See the attribution model. A buyer who taps the ad in the X app and pays in another browser can have two visitor IDs; see cross-device attribution.

Step 4: read payments per campaign

  • In the Channels card, click Paid. The page filters to visits with a paid medium, from X and any other platform you tag the same way.
  • Open the Campaign tab. Each utm_campaign is a row with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  • Open a payment from the Payments page to see the visits before it.
  • Check Time to pay before you pause a campaign that looks weak.

To see profit, type what you spend on ads each month in Site settings, under Monthly spend per channel, such as Paid 1200. The Profit by channel card then shows spend, revenue and profit for the Paid channel as a whole, so include every platform you tag as Paid.

A made-up example

These numbers are invented to show the arithmetic. Two campaigns each cost $600, as read in Ads Manager. Campaign A got 500 clicks and 10 payments worth $1,500, so $1.20 per click and $3.00 of revenue per click. Campaign B got 800 clicks and 6 payments worth $600, so $0.75 per click and $0.75 of revenue per click. At a 60% margin, the break-even cost per click is $1.80 for A and $0.45 for B. A makes money and B loses it, even though its clicks were cheaper. Work out your own limit with the CPC calculator.

Why X’s numbers differ from your payments

  • Clicks and visits. X says Ads Manager counts a click as soon as it happens, while analytics tools count a visit once the page and their code have loaded (source: X, Common analytics discrepancies).
  • Views and engagements count too. X recommends a default window of 30 days after an engagement and 1 day after a view for website conversions (source: X, Create a website conversions campaign). A view with no click never reaches your site, so it cannot be credited to a visit.
  • Different window and credit. Virling looks back 90 days by default and gives a payment to one visit. If the buyer clicked an ad and later came from your newsletter, the default model credits the newsletter, while X can still count the ad.

What to check if the numbers look off

  • Ad revenue shows as Social. The ad has no paid utm_medium, or you promoted an organic post tagged social.
  • Ad revenue shows as Direct. The tags were dropped by a redirect, or the ad has none and the click had no referrer.
  • Paid visits keep coming after the ad stops. The tagged link is still in a post or was shared. Use it in the ad and nowhere else.
  • Revenue shows as Unattributed. The visitor ID is not reaching checkout.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which X ad campaign brought a paying customer?

Add utm_source=x, utm_medium=paid_social and a utm_campaign to the website URL of your ads, add the Virling script to your site, and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Each payment is credited to the tagged visit, and the Campaign tab shows revenue per campaign.

Which channel does an X ad click land in?

Paid, if the link carries utm_medium=paid_social, paidsocial, paid, cpc, ppc or paidsearch. The medium is checked first, so it wins over a t.co referrer. Without a paid medium the click is Social when the referrer or the source tag says X, and Direct when nothing does.

Can a t.co referrer tell an ad from an organic post?

No. X shortens the links shared on X with t.co, and the referrer names only that host. Virling puts t.co, x.com and twitter.com in Social, in one row, x.com. Only the tag on the link says that the post was paid.

How do I separate paid posts from organic ones on X?

Give the ad its own promoted-only post with utm_medium=paid_social, and tag the links in your organic posts with utm_medium=social. If you promote a post you already published, the paid and unpaid clicks share one link, so they share one tag and one row.

Does Virling read the twclid?

Only to see whether it is there: a Direct visit with a twclid can be labelled a shared link. Virling does not store its value or use it to pick the channel. It can stay on the link for X.

Do I need to connect my X ad account?

No. The setup on this page uses only the tags on your ads, the Paid channel and your payments. You can type what you spend on Paid each month in Site settings, and the Profit by channel card uses that amount.
