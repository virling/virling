Mailchimp revenue tracking: see which campaign made sales

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-23.

Mailchimp revenue tracking means joining each Stripe, Shopify or WooCommerce payment to the Mailchimp campaign that sent the buyer. Tag every link in your campaigns with utm_medium=email, the same utm_source each time and a utm_campaign per send, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and Virling credits each payment to the tagged visit, so the Campaign tab shows revenue per campaign. Mailchimp’s own reports count opens and clicks; they stop at the click and cannot see the payment that came after.

Why opens and clicks are not revenue

Mailchimp tells you which campaigns get read and clicked. That is not the same as which ones sell, because the payment happens on your site or in your billing provider, often days later and in another tab.

  • Opens are not always people. Some mail apps load every email on their own to protect privacy, and each load counts as an open.
  • A click is not a payment. One subscriber clicks every campaign and never buys; another clicks once and pays the same day.
  • The payment is elsewhere. To join a campaign to a sale, you need the click, the visits after it and the payment in one place, tied to the same visitor.

Virling credits payments to visits, so a campaign counts only when someone clicks a tagged link and lands on your site. An email that was only opened is never credited.

Why untagged clicks show as Direct

Most subscribers read your campaign in an email app, and most email apps send no referrer when a link is clicked. A link with no UTM tags has nothing that says it came from Mailchimp, so the visit is counted as Direct, and so is the payment that follows. Tagging every link is what fixes it.

How to tag Mailchimp campaign links

Mailchimp has a Google Analytics link tracking option in each email’s settings. According to Mailchimp’s help article, it needs a connected Google Analytics account, and its campaign value combines the email title with the date. Mailchimp also recommends choosing either its tracking or your own UTM tags, not both. If you do not use Google Analytics, or want short values you choose yourself, tag the links by hand: paste the tagged URL as the link in the email builder.

https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=mailchimp&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=black-friday-2026&utm_content=hero-button

  • utm_medium=email on every link. This is what puts the visit in the Email channel.
  • One source for all of Mailchimp. Use mailchimp, or split mailchimp campaigns from mailchimp-flow automations, so all of it adds up under one or two sources.
  • One campaign per send. Use a short lowercase name, such as black-friday-2026, so each campaign has its own row. Give each automation one value that never changes, such as flow-welcome.
  • utm_content for the link. It tells two links in the same email apart, such as hero-button and footer. It is stored with the visit but is not a report row.

The free UTM builder makes the tagged URLs for you. Before you send, click a link in a test email and check the address bar on your site: the tags that arrive there are the ones Virling reads.

You can also share a Virling tracked link instead. In Links, set the source to newsletter and the channel to Email: the short URL redirects with utm_medium=email and the link’s id as utm_campaign, keeps any tags already on the destination, and Links shows the clicks, visitors and revenue it brought. How Virling sorts a Mailchimp visit

Every session gets one channel from its first pageview, and utm_medium is checked first, ignoring case. A medium of email, newsletter or e-mail is the Email channel, whatever the referrer or source says, so utm_source=mailchimp with utm_medium=email lands in Email. The source and campaign are stored with the visit. See how channels are decided.

When a payment comes in, it is joined to the visits before it. Under the default model the whole payment, after refunds, is credited to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct, and to that visit’s channel, source, campaign and landing page. A subscriber who clicks your campaign on Monday and comes back on Thursday by typing your address is still credited to the campaign. A subscriber who clicks it and later returns from a search result is credited to Organic search. See the attribution model.

Renewals and repeat orders

A subscription renewal takes the attribution of the customer’s first payment, matched by the provider’s customer ID. If someone first paid after clicking your Mailchimp campaign, every renewal is credited to that campaign, even though the renewal itself has no visit. Upgrades and plan changes count as renewals too. For stores, Shopify Subscriptions and WooCommerce Subscriptions renewal orders work the same way. See renewals.

A one-off repeat order is a new payment, so it is credited to the visits before it: if the buyer came back by clicking another campaign, that campaign gets the order. The Retention page groups each customer’s whole lifetime value under the channel and campaign of their first payment, so you can see which campaigns bring customers who keep paying.

See payments per campaign

1. Open the Email channel

On the dashboard, the Channels card lists each channel with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. Click Email and the whole page filters to visits from your emails.

2. Switch to the Campaign tab

The Campaign tab on the same card has one row per utm_campaign, so each Mailchimp campaign shows its own visitors and revenue. Compare campaigns by revenue per visitor, not by visitors: a small send to buyers can be worth more than a big one to people who never pay.

3. Open the payments

The Payments page lists every payment with its source; open one to see the journey: the sessions before the payment and which one got the credit.

What to check if the numbers look off

  • Mailchimp revenue shows as Direct. A link was left untagged, or the tags were lost in a redirect before your page. Click a link from a sent campaign and check the address bar.
  • Revenue is lower than an email tool reports. Expected if that tool credits opens, or orders that came through another channel. Virling counts each payment once, for the visit it credits.
  • Campaigns are split across rows. Values differ in case or spelling, such as Black-Friday-2026 and black-friday-2026. Pick one format and keep it.
  • Revenue shows no channel at all. The payment was not matched to a visit. Pass the visitor ID into checkout; see link checkout to visits.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which Mailchimp campaign made sales?

Tag every link in the campaign with utm_medium=email, one utm_source such as mailchimp and a utm_campaign per send. Connect Stripe, Shopify or WooCommerce and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Each payment is then credited to the tagged visit, and the Campaign tab of the Email channel shows revenue per campaign.

Should I use Mailchimp’s Google Analytics link tracking or my own UTM tags?

Either works if utm_medium arrives as email. Mailchimp’s option needs a connected Google Analytics account and builds the campaign value from the email title and date. Mailchimp recommends choosing one or the other, not both. Hand-written tags let you pick short values that stay the same across sends.

Does an opened Mailchimp email count as revenue in Virling?

No. Virling credits payments to visits, so a campaign counts only when someone clicks a tagged link and lands on your site. If the buyer later arrives from another channel, the default model credits the latest visit that was not Direct.

Which channel does Mailchimp traffic land in?

A link tagged utm_medium=email lands in Email, whatever the source or referrer, because the medium is checked first and case is ignored. An untagged click from an email app usually has no referrer, so it lands in Direct.

Do renewals stay with the Mailchimp campaign that brought the customer?

Yes. A subscription renewal takes the attribution of the customer’s first payment, matched by the provider’s customer ID, and upgrades count as renewals too. A one-off repeat order is a new payment and is credited to the visits before it.
