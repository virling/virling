How to track influencer sales: which creators bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

To track influencer sales, give every sponsored post its own tagged link, so you can see which posts turn followers into paying customers and set that revenue against the fee you paid. Put the platform in utm_source and the creator and post in utm_campaign, carry the visitor ID into checkout, and keep the discount code as a second count. You then judge each post by the revenue its followers paid, over days and weeks, not by likes or reach on the day it went live.

This is written for ecommerce stores that pay creators to post, and for agencies that run creator campaigns for clients. A creator is usually paid a flat fee before the post goes live, whatever it sells, so the question after every campaign is simple: did this post earn back its fee, and is this creator worth booking again?

Why follower visits lose the creator's name

Most follower clicks happen inside an app, where the referrer is often missing or names only the platform, so every creator on Instagram looks the same and some visits count as Direct. A tag in the link travels with the click, so it survives the app. The rules for in-app browsers are in channels.

A flat fee needs one row per post

When you pay per post, the post is what you bought, so it needs its own row. Virling groups revenue by campaign, and does not report utm_content separately, so put the post in the campaign value: start with the creator's name so one creator's posts sort together, then add the format and month.

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=influencer&utm_campaign=jane-doe-reel-nov
https://yourdomain.com/products/starter-kit?utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=influencer&utm_campaign=alex-kim-video-nov

Known platforms such as instagram, tiktok and youtube keep these visits in the Social channel. Keep cpc as the medium only for ads you run on a creator's content, since it moves the visit into Paid. The influencer UTM link generator makes one link per creator and post from a list.

Discount codes versus links

Most creator deals come with a discount code, and a code and a link miss different buyers, so use both.

  • A discount code is counted by your store whenever it is used, so it catches followers who typed your plain URL or searched for your brand. It misses followers who clicked, browsed and paid full price without it, it says nothing about visitors who did not buy, and once it leaks to a coupon site it credits the creator for sales they did not drive.
  • A tagged link counts every visit the post sent and which of those visitors paid, code or no code. It misses followers who never click.

Virling credits an order by the visit before it and does not read discount codes, so redemptions stay in your store's reports. If the link brings far more buyers than the code, followers are skipping the code; if the code is used far more than the link brings buyers, followers arrive another way or the code has leaked. Cross-checking codes with links covers both, and coupon site revenue tracking covers leaks.

Link-in-bio and story links

Where the link sits changes how long it sells and how you should read it.

  • Story links on Instagram disappear with the story after 24 hours, so almost all of their visits arrive on day one. Give each story its own campaign value, and read its revenue over the following weeks, since the buying comes later than the clicking.
  • Bio links stay up for weeks and collect clicks from every post that says "link in bio", including the creator's unpaid posts. A bio link with its own campaign value tells you what the bio sent, not what one post sent. If the deal is for one post, ask the creator to swap the bio link back when the deal ends, so later visits do not pile onto it.
  • TikTok videos usually have no clickable link, so a creator's TikTok link mostly lives in the bio; TikTok revenue tracking covers how those visits arrive, and link-in-bio revenue tracking covers bio tools.

Judging one sponsored post against its fee

With a fee, every post starts in the red, and the only question is whether its buyers climb out. Here is a made-up example of three posts read four weeks after they went live.

The video sent the most visitors and still lost money, because each visitor paid $0.42 on average against $1.09 for the reel. Revenue here is what you were paid, not profit, so take your product cost off before deciding a post paid for itself. The influencer ROI calculator turns a fee and the clicks a post sent into its return and the conversion rate it needs to break even. If you enter your monthly creator spend for Social in Site settings, the Profit by channel card sets it against all Social revenue.

Followers who buy days later

A follower who sees a post on their phone often buys days later, after thinking about it, reading reviews or waiting for payday. Three things keep that sale with the creator, or explain why it is lost.

  • Typing your URL later is fine. With the default model, last non-direct, a buyer who clicked the creator's link and later typed your address is still credited to the creator. A later visit from a search or an ad takes the credit instead.
  • The window has to be long enough. Only visits inside the lookback window count, 90 days by default. Most posts sell within that, but a 30-day window can cut off slow buyers.
  • A different device breaks the chain. A follower who clicked on their phone and pays on a laptop has no link visit on the laptop, so that sale shows up only against the code, if they used it. See cross-device attribution.

So do not judge a post on the day it went live. Read it after a few weeks, and check the Time to pay card to see how long your Social buyers usually take.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track influencer sales?

Give each sponsored post its own tagged link, with the platform in utm_source and the creator and post in utm_campaign, or add the creator as a partner in Virling. Pass the visitor ID into checkout so each order joins to the visit before it. The Campaign tab then shows visitors, paying customers and revenue per post.

Are discount codes enough to track influencer sales?

No. A code only counts buyers who type it at checkout. Followers who click and pay full price without it are missed, the code says nothing about how many people visited, and a code that leaks to a coupon site credits the creator for sales they did not drive. Use a link and a code together.

How do I know if a sponsored post paid for its fee?

Give the post its own campaign value, wait a few weeks, and compare the revenue credited to that campaign with the fee. Revenue per visitor shows whether a large audience bought or only clicked, and the post has paid for itself only once revenue, less product cost, is above the fee.

Should a creator's bio link and story links share one campaign?

No. A bio link stays up for weeks and collects clicks from many posts, while a story link gets almost all its clicks in the 24 hours the story is up. Give each its own campaign value, starting with the creator's name, so you can see what each one sent.

How long after a sponsored post should I wait before judging it?

A few weeks. Followers often buy days after they see a post, and a buyer who later types your URL is still credited to the creator under the default model. Check the Time to pay card to see how long your Social buyers usually take.
