How to track influencer sales: which creators bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

To track influencer sales, give every creator their own tagged link, so you can see which of their visits turn into paying customers. Put the platform in utm_source and the creator's name in utm_campaign, or make a partner link in Virling. Carry the visitor ID into checkout, then join each order or payment to the visit that came before it. You then compare creators by the revenue their followers paid, not by likes, reach or clicks.

This is written for ecommerce stores that pay creators to post, and for agencies that run creator campaigns for clients. Both face the same question after every campaign: which creators sold, which only got attention, and who is worth booking again.

One tagged link per creator

A follower's click can reach your site in three ways, and only one of them keeps the creator's name attached.

  • The referrer. A click from instagram.com or tiktok.com in a browser arrives with that host as the referrer, and counts in the Social channel. The referrer is a host only, so every creator on that platform looks the same.
  • The in-app browser. Most clicks happen inside the app and often arrive with no referrer. Virling credits Instagram, TikTok and Facebook in-app browsers to the app when it names itself in the user agent. Other apps, and links copied into a chat or a browser, arrive with nothing and count as Direct. None of this names the creator.
  • A tag in the link. Virling reads utm_source and utm_campaign from the landing page. The tags travel in the link itself, so they survive the app and a missing referrer, and the campaign tells you which creator sent the visit.

So give each creator a link of their own, and never let two creators share one. The full rule order is in channels.

Discount codes versus links

Most creator deals come with a discount code. A code and a link answer different questions, so use both.

  • A discount code is counted by your store when it is used, however the buyer got to checkout. It catches followers who typed your plain URL or searched for your brand. But it only counts buyers who enter it. A follower who clicks the creator's link, browses, and buys without the code, or comes back a week later and pays full price, never shows up against that code. It also says nothing about how many people visited and did not buy, and a code that leaks to a coupon site gets credited to the creator for sales they did not drive.
  • A tagged link counts every visit the creator sent, which of those visitors paid, and how much, whether they used the code or not. It misses followers who never click the link.

Virling credits an order by the visit that came before it, not by the code. It does not read discount codes, so code redemptions stay in your store's reports. Comparing the two for one creator is a fair check: if the link brings far more buyers than the code, most followers are skipping the code; if the code is used far more than the link brings buyers, followers are arriving another way, or the code has leaked. Cross-checking codes with links covers both cases. For codes that leak, see coupon site revenue tracking.

Compare creators by revenue, not clicks

Clicks, reach and engagement are easy to count and easy to inflate. A creator with a large audience that never buys looks great on a click report. A creator with a small audience that buys the full-price bundle looks small on the same report.

  • Revenue from paying customers the creator sent, net of refunds.
  • Revenue per visitor, so a small creator and a large one can be compared on the same scale. See what revenue per visitor means.
  • Refund rate, since a creator whose buyers return the product is worth less than the first order suggests.
  • Renewals, if you sell subscriptions: a subscription credited to a creator keeps counting toward them every time it renews.

Set it up with Virling

  1. Tag each creator's link. Use the platform as the source, influencer as the medium, the creator as the campaign and the post or spot as the content, all lowercase with hyphens. Do not use cpc or email as the medium, since those move the visit into the Paid or Email channel; keep cpc for ads you run on a creator's content. The UTM builder makes the link for you, and the UTM naming template keeps the values consistent across creators and clients. For a list of creators, the influencer UTM link generator makes one tagged link for each.
    https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=influencer&utm_campaign=jane-doe&utm_content=reel
https://yourdomain.com/products/starter-kit?utm_source=tiktok&utm_medium=influencer&utm_campaign=sam-lee&utm_content=bio-link
    Known platforms such as instagram, tiktok and youtube keep these visits in the Social channel. Virling's reports group revenue by campaign, so the creator goes in utm_campaign: one value per creator, never reused. For agencies, prefix the client if you share one naming sheet, but keep each client on its own site in Virling.
  2. Or create partner links. In Virling, open Partners, add the creator by name and the page their link should open. Virling makes a short link that adds the creator's name in dashed words as utm_source, such as jane-doe, referral as the medium and its own id as the campaign. These visits count in the Referral channel, since a creator's name is not a known source. Partners are ranked by revenue, with visitors, paying customers and refund rate for the range. See the dashboard guide and tracked links.
  3. Add the script. Put the Virling script in the head of every page, with your domain in data-site. It reads the tags on the landing page and sets the visitor ID. See add the script.
  4. Connect your store or payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout. For Shopify, connect a read-only Admin API token and add a short snippet to theme.liquid that stores the visitor ID on the cart, which Shopify copies onto the order; see Shopify setup. For WooCommerce, a snippet in functions.php saves the virling_vid cookie on each order; see WooCommerce setup. Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem and RevenueCat are covered in link checkout to visits, and all of them are listed under integrations. More on stores is in revenue attribution for ecommerce.
  5. Read the numbers. Pick a range of a few weeks or more, since followers keep finding older posts. Open the Campaign tab in the Channels card for links you tagged yourself, or Partners for partner links, and compare visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor per creator. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey, with the credited visit marked.

Share results with creators

Creators ask what they sold, and agencies need to show clients what each creator did. On the Partners page, Copy report link gives a creator a read-only page with only their own visitors, paying customers and revenue for the last 7, 30 or 90 days, without signing in. They never see your other creators or channels. Removing the partner stops both their short link and their report link. Virling does not pay creators or calculate commissions.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Social or Direct jumped the day a creator posted, but their campaign is small. Some links went out untagged, or the creator shared your plain URL. Check the bio, the post and any link stickers.
  • A creator has visits but no revenue. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If much of your revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • The code shows sales the link does not. Those buyers reached checkout another way, such as a search for your brand. With the default model, last non-direct, a buyer who clicked the creator's link and later typed your URL is still credited to the creator, inside the lookback window of 90 days by default, but a later search visit takes the credit. Switch to first touch in Site settings to see which creators start journeys; model and lookback window explains both.
  • Two creators share one row. They use the same campaign. Give each creator a value of their own.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track influencer sales?

Give each creator their own tagged link, with the platform in utm_source and the creator in utm_campaign, or a partner link from Virling. Pass the visitor ID into checkout and use a tool that joins payments to visits. The campaign report then shows visitors, paying customers and revenue per creator.

Are discount codes enough to track influencer sales?

No. A code only counts buyers who type it at checkout. Followers who click the link and buy without the code, or come back later and pay full price, are missed, and the code says nothing about how many people visited. A code that leaks to a coupon site also credits the creator for sales they did not drive. Use a link and a code together.

What UTM tags should I use for influencer links?

Use the platform as utm_source, such as instagram, tiktok or youtube, influencer as utm_medium, the creator as utm_campaign and the post or spot as utm_content, all lowercase with hyphens. Do not use cpc or email as the medium, since those move the visit into the Paid or Email channel.

How do I compare influencers by revenue instead of clicks?

Give every creator one campaign value, pick a range of a few weeks, and compare revenue and revenue per visitor in the Campaign tab, or on the Partners page for partner links. Revenue per visitor puts a small creator and a large one on the same scale.

Why do influencer sales show up as Direct?

Links opened in some mobile apps arrive with no referrer. Virling credits Instagram, TikTok and Facebook in-app browsers to the app when it names itself, but other apps and copied links stay Direct. Even when the app is credited, the referrer never names the creator. A tag in the link fixes both.
