Which UTM parameters to use to trace revenue

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-10-26.

Use utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign on every link you control, keep the names lowercase and consistent, and never tag links inside your own site. That is enough to trace revenue back to a post, an email or an ad.

The five parameters

  • utm_source: where the link lives, such as newsletter, x or reddit
  • utm_medium: the kind of channel, such as email, social or cpc
  • utm_campaign: the push it belongs to, such as launch-oct
  • utm_content: which variant, useful when you test two links
  • utm_term: the paid keyword, only for search ads

To tell the first two apart, see utm_source vs utm_medium, with right and wrong pairs. For utm_id and the other tags Google added later, see utm_id and the newer UTM parameters.

A tagged link

https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=launch-oct

Example links by channel

Copy a link, swap in your own page and names, and keep the shape. The newsletter and partner names are made up. For a whole list of pages, the bulk UTM builder tags them in one go.

Email

https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=launch-oct&utm_content=header-button

For your own list. The medium puts it in Email. utm_content tells this button from another link to the same page; utm_content vs utm_term explains when to use each.

Paid search

https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=brand-search&utm_term={keyword}

For Google Ads, which replaces {keyword} with the keyword from your account that matched the search. The medium puts it in Paid, even though the visit comes from a search engine.

Paid social

https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=paid_social&utm_campaign=launch-oct

The medium puts it in Paid, apart from your unpaid posts. Write paid_social or paidsocial: the hyphenated paid-social is not in the list below, so it does not count as Paid.

Newsletter sponsorship

https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=indie-weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=indie-weekly-2026-11

For a slot in someone else's newsletter: their name is the source and the issue month is in the campaign. It lands in Email. Use cpc as the medium if you want it counted with your ads in Paid.

Partner

https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=acme-agency&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=acme-agency

One source per partner. The medium does not set a channel, so a click from the partner's own website, or a click with no referrer, lands in Referral.

QR code

https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=qr&utm_medium=print&utm_campaign=qr-spring-flyer

A scan usually has no referrer. qr is not a known source, so a tagged scan lands in Referral instead of Direct. Use one campaign per printed piece.

utm_medium values and the channel each gets

These are the rules Virling uses. Upper and lower case make no difference here, and the medium is checked before the referrer.

utm_mediumChannel
cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social, paidsocialPaid
email, newsletter, e-mailEmail
Any other value, such as social, referral, printNot set by the medium: the referrer decides, then utm_source, or ref when it is missing. With none of them, Referral

So a link tagged utm_medium=social is Social because of where it was clicked or its source, such as linkedin, not because of the medium. With no referrer, a source that is not a known AI, search, community or social name becomes Referral.

Naming rules that keep reports clean

  • Lowercase everything. X and x become two rows in most tools
  • Use hyphens, not spaces
  • Write the names down in one list, such as the UTM naming template, and reuse them
  • One value per meaning: pick email or newsletter for the medium, not both

What not to tag

  • Links between your own pages. They overwrite the real source with an internal one; for the search side, see whether UTM parameters affect SEO
  • Organic search results. Search engines already pass a referrer
  • Links in places that strip them, such as some private messages

Why revenue goes to Direct

When a visit has no UTM tags and no referrer, it is counted as direct. Most untagged email clicks, app links and shared messages end up there. Tag what you can and the Direct row shrinks.

Checklist for a first campaign

  1. Pick one campaign name, and one source and medium for each place you will share it. Write them down before you make any link
  2. Choose a page on your own site that has your analytics script, not a bare payment link
  3. Make one link per place, and never use the same link in two places
  4. Check the links for capitals, spaces and missing tags
  5. Open each link in a private window and see that the tags are still in the address bar after any redirect
  6. Make a test payment from one tagged link and see that it shows up under the right campaign
  7. After launch, compare revenue by campaign, not only visits

Frequently asked questions

Which UTM parameters are required?

None are required technically, but utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign together give enough detail to trace revenue to a campaign.

Should I add UTM tags to links on my own site?

No. Tagging internal links replaces the visitor's real source with an internal one and breaks attribution.

Are UTM parameters case sensitive?

Most analytics tools treat different capitalisations as different values, so keep every value lowercase.

Do I need all five UTM parameters?

No. Source, medium and campaign are enough for most links. Add utm_content when two links share all three, and utm_term only on search ads.

What happens when UTM tags are lost?

The visit falls back to its referrer. With no referrer it usually counts as Direct. The campaign is not recorded, so a payment that follows cannot be traced to that link through that visit. Redirects that drop the query string are a common cause.

Where do UTM tags go if the URL already has a question mark?

Add them after the existing parameters with an ampersand, not a second question mark. If the URL has a # part, put the tags before it.
