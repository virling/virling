Do software directories bring paying customers? How to measure it

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

To find out whether a software directory brings paying customers, split its links into the ones you edit yourself and the ones written by a review site, give each directory a fixed source name, and let a payment provider tell you who paid. Then read each directory as one row over 90 days: visitors, new customers, revenue and revenue per visitor against your site average. Listing views, upvotes and badge clicks do not answer it.

A typical SaaS ends its first year listed on dozens of directories, launch sites and review sites. Some of those pages you filled in through a form, some were written about you without asking, and a few you paid to feature. They behave differently, so they are measured differently.

Listing links you control, review links you do not

  • Listing links you control. On a directory where you submit the product and can edit the profile later, you choose the website URL. That is where a tag goes, and you can fix it any time.
  • Review-site links you do not control. On review sites, comparison lists and "best tools for X" pages, the site writes the outbound link. It may point at your homepage with no tag, send the click through its own redirect, add its own ref value, or put the website button behind a paid profile. Some review pages do not link to you at all, and a reader who never clicks leaves no visit to count.

For the second kind you measure what arrives. A click that keeps its referrer shows under the review site's host in the Referral channel of the Source tab. A click that loses it, and has no tag, is Direct. Paste the outbound link the site uses into the traffic source checker to see where Virling would put it.

ref and utm values per directory

Pick one lowercase name per directory and use it as both utm_source and utm_campaign on every link you place there. The campaign gives each directory its own row in the Campaign tab, even for clicks that arrive with no referrer and so have no host to group by:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=producthunt&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=producthunt
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=examplelaunchsite&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=examplelaunchsite
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=examplereviews&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=examplereviews-featured
  • Known names stay in Communities. Write producthunt and indiehackers exactly like that. Any other name lands in Referral when the click has no referrer; when it has one, the directory's host decides.
  • A paid slot is its own link. If you pay for a featured placement, tag that link with utm_medium=cpc and its own campaign, so it is counted as Paid and its price can be checked against its revenue. Keep the free listing on referral.
  • Directories that add their own ref. Some directories append ?ref= and their name to every outbound link. Virling reads ref only when there is no utm_source, and either one only when the click has no referrer. Your own utm_source wins, so the directory's spelling does not split your rows.
  • Directories that drop query strings. Use a tracked link instead: in Virling, open Links, set the source to the directory's name, the channel to Referral, and the page it should open. The short link adds the tags on the click and its id becomes the campaign, so make one per directory. See tracked links.

For a review site that will not take your link, ask whether the profile has a website field you can edit. If it does not, leave it untagged and read it by host. The UTM builder makes the tagged links, and channels has the full rule order.

How a review-driven visit that comes back later is credited

Review sites are read by buyers who are comparing tools, often weeks before they pick one. A common path: they click through from a review, look around, leave, and return later by searching your name or typing your URL, then start a trial and pay when it ends. Virling ties those visits together with the virling_vid cookie and credits the payment by your attribution model:

  • They came back by typing your URL. With the default, last non-direct, the Direct visit is skipped and the review site keeps the credit, as long as its visit is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default.
  • They came back through a search for your name. The search visit is newer and not Direct, so last non-direct gives it to Organic search. First touch gives it back to the review site.
  • They read the review and never clicked. There is no review visit to credit. The payment goes to whatever brought them, often a branded search, and nothing in any analytics tool can link it to the review.

To see how often reviews start a purchase, switch the model to first touch in Site settings and compare the review site's row before and after; history is recomputed and you can switch back. A single payment's journey, every visit before it with the credited one marked, is on the Payments page.

What Virling needs

The tag goes in the <head> of every page a listing can open (add the script). A payment provider is connected from the integrations page. The visitor ID has to reach checkout: payment links from Stripe, Polar and Dodo Payments get it from the script, and a checkout built on your server takes the virling_vid cookie (link checkout to visits). Once your listings are updated, open one, click your own website link, and look for that visit with the right campaign on the dashboard.

A made-up example: three directories over 90 days

Example only: made-up numbers for an imaginary SaaS on a $29 monthly plan, not results from a real business. Last 90 days, first payments only, site average $0.85 per visitor:

  • examplelaunchsite (tagged, Campaign tab): 1,100 visitors, 1 new customer, $29. That is $0.03 per visitor: a burst of curious founders, almost none of them buyers.
  • exampledirectory (tagged, Campaign tab): 420 visitors, 3 new customers, $87. About $0.21 per visitor, a quarter of the site average.
  • examplereviews.com (untagged, Source tab under Referral): 90 visitors, 2 new customers, $58. About $0.64 per visitor from the smallest row, because the people who read reviews were already shopping.

The loudest listing earned the least per visitor, and the quiet review page came closest to the site average. If a featured slot on the review site cost money, its visitors, revenue and price go into the revenue per visitor calculator to see whether it pays back. Rows this small move a lot with one customer, which is another reason to wait the full 90 days.

Launch sites are a burst rather than a trickle; Product Hunt launch revenue and revenue from a Hacker News post cover that shape. For a Shopify app or a browser extension store, see do app marketplace listings bring paying customers.

When a directory's numbers look off

  • The directory's host is in Referral but its campaign is missing. The listing still has your plain URL, or the directory stripped the tags. Edit the listing or switch to a tracked link.
  • A directory you never submitted to shows up. Scraper and copy sites republish listings. They show under their own hosts; leave them untagged.
  • A review site's clicks are all Direct. Its redirect or link settings hide the referrer. If you cannot add a tag there, watch Direct on the days the review page gets attention.
  • Visitors but no customers from any directory. Look at Matched revenue under the Overview numbers first. A low share means the visitor ID is missing at checkout, not that directories do not sell; see Unattributed revenue.

Frequently asked questions

Are SaaS directories worth it?

Some are and many are not, and it varies by product. Give each listing its own campaign, then compare new customers and revenue per visitor by directory against your site average after 90 days. Keep the ones near or above it updated and stop spending time on the rest.

Should a review site link get UTM tags?

Only if the profile lets you set the website URL. Otherwise the review site writes the link, and its clicks are measured by referrer host in the Referral channel, or as Direct when the referrer is hidden.

A directory adds its own ref to my link. Is that a problem?

No. Virling uses ref only when there is no utm_source, and only for clicks with no referrer. Put your own utm_source on the listing link and the directory's ref is ignored.

Who gets the credit if someone reads a review, then searches my name and pays?

With the default model, last non-direct, the later search visit gets it. With first touch, the review site gets it, if the buyer clicked through from the review inside the 90-day lookback window. If they never clicked, no visit links the payment to the review.

How should I tag a paid featured listing?

Give it its own link with utm_medium=cpc and its own utm_campaign. It then counts as Paid and has its own row, so you can compare its revenue with what the slot cost.
