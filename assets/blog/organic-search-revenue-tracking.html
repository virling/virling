Organic search revenue tracking: which Google visits pay

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-16.

Organic search revenue tracking means crediting each payment to the Google or Bing visit that brought the customer, then reading the result by landing page. Search tools show clicks and positions. Your payment provider shows who paid. Neither joins the two, so neither can tell you which searches turn into revenue.

This guide shows how to make that join, where to read it, and why the search keyword is the one thing you will not get.

What counts as organic search

Virling gives every session one channel, decided by its first pageview. A session is Organic search when it arrives from a known search host: any Google country domain, bing.com, duckduckgo.com, Yahoo, ecosia.org, Yandex, baidu.com, search.brave.com, startpage.com, kagi.com or qwant.com. With no referrer, a utm_source such as google or bing counts too. Every Google country domain shows as one google.com row. See the channel rules.

Two cases need care:

  • Ads without tags. A Google Ads or Microsoft Ads click that arrives from the search engine with no utm_medium looks like an organic click. Add utm_medium=cpc to every ad link and those visits move to Paid, where they belong. See which UTM parameters to use.
  • AI answers. Visits from gemini.google.com are counted as AI, not search, because AI hosts are checked first.

Why the keyword is missing

When someone clicks a search result, the search engine tells your site which engine sent them, not what they typed. No script on your site can recover the query, so no analytics tool that runs on your pages can show revenue by organic keyword.

The queries live in the search engine’s own reports, such as Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools. Those count clicks per query and per page, added up across everyone, not per visitor. A payment belongs to one person, so those totals cannot be joined to it. Virling does not read those reports.

The Keyword tab on the Channels card shows utm_term, which only exists on links you tag yourself, such as ads. Organic results carry none. The landing page is the closest stand-in: it is the page that ranked for what the visitor searched, so it narrows the query to the topics that page ranks for.

What you need first

Two links must exist before search revenue means anything. The payment provider must be connected, and the visitor ID must reach checkout, so a payment can be joined to the visit before it. See how to track Stripe revenue by traffic source and link checkout to visits.

When a payment is matched, the whole payment, after refunds, is credited to that session’s channel, referrer, campaign and landing page. See the attribution model.

How to read it in Virling

1. Filter to Organic search

On the Channels card, click Organic search. The whole dashboard filters to it: revenue, new customers and revenue per visitor are now for search visitors only. Compare its revenue per visitor with the other channels on the Channel tab before you decide how much search is worth to you.

2. Split it by search engine

Open the channel, or the Source tab, to see google.com, bing.com, duckduckgo.com and the rest as separate rows, each with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. A smaller engine with a higher revenue per visitor is worth knowing about, even if it never matches Google on visitors.

3. Read the landing pages

With the filter on, the Landing page tab of the Pages card lists the pages searchers arrived on and the revenue credited to each. This is the answer to which rankings pay. Sort by revenue per visitor, not by visitors, and wait until a page has a few hundred visitors before you trust it. See which blog posts bring paying customers for how to read this card.

4. See what closes the sale

The Before paying tab lists the last page viewed before the payment. If searchers who land on a guide tend to go through the same comparison or pricing page before paying, link that page more clearly from the guide.

5. Give search time

Search visitors often read, leave and pay days later. The Time to pay card shows, for each channel with at least three first payments in the range, the median time from first visit to first payment. If search is slow, use a longer date range before judging it. See how long a visitor takes to become a customer.

What is a search visitor worth

Revenue per visitor turns rankings into money. Put the Organic search visitors and revenue for a range, and what you spent on content in that range, into the revenue per visitor calculator to get revenue per visitor, return on spend and the break-even point. The same numbers for a single landing page tell you what one more ranking page of that kind is likely to be worth. To keep this on the dashboard, add a line such as Organic search 500 under Monthly spend per channel in Site settings and the Profit by channel card appears.

What to check if the numbers look off

  • Search revenue looks too low. By default the payment is credited to the latest visit that was not Direct. A customer who found you through search and came back through a newsletter is credited to Email. Switch the attribution model to first touch in Site settings to credit the visit that found them, and compare. See first touch or last touch.
  • Direct is large and search is small. Searchers often come back by typing your address. If the visitor ID reached checkout, the payment still finds the search visit; if it did not, it shows as unmatched. See why Direct is your biggest source.
  • Most revenue has no channel. The payment was not matched to a visit. The Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers gives the share matched and why the rest was not. See Unattributed revenue.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from organic search?

Connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout, so each payment joins the visit before it. Payments from customers whose credited visit came from a search engine are then counted under the Organic search channel, with the landing page they arrived on.

Can I see which keywords bring paying customers?

Not from organic search visits. Search engines send visitors without the search query, so no script on your site can record it. The landing page is the closest stand-in: it is the page that ranked for what they searched.

Does Virling connect to Google Search Console?

No. Search Console reports clicks per query and per page, not per visitor, so its numbers cannot be joined to a payment. Virling credits revenue to the search engine and the landing page instead.

Why do my Google Ads visits show as organic search?

An ad click that arrives from google.com with no utm_medium looks the same as an organic click. Add utm_medium=cpc to your ad links and those visits are counted as Paid.

Which search engines count as organic search?

Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Yahoo, Ecosia, Yandex, Baidu, Brave Search, Startpage, Kagi and Qwant, by referrer, or by utm_source when there is no referrer. Gemini is counted as AI, not search.
