Pinterest Ads revenue tracking: see which campaign brings paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-24.

Pinterest Ads revenue tracking means joining each payment on your site to the paid Pin click that brought the buyer. Add utm_source=pinterest, utm_medium=cpc and a utm_campaign to the destination URL of your ads, add the Virling script to your site, and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Virling counts the click as Paid and credits the payment to it, so the Campaign tab shows payments and revenue per campaign, apart from your organic Pins.

Step 1: tag the ad

Put the tags on the destination URL of each ad:

https://yourstore.com/products/linen-apron?utm_source=pinterest&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign={campaignid}

Pinterest fills in the part in braces. For the destination URL of a standard ad, its help page lists {campaignid}, {campaignname}, {adgroupid}, {adgroupname}, {adid}, {creative_id} and {device}, and it says {keyword} is not supported for tracking links on any ads (source: Pinterest, Add third-party and dynamic tracking). Shopping campaigns have their own list, which also has {campaignid}, {adgroupid} and {product_id}; collections ads that you create by hand have a shorter one, so type the campaign name there.

  • ID or name. {campaignid} gives a number you match to the campaign in Ads Manager. A name that you type, such as spring-sale, is easier to read in a report. Pick one style and keep it, because a campaign tagged two ways becomes two rows.
  • Ad and ad group. You can add utm_content={adid} and utm_term={adgroupid}, which Virling stores with the pageview, but reports group by campaign. A value under another key, such as cid={campaignid}, is not a campaign row in Virling.
  • Previews. The same page says the parameters expand only when the ad is live and being served, so a preview shows the braces as typed.

Pinterest can also add UTM tags for you. Its help page says it adds utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign and utm_content when they are missing, does not overwrite the ones you set, and skips them when the URL would pass 2,000 characters (source: Pinterest, Add URL parameters to your ads). It does not list the values, so set the tags yourself rather than rely on the medium it picks. The free UTM builder makes tagged URLs for you.

Telling paid Pins from organic Pins

A paid Pin and an organic Pin can open the same page from the same app, so the referrer cannot separate them. The medium on the link does.

  • Ads. A paid medium, such as cpc, makes the click Paid.
  • Organic Pins. Pinterest says it applies utm_source as Pinterest and utm_medium as Organic to the destination URLs of your organic Pins, unless you turn it off in Business settings (source: Pinterest, URL parameters for organic content). Organic is not a paid medium in Virling, so these clicks stay out of Paid. If you tag organic Pins yourself, use utm_medium=social.
  • Saved ads. People save ads to boards, and others click them there. Pinterest adds pp=0 to the URL for a click on the ad itself, and pp=1 for an earned click on the ad after it was saved to a board (source: Pinterest, The PP query string parameter). Virling does not use pp. If a saved ad keeps your paid tag, its clicks are Paid too, although Pinterest did not charge for them.

Do not put a paid medium on a Pin you also publish as an organic Pin, or its free clicks count as Paid. For unpaid Pins, boards and your profile link, see Pinterest revenue tracking.

How Virling sorts a Pinterest ad visit

Every session gets one channel from its first pageview, and the first matching rule wins:

  • A paid medium. cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social or paidsocial, in any case, is Paid. It is checked first, so a tagged click from pinterest.com is Paid, not Social.
  • No paid medium, with a referrer. pinterest.com and its subdomains, such as uk.pinterest.com, are Social, so the ad is mixed in with your Pins. A country domain such as pinterest.de or pinterest.co.uk is not on the list, so it is Referral.
  • No referrer, with a source tag. pinterest, in any case, is Social. A value that is not a known source is Referral.
  • No referrer and no tag. The visit is Direct. Virling recognises some in-app browsers, but Pinterest is not one of them.

Spelling counts. paid-social with a hyphen and cpm are not on the list, so those clicks are not Paid. See how channels are decided.

Step 2: add the script

Add the Virling script to the <head> of every page the ad can land on. It reads the tags on the landing page and sets the first-party visitor ID. Redirects on your site must keep the query string; see UTM parameters lost after redirect. The setup is in the script docs.

Step 3: link checkout

Connect your store or payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout, so each payment can be matched to a visit. On Shopify and WooCommerce, a short snippet stores the ID on the order. Payment links from Stripe, Polar, Dodo Payments and LemonSqueezy get the ID from the script when they are on a page that runs it; a checkout you create on your server needs the virling_vid cookie. See link checkout to visits.

Under the default model, the whole payment, after refunds, is credited to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct. Someone who clicks your ad on Monday and types your address on Thursday is still credited to the ad. A buyer who clicks in the Pinterest app and pays later in another browser can have two visitor IDs; see cross-device attribution for what can be joined. The rules are in the attribution model.

Step 4: read payments per campaign

  • In the Channels card, click Paid. The page filters to visits with a paid medium, from Pinterest and any other platform you tag the same way.
  • Open the Campaign tab. Each utm_campaign is a row with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  • Open a payment from the Payments page to see the visits before it.
  • Check Time to pay before you pause a campaign that looks weak. People save ideas and come back later, so give a campaign time.

To see profit, type what you spend on ads each month in Site settings, under Monthly spend per channel, such as Paid 1000. The Profit by channel card then shows spend, revenue and profit for the Paid channel as a whole, so include every platform you tag as Paid. Nothing connects to an ad platform, and the result is only as good as the amount you type.

A made-up example

These numbers are invented to show the arithmetic. Two campaigns each cost $500, as read in Ads Manager. Campaign A has 350 visitors and 10 orders of $80, so $800 of revenue, about $2.29 per visitor and $50 of spend per order. Campaign B has 1,000 visitors and 5 orders of $80, so $400 of revenue, $0.40 per visitor and $100 per order. At a 65% margin with one $80 order per customer, the break-even cost per customer is $52, so A is above water and B is not, even though B brought more visitors. Work out your own limit with the break-even CPA calculator.

Why ad-reported conversions differ from payments

  • Views can count. Pinterest’s Conversion insights page follows the conversion window set in Ads Manager, and shows 1-day view and 30-day click data when none is set (source: Pinterest, Review Conversion insights). A view with no click never reaches your site, so it cannot be credited to a visit.
  • Earned activity counts. The same page says ad-driven conversions include downstream activity on your ads, known as earned metrics.
  • Different credit. Pinterest describes that report as based on the last Pin a person engaged with before checking out. Virling looks at every channel. If the buyer clicked an ad and later came from your newsletter, the default model credits the newsletter.
  • Different visitors. A buyer who changed browser, or blocked the script, may be in one count and missing from the other.

Use Ads Manager to run the ads and the payments to decide which campaigns to keep. The same setup for search ads is in Google Ads revenue tracking, and for Facebook and Instagram in Meta Ads revenue tracking. For video campaigns, see YouTube Ads revenue tracking.

What to check if the numbers look off

  • Ad revenue shows as Social or Referral. The ad has no paid utm_medium. Click a live ad and check the address bar.
  • Ad revenue shows as Direct. The tags were dropped by a redirect, or the ad has none and the app sent no referrer.
  • A campaign row shows braces. If {campaignid} appears as typed, the click came from a preview, or the spelling is wrong for that ad type.
  • Paid has more visitors than the clicks you bought. A saved ad, or an organic Pin with the same link, may carry the paid tag.
  • Revenue shows as Unattributed. The visitor ID is not reaching checkout.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which Pinterest ad campaign brought a paying customer?

Add utm_source=pinterest, utm_medium=cpc and a utm_campaign, such as {campaignid}, to the destination URL of your ads. Connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Each payment is credited to the tagged visit, and the Campaign tab shows revenue per campaign.

How do I tell paid Pins from organic Pins?

By the medium on the link. Use a paid medium such as cpc on ads, so they are Paid. Pinterest says it adds utm_source=Pinterest and utm_medium=Organic to organic Pins unless you opt out. Organic is not a paid medium, so those clicks are sorted by their referrer, or as Social when there is none.

Does Pinterest add UTM parameters to ads by itself?

Yes, its help page says it adds utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign and utm_content when they are missing and does not overwrite the ones you set. The page does not list the values, so set your own: Virling only counts the click as Paid when the medium is one it knows.

Why do I see {campaignid} in the address bar?

You are probably previewing the ad. Pinterest says dynamic parameters expand only when the ad is live and being served. If the braces also show up in your campaign rows from live clicks, check the spelling.

Does Virling use the pp parameter?

No. Pinterest adds pp=0 to clicks on an ad and pp=1 to clicks on an ad that someone saved to a board. Virling does not use it to pick the channel or the campaign. Those come from utm_medium, the referrer, utm_source and ref, and utm_campaign.

Does Virling connect to my Pinterest ad account?

No. The setup on this page uses only the tags on your ads, the Paid channel and your payments. You can type what you spend on Paid each month in Site settings, and the Profit by channel card uses that amount.
