Pinterest Ads revenue tracking: see which campaign brings paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-24.

Pinterest Ads revenue tracking means joining each payment on your site to the paid Pin click that brought the buyer. Add utm_source=pinterest, utm_medium=cpc and a utm_campaign to the destination URL of your ads, add the Virling script to your site, and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Virling counts the click as Paid and credits the payment to it, so the Campaign tab shows payments and revenue per campaign, apart from your organic Pins.

Step 1: tag the destination URL

Put the tags on the destination URL of each ad:

https://yourstore.com/products/linen-apron?utm_source=pinterest&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign={campaignid}

Pinterest fills in the part in braces. For the destination URL of a standard ad, its help page lists {campaignid}, {campaignname}, {adgroupid}, {adgroupname}, {adid}, {creative_id} and {device}, and it says {keyword} is not supported for tracking links on any ads (source: Pinterest, Add third-party and dynamic tracking). Shopping campaigns have their own list, which also has {campaignid}, {adgroupid} and {product_id}; collections ads that you create by hand have a shorter one, so type the campaign name there. Names change, so check that page before you copy one.

  • ID or name. {campaignid} gives a number you match to the campaign in Ads Manager. A name that you type, such as spring-sale, is easier to read in a report. Pick one style and keep it, because a campaign tagged two ways becomes two rows.
  • Ad and ad group. You can add utm_content={adid} and utm_term={adgroupid}, which Virling stores with the pageview, but reports group by campaign. A value under another key, such as cid={campaignid}, is not a campaign row in Virling.
  • Previews. The same page says the parameters expand only when the ad is live and being served, so a preview shows the braces as typed.

Pinterest can also add UTM tags for you. Its help page says it adds utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign and utm_content when they are missing, does not overwrite the ones you set, and skips them when the URL would pass 2,000 characters (source: Pinterest, Add URL parameters to your ads). It does not list the values, so set the tags yourself rather than rely on the medium it picks. The free UTM builder makes tagged URLs for you.

Product Pins from a catalog

Shopping ads are built from your catalog, so the destination is the product link in your data source, not a URL you type for each ad. The same catalog also makes product Pins that people can find and save without any ad. If you put utm_medium=cpc on the links in the feed, those unpaid clicks count as Paid too. Add the tags where the help page above says shopping ads take tracking parameters, and use utm_content={product_id} if you want the product stored with the visit.

Saves, boards and late clicks

On Pinterest, people often save an idea to a board and click it later, so the visit can come long after the ad was shown. Pinterest adds pp=0 to the URL for a click on the ad itself, and pp=1 for an earned click on the ad after it was saved to a board (source: Pinterest, The PP query string parameter). Virling does not use pp. A click from a board that keeps your paid tag is a new Paid visit for the campaign, although Pinterest did not charge for it.

That suits a slow buyer. Under the default model, a payment goes to the latest visit in the 90 days before it that was not Direct, so someone who clicks a saved ad, leaves, and comes back weeks later by typing your address is still credited to the campaign. A payment more than 90 days after the last visit is Unattributed. Look at Time to pay before you pause a campaign, and see how long a visit gets credit to choose a window.

Telling paid Pins from organic Pins

A paid Pin and an organic Pin can open the same page from the same app, so the referrer cannot separate them. The medium on the link does.

  • Ads. A paid medium, such as cpc, makes the click Paid.
  • Organic Pins. Pinterest says it applies utm_source as Pinterest and utm_medium as Organic to the destination URLs of your organic Pins, unless you turn it off in Business settings (source: Pinterest, URL parameters for organic content). Organic is not a paid medium in Virling, so these clicks stay out of Paid. If you tag organic Pins yourself, use utm_medium=social.

Do not put a paid medium on a Pin you also publish as an organic Pin, or its free clicks count as Paid. For unpaid Pins, boards and your profile link, see Pinterest revenue tracking.

What Virling does with epik

Clicks from Pinterest can arrive with an epik parameter on the URL. It is Pinterest’s click ID: the Pinterest tag keeps it in an _epik cookie, and the conversions API sends it back as click_id, so Pinterest can match a conversion to the click. Virling does not read epik. It plays no part in the channel or the campaign, and it is not stored, because a pageview keeps its path without the query string, plus the UTM tags and ref.

Leave epik in place for the Pinterest tag and keep your UTM tags next to it. An untagged click that arrives with epik and no referrer is Direct, listed as Typed or bookmark, because epik is not one of the click IDs Virling reads as a sign of a shared link.

How Virling sorts a Pinterest ad visit

Virling looks at utm_medium before anything else: cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social and paidsocial, in any case, make the session Paid even when pinterest.com sent it. Untagged, a referrer from pinterest.com, a subdomain such as uk.pinterest.com, or a pin.it short link is Social, while a country domain such as pinterest.de or pinterest.co.uk is Referral. With no referrer, utm_source=pinterest is Social, and with neither the visit is Direct, since the Pinterest app is not an in-app browser Virling recognises. A hyphenated paid-social and cpm are not paid media. See how channels are decided.

Linking the order and reading the result

Put the script on every page a Pin can land on, keep the query string through redirects, and send the visitor ID into checkout; on Shopify and WooCommerce a short snippet stores it on the order. The steps are in find which channel pays. Filter the dashboard to Paid and the Campaign tab lists revenue per utm_campaign. A buyer who taps a Pin in the Pinterest app and pays later in another browser can have two visitor IDs; see cross-device attribution.

Why ad-reported conversions differ from payments

  • Views can count. Pinterest’s Conversion insights page follows the conversion window set in Ads Manager, and shows 1-day view and 30-day click data when none is set (source: Pinterest, Review Conversion insights). A view with no click never reaches your site, so it cannot be credited to a visit.
  • Earned activity counts. The same page says ad-driven conversions include downstream activity on your ads, known as earned metrics.
  • Different credit. Pinterest describes that report as based on the last Pin a person engaged with before checking out. Virling looks at every channel, so a buyer who last came from your newsletter is credited to the newsletter.

Ads Manager is where you buy and pace the ads; the payments show which campaigns earn their budget. The same setup for search ads is in Google Ads revenue tracking, and for Facebook and Instagram in Meta Ads revenue tracking. For video campaigns, see YouTube Ads revenue tracking, and for ads on TikTok, TikTok Ads revenue tracking.

What to check if the numbers look off

  • Ad revenue shows as Social or Referral. The ad has no paid utm_medium, or Pinterest added its own medium. Click a live ad and check the address bar.
  • Ad revenue shows as Direct. The Pinterest app sent no referrer and the tags were missing or dropped by a redirect. An epik on the URL does not change that.
  • A campaign row shows braces. If {campaignid} appears as typed, the click came from a preview, or the spelling is wrong for that ad type.
  • Paid has more visitors than the clicks you bought. A saved ad, a tagged catalog link, or an organic Pin with the same link may carry the paid tag.
  • Orders show as Unattributed. The store snippet or your checkout is not passing the visitor ID.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which Pinterest ad campaign brought a paying customer?

Add utm_source=pinterest, utm_medium=cpc and a utm_campaign, such as {campaignid}, to the destination URL of your ads. Connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Each payment is credited to the tagged visit, and the Campaign tab shows revenue per campaign.

How do I tell paid Pins from organic Pins?

By the medium on the link. Use a paid medium such as cpc on ads, so they are Paid. Pinterest says it adds utm_source=Pinterest and utm_medium=Organic to organic Pins unless you opt out. Organic is not a paid medium, so those clicks are sorted by their referrer, or as Social when there is none.

Does Pinterest add UTM parameters to ads by itself?

Yes, its help page says it adds utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign and utm_content when they are missing and does not overwrite the ones you set. The page does not list the values, so set your own: Virling only counts the click as Paid when the medium is one it knows.

Why do I see {campaignid} in the address bar?

You are probably previewing the ad. Pinterest says dynamic parameters expand only when the ad is live and being served. If the braces also show up in your campaign rows from live clicks, check the spelling.

Does Virling use the pp parameter?

No. Pinterest adds pp=0 to clicks on an ad and pp=1 to clicks on an ad that someone saved to a board. Virling does not use it to pick the channel or the campaign. Those come from utm_medium, the referrer, utm_source and ref, and utm_campaign.

Does Virling use the epik parameter?

No. epik is the click ID Pinterest adds for its own tag and conversions API. Virling does not read or store it, and it does not make a click Paid. Leave it on the URL for Pinterest and add UTM tags for Virling.
