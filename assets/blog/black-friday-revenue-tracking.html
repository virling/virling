Black Friday revenue tracking: which channel drove each sale

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-19.

Black Friday revenue tracking comes down to three steps. Before the sale, tag every link you share, in emails, posts, ads and partner pages. Keep one naming scheme so every channel uses the same campaign names. After the sale, credit each payment to the tagged visit before it and read revenue by channel and campaign. This post gives a UTM plan for a sale running from Black Friday, November 27, to Cyber Monday, November 30, 2026, and what to check the week after.

It is written for online stores and SaaS founders who run one sale across their own list, social posts, ads, affiliates and newsletter sponsors, and want to know which of them brought paying customers.

Why sale weekend revenue is hard to read

  • Many channels at once. In four days the same buyer may see an early access email, a post, an ad and an affiliate's review. Without tags on each link you cannot tell which visit came before the payment.
  • Untagged clicks look like Direct. Email apps, chat apps and in-app browsers often send no referrer, so an untagged click from your sale email lands in Direct, next to people who typed your address.
  • Names drift. BlackFriday in one email and black-friday in another become two rows. Decide the names before the first email goes out.

A few rules behind the table:

  • The medium is checked first. email, newsletter or e-mail is always Email, and cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social or paidsocial is always Paid, whatever the referrer says.
  • Keep sponsors out of Email. If a sponsored newsletter link uses utm_medium=newsletter, its visits land in Email next to your own list. A medium such as sponsor puts them in Referral, with the newsletter's name as the source.
  • Use known source names for social. instagram, linkedin, x, facebook and tiktok are known social sources. An unknown source with no referrer counts as Referral.
  • One value per affiliate. Put the affiliate's name in the source and the campaign, so the Source tab and the Campaign tab both show them on their own row.

Build each link with the UTM builder and write the plan down once with the UTM naming template. For email flows and send-by-send naming, see email marketing revenue tracking for online stores.

If you would rather not type tags, open Links in Virling and create a tracked link with a destination, a source and a channel. Its short URL redirects with utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign added, and the link's id becomes the campaign, so its visits show in the Campaign tab under that id. For affiliates and sponsors, Partners makes a named short link per partner and ranks partners by revenue.

Discount codes and tracked links

Sale codes often get shared far beyond the partner who was given them. Virling credits each payment to the visit before it, not to the code, and does not read discount codes, so code redemptions stay in your store or payment provider. Give each affiliate a tagged link as well as a code, and compare the two after the sale. Discount code attribution explains how to read the gap.

Set it up before the sale

  1. Add the script. Put the Virling script in the head of every page, with your domain in data-site. It reads the tags on the landing page and sets the visitor ID. See add the script.
  2. Pass the visitor ID into checkout. Connect your store or payment provider so each order or payment carries the visitor ID. See link checkout to visits and the list of integrations.
  3. Test every link. Click each tagged link from the email, post or partner page it lives in, look for the tags in the address bar, and check that the visit shows in the right channel.

What to check the week after

Wait a few days after Cyber Monday so late payments come in, then work through these:

  1. Set the range. Use Custom range for November 27 to 30, and Compare to other dates against a normal week before the sale. Revenue counts on the payment date, so some sale revenue can come from visits before the range.
  2. Read the Channels card. Each channel shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. Compare channels by revenue per visitor, not by visitors: a channel that sent few buyers who paid can beat one that sent many who did not.
  3. Open the Campaign tab. Every bf-2026 row is one piece of the plan: each email, each affiliate, each sponsor. The Source tab splits a channel by where the visits came from.
  4. Check who got the credit. By default a payment goes to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct. A buyer who clicked the early access email on November 20 and came back on Black Friday by typing your address is credited to that email. First touch and last touch are in site settings.
  5. Look at Direct and Unattributed. A jump in Direct on the day an email went out usually means untagged links. Payments with no visitor ID show as Unattributed; check the checkout step for those.
  6. Watch refunds. Revenue is net of refunds, and the Channels card shows the share of each row's payments that were refunded. Check again a few weeks later, when sale returns have come in.
  7. Open single payments. The Payments page lists every payment with its source. Open one to see the visits before it and which one got the credit.

If you put a monthly spend per channel in site settings, the Profit by channel card shows spend, revenue and profit for the range. Nothing connects to an ad platform, so the numbers are only as good as the amounts you type.

If affiliates carry part of the sale, compare affiliates by paid revenue rather than clicks. Once the late payments are in, the sale belongs in your annual review by revenue per channel.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track Black Friday sales by channel?

Tag every link you share for the sale with utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign, using one naming scheme for every channel. Credit each payment to the tagged visit before it, then read revenue by channel and campaign for the sale dates.

What UTM campaign name should I use for Black Friday?

Use one lowercase prefix for the whole sale, such as bf-2026, and add a short suffix when you want a piece as its own report row, such as bf-2026-early-access for one email or bf-2026-jane-doe for one affiliate. Write the names down before the sale and do not change them halfway.

Should newsletter sponsors use utm_medium=newsletter?

Not if you want them apart from your own list. In Virling, utm_medium of email, newsletter or e-mail puts the visit in the Email channel. Use a medium such as sponsor and the newsletter's name as the source, and the visit lands in Referral, with its own row under Source and Campaign.

Why do Black Friday orders show as Direct?

Usually a link was left untagged. Email apps, chat apps and in-app browsers often send no referrer, so an untagged click looks like a typed URL. Tag every link before the sale and test each one by clicking it and looking for the tags in the address bar.

Who gets the credit if a buyer clicks an early access email and buys on Black Friday by typing the URL?

Under Virling's default model, last non-direct with a 90-day window, the email gets the credit, because it was the latest visit that was not Direct. You can switch to first touch or last touch in site settings, and every past payment is rebuilt with the new model.
