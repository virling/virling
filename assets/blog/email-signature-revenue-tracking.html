Email signature tracking: which links bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-24.

Email signature tracking that ends in revenue needs one thing in the signature: a tagged link of its own for each person. Put utm_source=signature, utm_medium=email and a utm_campaign such as signature-maya on the link, carry the visitor ID into checkout, and join each payment to the visit before it. You then see which signatures bring paying customers, with no pixel in the email and no open tracking.

This is for founders and sales teams who send one-to-one email all day. The signature is on every message, and it usually links to a plain homepage URL, so nobody can tell whether it ever brought a customer.

Why signature clicks look like Direct

A visit gets its channel from its first pageview, and a click in an email carries few clues:

  • Mail apps send no referrer. A desktop or phone mail app opens the link in a browser with nothing saying where it came from. With no referrer and no tag, the visit counts as Direct.
  • Webmail may name itself. When webmail passes a referrer, it is the mail host, not the person who sent the email. A Google host matches the Organic search list, so an untagged click can even land there.
  • Replies and forwards keep the signature. A colleague further down the thread can click it weeks later. Nothing but a tag on the link connects that visit to the email.

Why Direct is your biggest traffic source covers the other causes.

How the Email channel is decided

Virling checks utm_medium before anything else. A value of email, newsletter or e-mail, upper or lower case, is the Email channel whatever the referrer says. Only without a deciding medium does it look at the referrer, then at utm_source. So utm_medium=signature does not work: with no referrer and utm_source=signature, the visit lands in Referral. Keep the medium as email and say "signature" in the other tags. The full rule order is in channels.

One tagged link per person

Use the same source and medium for the whole team and a campaign per person, all lowercase with hyphens:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=signature&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=signature-maya
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=signature&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=signature-jon
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=signature&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=signature-support
  • The person goes in utm_campaign. It is the tag with a report row in Virling. A bare utm_source such as signature has no row of its own, and utm_content is stored with the visit but is not a report row.
  • Start every value with signature-. Signature rows are then easy to tell from newsletters and sequences in the same Email channel.
  • Keep the value for good. A new job title or design should not start a new row. Change it only when you want a fresh count.
  • Link text, not a bare URL. Put the link on words such as your company name, so the reader does not see the tags. Set it in every mail app the person uses, because the phone often has its own signature.
  • Never put the recipient in a tag. Name whose signature it is, not who received it. If you would rather not put a colleague's name in a URL, use initials or a role.

The UTM builder makes each link. Links in outreach itself need their own campaign, not the signature's; see which cold email sequences bring paying customers. The same goes for onboarding and trial emails, covered in which lifecycle emails lead to paid plans.

The alternative: a tracked short link

If a long URL is in the way, for example in a plain-text signature, make a tracked link per person instead. It is a short URL on your Virling server that redirects to your page with the tags added, and it counts clicks.

  1. On the dashboard, open the Campaign tab of the Channels card and click + Tracked link.
  2. Choose Other as the place and type signature. A bare name is fine here, since the medium decides the channel.
  3. As the page, enter /?utm_medium=email. Tags already on the page are kept, so the visit lands in Email. Without it, a click with no referrer lands in Referral. Do not add a utm_campaign, since the link's own id fills it.
  4. Name it after the person, such as "Signature Maya". The Campaign tab shows that name.
  5. Copy the short URL when it appears under the list. The link gets its own row only once it has a visit in the range.

Two things to know. A link's page cannot be changed later, so settle on it before the team pastes it in. And clicks are counted by the server for all time, while visitors are counted by the script for the range you picked. A mail security scanner that opens the link without naming itself as a bot adds a click; if it does not run JavaScript, it is never a visitor. Judge by visitors and payments, not clicks.

Set it up with Virling

  1. Add the script to the head of every page of your marketing site and app. It reads the tags on the landing page and sets the visitor ID. See add the script.
  2. Connect your payment provider with a read-only key under Setup, then Connect payments. See payments and revenue attribution for SaaS.
  3. Pass the visitor ID into checkout. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Stripe Payment Links and Dodo Payments links get it from the script on click. See link checkout to visits.
  4. Cover invoiced deals. When a deal closes on a Stripe invoice there is no checkout on your site. Send the buyer's email once from your demo or signup form, and a later payment with the same email can be matched to their visits. See set up Virling for sales-led B2B SaaS.
  5. Swap the links and test one. Send yourself an email, click the signature in a normal browser window, and check that the tags reach the address bar.

How to read payments per person

In the Channels card, click Email, then open the Campaign tab. Each signature is a row with its visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. These numbers are made up to show the reading:

CampaignVisitorsRevenueRevenue per visitor
signature-maya40$600$15.00
signature-jon120$240$2.00
signature-support90$0$0.00

In this example Jon's signature sends three times the visitors of Maya's and less than half the revenue. Before you act on a table like this:

  • Open the payments. A handful of payments can decide a row. On the Payments page, open one to see the buyer's visits, with the credited visit marked.
  • Compare roles with care. Support mostly writes to people who already pay. Their renewals and upgrades keep the channel of the customer's first payment, so a support signature earns little by design.
  • Check the attribution model. Under last non-direct, the default, a signature click during a sales conversation can take the credit from the search or post that found the buyer. First touch shows what introduced them. For long deals, choose a longer lookback window than the default 90 days. See model and lookback window.
  • Give it time. Use a range that covers your usual time from first visit to payment.
  • Large teams. The Campaign tab lists the top 6 campaigns in the range. Read the rest with the API, whose campaign breakdown returns up to 1,000 rows.

A credited payment means the buyer came through that link. It does not measure how good the person is at their job, and it misses buyers who read the email and typed your address without clicking, or who clicked on a phone and paid on a laptop.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • No visits for one person. Their signature still has the plain link in one mail app, or your site redirects and drops the query string. Link straight to the final page.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If it is low, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A person is split across rows. Values differ in case or spelling, such as Signature-Maya and signature-maya.
  • Your own team shows up. Colleagues click each other's signatures too. List your office address under Ignored IP addresses in Site settings, or open your site with #virling-ignore on the end in their browsers.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track clicks on an email signature link?

Give each person one link with utm_source=signature, utm_medium=email and a utm_campaign of their own, such as signature-maya. With the Virling script on your site, each visit from the link shows under that campaign. A tracked short link also counts every time the link is opened.

How do I see revenue from email signatures?

Connect your payment provider, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and give every signature its own utm_campaign. Each payment is then credited to one visit before it, and the Campaign tab shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor for each signature.

Why do email signature clicks show up as Direct?

Mail apps on phones and desktops usually send no referrer when a link is clicked. With no referrer and no tag, the visit counts as Direct. A utm_medium of email puts it in the Email channel whatever the referrer says.

Can email signature tracking work without a pixel?

Yes. A tagged link needs nothing inside the email: the tags are read on your site after the click. Virling does not track opens, so an email that was read but not clicked leaves no record.

Should each employee have a different link in their signature?

Yes, if you want to compare people. Links with the same utm_campaign share one row. Give each person their own value, and keep it when the job title or design of the signature changes so the history stays in one row.

Can I put the recipient's email address in the link?

Do not. URLs end up in browser history, logs and forwarded emails. The tag should name whose signature it is, never who received the email.
