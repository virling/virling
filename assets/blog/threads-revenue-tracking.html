Threads revenue tracking: which Threads posts bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-18, updated 2026-11-26.

To find out which Threads posts lead to sales, Virling has to recognise a Threads click in three forms: a visit referred by threads.com or by l.threads.com, the redirect Threads wraps around outbound links; an app click that arrives with nothing, which only a utm_source=threads.com tag can rescue; and the payment that follows, joined back to the visit by a visitor ID passed into checkout. Views, likes and reposts measure attention on Threads. They say nothing about who bought.

Threads is run from the same account as Instagram, and many brands post a drop on both on the same day. That makes the useful question narrower than "does Threads work": for the same product and the same day, did a Threads visitor end up worth more or less than an Instagram one?

l.threads.com and the other Threads referrers

Threads does not hand your link to the browser directly. An outbound tap goes through a redirect at l.threads.com first, so the referrer your site receives is often l.threads.com rather than the profile or post the reader was looking at. Older links and some web sessions still show threads.net, from before Threads moved to threads.com.

Virling folds all three hosts into one Source row, threads.com, labelled Threads and placed in the Social channel. You do not need to add up l.threads.com by hand the way you do with l.instagram.com, which keeps its own row next to instagram.com. Because Virling keeps only the host of a referrer, every Threads click looks the same at this level, whichever post it came from. Telling posts apart is the job of campaign tags, below.

The app, and the Instagram account behind it

Most people read Threads on their phone, and a link tapped in the app opens in the Threads in-app browser. Those visits frequently carry no referrer. Virling credits a handful of apps from their in-app browser's user agent, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and LinkedIn among them, but there is no Threads entry in that list. So an untagged, referrer-less tap from Threads lands in Direct.

There is one twist. Threads signs you in with your Instagram account, and if the in-app browser's user agent mentions Instagram, Virling's Instagram rule matches and the visit is credited to instagram.com instead. Either way the click is lost to Threads, which is why a tag matters more here than on most networks: a utm_source is read before the user agent is consulted, and threads.com resolves to the Threads row.

Sharing a login does not mean sharing cookies. The Threads in-app browser, the Instagram in-app browser and the phone's own browser each keep their own cookie jar, so the same person tapping your link in Threads, then in Instagram, then buying in Safari can appear as three visitors. Only the visit made in the browser that reached checkout is joined to the payment. Links that send people straight to a product page, where they can buy in one sitting, keep more of these sales tied to the Threads visit.

Tags for your Threads profile, posts and replies

Give every Threads link the source threads.com, a social medium and a campaign that says where it was posted:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=threads.com&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=profile
https://yourdomain.com/products/new-drop?utm_source=threads.com&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=post-new-drop
https://yourdomain.com/products/new-drop?utm_source=threads.com&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=reply-new-drop
  • Source. Use the host, threads.com. The bare word threads is still read as Social, but its visits sit in a separate row instead of joining the Threads referrer row.
  • Medium. social works. Only paid mediums such as cpc and email mediums move a visit out of Social.
  • Campaign. Name the place: profile for the profile link, one value per post, and a separate value when you drop the same link in your own replies, so you can see whether the post or the reply drove the clicks. When you swap the profile link for a sale, rename its campaign too, for example profile-sale.

The UTM builder assembles these links, and which UTM parameters to use covers naming. The link generator for social posts fills in threads as the source by default, so edit it to threads.com. For a shorter link in a post, create a tracked link in Virling under Links with Other as the place and threads.com typed as the source; the click arrives with the source, a social medium and the link's own campaign already set. Details are in tracked links.

Payments still need the visitor ID at checkout. Stripe Payment Links, Polar and Dodo Payments links get it added on click by the script, Shopify and WooCommerce stores add a theme snippet, and a server-created checkout passes the virling_vid cookie; see link checkout to visits. Under the default last non-direct model, a Threads visit stays credited when the buyer returns by a typed URL within the 90-day lookback, and renewals keep counting toward it.

Compare Threads with Instagram

Visit counts mislead when one drop goes out on both apps, since the bigger audience wins them whether or not it buys. Judge the two on paying customers and revenue per visitor instead.

  1. Choose a date range that starts on the day of the drop and runs at least a few weeks, so slower buyers are included.
  2. Go to the Source tab of the Channels card. Note threads.com, then add instagram.com and l.instagram.com together, since Instagram's redirect host is not merged.
  3. Compare revenue per visitor on those rows, and the chip that sets each against your site average.
  4. Filter by the Threads row and look at the Pages card to see which product pages Threads visitors landed on, then repeat for Instagram.
  5. In the Campaign tab, line up post-new-drop on each app to compare the same post, not the whole account.
  6. A month or two later, check Retention, which groups customers by the channel and referrer of their first payment, to see which app's buyers kept paying.

Tag both sides the same way, or the comparison tilts. instagram.com as the Instagram source joins the instagram.com row, where instagram or ig sits apart from it. Untagged app clicks tilt it further: Instagram gets credit from its user agent and Threads does not, so an untagged drop flatters Instagram. Instagram revenue tracking covers bio links, story stickers and l.instagram.com, and tracking Facebook posts and groups covers the Facebook side.

A Threads post can introduce a buyer who later arrives through Google. To see that, switch to first touch in Site settings and compare both apps again; Virling recomputes history and you can switch back. First-touch or last-touch attribution explains the choice, and X revenue tracking covers the same drop posted to X.

When the Threads numbers look off

  • A Threads post took off and Direct rose with it. The post's link was untagged and the app sent no referrer. Tag the next one. Links copied into DMs and group chats end up in Direct as well; see dark social revenue tracking.
  • Instagram rose on a day you only posted to Threads. The Threads in-app browser probably matched Virling's Instagram user agent rule. A tag on the link overrides it.
  • Tagged Threads clicks appear in Social but not in the threads.com row. The source was written as threads. Switch to threads.com.
  • An unfamiliar domain appears under Referral. Your profile probably points at a link-in-bio service. Link straight to your site, or host the links page on your own domain.
  • Threads has visitors but zero revenue. Look at the Matched revenue line on the Overview. A low share means checkout is not getting the visitor ID; Unattributed revenue walks through the fixes.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track sales from Threads?

Let Virling count visits referred by threads.com, threads.net or l.threads.com, add utm_source=threads.com and a per-post campaign to every link so app clicks without a referrer still count, and pass the visitor ID into checkout so payments join to visits. The threads.com row then shows Threads revenue and revenue per visitor.

Why do clicks from Threads show up as Direct?

The Threads app often opens links with no referrer, and Virling has no user agent rule for the Threads in-app browser, so an untagged tap is Direct. A utm_source of threads.com on the link credits it to Threads.

Why are some Threads clicks credited to Instagram?

Threads runs on your Instagram account. If its in-app browser's user agent mentions Instagram, an untagged click with no referrer matches Virling's Instagram rule. A utm_source on the link is read first and keeps the click on Threads.

What is l.threads.com in my referrers?

It is the redirect Threads sends outbound link taps through, so your site often sees l.threads.com instead of the post. Virling merges it, and threads.net, into one Threads row under threads.com.

How do I compare Threads with Instagram?

Tag both with host sources, threads.com and instagram.com, give the same post the same campaign on each app, then compare paying customers and revenue per visitor in the Source and Campaign tabs. Add l.instagram.com to instagram.com, because it keeps its own row.
