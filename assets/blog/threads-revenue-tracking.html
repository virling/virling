Threads revenue tracking: which Threads posts bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-18.

To see which Threads posts bring paying customers, you need three things: visits that arrive from threads.com, threads.net or l.threads.com counted as Threads, links in your profile and posts tagged with utm_source=threads.com and a campaign per post so clicks without a referrer still count, and each visit joined to the payment that followed by a visitor ID carried into checkout. Then you can read the revenue, and the revenue per visitor, that Threads brings. Likes, reposts, views and follower counts do not tell you this.

Stores and creators now post product drops, launches and links on Threads, often next to the same content on Instagram. Whether that brings sales comes down to one question: did anyone who clicked through from Threads pay, and how much was each Threads visitor worth compared with Instagram and your other channels?

How Threads visits arrive

A click from Threads reaches your site in one of four ways, and Virling handles each one differently.

  • threads.com or threads.net as the referrer. Clicks from Threads on the web can arrive with the Threads site as the referrer. Virling puts both in the Social channel, names them Threads and merges threads.net into threads.com, so you see one Threads row.
  • l.threads.com as the referrer. Threads sends outbound clicks through its own link address, so a click often arrives with l.threads.com as the referrer. Virling treats it as Threads too and merges it into the same threads.com row.
  • No referrer. Most Threads use happens in the mobile app, and links opened in its in-app browser often arrive with no referrer at all. Virling can credit some apps from the user agent of their in-app browser, such as Instagram, Facebook or LinkedIn, but it has no rule for Threads. An untagged click with no referrer is therefore not credited to Threads: it is Direct, or Instagram if the in-app browser names Instagram in its user agent.
  • A UTM tag. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, or ref when it is missing. threads.com is read as the Threads host, so the visit lands in Social and in the same Threads row as referrer clicks.

A referrer always wins over the tag, so a tagged click that does carry a referrer still shows as Threads from that referrer. The tag matters for the clicks that arrive with nothing else. The full rule order is in channels.

Filter Virling to the threads.com row and the Overview shows revenue, new customers and revenue per visitor for Threads visits alone.

Tag your profile and post links

The referrer only says threads.com or l.threads.com. It cannot tell your profile link from a post or a reply, because Virling stores the referrer host, not the full URL. Tag each place with its own campaign:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=threads.com&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=profile
https://yourdomain.com/products/new-drop?utm_source=threads.com&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=post-new-drop
  • utm_source=threads.com for every Threads link. threads also counts as Social, but it gets no source row of its own next to threads.com, so its visits are easy to miss when you compare sources.
  • utm_medium=social for posts, replies, your profile and messages. Anything except a paid or email medium keeps the visit in Social.
  • utm_campaign names the place: one per post you want to measure, and a fixed one, such as profile, for the link that stays on your profile. If you change the profile link, change the campaign too, for example profile-sale, so each version gets its own row.

More naming rules are in which UTM parameters to use, and the UTM builder makes the link for you. If you post the same link on Instagram or X, the link generator for social posts makes one per platform; change its Threads source from threads to threads.com. If a long tagged URL looks messy in a post, use a tracked link instead: in Virling, open Links, pick Other as the place, type threads.com as the source and choose the page it should open. The short link adds the source, a social medium and its own campaign on the click, and the Campaign tab lists it with its visitors and revenue. See tracked links.

Make sure payments can be credited

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs the visitor ID in checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. Shopify and WooCommerce stores add a short snippet to their theme. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Every provider is covered in link checkout to visits.

Few people buy on the first click from a post. With the default model, last non-direct, someone who tapped your post on Monday and came back on Friday by typing your URL is still credited to Threads, as long as the Threads visit is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. If they subscribe, every renewal keeps counting toward Threads.

Compare Threads with Instagram

If you post the same drop on Threads and Instagram, compare paying customers and revenue per visitor, not visits. A channel with fewer visitors can still bring more revenue.

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more, so slow buyers have time to pay.
  2. In the Channels card, open Social, or switch to the Source tab, and find threads.com, instagram.com and l.instagram.com. Each row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average. l.instagram.com has a row of its own, so add it to instagram.com when you compare.
  3. Click the Threads row to filter the whole page. The Pages card now shows which landing pages Threads visitors arrived on; do the same for Instagram.
  4. Switch to the Campaign tab to split each app by post and by profile link, if you tagged them.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.
  6. Check the Retention page after a month or two. It groups customers by the channel and referrer of their first payment, so you can see whether Threads customers keep paying as long as Instagram ones.

Keep the tagging the same on both sides. If Threads links use threads.com as the source but Instagram links use instagram, tagged Instagram clicks count in Social without joining the instagram.com row, and the comparison is lopsided. Using instagram.com as the source fixes that. And because Virling credits Instagram's in-app browser from its user agent but has no such rule for Threads, untagged app clicks favour Instagram, so tag both. Instagram revenue tracking covers bio links, story stickers and l.instagram.com in detail. If the drop also goes to a Facebook page or group, tracking Facebook posts and groups has the steps for those.

A post often introduces a buyer who later comes back through search or a typed URL. Last-touch models can undercount that. Switch the model to first touch in Site settings and compare the Threads and Instagram rows under both; history is recomputed, and you can switch back. First-touch or last-touch attribution explains when each one fits. If you post on X too, see what your posts on X bring.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Threads looks small but Direct jumped the day a post took off. App clicks on untagged links arrived with no referrer. Tag the profile link and the next post. Links passed on in private messages and chats land in Direct the same way, as covered in dark social revenue tracking.
  • Instagram jumped the day you posted on Threads. Untagged clicks from the Threads app may have been credited to Instagram from the in-app browser's user agent. A utm_source on the link wins over that guess.
  • A Threads campaign shows under Social but not in the Threads row. The tag probably says threads. Use threads.com.
  • A domain you do not recognise shows up in Referral. It is likely a link-in-bio page on another domain. Point the profile link straight at your site, or at a links page on your own domain.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so Threads' real visitor number can be higher than what any analytics tool shows. Revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track sales from Threads?

Count visits with threads.com, threads.net or l.threads.com as the referrer, tag the links in your profile and posts with utm_source=threads.com and a campaign per post, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and use a tool that joins payments to visits. Then read Threads' revenue and revenue per visitor next to your other channels.

Why do clicks from Threads show up as Direct?

Links opened in the Threads app often arrive with no referrer. Virling has no in-app browser rule for Threads, so an untagged click with no referrer is not credited to Threads. A utm_source on the link keeps it credited to Threads.

What utm_source should I use for Threads?

Use threads.com. Virling reads it as the Threads host, so tagged clicks land in the Social channel and in the same Threads row as referrer clicks. threads also counts as Social, but it gets no source row of its own.

What is l.threads.com in my referrers?

It is the address Threads routes outbound links through, so a click can arrive with l.threads.com as the referrer. Virling counts it as Social and merges it, and the older threads.net, into one Threads row with threads.com.

How do I compare Threads with Instagram?

Read the threads.com row next to the instagram.com and l.instagram.com rows in the Source tab, and compare revenue per visitor and paying customers rather than visits. Tag both with a host as the source, threads.com and instagram.com, so tagged clicks join those rows.
