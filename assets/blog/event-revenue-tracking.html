Event revenue tracking: which conferences bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-20.

Event revenue tracking means seeing which visits from a conference or meetup turn into paying customers, not only how many people scanned or clicked. Give every link you put in front of the audience its own UTM tag: the link on your slides, the QR code on your booth and the follow-up email. Carry the visitor ID into checkout, then join each payment to the visits before it. Weeks later you see the revenue each event brought. Badge scans, booth traffic and lead lists do not tell you this.

This guide is for B2B founders who sponsor, speak at or exhibit at conferences and meetups. An event costs a ticket or a sponsorship fee, travel and days away from the product. Whether it paid comes down to one question: did anyone who reached your site from it become a paying customer, and how much were those visitors worth compared with your other channels?

What event visits look like without tags

Nobody clicks a link from a stage. People reach your site after an event in ways that leave little trace of where they heard about you:

  • They scan a QR code on a slide, a banner or a flyer. The phone's camera opens the URL with no referrer, so an untagged code counts as Direct.
  • They type your URL from the last slide or your badge. Also no referrer: Direct.
  • They search your name later. The visit comes from a search engine, so it counts as Organic search.
  • They find you on LinkedIn after the talk. The visit counts as Social, from linkedin.com.

None of these says "the conference". If Direct and branded search rise the week after an event, that is usually the event, but you cannot see which payments came from it. Tags fix the first two cases, because they travel inside the URL. The full rule order is in channels, and why Direct is your biggest traffic source covers the other causes.

Set it up with Virling

1. Tag one link per placement, per event

Pick one name per event, lowercase with hyphens and the month at the end, such as event-saasconf-2026-11. The Campaign tab groups visits and revenue by utm_campaign only, so give each placement its own campaign that starts with the event's name:

  • utm_source=event on every event link, so all of them group under one source. It is not a known social or search source, so with no referrer the visit lands in Referral instead of Direct.
  • utm_medium=event, or another value you keep fixed, for slides and booth. Do not use email or a paid medium such as cpc there: those put the visit in the Email or Paid channel. The follow-up email uses email on purpose.
  • utm_campaign names the event and the placement. Add the month, so next year's edition gets its own rows.

Point each link at a page on your site with the script, such as a landing page for the talk or the booth offer. The UTM builder makes each link for you, and the UTM naming template keeps the values the same across events. For slides and banners, the QR code generator with UTM tags makes the code too. Which UTM parameters to use has more naming rules.

A tagged URL is long for a slide or a printed code. A tracked link keeps it short: in Virling, open Links, give it a name, the page it should open, a source such as saasconf and the Referral channel. You get a short URL on your Virling server, such as https://stats.example.com/l/35b6cac, that adds utm_source, utm_medium=referral and the link's id as utm_campaign on each click, and Links shows its clicks, visitors and revenue. Make one per placement, and do not delete one that is printed: its short URL returns 404 right away. See tracked links, and track QR code sales for printing codes that scan well.

2. Add the script

Put the Virling script in the head of every page, including the pages your event links open, with your domain in data-site. It reads the tags on the landing page and sets the first-party visitor ID that payments need later. See add the script.

Do not point a slide or booth code straight at a Stripe, Polar or Dodo payment link. Opened from a QR code, it carries no visitor ID, so the payment comes in as Unattributed. Send people to a page on your site, and let them go to checkout from there.

3. Connect a payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs the visitor ID in checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links on your site, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. The setup is in link checkout to visits, and each provider has its own page: Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce. All of them are listed under integrations.

Before the event, scan your own code or open the slide link on your phone, then check that the visit shows up with the right campaign.

Read revenue per event weeks later

B2B buyers rarely pay at the booth. They visit, start a trial, maybe book a call, and pay weeks later. Two settings decide whether that payment is credited to the event, both in Site settings. See model and lookback window.

  • The lookback window. Only visits that started inside the window before the payment are considered. The default is 90 days; you can choose 30, 60, 90 or 365. If deals often close more than 90 days after an event, choose 365, or the event visit drops out and the payment goes to a later visit or to Unattributed.
  • The model. With the default, last non-direct, the latest visit that was not Direct gets the credit. Someone who scanned your booth code and later typed your URL to pay is credited to the booth. Someone who scanned your booth code, then clicked your newsletter and paid, is credited to the newsletter. First touch credits the earliest visit in the window, which shows which events start journeys.

Saving either setting rebuilds attribution for all your payments, history included, so you can look at the same event both ways and switch back. First-touch or last-touch attribution explains the trade-off.

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range that starts the day of the event and runs long enough to cover your time to pay.
  2. In the Channels card, switch to the Campaign tab. Each placement is a row with its own visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average. Add up the rows that start with the event's name for the event's total.
  3. Click a row to filter the whole page. The Pages card now shows which pages those visitors landed on.
  4. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

Compare events by revenue and revenue per visitor, not by booth scans. A small meetup that sends a few visitors who are close to buying can be worth more than a large conference. The same method works for a talk you give online; webinar revenue tracking covers registration pages and replays.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • No event rows, but Direct jumped the week of the event. Some links or codes are untagged. Open each one on your phone and look at the address bar.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A customer says they met you at the event, but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may never have used a tagged link, and searched for you or typed your URL instead. No analytics tool can link a conversation at a booth to a visit that carries no tag. Switch to first touch to check whether an earlier tagged visit exists.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all. Their payments still count in your total, as Unattributed.

If you paid to be there, compare the sponsorship fee with the revenue it brought. To count travel and staff days too, the event ROI calculator works out the cost per lead, the customers you need to break even and the ROI.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from a conference or event?

Give every link you put in front of the audience a UTM tag: the slides, the booth QR code and the follow-up email, each with its own utm_campaign that starts with the event's name. Point them at pages on your site with the analytics script, pass the visitor ID into checkout and use a tool that joins payments to visits. The Campaign tab then shows visitors and revenue per link, and adding up one event's rows gives revenue per event.

What UTM tags should I use for an event?

Use one fixed source such as utm_source=event and a fixed medium such as utm_medium=event for the slides and the booth, and utm_medium=email for the follow-up. Put the event and the placement in utm_campaign, such as event-saasconf-2026-11-booth, since the Campaign tab groups revenue by that value.

Why do visits from an event show up as Direct?

People who scan an untagged QR code or type a URL from a slide arrive with no referrer and no tag, so analytics tools count them as Direct. People who search your name after a talk arrive as Organic search. A tagged link or a short tracked link keeps the visit credited to the event.

How long after an event should I wait before judging it?

Long enough to cover your usual time from first visit to first payment. B2B buyers often take a trial or a sales call first, so look again weeks later, and choose a 365-day lookback window if payments can come more than 90 days after the event.

Why is a payment from an event lead credited to another channel?

With the default model, last non-direct, the latest visit that was not Direct gets the credit. If the lead clicked a newsletter or an ad after the event, that later visit wins. Switch to first touch in Site settings to see which event started the journey.
