Google AI Overviews revenue tracking: which AI Overview clicks pay

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-19.

Google AI Overviews revenue tracking starts with one fact: AI Overviews and AI Mode are part of Google Search, so a click on a link in them arrives from google.com, like any other Google result, and counts as Organic search. Nothing in the visit says which part of the results page was clicked, so no analytics tool on your site can split AI Overview or AI Mode clicks from classic results. What you can see is the revenue Google search brings as a whole, by landing page, and the Gemini app on its own, because gemini.google.com counts as AI.

This post is for founders and store owners who see their brand cited in AI Overviews or AI Mode and want to know whether those clicks bring paying customers. For Google search in general, see organic search revenue tracking; for the Gemini app, see Gemini revenue tracking; for AI assistants in general, see is AI search traffic worth anything.

How AI Overview and AI Mode clicks show up

An AI Overview sits on the Google results page and links to pages that support it. AI Mode is a separate tab of Google Search with its own answers and links. In both, the link takes the visitor from Google Search to your page, so the visit carries the same thing a classic result does: a google.com referrer, with no search query and no mark of the AI feature.

  • Counted as Organic search. Virling puts any Google search host, google.com or a country domain such as google.de, in the Organic search channel, and every Google country domain shares one google.com row. An AI Overview click lands in that row.
  • Not counted as AI. The AI channel is for assistants with their own address. The only Google host in it is gemini.google.com, the Gemini app, and it is checked before the search rules, so Gemini visits never merge into the google.com row.
  • Ads stay apart. A paid medium wins over everything. A Google Ads link tagged utm_medium=cpc counts as Paid, even though it also arrives from google.com.

Google's own reports do not split it either. Google's AI features and your website page says sites in AI features are included in the overall search traffic in Search Console, in the Performance report under the Web search type. So if you see your brand cited in an AI Overview, the clicks are inside your Google numbers, mixed with classic results.

The full rule order is in channels. The traffic source checker shows how a given landing URL and referrer are credited: paste any URL with a google.com referrer to see it land in Organic search, then try gemini.google.com to see it land in AI.

What you can and cannot separate

  • You can see Google organic search as its own row, with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, apart from Bing, the Gemini app, ChatGPT and Perplexity.
  • You can see which landing pages Google sends people to and which of them lead to payments. A page that Google cites in AI Overviews and that earns more from Google after it started being cited is a lead worth following, but it is not proof: classic rankings move too.
  • You cannot split AI Overview, AI Mode and classic result clicks, by referrer, by tag or by Search Console. You cannot add a utm_source to the links either, because Google picks the URL it shows.
  • You cannot see the search query. Google sends visitors without it, as organic search revenue tracking explains, so the landing page is the closest stand-in.
  • You cannot tie a visit to an answer someone read without clicking. If they later type your URL or search your name, that visit is Direct or Organic search, with no trace of the AI Overview.

Let Google cite your pages

Google's page says that to be shown as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet, and that there are no additional technical requirements. It also says no special optimizations, files or schema are needed. The same robots.txt rules for Googlebot that control Search control these features.

To limit what Google shows from your pages in Search, Google points to the nosnippet, data-nosnippet, max-snippet and noindex controls. Google-Extended is a separate robots.txt token that Google describes for limiting training and grounding in some of its other systems; Google's page does not say it affects AI Overviews or AI Mode. The AI crawler checker reads your robots.txt and tells you whether Google-Extended and the other AI bots may read a page. The robots.txt generator for AI crawlers writes the rules.

How Virling credits the payment

People often click a result, leave, and come back days later by typing your URL. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the payment goes to the Google visit, as long as it started inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. If the buyer subscribes, every renewal keeps counting toward Google. If they come back through a newsletter or an ad before paying, that later visit takes the credit instead.

To see how often Google starts journeys that another channel finishes, switch the model to first touch in Site settings. First-touch or last-touch attribution explains the difference. Changing the model recalculates every past payment, so you can switch back.

Where AI answers fit in Virling

The Answers tab of the AI card shows how often assistants use and name your site. It does not cover AI Overviews: Google has no API for them, and scraping search results breaks Google's terms. Gemini with Google Search grounding is the closest stand-in, since it searches Google and answers from the results, so the Gemini line on that tab is the nearest signal for whether Google's AI names you. What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Google has visitors but no revenue. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A buyer says they found you in an AI Overview but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have read the overview without clicking, then come back through another channel. That visit has no trace of the AI Overview.
  • Google ad clicks show as Organic search. An ad link with no utm_medium looks the same as an organic click. Add utm_medium=cpc to your ad links.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted, so Google's real visitor number can be higher than any analytics tool shows. Their revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

The same limit applies to AI answers on a Bing results page, which count as Bing search; Microsoft Copilot traffic explains what does count as AI there. For an assistant whose cited links usually do arrive under its own address, see Perplexity referral traffic.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from Google AI Overviews?

Track Google organic search revenue as a whole. Clicks from AI Overviews and AI Mode arrive from google.com like any other Google result, so Virling counts them as Organic search. Pass the visitor ID into checkout and each payment is credited to the Google visit and landing page before it.

Can I separate AI Overview clicks from normal Google results?

No. The visit only says it came from google.com, not which part of the results page was clicked, so no script on your site can tell an AI Overview click from a classic result. Google's own Search Console also counts AI Overviews and AI Mode in the overall Web search traffic.

Is AI Mode traffic counted as AI or as Organic search?

Organic search. AI Mode is part of Google Search, so its clicks arrive from google.com. Only the Gemini app, at gemini.google.com, is counted in the AI channel.

Does Virling show whether I appear in AI Overviews?

No. Google has no API for AI Overviews, and scraping search results breaks Google's terms. The Answers tab of the AI card asks your buyer questions to Gemini with Google Search grounding, which is the closest stand-in.

How do I get my pages into AI Overviews?

Google says a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet, and that there are no additional technical requirements or special optimizations. Make sure robots.txt lets Googlebot crawl the page.
