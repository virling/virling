Lead magnet ROI: do free downloads bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-20.

To measure lead magnet ROI, send a goal each time someone opts in, with a different goal name for each lead magnet, and tag every link that promotes it. Pass the visitor ID into checkout so each payment joins the visits before it. Then filter the visitors who reached a lead magnet's goal down to the ones who paid. You see which lead magnet brings paying customers and which only brings email addresses. A download count or a list growth chart cannot tell those apart.

This is for SaaS founders, course sellers and stores that give away a checklist, a template, a free download or an email course. Each one takes time to make and to promote. Whether it pays comes down to one question: of the people who took it, how many bought something later, and how much?

Why signups are the wrong number

Your email tool counts subscribers. Your payment provider counts customers. Neither knows the other, so a lead magnet that adds hundreds of addresses looks like a win even if none of them ever pays, and a small one that quietly brings buyers looks like a failure.

  • The opt-in and the payment are often weeks apart. The visitor downloads today, reads your emails, and buys later. Most reports look only at the visit just before the payment.
  • Several lead magnets share one list. Once everyone is a subscriber, you can no longer tell which download brought them in.
  • Lead magnets are promoted in many places. A post, a newsletter swap, a pinned tweet, a page on your site. Without tags the clicks blur into Direct and Referral.

The fix is to record the opt-in on the same visitor that later pays. That is what a goal does.

When each lead magnet has its own goal, Virling's Visitors page can filter to the people who reached that goal and then to those who paid, with what each one spent.

Set it up in Virling

  1. Add the script. One tag in the <head> of every page, including the landing page for the lead magnet and its thank-you page. It sets a first-party virling_vid cookie that lasts a year, so a visitor who opts in today and pays next month is the same visitor. See add the script.
  2. Send a goal on the opt-in. Call virling("goal", "lead-pricing-checklist") when the form succeeds. If the form is hosted by your email tool and redirects to a thank-you page on your site, put the call on that page instead. Use one name per lead magnet, such as lead-pricing-checklist and lead-email-course, so each one is counted on its own. Names are cut to 64 bytes. Add the small stub from Goals if your code can run before the script loads.
  3. Tag the links that promote it. Each place you share the lead magnet gets its own tagged link, with the lead magnet in utm_campaign, for example ?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=pricing-checklist. Or open Links in Virling, pick the place and the landing page, and share the short tracked link it makes. A tracked link uses its own id as utm_campaign, so it shows as its own row on the Campaign tab. See tracked links and the UTM builder. To store the tags with the lead too, see capturing UTM parameters in hidden form fields. If the opt-in is a Tally form, Tally form UTM tracking covers its hidden fields and the goal on submit.
  4. Connect a payment provider. Open Setup, then Connect payments, and paste a key from your provider. The last 90 days of payments are imported (60 for Shopify), then synced every minute. Pass the virling_vid cookie into checkout so each payment joins its visitor; Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links and Dodo Payments links get it added by the script on click. Each provider has its own page: Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce. The setup for all of them is in link checkout to visits.

The Funnel tab of the Goals card treats any goal whose name contains signup, sign_up, sign-up or register as the Signup step. If that step should mean product signups only, keep those words out of your lead magnet goal names.

See which lead magnet brings buyers

  1. Pick a range long enough for people to buy after they opt in, such as the last 90 days.
  2. Open the Goals card and switch to the Goals tab. Each lead magnet is a row with how many times it was completed. This is your signup count.
  3. Open the Visitors page and filter by the lead magnet's goal. Then use Filters to keep only visitors who paid. The two lists side by side are the answer: how many took it, and how many of them paid, with what each one spent.
  4. On the Payments page, filter by the same goal to list the payments from people who took it. Open one to see the buyer's journey: the visit where they opted in, the visits after it and the payment, with the credited visit marked.
  5. Switch the Channels card to the Campaign tab. With one campaign per lead magnet, each row shows the visitors and revenue its promotion brought, with revenue per visitor and a chip that compares it with your site average.

Revenue on the Campaign tab is credited by your attribution model, last non-direct by default. If a visitor took the checklist from a newsletter link and later came back from a search, the search gets the credit there, while the goal is still in their journey on the Payments page. To credit the visit that brought them in first, switch the model to first touch in Site settings; first-touch or last-touch attribution explains the difference.

Put a value on each opt-in

Once you know how many people took a lead magnet and how much the ones who paid spent, divide the revenue by the opt-ins. That is what each new subscriber from that lead magnet is worth, and it tells you how much time or ad spend one is worth chasing. The email subscriber value calculator and the lead value calculator do the sums. Free tools work the same way as downloads; do free tools bring paying customers covers that case. A webinar is also an opt-in, with a live session and a replay to follow; see which webinars bring paying customers. A chat on your site can be a goal too; see live chat revenue tracking.

Give it time before you judge. A lead magnet with a few dozen opt-ins and no sales yet is too small a sample, and an email course may only start paying after its last lesson goes out.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Opt-ins but no goal. The form may be on another domain, or the call runs before the script loads. Fire the goal on a thank-you page on your own site, and add the stub from Goals.
  • Buyers you know took the download do not show the goal. They may have opted in on one device and paid on another, or cleared cookies. The visitor ID lives in one browser, so those journeys cannot be joined.
  • Payments but no revenue on the visitors. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If much of your revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • Promotion clicks land in Direct. Some links are untagged, or opened in an app that sends no referrer. Tag every link, or use a tracked link.

Frequently asked questions

How do I measure lead magnet ROI?

Send a goal when someone opts in, with one goal name per lead magnet, and tag every link that promotes it. Connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Then filter visitors or payments by the goal to see who went on to pay and how much.

Do lead magnets work?

Some do and some only collect email addresses. The download count cannot tell you which. Compare the visitors who opted in for each lead magnet with the ones who later paid, and keep the ones that lead to payments.

How do I track a lead magnet signup as a conversion?

Call virling("goal", "lead-pricing-checklist") when the opt-in form succeeds, or on the thank-you page if the form is hosted by your email tool. The goal is stored with the visitor, so it shows up in their journey next to any later payment.

What if the buyer pays weeks after downloading?

The visitor ID cookie lasts a year in the same browser, so the opt-in and the payment stay on one visitor. Credit for the payment follows your attribution model and lookback window, 90 days by default, and the goal stays in the buyer's journey either way.

Can I see revenue per lead magnet on one card?

Not yet. The Goals card shows how many times each goal was completed. To see the money, filter the Visitors or Payments page by the goal, or give each lead magnet its own campaign and read revenue per visitor on the Campaign tab.
