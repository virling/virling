LinkedIn Ads revenue tracking: see which campaign brings paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-24.

LinkedIn Ads revenue tracking means joining each payment to the LinkedIn ad click that brought the buyer. Add utm_source=linkedin, utm_medium=paid_social and a utm_campaign to your ads, add the Virling script to your site, and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Virling counts the click as Paid and credits the payment to it, so the Campaign tab shows revenue per campaign. Two things are different for B2B: the payment can come months after the click, and a lead form filled in on LinkedIn never sends a visit at all.

Step 1: tag the ad

Campaign Manager can add URL parameters to your ads for you. Set these as static parameters, with your own campaign name:

utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=paid_social&utm_campaign=demo-cfo-q4

LinkedIn’s help page says static parameters support any UTM parameter you choose, at the account, campaign or ad level, with letters, numbers and underscores in keys, and plus signs and hyphens as well in values. Parameters from the three levels are combined on the final link, so check that the same key is not set twice (source: LinkedIn, URL tracking parameters in Campaign Manager). The source and medium can be shared by the whole account; give each campaign its own utm_campaign.

  • Dynamic parameters. LinkedIn can also fill in values such as {{CAMPAIGN_ID}}. The help page shows it under the key campaign_id, which Virling does not read. LinkedIn’s API docs show utm_campaign filled with CAMPAIGN_NAME (source: LinkedIn, Dynamic UTM Tracking). Either way, the value must be under utm_campaign to show as a campaign row, and a typed name does that without it.
  • Mind the rename. The help page says the names of the levels changed starting in October 2025: the new dynamic parameters list ad sets, the old ones list campaign groups. Check what a campaign means in your account before you compare old rows with new ones.
  • Keep one name. A campaign tagged two ways becomes two rows.
  • Tag message ads by hand. The API docs say dynamic tracking does not support Conversation Ads and Message Ads. Put the full tagged URL in the message.

The free UTM builder makes tagged links for you. If you use dynamic parameters, the LinkedIn ads dynamic UTM builder writes the string to paste in Campaign Manager.

How Virling sorts a LinkedIn ad visit

Every session gets one channel from its first pageview, and the first matching rule wins:

  • A paid medium. cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social or paidsocial, in any case, is Paid. It is checked first, so a tagged click from linkedin.com is Paid, not Social.
  • No paid medium, with a referrer. linkedin.com and lnkd.in are Social, so the ad is mixed in with your posts.
  • No referrer, with a source tag. utm_source=linkedin is Social. A value that is not a known source is Referral.
  • No referrer and no tag. The visit is Social when the LinkedIn app names itself, and Direct when it does not.

Spelling counts. paid-social with a hyphen and sponsored are not on the list, so those clicks are not Paid. See how channels are decided. For unpaid posts and profile links, see LinkedIn revenue tracking.

What about the li_fat_id?

LinkedIn says that turning on first-party cookies adds a li_fat_id parameter to the URLs of your ads (source: LinkedIn, Troubleshoot implementing first-party cookies). It is for LinkedIn’s own reports. Virling does not store it or use it to pick the channel. It only checks whether it is there, so a Direct visit can be labelled a shared link. It does not name your campaign, so leave it on and add UTM tags.

Step 2: add the script

Add the Virling script to the <head> of every page an ad can land on, and to your app. It reads the tags on the landing page and sets the first-party visitor ID. Redirects on your site must keep the query string; see UTM parameters lost after redirect. The setup is in the script docs.

Step 3: link checkout

Connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout, so each payment can be matched to a visit. Payment links from Stripe, Polar and Dodo Payments get the ID from the script when they are on a page that runs it; a checkout you create on your server needs the virling_vid cookie. See link checkout to visits.

If deals close with a Stripe invoice, there is no checkout to carry the ID. Send the buyer’s email as an identify event when they fill in the demo or signup form on your own site, and a later Stripe payment with the same payer email is credited to that visitor. Email matching works for Stripe, Shopify and WooCommerce only. See Stripe invoice attribution.

Step 4: read payments per campaign

  • In the Channels card, click Paid. The page filters to visits with a paid medium, from LinkedIn and any other platform you tag the same way.
  • Open the Campaign tab. Each utm_campaign is a row with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  • Open a payment from the Payments page to see the visits before it, with the credited one marked.
  • Check Time to pay before you pause a campaign that looks weak.

To see profit, type what you spend on ads each month in Site settings, under Monthly spend per channel, such as Paid 2000. The Profit by channel card then shows spend, revenue and profit for the Paid channel as a whole, so include every platform you tag as Paid. Nothing connects to an ad platform, and the result is only as good as the amount you type.

Long sales cycles

A B2B buyer may click an ad, read your docs a month later, book a call and pay the month after that. Two settings decide whether the ad still gets the credit.

  • The lookback window. Only visits inside it count. The default is 90 days, and you can choose 30, 60, 90 or 365. If your deals take longer than three months, choose 365. See how long a visit gets credit.
  • The model. Last non-direct, the default, credits the latest visit that was not Direct, so a later visit from your newsletter takes the credit from the ad. First touch credits the earliest visit in the window, which shows the campaigns that introduce buyers. Compare both; saving a change rebuilds the history, and you can switch back.

Judge a campaign by the customers it has had time to bring. Last month’s clicks may not have paid yet, so compare older campaigns with each other. A buyer who clicks on a phone and pays on a work laptop is two visitors unless something joins them; see cross-device attribution. Nor is the person who clicks always the person who pays.

Lead forms that never send a visit

A LinkedIn Lead Gen Form opens from the ad and is filled in on LinkedIn, so your landing page never loads, the script never runs, and Virling has nothing to credit. If that lead pays and never visited your site, the payment is Unattributed: it counts toward revenue, but no campaign gets it. What you can do:

  • Tag the confirmation link. LinkedIn lets you add a landing page or a booking link after the form (source: LinkedIn, Lead Gen Forms). Point it at your site with the same tags as the ad. Those who click become Paid visits.
  • Tag the follow-up email. With utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=email and the same utm_campaign, a click is in the Email channel and keeps the campaign name.
  • Get one form on your own site. If leads book the demo on your page, the visit and the email can both be joined to the payment.
  • Keep the campaign in your CRM. Hidden fields on the form can hold the campaign, ad set and ad ID or name (source: LinkedIn, Lead Gen Form hidden fields). Virling does not read them, but they let you check by hand.

To compare fairly, do not set a lead form campaign against a website campaign on Virling revenue alone. The first will look worse than it is.

A made-up example

These numbers are invented to show the arithmetic. Two website campaigns each cost $2,000, as read in Campaign Manager. Campaign A has 250 visitors and 5 customers who paid $1,200 each, so $6,000 of revenue, $24.00 per visitor and $400 of spend per customer. Campaign B has 500 visitors and 2 customers who paid $1,200 each, so $2,400 of revenue, $4.80 per visitor and $1,000 per customer. At a 70% margin with one $1,200 payment per customer, the break-even cost per customer is $840, so A is above water and B is not, even though B brought twice the visitors. Work out your own limit with the break-even CPA calculator.

What to check if the numbers look off

  • Ad revenue shows as Social. The ad has no paid utm_medium. Click your own ad preview and check the address bar.
  • Ad revenue shows as Direct. The tags were dropped by a redirect, or the ad has none and the app did not name itself.
  • A campaign row is missing. Its ads have no utm_campaign. A campaign_id parameter alone is not read.
  • Campaign Manager shows more clicks than Virling shows visitors. LinkedIn lists reasons, such as chargeable clicks that do not go to the landing page, reports in GMT, and browsers that block scripts or cookies (source: LinkedIn, Third-party reporting doesn’t match Campaign Manager).
  • Revenue shows as Unattributed. The visitor ID is not reaching checkout, the visit is older than the window, or the lead never visited your site.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which LinkedIn ad campaign brought a paying customer?

Add utm_source=linkedin, utm_medium=paid_social and a utm_campaign to your ads, add the Virling script to your site, and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Each payment is credited to the tagged visit, and the Campaign tab shows revenue per campaign.

Which channel does a LinkedIn ad click land in?

Paid, if the link carries utm_medium=paid_social, paidsocial, paid, cpc, ppc or paidsearch. The medium is checked first, so it wins over a linkedin.com referrer. Without a paid medium the click is Social when the referrer, the source tag or the in-app browser says LinkedIn, and Direct when nothing does.

Can Virling track a LinkedIn lead form?

Not the form itself. It is filled in on LinkedIn, so your site gets no visit. If the lead later opens a link to your site, such as the landing page on the confirmation screen, that visit is tracked. Tag the link so it is credited to the campaign.

Does Virling read li_fat_id?

Only to see whether it is there: a Direct visit with a li_fat_id can be labelled a shared link. Virling does not store its value or use it to pick the channel. It can stay on the link for LinkedIn.

What attribution window should I use for LinkedIn Ads?

One longer than your usual sales cycle. Virling looks back 90 days by default and offers 30, 60, 90 or 365. If deals take more than three months from the first click, choose 365. Saving the setting rebuilds attribution for past payments too.

Does Virling connect to my LinkedIn ad account?

No. The setup on this page uses only the tags on your ads, the Paid channel and your payments. You can type what you spend on Paid each month in Site settings, and the Profit by channel card uses that amount.
