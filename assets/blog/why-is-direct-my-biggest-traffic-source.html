Why is Direct your biggest traffic source? What it hides

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-02.

TL;DR: Direct is not a channel, it is the bucket for every visit that carried no clue about where it came from. A big Direct share usually means untagged links, apps that hide the referrer, and returning buyers. Tag your links, look at how Direct visitors arrived, and credit sales with a last non-direct view so the channel that found the buyer gets the revenue.

What lands in Direct

What it isWhy it has no sourceWhat to do
Typed addresses and bookmarksThe visitor already knows you. This is real Direct, and often a return visit by someone another channel broughtCheck which channel the same person came from first
Shared linksChats, documents, PDFs and QR codes pass no referrerTag the links you share on purpose
Email and SMSMany mail and messaging apps send no referrer, so an untagged campaign looks like DirectAdd utm_medium=email to every campaign link
Apps that open links in their own browserThe visit arrives with no referrer. Some apps name themselves in the user agent and can be recognised, others open a plain system browser and cannotTag bio and story links
Returns from checkoutA buyer who leaves for a payment page and comes back arrives with no referrerDo not count these as a source of their own

Why the last click gets this wrong

Take a founder who finds you in a Hacker News thread on Monday, reads two pages, and buys on Friday by typing your address. A last-click report says Direct. A last non-direct report says Hacker News, because it skips the Direct visit and credits the latest visit that had a real source. If every visit in the window was Direct, the payment stays Direct.

First touch is the other useful view: it credits the earliest visit in the window, so you see which channels introduce people who later pay. Reading both side by side shows which channels find buyers and which only close them. More on the models in first touch vs last touch for SaaS.

How to shrink Direct and read what is left

  1. Tag every link you control with utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign. The rules are in which UTM parameters to use.
  2. Pick a last non-direct model, so a bookmark visit does not erase the channel that came before it.
  3. Look at revenue per channel, not visits. A small Direct share of visits can still carry a large share of payments.
  4. Split what remains. Typed or bookmarked, shared link and return from checkout need different fixes, and only the first is a sign of a known brand.

Virling uses last non-direct by default with a 90-day lookback, and you can switch to first touch or last touch in site settings. It also splits Direct by how visitors arrived on the Channels tab, and shows revenue next to each channel. See the model docs. For store orders, read why Shopify orders show up as Direct.

How to spot each cause and fix it

CauseHow to spot itHow to fix it
Untagged emailDirect rises in the hours after a send, on the pages the email links toPut utm_source and utm_medium=email on every link, including automated emails and receipts
Chat appsDirect visits land on deep pages nobody would type, such as a post or a docs pageTag the links you share, and put a tagged link behind your share and copy buttons. Links other people paste stay partly hidden
App browsersDirect visits from phones rise on the day you post. Opened on your phone, your own link shows no referrerTag bio, story and post links, so the source does not depend on the app naming itself
QR codesDirect visits from phones on the page the code opens, around an event or a mailingPut the tags in the address inside the code, and point it at a page with your script
RedirectsA tagged link reaches the page without its tags in the address barKeep the query string in every redirect: http to https, www, trailing slash and short links
Referrer policyA site you know links to you never shows up as a referrerRemove no-referrer and same-origin from your own redirects. You cannot change another site, so ask for a tagged link
Missing script on a pageA page that gets visitors is absent from your landing pages. The visit is first seen on the next page, whose referrer is your own domainAdd the script to every page and template, including landing pages, the blog and checkout
Checkout on another domainThank-you or account pages show up as Direct landing pages, or payments have no visit before themPass the visitor ID into checkout, and stop a return from checkout from starting a new visit

A ten-minute checklist

Work in this order. The first steps show where to look, the rest test one cause each.

  1. Read Direct by landing page. The homepage, login and pricing are mostly typed or bookmarked. Deep pages are mostly shared links, which dark social traffic covers.
  2. Compare days. Match each jump in Direct with what you sent, posted or printed that day. That names the links to check next.
  3. Check those links. Paste your latest email, bio and QR code links into the UTM checker and fix missing or misspelled tags.
  4. Follow the redirects. Run curl -sIL on one tagged link, in quotes, and confirm that every Location line still has the tags. If one does not, see UTM parameters lost after redirect.
  5. Check the referrer. Look for a referrer-policy line in the same output, then click a plain link to your site from a page on another domain and read document.referrer in the console. If it is empty, see why document.referrer is empty.
  6. Look for pages without the script. Open a landing page, a blog post, pricing and the page that opens checkout, and confirm in the network tab that the script loads on each.
  7. Classify one visit. Paste a landing address and its referrer into the traffic source checker. If it says Direct, the visit carried nothing to credit.
  8. Make a test purchase. In a private window, open a tagged link and pay in your provider's test mode. If the sale does not show that source, see cross-domain checkout attribution.

A fix changes new visits only. Visits already recorded as Direct keep that channel, so compare the weeks before and after.

Direct against Unattributed

In Virling these are two different things. Direct is a channel: there was a visit, and it carried no referrer, tag or app name that the channel rules recognise. Unattributed is a payment that is not credited to any visit. It still counts toward revenue, but it belongs to no channel.

DirectUnattributed
What it describesA visit, and the payments credited to itA payment with no visit to credit
Usual causeNo referrer, no tag and no known appNo visitor ID reached checkout, or the visitor has no visit inside the lookback window
Where to fix itLinks, redirects and the script on every pageThe checkout code, the lookback window, or matching by email where your provider supports it

So tags shrink Direct but do nothing for a checkout that carries no visitor ID, and a better checkout does not name the source of an untagged visit. The Unattributed docs list the causes for each payment provider.

Frequently asked questions

Why is Direct my biggest traffic source?

Because Direct is what is left when a visit has no referrer, UTM tag or known app. Untagged email, chat links, some apps and returning bookmarks all end up there.

Is Direct traffic people typing my URL?

Only partly. Typed addresses and bookmarks are real Direct, but shared links, in-app browsers and returns from checkout land there too. Virling splits Direct by how visitors arrived so you can tell them apart.

How do I reduce Direct traffic?

Tag every link you control with UTM parameters, and read a last non-direct view so a return visit by bookmark does not take the credit from the channel that brought the buyer.

Does Direct traffic mean my marketing is not working?

No. Often it means the first visit was credited elsewhere and the buyer came back later. Look at the channel of the first visit before you judge a channel by its last click.

What is last non-direct attribution?

A model that credits a payment to the latest visit in the lookback window that was not Direct. If every visit was Direct, it credits the latest Direct visit.
