TikTok Ads revenue tracking: see which campaign brings paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-24.

TikTok Ads revenue tracking means joining each payment on your site to the TikTok ad click that brought the buyer. Add utm_source=tiktok, utm_medium=paid_social and a utm_campaign to the destination URL of your ads, add the Virling script to your site, and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Virling counts the click as Paid and credits the payment to it, so the Campaign tab shows payments and revenue per campaign. Ads Manager shows the purchases TikTok attributes to its ads; your payment provider shows the money you received.

Step 1: tag the ad

Put the tags on the destination URL of each ad:

https://yourstore.com/products/desk-lamp?utm_source=tiktok&utm_medium=paid_social&utm_campaign=__CAMPAIGN_ID__

TikTok fills in the part between the double underscores. Its help page lists these macros (source: TikTok, About UTM Parameters):

  • Campaign. __CAMPAIGN_ID__ and __CAMPAIGN_NAME__.
  • Ad group. __AID__ and __AID_NAME__.
  • Creative. __CID__ and __CID_NAME__.
  • Ad, in upgraded Smart+ campaigns. __ADID_V2__ and __ADID_V2_NAME__. The page notes that the macros differ there, so check the list before you reuse an older tag.
  • Placement. __PLACEMENT__, such as TikTok or Pangle.

Put the campaign ID in utm_campaign if you rename campaigns, or type a plain name such as spring-sale if you want rows that are easy to read. Pick one style and keep it, because a campaign tagged two ways becomes two rows. You can add utm_content=__CID__, which Virling stores with the pageview, but reports group by campaign. The free UTM builder makes tagged URLs for you.

TikTok can also add tags for you. Its Auto-attach setting, under Edit next to the ad preview, fills in the campaign source (TikTok), the medium (“Paid”), an ID and a name (source: TikTok, How to add URL parameters). Paid is a medium Virling knows in any case, so those clicks are Paid. The page does not say what happens when you also type your own tags, so use one or the other and read the URL in the preview before you publish.

What about the ttclid?

TikTok describes the click ID as a tracking parameter appended to the landing page URL when someone clicks an ad on TikTok (source: TikTok, About TikTok Click ID). It arrives as the ttclid parameter and is useful to TikTok. Virling does not store it or use it to pick the channel. It only checks whether it is there, so a Direct visit can be labelled a shared link. It does not name your campaign, so leave it on and add UTM tags. More on click IDs is in click IDs and UTM parameters.

How Virling sorts a TikTok ad visit

Every session gets one channel from its first pageview, and the first matching rule wins:

  • A paid medium. cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social or paidsocial, in any case, is Paid. It is checked first, so a tagged click from tiktok.com is Paid, not Social.
  • No paid medium, with a referrer. tiktok.com and its subdomains are Social, so the ad is mixed in with your posts.
  • No referrer, with a source tag. tiktok, in any case, is Social. A value that is not a known source, such as tiktok-ads, is Referral.
  • No referrer and no tag. The visit is Social when the in-app browser names TikTok, and Direct when it does not.

Spelling counts. paid-social with a hyphen and cpm are not on the list, so those clicks are not Paid. See how channels are decided. Keep paid media off creators’ own links, or their visits count as Paid too; influencer revenue tracking has tags for those.

The in-app browser and the visitor ID

Ad clicks can open inside the TikTok app, and links opened in an app often arrive with no referrer. TikTok’s own help page says its in-app browser can stop an analytics tool from logging the proper referral channel (source: TikTok, conversion discrepancies). Tags travel in the URL, so a tagged ad is Paid either way.

The visitor ID is the other half. The script sets it in a first-party cookie in the browser that opened the page, and an in-app browser can keep its cookies apart from the phone’s main browser. A buyer who taps the ad in TikTok and pays in that same browser keeps one ID, and the payment goes to the ad. A buyer who comes back later in the phone’s own browser, or on a laptop, can have a second ID, and the payment is credited to the visits of the one that reached checkout. See cross-device attribution for what can be joined.

Step 2: add the script

Add the Virling script to the <head> of every page the ad can land on. It reads the tags on the landing page and sets the first-party visitor ID. Redirects on your site must keep the query string; see UTM parameters lost after redirect. The setup is in the script docs.

Step 3: link checkout

Connect your store or payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout, so each payment can be matched to a visit. On Shopify and WooCommerce, a short snippet stores the ID on the order. Payment links from Stripe, Polar, Dodo Payments and LemonSqueezy get the ID from the script when they are on a page that runs it; a checkout you create on your server needs the virling_vid cookie. See link checkout to visits.

Under the default model, the whole payment, after refunds, is credited to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct. Someone who clicks your ad on Monday and types your address on Thursday in the same browser is still credited to the ad. See the attribution model.

Step 4: read payments per campaign

  • In the Channels card, click Paid. The page filters to visits with a paid medium, from TikTok and any other platform you tag the same way.
  • Open the Campaign tab. Each utm_campaign is a row with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  • Open a payment from the Payments page to see the visits before it.
  • Check Time to pay before you pause a campaign that looks weak.

To see profit, type what you spend on ads each month in Site settings, under Monthly spend per channel, such as Paid 900. The Profit by channel card then shows spend, revenue and profit for the Paid channel as a whole, so include every platform you tag as Paid. The result is only as good as the amount you type.

A made-up example

These numbers are invented to show the arithmetic. Two campaigns each cost $450, as read in Ads Manager. Campaign A has 300 visitors and 10 orders of $60, so $600 of revenue, $2.00 per visitor and $45 of spend per order. Campaign B has 1,200 visitors and 6 orders of $60, so $360 of revenue, $0.30 per visitor and $75 per order. At an 80% margin with one $60 order per customer, the break-even cost per customer is $48, so A is above water and B is not, even though B brought more visitors. Work out your own limit with the break-even CPA calculator.

Why TikTok’s purchase count differs from payments

  • Different event. TikTok counts purchase events that reach it and that it matches to an ad. A payment is recorded by your provider, after the charge, and Virling takes refunds off.
  • Views can count. An ad group’s settings have a click window and a view window, such as 7 days after a click and 1 day after a view, and the view window can be off (source: TikTok, attribution windows for an ad group). A view with no click never reaches your site, so it cannot be credited to a visit.
  • Different dates. TikTok reports a conversion at the time of the ad impression that led to it, and a different time zone can move it again (source: TikTok, conversion discrepancies). Virling dates a payment by when it was made.
  • Different matching. The same page says TikTok uses email, phone, IP, user agent and other techniques. Virling uses the visitor ID that reached checkout.
  • Different credit. Virling looks back 90 days by default and gives a payment to one visit, from any channel. If the buyer clicked an ad and later came from your newsletter, the default model credits the newsletter, while TikTok can still count the ad.

Use Ads Manager to run the ads and the payments to decide which campaigns to keep. The same setup for Facebook and Instagram is in Meta Ads revenue tracking, and for other video ads in YouTube Ads revenue tracking.

What to check if the numbers look off

  • Ad revenue shows as Social. The ad has no paid utm_medium. Click a live ad and check the address bar.
  • Ad revenue shows as Direct. The tags were dropped by a redirect, or the ad has none and the app did not name itself.
  • A campaign row shows underscores. If __CAMPAIGN_ID__ appears as typed, check the spelling, the capitals and the two underscores on each side.
  • One campaign has two rows. Some ads use the ID and others a name, or Auto-attach is on for only some of them.
  • Revenue shows as Unattributed. The visitor ID is not reaching checkout.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which TikTok ad campaign brought a paying customer?

Add utm_source=tiktok, utm_medium=paid_social and utm_campaign=__CAMPAIGN_ID__ to the destination URL of your ads. Connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Each payment is credited to the tagged visit, and the Campaign tab shows revenue per campaign.

Which TikTok macros can I use in a destination URL?

TikTok lists __CAMPAIGN_ID__, __CAMPAIGN_NAME__, __AID__ and __AID_NAME__ for the ad group, __CID__ and __CID_NAME__ for the creative, and __PLACEMENT__. Upgraded Smart+ campaigns also have __ADID_V2__ and __ADID_V2_NAME__ for the ad. Put the one you want to report on in utm_campaign.

Which channel does a TikTok ad click land in?

Paid, if the link carries utm_medium=paid_social, paidsocial, paid, cpc, ppc or paidsearch. The medium is checked first, so it wins over a tiktok.com referrer and over the TikTok app. Without a paid medium, the click is usually Social, or Direct if nothing names TikTok.

Does Virling read the ttclid?

Only to see whether it is there: a Direct visit with a ttclid can be labelled a shared link. Virling does not store its value or use it to pick the channel or the campaign. The ttclid can stay on the link for TikTok.

Why does TikTok report a different number of purchases than my payment provider?

TikTok counts the purchase events it attributes to its ads within its click and view windows, and reports them by the time of the ad impression. A payment is money your provider recorded. The two count different things, so they will rarely match.

Do I need to connect my TikTok ad account?

No. The setup on this page uses only the tags on your ads, the Paid channel and your payments. You can type what you spend on Paid each month in Site settings, and the Profit by channel card uses that amount.
