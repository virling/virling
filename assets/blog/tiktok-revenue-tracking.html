TikTok revenue tracking: which videos bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

To see which TikTok visits turn into paying customers, tag the link in your TikTok bio with utm_source=tiktok and a campaign, add the tracking script to your site, and carry the visitor ID into checkout so each payment is joined to the visit that came before it. You then see the revenue and revenue per visitor TikTok brings. Views, likes and profile visits in TikTok's own analytics do not tell you this.

Most TikTok posts have no clickable link, so nearly every visit comes through one place: the website link in your bio, or a link-in-bio page it points to. That makes TikTok easy to tag and easy to get wrong.

Why TikTok visits lose the referrer

There are three ways a visit gets marked as TikTok:

  • The referrer. A click on tiktok.com in a desktop browser usually arrives with tiktok.com as the referrer, which is in the Social channel. The referrer is a host only, so it cannot tell you which video or profile the click came from.
  • The in-app browser. Most TikTok viewing happens in the mobile app, and a link tapped there opens in TikTok's in-app browser. Those visits often arrive with no referrer, so most tools count them as Direct. When the in-app browser names TikTok in its user agent, Virling credits the visit to tiktok.com in Social instead. If the viewer chooses to open the link in their phone's own browser, nothing names TikTok and an untagged visit stays Direct.
  • A UTM tag. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, and tiktok is a known Social source. The tag travels in the link itself, so it survives the in-app browser, the system browser and a missing referrer.

A referrer or a utm_source always wins over the in-app browser guess. The full rule order is in channels, and why Direct is your biggest traffic source covers the other causes.

Bio links and link-in-bio pages

Where your bio link points decides what you can see:

  • Straight to your site. The simplest setup. The tagged bio link lands on your page and the visit counts as TikTok in Social.
  • A link-in-bio page on your own domain. Also fine. Tag the bio link that opens the page; the page is the first pageview of the visit, and clicks from it to your other pages are same-site, so the visit stays TikTok.
  • A link-in-bio page hosted on another domain. A click from that page to your site arrives with the page's host as the referrer. The referrer wins over utm_source, so the visit counts as Referral under that host, not as TikTok. Tag each button on the page with its own utm_campaign anyway: the campaign is stored with every visit, so the Campaign tab still shows revenue per button.

If TikTok is a channel you want to compare with others, point the bio link at your own site or at a link page on your own domain.

Set up TikTok revenue tracking with Virling

  1. Tag the bio link. Use utm_source=tiktok, utm_medium=social and a utm_campaign that names what the link promotes, lowercase with hyphens. Use utm_content for the spot, such as bio. The UTM builder makes the link for you, and which UTM parameters to use has more naming rules.
  2. Or use a tracked link. In Virling, open Links, set the source to tiktok.com, the channel to Social and the page it should open. The short link adds the tags on the click and Links shows its clicks, visitors and revenue. See tracked links.
  3. Add the script. Put the Virling tag in the <head> of every page. The setup screen in the app shows the exact tag for your site. See add the script.
  4. Connect your payment provider. Revenue needs the visitor ID in checkout. With Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds it on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Each provider has its own page: Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce, all listed on integrations. The setup for all of them is in link checkout to visits.

A tagged bio link looks like this:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=tiktok&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=spring-offer&utm_content=bio

Viewers often watch, leave, and come back later by typing your URL. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the payment goes to the tagged TikTok visit, as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. If they subscribe, every renewal keeps counting toward TikTok.

See which TikTok visits pay

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more, since a video can keep sending profile visits long after it goes out.
  2. In the Channels card, open Social, or switch to the Source tab, and find TikTok. The row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click the row to filter the whole page. The Pages card now shows which landing pages TikTok visitors arrived on.
  4. Switch to the Campaign tab to split TikTok by bio link campaign, or by link-in-bio button if you tagged them.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

Because the bio link carries most clicks, the tag cannot say which video a viewer watched before tapping it. When you start a new series or offer, change the bio link's utm_campaign and compare the periods.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • TikTok looks small but Direct jumped the day a video took off. The bio link is untagged and viewers opened it in their phone's own browser. Tag it.
  • TikTok is missing but a link-in-bio host shows up in Referral. Your bio points at a link page on another domain. Its referrer wins over the tag; see above.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A buyer says they found you on TikTok but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have watched without tapping and searched for you later. No analytics tool can count a view that never reached your site. Switch the model to first touch in Site settings to see which channels start journeys; first-touch or last-touch attribution explains the difference.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so TikTok's real visitor number can be higher than what any analytics tool shows. Revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from TikTok?

Tag the link in your TikTok bio with utm_source=tiktok and a utm_campaign, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and use a tool that joins payments to visits. The TikTok row then shows the visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor TikTok brings.

Why do TikTok visits show up as Direct?

Links opened in TikTok's in-app browser often arrive with no referrer. Virling credits them to TikTok when the in-app browser names TikTok in its user agent, but a visit opened in a plain system browser does not, so an untagged one is Direct. Adding utm_source=tiktok to the link keeps it credited to TikTok either way.

Does a link-in-bio page break TikTok tracking?

It changes the source. A visit from a link-in-bio page on another domain arrives with that page's host as the referrer, and the referrer wins over utm_source, so it counts as Referral under that host. The utm_campaign on each button is still stored, so the Campaign tab still shows revenue per button.

Can I see which TikTok video sent a paying customer?

Only with your own tags. Most TikTok posts have no clickable link, so the bio link carries the visit, and neither the referrer nor the tag can tell which video the viewer watched first. Change the bio link's utm_campaign when you start a new series or offer, and compare the periods.

Should TikTok ads use the same tags?

Use utm_source=tiktok with a paid medium such as cpc or paid_social, so ad clicks land in the Paid channel and organic posts stay in Social.
