Do app marketplace listings bring paying customers? How to track it

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-21.

To see whether visits from an app marketplace listing turn into paying customers, tag the website link on the listing with UTM tags, add a tracking script to your own site, carry the visitor ID into your checkout, and join each payment to the visit that came before it. Then compare paying customers and revenue per visitor for each listing over 90 days. Installs, ratings and listing views do not tell you this, because they are counted on the marketplace, not on your site.

If you sell a Shopify app, a WordPress plugin, a Chrome extension, a Slack app or a Zapier integration, the listing is often the first place a buyer meets your product. Many of them then visit your website for pricing, docs or a paid plan. This post covers that path: a click from the listing to your site, and a payment through your own checkout.

One limit first. If the marketplace bills the customer itself, as with Shopify app subscriptions charged through Shopify's billing, the payment never passes through your checkout and carries no visitor ID, so no tool on your site can join it to a visit. The method below works for payments you take with your own payment provider.

How Virling classifies marketplace referrers

Virling gives every session one channel, from its first pageview. When a click arrives with a referrer, the referrer host decides the channel, and a utm_source cannot change it. Only a utm_medium such as cpc or email is checked first. Here is where the common marketplaces land, following the rules in channels:

  • Shopify App Store (apps.shopify.com): not on a known host list, so Referral, shown as apps.shopify.com in the Source tab.
  • WordPress.org plugin directory (wordpress.org): Referral, shown as wordpress.org. Clicks from the support forums on the same domain share that row.
  • Chrome Web Store (chromewebstore.google.com): every host with google. in it is a search host, so these clicks land in Organic search and are grouped with Google in the Source tab. Only a utm_campaign on the link can split them out.
  • Slack Marketplace (slack.com): slack.com is a known social host, so these clicks land in Social under Slack, in the same row as links people share in Slack messages. A link opened in the Slack app with no referrer is also credited to Slack.
  • Zapier app directory (zapier.com): Referral, shown as zapier.com.

The referrer is a host only, so it cannot tell your listing page apart from any other page on the same marketplace. You can paste a listing link into the traffic source checker to see where it lands.

When a marketplace sends no referrer

Some clicks arrive with no referrer at all: a marketplace can set a policy that hides it, and links opened from inside an installed app, such as an admin screen, an extension popup or a settings page, often carry none. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, and when that is missing it reads ref. With no referrer and no tag, the visit is Direct, and the listing gets no credit for it.

The fix is the same for every marketplace: put UTM tags on the website link of the listing. The UTM builder makes each link:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=shopifyappstore&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=shopifyappstore
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=wordpressorg&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=wordpressorg
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=chromewebstore&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=chromewebstore
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=slackmarketplace&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=slackmarketplace
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=zapier&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=zapier
  • utm_source names the marketplace, lowercase, one word. None of these is a known source, so a tagged visit with no referrer lands in Referral. Avoid plain slack or google, which are known Social and search sources.
  • utm_medium=referral keeps the channel decided by the referrer or source. Do not use cpc unless you paid for the placement and want it counted as Paid.
  • utm_campaign repeats the marketplace's name, so each listing is one row in the Campaign tab whatever the referrer did. That row is how you split Chrome Web Store clicks from Google search and Slack Marketplace clicks from Slack messages.

Use a separate campaign for links inside the product itself, such as an upgrade button in a WordPress admin screen or a Chrome extension popup, so the listing and the installed app are two rows. If a marketplace strips query strings from the URL you submit, use a tracked link: in Virling, open Links, set the source to the marketplace's name, the channel to Referral, and the page it should open. The short link adds the tags on the click, and its id becomes the campaign. See tracked links. Plugin and theme sellers can find more on this in plugin sales attribution.

Set up Virling

  1. Add the script. Put the Virling tag in the <head> of every page, including the pages your listings link to. See add the script.
  2. Connect a payment provider. Virling reads payments from Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce; all of them are on the integrations page.
  3. Pass the visitor ID into checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. See link checkout to visits. Without this step you get visits but no revenue.
  4. Update your listings. Replace the plain website URL on each listing with its tagged link, then click one yourself and check that the visit shows on the dashboard with the right source and campaign.

Read the numbers after 90 days

A buyer who finds you on a marketplace often installs the free version first and pays weeks later. With the default model, last non-direct, a payment goes to the latest visit that was not Direct inside the lookback window, 90 days by default, so a buyer who clicked your listing and later typed your URL is still credited to the listing. See the model and lookback window.

  1. Pick Last 90 days in the date picker, or a custom range that starts when you tagged your listings.
  2. On the Channels card, switch to the Campaign tab. Each listing you tagged is a row with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, and a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click a listing's row to filter the whole page. The New customers number at the top is now the paying customers that listing brought in the range.
  4. Switch to the Source tab and open Referral, Social and Organic search to see untagged clicks by host.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

A listing with paying customers and revenue per visitor near or above your average is worth keeping up to date. One with visitors and no customers after 90 days is sending people who install but do not buy on your site. The same method for SaaS directories and launch sites is in do software directories bring paying customers. If developers also install your product from a package registry, npm package traffic to customers covers those visits.

Frequently asked questions

Do app marketplace listings bring paying customers?

It depends on the product and the listing, and the only way to know is to measure it. Tag the website link on each listing, pass the visitor ID into your checkout, and compare paying customers and revenue per visitor by listing over 90 days with your site average.

Which channel do marketplace clicks land in?

In Virling, clicks from apps.shopify.com, wordpress.org and zapier.com land in Referral under their own host. Clicks from chromewebstore.google.com land in Organic search with Google, and clicks from slack.com land in Social under Slack. A utm_campaign on the listing link gives each listing its own row in the Campaign tab.

Why do some marketplace clicks show up as Direct?

The click arrived with no referrer, for example from a link inside an installed app or a page that hides the referrer, and the link had no tag. Add utm_source and utm_campaign to the link and the visit is credited to the marketplace.

Can I track payments the marketplace bills for me?

No. When the marketplace charges the customer, as with Shopify app subscriptions billed through Shopify, the payment does not pass through your checkout and has no visitor ID to join to a visit. Virling credits payments taken with your own payment provider.
