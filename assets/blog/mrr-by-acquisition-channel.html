MRR by acquisition channel: which channels bring MRR

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-19.

To see MRR by acquisition channel, credit each customer's first payment to the visits that led to it, then let every renewal and plan change keep that first channel. New MRR for a channel is what its new customers pay per month; churned MRR is what its customers stopped paying. Add the two up per channel and you see which marketing channels bring recurring revenue, not only signups.

Most SaaS dashboards split signups by channel and stop. Payments live in Stripe, Paddle or Polar, which know the customer but not the visit. Joining the two is what turns a list of channels into a list of channels that pay every month.

Why signups by channel mislead

  • Signups are not payers. A launch post can send hundreds of signups who look around and leave. A search result can send a handful who all buy.
  • The first payment is not the whole customer. Two channels can bring the same number of customers on the same plan, and one of them keeps them for a year while the other loses half in the first month.
  • Renewals have no visit. The charge in month six never went through your site, so a tool that credits payments to the visit before them puts every renewal in Unattributed or Direct. The channel that won the customer looks smaller every month.
  • Plans differ. A channel that brings customers on your top plan is worth more per customer than one that brings the cheapest plan, which a signup count cannot show.

How to credit MRR to the channel that brought the customer

The rule is simple: the channel that brought the customer keeps the credit for as long as they pay.

  1. Credit the first payment. Pass the visitor ID into checkout, so the first payment is joined to the visits before it and credited to a channel. By default Virling credits the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct. See link checkout to visits and model and lookback window.
  2. Copy that channel to every renewal. A renewal is matched to the customer's first payment by the provider's customer ID and takes its channel, referrer, campaign, landing page, country and device. Upgrades and plan changes count as renewals, so expansion stays with the same channel. See renewals.
  3. Credit payments after a free trial. A trial charges nothing at checkout, so the first real charge has no visitor ID. Virling reads the trial start, never counts it as revenue, and credits the customer's later payment to the visits before the trial.
  4. Group customers by their first channel. New MRR, renewals, refunds and cancellations of a customer all land on the channel of their first payment.

By new MRR, communities win. By net new MRR, organic search brings almost twice as much, because two thirds of what communities add leaves again. The newsletter sends less than communities but keeps most of it. With numbers this small, one customer can move a row, so read several months before you move budget. To check the totals, use the free MRR calculator, and the NRR calculator for how much of a channel's starting MRR is left after churn and upgrades.

How to read retention per channel

Net new MRR tells you about one month. Retention per channel tells you whether a channel's customers are worth what they cost to win.

  • Still paying. Of the customers a channel brought in a range, how many still have a live subscription today. A channel with 60% of your customers and 25% of the ones still paying is losing its customers faster than the rest.
  • 30-day churn. Of the customers whose first payment was 30 to 60 days ago, the share with no payment and no live subscription in the last 30 days. It works before you have months of history.
  • Net LTV. Every payment the channel's customers made so far, net of refunds. This is the number to set against what you spend on the channel; see how to calculate CAC by channel.
  • Refund rate. A channel with many refunds may promise something the product does not do.

Give a channel time before you judge it. LTV needs months, so in the first 60 days lean on refund rate and 30-day churn. See retention by channel.

Set it up with Virling for Stripe, Paddle and Polar

1. Add the script

Paste the tag before </head> on your marketing site and your app. The visitor ID cookie is shared across subdomains, so yourproduct.com and app.yourproduct.com see the same visitor.

<script defer src="https://app.virling.com/v.js" data-site="yourproduct.com"></script>

2. Connect your billing provider with a read-only key

  • Stripe: a restricted key with read access, including Subscriptions. Renewals are invoices with billing reason subscription_cycle. See Stripe.
  • Paddle: an API key with transaction.read and adjustment.read, plus subscription.read so retention knows who still pays. See Paddle.
  • Polar: an organization access token with orders:read and refunds:read, plus subscriptions:read. See Polar.

Virling imports the last 90 days and then checks for new payments every minute. It never creates, changes or refunds anything. Subscriptions whose first payment is older than the import come in as Unattributed renewals, so channel MRR fills in as new customers arrive.

3. Pass the visitor ID to checkout

Hosted checkout links on buy.stripe.com and buy.polar.sh need nothing: the script adds the visitor ID. Checkout you create on your server needs one field, such as client_reference_id for Stripe Checkout or custom data for Paddle. The checkout snippet generator writes the code for your provider.

4. Read MRR and retention by channel

  • Channels card: revenue per channel, with renewals credited to the channel of the first payment.
  • Customers who stay (the Retention page): customers grouped by the channel of their first payment, with first-payment revenue, how many still pay, net LTV and 30-day churn, plus what the customers from the range pay per month now.
  • Latest payments: your current MRR, from live subscriptions past any trial, at list price before discounts and tax.

What to check if the numbers look off

  • Renewals show as Unattributed. The first payment was not attributed, or it is older than the 90-day import. Payments without a customer ID cannot be linked to a first payment. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A customer counts twice. Customers are matched per provider, so someone who moved from one provider to another counts as two.
  • MRR looks higher than what you collect. MRR uses the list price before discounts and tax.
  • Still paying looks low on Paddle or Polar. Without the subscription read permission, retention falls back to payment recency, and the page says so.

Frequently asked questions

What is MRR by acquisition channel?

It is your monthly recurring revenue split by the marketing channel that brought each customer. Each subscription is credited to the channel of the customer's first payment, so new MRR, renewals and churned MRR all stay with the channel that won the customer.

Why not judge channels by signups?

Signups count interest, not money. A channel can send many signups who never pay, or customers who cancel after a month. MRR per channel shows what each channel still brings in after trials, cancellations and refunds.

Which channel gets the credit for a renewal?

The channel of the customer's first payment. A renewal has no visit of its own, so Virling copies the channel, referrer and campaign of the first payment, matched by the provider's customer ID. Upgrades and plan changes keep that channel too.

How do I see churned MRR by marketing channel?

Group customers by the channel of their first payment, then count the ones whose subscription ended or who stopped paying. In Virling the Retention page shows, per channel, how many customers were acquired, how many still pay, and the 30-day churn.

Does it work for Stripe, Paddle and Polar subscriptions?

Yes. Virling reads payments with a read-only key, marks renewals and plan changes for each provider, credits payments after a free trial to the visits before the trial, and reads subscription status once an hour to tell who still pays.
