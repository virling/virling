Is AI search traffic worth anything? How to measure it

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-10-26.

It can be, but you have to measure it per visitor, not per visit count. AI assistants send far fewer people than search, so the total looks small. What matters is how much each of those visitors pays.

Why the total misleads

A channel with 40 visitors will never top a channel with 4,000 on a traffic graph. If those 40 pay at five times the rate, they are worth more per head. You only see that with revenue next to traffic. See what revenue per visitor is.

Virling groups visits from the AI assistants it knows into one AI channel, so a small number of visitors can be judged by revenue per visitor on the same card as search, social and email.

Step 1: count the visits you can see

When someone clicks a link inside ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude or Gemini, the visit usually arrives with that assistant's site as the referrer. Your analytics should group those referrers into one AI channel. Virling does this and lists each assistant by name.

In GA4, open the Traffic acquisition report, set the dimension to session source, and filter for chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, claude.ai, gemini.google.com and copilot.microsoft.com. Add purchase revenue as a column, or put those sources into a custom channel group so they show as one AI row.

Step 2: know what you cannot see

Some answers are read and never clicked, and some assistants strip the referrer, so those visits land as Direct. Treat the AI number as a floor, not a total.

Step 3: join the visits to payments

Pass the visitor ID into checkout so each payment is tied to the visit before it. Without that step the AI channel shows traffic and no revenue, which is the most common reason it looks worthless. The steps are in tracking Stripe revenue by traffic source.

Step 4: compare revenue per visitor

Put the AI channel next to search, social and email. If it sits above your site average, find out which pages the assistants send people to and make them better. You can also list them in an llms.txt file with the llms.txt generator; support varies between assistants.

Step 5: check whether assistants name you

Visits are only one signal. Whether an assistant recommends you when asked is another. Virling's AI answers tracking asks assistants the questions your customers ask and records whether you are named; see AI answers.

AI has the smallest revenue and the highest revenue per visitor, about four times the average of these channels. But 60 visits are too few to trust that rate: one customer more or fewer moves AI to $2.67 or $1.33, while one more organic search customer moves search only from $0.60 to $0.61. Read it as a reason to look closer, and use a longer date range before you rank AI above email.

A monthly check in six steps

Set the same range every month, such as Last 30 days, or Last 90 days while AI visits are few. The cards are described in reading your dashboard.

  1. Which assistants sent visits. On the Channels card, click AI to filter the page, then open the Source tab. Assistants that count as Referral, listed below, are not in this filter.
  2. Which pages they landed on. With the filter still on, open the Landing page tab of the Pages card.
  3. What they paid. Read revenue and revenue per visitor for each row on the Source and Landing page tabs. Revenue counts on the payment date, so some of it can come from visits before the range.
  4. Whether the assistants name you. Remove the filter and open the Answers tab of the AI card. Mentioned needs a paid hosted plan, or provider keys when self-hosting; see AI answers.
  5. What to change. Click an assistant on the Answers tab to see the buyer questions where it left you out and the pages it cites. Pick one page to improve: one that answers a question you are missing from, or one on the Pages card that AI visitors land on and do not pay after.
  6. What to leave alone. Leave a page or assistant with only a few customers on the Channels and Pages cards until a longer range says the same. Do not act on one month of Mentioned either: answers change from run to run, as the AI answers docs explain.

AI crawlers are not AI visits

An AI crawler is a bot that reads your pages to train a model, build a search index or answer someone live. An AI visit is a person who clicked a link in an assistant and opened your site, and only a person can pay. Many pages read does not mean many visits, so keep crawler hits out of revenue per visitor. Virling stores them apart from visits and shows them on the Crawls tab of the AI card; most crawlers never run JavaScript, so counting them all needs the server snippet in the AI crawlers docs. To see which bots your robots.txt allows, use the AI crawler checker. To count the hits each bot left in your access log, use the AI crawler log analyzer.

By assistant

How each one passes the referrer, or drops it:

  • ChatGPT usually passes chatgpt.com; OpenAI says it also adds utm_source=chatgpt.com to referral links, which still names it when the referrer is missing.
  • Claude usually passes claude.ai in the browser; links opened from the desktop or mobile apps can arrive with no referrer.
  • Gemini usually passes gemini.google.com in the browser; links opened from the Gemini app can arrive with none.
  • Microsoft Copilot usually passes copilot.microsoft.com in the browser; an AI answer on a Bing results page passes bing.com and counts as Organic search.
  • Perplexity usually passes perplexity.ai or www.perplexity.ai in the browser; its apps can drop it.
  • DeepSeek usually passes chat.deepseek.com from the web chat; its mobile apps can drop it.
  • Grok usually passes grok.com from the web chat, which Virling counts as Referral; used inside another site, it carries that site's host, if any.
  • Le Chat passes chat.mistral.ai on the web when the browser sends a referrer, which Virling counts as Referral; its mobile apps can drop it.
  • Meta AI can pass meta.ai from its web chat, which Virling counts as Referral; taps inside the Meta apps usually land in Social or Direct.
  • Google AI Overviews and AI Mode pass google.com like any other Google result, so they count as Organic search and cannot be split out.

Grok, Le Chat, DeepSeek and Meta AI

Of these four, only DeepSeek is in Virling's AI channel. Any other referrer host that is not a known search, community or social host counts as Referral, so add the Grok, Le Chat and Meta AI rows to your AI total by hand. When a referrer is present it decides the channel; utm_source is read only when there is none. A tag that names a host Virling does not know joins no Source tab row, so add a utm_campaign and find those visits, with their revenue, on the Campaign tab of the Channels card. The UTM builder makes the links, and the traffic source checker shows how a given referrer and URL are credited.

Grok

A click in the Grok web chat usually arrives with grok.com as the referrer, which Virling counts as Referral and shows as grok.com. Used inside another site, Grok carries that site's host, if any. With no referrer, utm_source=grok or utm_source=grok.com is not a known source, so a tagged visit counts as Referral rather than Direct. Avoid utm_source=x or twitter, which count as Social and mix the visits with your posts.

Le Chat

Le Chat on the web runs at chat.mistral.ai, and a click there passes that host when the browser sends a referrer. Virling counts it as Referral and shows it as chat.mistral.ai. Links opened from the mobile apps can arrive with no referrer and count as Direct. A tag such as utm_source=chat.mistral.ai is not a known source, so a tagged visit counts as Referral.

DeepSeek

A click in the DeepSeek web chat usually passes chat.deepseek.com, which Virling counts as AI and lists as DeepSeek. Only the chat host counts: a visit referred by deepseek.com or its docs is Referral, like any other website. With no referrer, utm_source=chat.deepseek.com also counts as AI and joins the DeepSeek row, but the bare name utm_source=deepseek is Referral. Links opened from the mobile apps can drop the referrer and land as Direct.

Meta AI

A click in the Meta AI web chat can pass meta.ai, which Virling counts as Referral and shows as meta.ai. Inside WhatsApp, Instagram or Messenger, a link can carry a facebook.com or instagram.com referrer, which counts as Social, or open in the app's own browser with none. With no referrer and no tag, Virling credits the visit in Social to the app the user agent names, and counts it as Direct when nothing names one. None of these visits say they came from a Meta AI answer rather than a chat or a post. Tag your own links with utm_source=meta.ai and utm_campaign=meta-ai; avoid utm_source=facebook or instagram, which count as Social.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see visitors from ChatGPT and Perplexity?

Look for their sites in your referrers. Group them into one AI channel and list each assistant, which Virling does automatically.

Why does my AI traffic show no revenue?

Usually because payments are not linked to visits. Pass the visitor ID into checkout so each payment can be tied to the visit before it.

Can I see AI visits that arrive with no referrer?

Not directly. Those visits land as Direct, so the AI channel is a minimum, not the full count.

How do I track ChatGPT referral revenue in GA4?

Filter the Traffic acquisition report by session source for chatgpt.com and the other assistants, and add purchase revenue as a column. It only counts revenue from purchase events GA4 received.

Why are Grok, Le Chat and Meta AI not in the AI channel?

The AI channel covers a fixed list of assistant hosts: chatgpt.com, chat.openai.com, perplexity.ai, claude.ai, gemini.google.com, copilot.microsoft.com and chat.deepseek.com. grok.com, chat.mistral.ai and meta.ai are not on it, so their visits count as Referral. Add those rows to your AI total by hand when you compare assistants.

Why is utm_source=deepseek counted as Referral?

Virling only counts a bare source name as AI for the assistants it knows by name, and deepseek is not one of them. Tag your own links with utm_source=chat.deepseek.com instead; that matches the DeepSeek host and lands in the AI channel.
