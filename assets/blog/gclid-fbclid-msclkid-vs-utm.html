gclid vs UTM tags: gclid, fbclid and msclkid explained

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-24.

gclid vs UTM: a gclid is a code Google Ads adds to your link so that Google can recognise the click. UTM tags are words you write, such as utm_campaign=brand-search, that any analytics tool can read. fbclid does the same job for Meta and msclkid for Microsoft Advertising. A click ID names no campaign to anyone but the ad platform that made it, so keep click IDs on for the platform’s own reports and add UTM tags for everything else. They work together on one link.

The common click IDs

Click IDWho adds itWhat it is forWho can read it

fbclidMeta, on a click on a Facebook or Instagram adMatching the events your site sends to Meta with the clickMeta
msclkidMicrosoft Advertising, when auto-tagging of the MSCLKID is onTracking leads with offline conversion goals; the ID is unique for each ad clickMicrosoft
ttclidTikTok, on a click on an adMatching your site’s events with the action on TikTok, for attribution, audiences and deliveryTikTok

“Read” here means turn the code into a click, an ad or a campaign. Any script on your page can see the characters in the URL, and you can store them and send them back to the platform. The platform holds the other half.

Sources: Google, GCLID definition and About auto-tagging; Meta, ClickID and the fbp and fbc parameters; Microsoft, AccountProperty; TikTok, About TikTok Click ID; X, Conversion tracking for websites; LinkedIn, first-party cookies and cookie table.

Why a click ID alone names no campaign

Compare the two on one link. The values are made up:

https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=brand-search&gclid=EXAMPLE123

The first three say where the click came from, that it was paid and which campaign it was. The last one is a key to a record in Google’s systems. Google can look it up, and so can you with access to the ad account: the Google Ads API has a click report that lists gclids with their campaigns, one day at a time and up to 90 days back. A tool without that access sees only a string.

Grouping by a click ID does not help either, because it marks clicks and not campaigns. (Google says the same gclid can be used again when one person clicks the same ad twice, but it still names no campaign.) These numbers are made up to show the difference:

?gclid=EXAMPLE21$0Another click. The same campaign or not? Unknown

Why you still need UTM tags

  • Every tool can read them. Your analytics, your CRM and your own database see the same campaign name without a call to the ad platform.
  • They cover links that are not ads. A newsletter, a partner or a podcast can carry a UTM tag. No ad platform adds a click ID for you there.
  • They compare platforms in one report. The same utm_campaign scheme works on Google, Meta and LinkedIn. A click ID belongs to one platform.
  • They say paid or not. utm_medium=cpc separates an ad from an unpaid link on the same site.
  • They do not single out a click. Everyone who clicks the ad gets the same tag. Apple names no parameters in its privacy feature, but it aims at identifying ones; see iOS Link Tracking Protection and UTM parameters.

When the click ID is the right tool

UTM tags do not replace click IDs. The platform uses them to match conversions to ads, and Google requires the gclid for its website conversion tracking. Turning them off takes that away from the platform and adds nothing to your own reports, so leave them on. Google Ads can add other parameters next to the gclid; see what gbraid, wbraid and gad_source mean.

What Virling does with each one

The Virling script sends the landing URL. The server reads utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_content and utm_term from its query string, or ref when utm_source is missing, and stores the path without the query. No click ID value is stored, and none decides the channel, source or campaign.

The label for the four is narrow. When a visit is Direct and its landing URL has fbclid, ttclid, twclid or li_fat_id, it can show under Direct as Shared link instead of Typed or bookmark. A return from checkout comes first, and so does an app the browser names, such as the X app. The visit stays Direct either way, so the label is not a way to count ad clicks. gclid and msclkid are not on that list.

With utm_medium=cpc or paid_social on the link, the same click is Paid, because the medium is checked before the referrer. See how channels are decided.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between gclid and UTM tags?

A gclid is a code that Google Ads adds to the landing URL so Google can tie the visit to the ad click. UTM tags are words you choose, such as utm_campaign=brand-search, that any analytics tool can read. The gclid is for Google; the UTM tags are for everyone else.

Do I need UTM tags if auto-tagging is on?

Yes, if you want any tool outside Google to show the campaign. Auto-tagging adds a gclid, which does not name the campaign to other tools. Leave it on for Google and put UTM tags in the final URL suffix.

Can I use a click ID and UTM tags on the same link?

Yes. They are separate query parameters. The ad platform adds the click ID on the click, and the tags come from the URL you set on the ad.

Can I look up a campaign from a gclid?

Only through Google. The Google Ads API has a click report that lists gclids with their campaigns, one day at a time and up to 90 days back, for an account you have access to. Nothing in the code itself spells out the campaign name.

Does Virling read gclid, fbclid or msclkid?

Virling does not read gclid or msclkid. For fbclid, ttclid, twclid and li_fat_id it checks only whether the parameter is there, so a Direct visit can be labelled Shared link. It stores none of their values and none of them decides the channel or campaign.

Is fbclid proof that a visit came from a paid ad?

Do not treat it that way. A link that someone copies from the address bar keeps its fbclid, so a later visitor can arrive with the code from another person's click. Use utm_medium=paid_social to mark the ad.
