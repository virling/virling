YouTube Ads revenue tracking: see which campaign brings paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-24.

YouTube Ads revenue tracking means joining each payment on your site to the YouTube ad click that brought the buyer. Put utm_source=youtube, utm_medium=cpc and a utm_campaign in the campaign’s URL options, add the Virling script to your site, and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Virling counts the click as Paid and credits the payment to it, so the Campaign tab shows payments and revenue per campaign. Google Ads reports views, clicks and the conversions it attributes; your payment provider shows the money you received.

Step 1: tag the ad

YouTube ads run from Google Ads, so the tag goes in its URL options. The final URL suffix field holds parameters that Google attaches to the end of your landing page URL, and you can set it for the account, a campaign, an ad group or an ad (source: Google Ads Help, Add a Final URL suffix). Set it on each video campaign, with a name you type:

utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=spring-demo-video

Leave the question mark out of the suffix. Use lowercase and hyphens, and keep one name per campaign, because a campaign tagged two ways becomes two rows. The free UTM builder makes tagged URLs for you, which you can also use as the ad’s final URL. Put the tags in one place, not both.

ValueTrack parameters on video campaigns

ValueTrack parameters are placeholders in braces that Google fills in when the ad is clicked. Its help page has a list for video campaigns that includes {campaignid}, {adgroupid}, {creative} (the ad’s ID), {device}, {placement} and {network}. {network} returns ytv for YouTube, vp for Google video partners and gtv for Google TV. The same page says Demand Gen ads do not support {network} or {placement}, so leave those two out there (source: Google Ads Help, ValueTrack parameters).

To tag a whole campaign by ID instead of by name, use utm_campaign={campaignid}. Google’s page names the final URL, the tracking template and custom parameters as places for ValueTrack, so the ad’s final URL is the documented spot. If you put it in the suffix, click a test ad and check that the address bar shows a number, not braces. You can add utm_content={creative}, which Virling stores with the pageview, but reports group by campaign.

A campaign that also runs on Google video partners carries the same tag on those sites. If you want to compare YouTube with partners, give each its own campaign with its own utm_campaign.

What about the gclid?

Auto-tagging adds a gclid parameter to the landing URL after someone clicks your ad (source: Google Ads Help, About auto-tagging). It is useful to Google. Virling does not use it to pick the channel, and it does not name your campaign, so leave it on and add UTM tags. See Google Ads revenue tracking for search and the other campaign types.

Why a view-through never shows as a click

Google’s help page says the view-through conversions column tells you when people view but do not interact with your ad and later complete a conversion on your site. It is kept out of the main Conversions column and appears in View-through conversions and All conversions (source: Google Ads Help, conversion tracking data). Nobody clicked, so no browser opened your landing page with your tag on it. Virling credits visits, and a view is not a visit. If that person later searches for your name and buys, the payment is credited to the search visit.

Engaged views are a second kind. On a skippable ad, Google counts an engaged-view conversion when someone does not click, watches at least 10 seconds, and then converts within the window (source: Google Ads Help, YouTube ads and view metrics). Again there is no click, so there is no tagged visit.

So payments per campaign show what clicks brought. Virling does not estimate sales from people who only watched.

How Virling sorts a YouTube ad visit

Every session gets one channel from its first pageview, and the first matching rule wins:

  • A paid medium. cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social or paidsocial, in any case, is Paid. It is checked first, so a tagged click from youtube.com is Paid, not Social.
  • No paid medium, with a referrer. youtube.com and youtu.be are Social, so the ad is mixed in with your videos. A Google search host is Organic search, and a host that is not on a known list is Referral.
  • No referrer, with a source tag. youtube is Social and google is Organic search. A value that is not a known source is Referral.
  • No referrer and no tag. The visit is Direct. The YouTube app is not one of the in-app browsers Virling recognizes.

Spelling counts. paid-video, video and cpv are not on the paid list, so those clicks are not Paid. See how channels are decided.

Step 2: add the script

Add the Virling script to the <head> of every page the ad can land on. It reads the tags on the landing page and sets the first-party visitor ID. Redirects on your site must keep the query string; see UTM parameters lost after redirect. The setup is in the script docs.

Step 3: link checkout

Connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout, so each payment can be matched to a visit. Payment links from Stripe, Polar, Dodo Payments and LemonSqueezy get the ID from the script when they are on a page that runs it; a checkout you create on your server needs the virling_vid cookie. See link checkout to visits.

Under the default model, the whole payment, after refunds, is credited to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct. Someone who clicks your ad on Monday and types your address on Thursday is still credited to the ad. See the attribution model. A viewer who clicks on a phone and pays on a laptop can have two visitor IDs; see cross-device attribution.

Step 4: read payments per campaign

  • In the Channels card, click Paid. The page filters to visits with a paid medium, from YouTube and any other platform you tag the same way.
  • Open the Campaign tab. Each utm_campaign is a row with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  • Open a payment from the Payments page to see the visits before it.
  • Check Time to pay before you pause a campaign that looks weak.

To see profit, type what you spend on ads each month in Site settings, under Monthly spend per channel, such as Paid 1200. The Profit by channel card then shows spend, revenue and profit for the Paid channel as a whole, so include every platform you tag as Paid. The result is only as good as the amount you type.

A made-up example

These numbers are invented to show the arithmetic. Two campaigns each cost $500, as read in Google Ads. Campaign A has 250 visitors and 10 payments worth $1,000, so $4.00 per visitor and $50 of spend per payment. Campaign B has 700 visitors and 7 payments worth $700, so $1.00 per visitor and about $71.43 per payment. At a 60% margin with one $100 order per customer, the break-even cost per customer is $60, so A is above water and B is not, even though B brought more visitors. Work out your own limit with the break-even CPA calculator.

Why Google Ads conversions differ from payments

  • Views count in some columns. View-through and engaged-view conversions have no click, so no visit carries your tag.
  • Different event. A conversion is an action your site reports to Google. A payment is recorded by your provider, after the charge, and Virling takes refunds off.
  • Different credit. Virling gives a payment to one visit in its window. If the buyer clicked an ad and later came from your newsletter, the default model credits the newsletter, while Google can still count the ad.
  • Different visitors. A buyer who changed browser, or blocked the script, may be in one count and missing from the other.

Use Google Ads to run the ads and the payments to decide which campaigns to keep. For the unpaid side of the same channel, see YouTube revenue tracking, which covers links under your own videos. For promoted Pins, see Pinterest Ads revenue tracking.

What to check if the numbers look off

  • Ad revenue shows as Social. The ad has no paid utm_medium. Click a test ad and check the address bar.
  • Ad revenue shows as Direct. The tags were dropped by a redirect, or the ad has none and no referrer came through.
  • A campaign row shows braces. If {campaignid} appears as typed, check the spelling, and move it from the suffix to the ad’s final URL.
  • Clicks in Google Ads but no Paid visits. Check that the script is on the landing page and that the campaign sends people to your site.
  • Revenue shows as Unattributed. The visitor ID is not reaching checkout.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which YouTube ad campaign brought a paying customer?

Add utm_source=youtube, utm_medium=cpc and a utm_campaign to the final URL or final URL suffix of the campaign. Connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Each payment is credited to the tagged visit, and the Campaign tab shows revenue per campaign.

Which channel does a YouTube ad click land in?

Paid, if the link carries utm_medium=cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social or paidsocial. The medium is checked first, so it wins over a youtube.com or Google referrer. Without a paid medium, the click is sorted by its referrer, or by utm_source when there is no referrer.

Can Virling track a view-through conversion from YouTube?

No. Someone who sees the ad and does not click has not opened your site from it, so there is no visit and no tag to credit. If they come later through another route, the payment is credited to that visit under the attribution model you chose.

Does Virling read the gclid?

Not to pick the channel or the campaign. Those come from utm_medium, the referrer, utm_source and ref, and utm_campaign. Auto-tagging can stay on for Google, with UTM tags added for Virling.

Should YouTube ads and organic videos share the same tags?

They can share utm_source=youtube, but not the medium. Use a paid medium such as cpc on ads, so they are Paid, and utm_medium=social on links under your own videos, so those stay in Social.

Does Virling connect to my Google Ads account?

No. The setup on this page uses only the tags on your ads, the Paid channel and your payments. You can type what you spend on Paid each month in Site settings, and the Profit by channel card uses that amount.
