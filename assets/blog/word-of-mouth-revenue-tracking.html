Word of mouth attribution: how to track word of mouth revenue

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-22.

TL;DR: To see how much revenue comes from people who heard about you from someone else, combine two records. First, give customers tagged links to share, so referred visits arrive with a source and their payments are credited to it. Second, ask every new customer “How did you hear about us?” and read the answers next to the tracked channel of the same payment. Word of mouth that leaves no link lands in Direct, and the survey is how you tell it apart from typed visits.

Why word of mouth shows up as Direct

A recommendation travels in ways analytics cannot see. A friend says your name over lunch, a colleague mentions you in a meeting, or someone pastes your link in a group chat. In the first two cases there is no link at all: the buyer types your address or searches your name. In the third, many chat and mail apps open the link with no referrer.

With no referrer and no UTM tag, a visit has nothing to say where it came from, so it is filed as Direct, next to bookmarks and typed addresses. A buyer who searched your name after hearing it from a friend shows up as Organic search instead. Either way, the person who sent them gets no credit. Links shared in private chats are covered in more depth in dark social traffic and the revenue it brings, and everything else that piles up in Direct in why Direct is your biggest traffic source.

Combine tracked sources with a survey answer

Neither record is complete on its own. Tracked sources show the last click Virling could see. A “How did you hear about us?” answer shows what the buyer remembers, often the first time they heard your name. Read them side by side:

Referral, from your own share or referral linkFriend or colleagueWord of mouth you tracked. Both records agree.
DirectFriend or colleagueWord of mouth that left no link. The survey fills the gap.
Organic searchFriend or colleagueThey heard about you, then searched your name. Search was the last step, not the reason.
Social, from a chat app such as WhatsApp or SlackFriend or colleagueA link shared in a chat. Virling credits the app when its in-app browser names itself.

The how did you hear about us template has ready answer options, including “Friend or colleague”, and tips on where to ask. Make the question optional and single choice, and ask it once, at signup or on the thank-you page. Self-reported attribution covers what such answers catch and get wrong.

Set up word of mouth tracking with Virling

  1. Add the Virling script to every page of your site, including your signup and checkout pages. See add the script.
  2. Connect your payment provider, such as Stripe, Paddle, Shopify or Polar, so each payment is joined to the visits before it. Stripe Payment Links, Polar and Dodo Payments checkout links get the visitor ID from the script; for a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie. See link checkout to visits.
  3. Give customers a link to share. Make a tracked link in Links with the Referral channel, add a partner in Partners for a creator or customer who refers often, or tag your URL with the UTM builder, for example utm_source=referral&utm_medium=referral. Put it in a share button, a referral email or your onboarding. See tracked links.
  4. Add the “How did you hear about us?” question to your signup form or thank-you page, and keep the answer with the customer record in your own database or CRM.
  5. Leave the attribution model on last non-direct, the default. It credits a payment to the latest visit in the lookback window that was not Direct, so a buyer who first clicked a shared link and later typed your address is still credited to that link. See the model docs.
  6. Each month, read revenue for the Referral channel and for your partners, then compare the Direct revenue with the customers who answered “Friend or colleague”.

To check how a link you plan to share will be classified, paste it into the traffic source checker.

What this will not tell you

Virling does not store survey answers, and goal properties are not available yet, so the survey answer lives in your own records and the comparison is done by you. Nothing in a typed visit says who recommended you, so word of mouth revenue is always an estimate: tracked referrals give you a floor, and survey answers give you a fuller picture that depends on what buyers remember.

Frequently asked questions

What is word of mouth attribution?

Crediting revenue to buyers who came because someone told them about you. Analytics sees only clicks, so it pairs tracked sources, such as share and referral links, with what buyers say when you ask how they heard about you.

Why does word of mouth show up as Direct traffic?

A recommendation said out loud leaves no link, and a link pasted in a chat or email often arrives with no referrer. With no referrer and no UTM tag, the visit has nothing to say where it came from, so analytics files it as Direct.

Can I measure all word of mouth revenue?

No. A buyer who heard your name from a friend and typed your address leaves no trace in analytics. Tagged share links and in-app browsers recover part of it, and a how did you hear about us question covers much of the rest, but it stays an estimate.

Should I trust the survey answer or the tracked source?

Use both. The tracked source is the last click that could be seen. The survey answer is what the buyer remembers, often the first time they heard your name. Where the click is Direct and the answer is a friend or colleague, the answer fills the gap.

How do I give customers a link to share?

Make a tracked link with the Referral channel or a partner link in Virling, or tag your URL with a UTM builder, for example utm_source=referral and utm_medium=referral, and put it in a share button, a referral email or your onboarding.
