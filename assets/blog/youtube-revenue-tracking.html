YouTube revenue tracking: which videos bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-16.

To track revenue from YouTube, put a UTM-tagged link in each video's description and pinned comment, with utm_source=youtube and the video's name in the campaign. Carry the visitor ID into checkout, then join each payment to the visit that came before it. You then see the revenue and revenue per visitor each video brings. Views, watch time and click-through rate in YouTube Studio do not tell you this.

A video can take a day to script, film and edit. Whether that pays comes down to one question: did anyone who clicked a link under this video pay, and how much was each of those visitors worth compared with your other channels?

Why YouTube clicks show up as Direct

There are three ways a visit gets marked as YouTube, and only one of them is under your control.

  • The referrer. A click on youtube.com in a desktop browser usually arrives with youtube.com as the referrer, and youtu.be links arrive with youtu.be. Both hosts are in the Social channel. The referrer is a host only, so it cannot tell you which video the click came from.
  • The in-app browser. Most YouTube viewing happens in the mobile app. Links opened there often arrive with no referrer. Virling credits some apps' in-app browsers, such as Instagram and LinkedIn, when the app names itself in the user agent, but YouTube is not on that list. With no referrer and no tag, a click from the YouTube app counts as Direct, in Virling and in most other tools.
  • A UTM tag. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, and youtube is a known Social source. The tag travels in the link itself, so it survives the app, the system browser and a missing referrer.

If your Direct traffic rises the day a video goes out, this is usually why. The full rule order is in channels, and why Direct is your biggest traffic source covers the other causes.

Tag every video link

Give each video its own campaign, and use utm_content to say where the link sat:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=video-setup-in-5-minutes&utm_content=description
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=video-setup-in-5-minutes&utm_content=pinned-comment
  • utm_source=youtube on every YouTube link, so they group under one source and stay out of Direct.
  • utm_medium=social for organic videos. Anything except a paid or email medium keeps the visit in Social. Use a paid medium such as cpc only for YouTube ads.
  • utm_campaign names the video: one value per video, lowercase, with hyphens. The Campaign tab groups visitors and revenue by this value, so this is the tag that gives you revenue per video.
  • utm_content names the spot: description, pinned-comment, end-screen or channel-banner. Virling stores it with each visit; keep the video in the campaign so it shows up in reports.

Links on your channel page, such as the banner links and the About tab, deserve a fixed campaign of their own, like channel-page, so you can tell them from video clicks. More naming rules are in which UTM parameters to use, and the UTM builder makes the link for you.

If a long tagged URL looks messy in a description, use a tracked link instead. In Virling, open Links, pick YouTube as the place and the page it should open. The short link adds utm_source=youtube.com, a social medium and its own campaign on the click, and the Campaign tab lists it with its visitors and revenue. Make one link per video. See tracked links.

Make sure payments can be credited

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs the visitor ID in checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Each provider has its own page: Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce. The setup for all of them is in link checkout to visits.

Viewers often watch a video, leave, and come back later by typing your URL. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the payment goes to the tagged YouTube visit, as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. If they subscribe, every renewal keeps counting toward that video.

See revenue per video

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more, since videos keep sending visitors long after they go out.
  2. In the Channels card, open Social, or switch to the Source tab, and find YouTube. The row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click the row to filter the whole page. The Pages card now shows which landing pages YouTube visitors arrived on.
  4. Switch to the Campaign tab. With one campaign per video, each video is a row with its own visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

A worked example

Made-up numbers to show the method, not results from a real channel. Over 90 days, three videos with tagged links:

Video (utm_campaign)VisitorsPaying customersRevenueRevenue per visitor
video-setup-in-5-minutes4008$1,200$3.00
video-pricing-teardown2,5005$750$0.30
video-year-in-review3000$0$0.00

Say the site average is $1.00 per visitor. The setup video sent far fewer visitors than the teardown, yet each visitor it sends is worth three times the average: people watching a setup guide are close to buying. The teardown wins on traffic and loses on pay, so its link might point at a better page, such as pricing instead of the home page. The year in review brought no sales, which is fine for a video made for existing viewers; 300 visitors and no sales is too little to judge either way.

Without the tags, a large share of those visitors would sit in Direct and none of the three rows would exist.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • YouTube looks small but Direct jumped the day a video went out. Some links are untagged. Check the description, the pinned comment and the channel page.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A buyer says they found you on YouTube but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have watched without clicking and searched for you later. No analytics tool can count a view that never reached your site. Switch the model to first touch in Site settings to see which videos start journeys; first-touch or last-touch attribution explains the difference.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so a video's real visitor number can be higher than what any analytics tool shows. Revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from YouTube?

Put a UTM-tagged link in each video's description and pinned comment, with utm_source=youtube and the video in utm_campaign. Pass the visitor ID into checkout and use a tool that joins payments to visits. The campaign report then shows revenue per video.

Why do YouTube visits show up as Direct?

Links opened in the YouTube mobile app often arrive with no referrer, and the YouTube app is not one of the in-app browsers Virling can recognize. With no referrer and no tag, the visit is Direct. Adding utm_source=youtube to the link keeps it credited to YouTube.

Can I see which YouTube video sent a paying customer?

Not from the referrer, which is only youtube.com. Give each video its own utm_campaign, or its own tracked link, and the Campaign tab shows visitors and revenue per video.

What should go in utm_content for YouTube links?

Where the link sat: description, pinned comment, end screen or channel page. Keep the video itself in utm_campaign, since that is the value revenue is grouped by.

Should YouTube ads use the same tags?

Use utm_source=youtube with a paid medium such as cpc, so ad clicks land in the Paid channel and organic videos stay in Social.
