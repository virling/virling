Instagram and Facebook revenue tracking: which posts, stories and groups bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

Instagram and Facebook hide most of your links: captions are not clickable, stories vanish after a day, Reels push people to your profile, and Facebook shares travel through groups and Messenger. To see which of those clicks turn into paying customers, tag every link you control with utm_source=instagram or utm_source=facebook and a campaign that names the post, story or group. Reach, likes and link taps in Meta's insights stop at the tap and never show the payment.

An untagged visit with no referrer lands in Direct, and Virling joins each payment to the visit before it through the visitor ID your checkout carries; channels has the full rules. Setup is the same as for any site, the script on every page and a connected payment provider, as in the quickstart. Paid ads are a different topic; see Meta Ads revenue tracking.

l.instagram.com, l.facebook.com and the in-app browsers

Meta sends outbound clicks through its own redirect hosts, and most taps happen inside its apps. Each of these ends up somewhere different in your reports:

  • l.instagram.com. A tap on a bio link or story sticker often arrives with this as the referrer. It is in the Social channel, but in the Source tab it is a row of its own next to instagram.com, so add the two when you compare.
  • l.facebook.com and lm.facebook.com. Page posts and group shares tend to come through l.facebook.com, which Virling merges into facebook.com. Links tapped in Messenger tend to come through lm.facebook.com, which stays its own row.
  • No referrer at all. The Instagram app puts Instagram in its in-app browser's user agent, and the Facebook and Messenger apps put markers such as FBAN, FBAV or FB_IAB there. Virling reads those and credits the visit to instagram.com or facebook.com in Social; Messenger counts as Facebook. If the viewer chooses to open the page in their phone's own browser, that marker is gone and an untagged visit is Direct.

A referrer or a utm_source always wins over the user agent guess, and instagram, ig, facebook and fb are all known Social sources. None of these hosts say which story or post the tap came from; only the campaign tag does.

Stories with link stickers

A link sticker is the only way to put a tappable link inside an Instagram story, so give every sticker its own campaign:

https://yourdomain.com/pricing?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=story-pricing-launch&utm_content=link-sticker
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=bio
  • One campaign per story. The Campaign tab groups visitors and revenue by utm_campaign, so a story named story-pricing-launch gets its own row. Lowercase, with hyphens.
  • Read stories by day. A story is shown for 24 hours, so its taps bunch into one day. Pick a range that starts the day before it went out.
  • Highlights keep the sticker alive. A story saved to a Highlight stays tappable on your profile, so tagged visits that arrive days after the story expired most likely came from the Highlight. Re-tag it with a campaign such as highlight-pricing if you want the two apart.

A long tagged URL is awkward to paste into a sticker on a phone. Make a short tracked link instead: in Virling, open the Campaign tab on the Channels card, click + Tracked link, pick Other, type instagram.com as the source and pick the page it should open. The tags are added on the click, and each story's link gets its own row. See tracked links.

Reels, captions and the bio link

A URL typed into a Reel or post caption is plain text, so viewers cannot tap it. That is why Reels say "link in bio": the Reel sends people to your profile, and the click you can measure happens on the bio link. Tag that link with a fixed bio campaign, or swap it per offer, for example bio-pricing then bio-webinar, so each version gets its own row.

The bio link cannot say which Reel a viewer just watched. Filter by Instagram and line up the daily spikes in visits with the days your Reels went out, or mention a short page such as yourdomain.com/spring in one Reel only and watch for it in the Pages card. If your bio opens a links page with several buttons, link in bio UTM tracking covers naming each button and where that page should live.

Shopping tags

Product tags on posts and Reels open the product page from your Instagram Shop catalog, not a link you type per post, so you cannot give a shopping tag its own campaign. The visit is still credited to Instagram through l.instagram.com or the in-app browser, and the Pages card shows which product pages those visitors landed on.

  • Catalog URLs with their own tags. If the product links in your catalog carry a utm_source that is not one of the Instagram or Facebook names above, a tap with no referrer counts as Referral under that name. Look for it in the Source tab.
  • Sales that never reach your site. An order placed without a visit to your store has no visitor ID to join, so it counts toward revenue as Unattributed.

Facebook page posts, groups and Messenger

On Facebook the links sit in page posts, group shares and Messenger chats. Tag each with utm_source=facebook and give every post and every group its own campaign:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=page-post-spring-sale
https://yourdomain.com/pricing?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=group-shopify-owners&utm_content=group
  • One campaign per group. When you share the same link in several groups, use a campaign per group, such as group-shopify-owners and group-indie-makers, so each group gets its own row with its own revenue.
  • utm_content for the place. Use page, group or messenger, and keep the post or group itself in the campaign.
  • Messenger forwards. A link forwarded from chat to chat keeps its tags, so a group link that spreads through Messenger still counts toward that group's campaign.
  • Ads are separate. Only paid ads get utm_medium=paid. A boosted post with an untagged link counts as Social, not Paid.

See revenue per story, Reel push and group

  1. Pick a range of a few weeks or more, so people who tapped a story and paid later have time to show up.
  2. In the Channels card, open Social, or switch to the Source tab, and add up instagram.com and l.instagram.com, then facebook.com and lm.facebook.com. Each row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click the instagram.com row to filter the whole page. The Pages card now shows which landing and product pages Instagram visitors arrived on.
  4. On the Campaign tab, compare bio, each story- campaign and each group- campaign side by side.
  5. Open any payment on the Payments page to check which Instagram or Facebook visit got the credit.

Instagram often introduces a buyer who comes back later through search. Switch the model to first touch in Site settings and compare the Instagram rows under both; history is recomputed, and you can switch back. First-touch or last-touch attribution explains the difference. For drops you post on both apps, see Threads next to Instagram; for Stories on Snapchat, tagging Snaps and Spotlight posts. What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Instagram looks small but Direct jumped the day a story went out. A sticker or bio link is untagged and viewers opened it in their phone's own browser. Check the bio, every link sticker and links you sent in DMs.
  • Instagram looks smaller than you expect. You may be reading instagram.com alone. Add the l.instagram.com row, and lm.facebook.com for Facebook.
  • A story campaign keeps getting visits a week later. The story is saved to a Highlight. Give the Highlight its own campaign if you want to tell them apart.
  • Instagram has visitors but no revenue. Look at the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers; a large unmatched share means the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A buyer says they found you on Instagram but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have seen a post without tapping a link and searched for you later. No analytics tool can count a view that never reached your site.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from Instagram?

Tag the link in your bio and each story link sticker with utm_source=instagram and a utm_campaign for the place or story. Pass the visitor ID into checkout and use a tool that joins payments to visits. The Campaign tab then shows visitors and revenue per link.

What is l.instagram.com in my referrers?

It is the address Instagram routes outbound links through, so a click from a bio link or story can arrive with l.instagram.com as the referrer. Virling counts it as Social. Facebook does the same with l.facebook.com and lm.facebook.com.

Why do Instagram visits show up as Direct?

Links opened in the Instagram or Facebook app often arrive with no referrer. Virling credits them to the app when its in-app browser names itself in the user agent. If the link opens in a plain browser that does not, and the link has no tag, the visit is Direct. A utm_source tag prevents this.

How do I track revenue from Facebook groups?

Share a link tagged with utm_source=facebook and a utm_campaign for each group, such as group-shopify-owners. Each group then gets its own row in the Campaign tab with visitors and revenue.

What are l.facebook.com and lm.facebook.com in my referrers?

They are the addresses Facebook routes outbound links through, so a click from a page post, group or Messenger can arrive with one of them as the referrer. Virling counts both as Social; l.facebook.com is merged into facebook.com, while lm.facebook.com shows as its own row.

Can I see which Instagram story sent a paying customer?

Not from the referrer, which only says instagram.com or l.instagram.com. Give each story link sticker its own utm_campaign, or its own tracked link, and the Campaign tab shows visitors and revenue per story.

Can I track which Reel sent a sale?

Not with a link in the Reel, because captions are not clickable and viewers tap your bio link instead. Swap the bio link's utm_campaign per offer and match daily spikes in Instagram visits to the days your Reels went out, or mention a page only one Reel names.
