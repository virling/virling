Instagram revenue tracking: which posts and stories bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

To see which Instagram visits turn into paying customers, tag the link in your bio and every story link sticker with utm_source=instagram and a campaign for that link. Carry the visitor ID into checkout, then join each payment to the visit that came before it. You then see the revenue and revenue per visitor that Instagram, and each link on it, brings. Reach, likes and link taps in Instagram's insights do not tell you this.

The same setup works for organic Facebook posts, pages and groups. Paid ads are a different topic and are not covered here.

How Instagram and Facebook visits get counted

There are three ways a visit gets marked as Instagram or Facebook, and you want all of them working.

  • The referrer. Instagram and Facebook send outbound clicks through their own link addresses, so a click from a bio link, a story or a Facebook post often arrives with l.instagram.com, l.facebook.com or lm.facebook.com as the referrer. All of them are in the Social channel. In the Source tab, l.facebook.com is merged into facebook.com, while l.instagram.com and lm.facebook.com show as rows of their own, so add them to instagram.com and facebook.com when you compare. The referrer is a host only, so it cannot tell you which story or post the click came from.
  • The in-app browser. Most Instagram and Facebook use happens in the mobile apps, and links opened there often arrive with no referrer. With no referrer and no tag, most tools count that visit as Direct. When the in-app browser names the app in its user agent, Virling credits the visit to instagram.com or facebook.com in Social instead; Messenger counts as Facebook. If the link opens in a plain system browser that does not name the app, the visit stays Direct.
  • A UTM tag. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, and instagram, ig, facebook and fb are known Social sources. The tag travels in the link itself, so it survives the app, the system browser and a missing referrer.

A referrer or a utm_source always wins over the in-app browser guess. The full rule order is in channels, and why Direct is your biggest traffic source covers the other causes of a large Direct row.

Step 1: add the script

Add the Virling script to the <head> of every page. It records each visit with its referrer and UTM tags, and sets the first-party visitor ID that checkout needs later. The app's setup screen shows the exact tag for your site, and add the script covers where it goes, with guides for common frameworks and site builders.

Step 2: tag every Instagram and Facebook link

Instagram captions are not clickable, so your links live in a few fixed places: the bio, story link stickers and direct messages. Give each one its own campaign:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=bio
https://yourdomain.com/pricing?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=story-pricing-launch
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=page-post-setup-guide
  • utm_source=instagram on every Instagram link and utm_source=facebook on every Facebook link, so each groups under one source and stays out of Direct.
  • utm_medium=social for organic posts, stories, bios and messages. Anything except a paid or email medium keeps the visit in Social.
  • utm_campaign names the link: a fixed bio for the bio link, and one value per story, post or group share, lowercase, with hyphens. The Campaign tab groups visitors and revenue by this value, so this is the tag that gives you revenue per story.
  • utm_content is optional. Use it for the spot, such as link-sticker or dm, and keep the story itself in the campaign so it shows up in reports.

If you change the bio link often, swap the campaign each time, for example bio-pricing then bio-webinar, so each version gets its own row. More naming rules are in which UTM parameters to use, and the UTM builder makes the link for you.

A link-in-bio page hosted on another domain becomes the referrer itself, so visits count as Referral from that domain, not as Instagram. Point the bio straight at your site, or at a links page on your own domain; clicks between your own pages never change where a visit came from.

If a long tagged URL is awkward to type into a story sticker, use a tracked link instead. In Virling, open the Campaign tab on the Channels card, click + Tracked link, pick Other, type instagram.com as the source and pick the page it should open. The short link adds the tags on the click, and the Campaign tab lists it with its visitors and revenue. Make one link per story. See tracked links.

Step 3: connect your payment provider

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs the payments and the visitor ID in checkout. In the app, open your site, then Setup, then Connect payments, paste the key your provider needs and click Connect and backfill; see payments. Each provider has its own page: Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce, all listed on integrations. The setup steps are in the docs for Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce.

If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the visitor ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Every case is covered in link checkout to visits.

People often tap a story, leave, and come back later by typing your URL or searching your name. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the payment goes to the tagged Instagram visit, as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. If they subscribe, every renewal keeps counting toward that link.

See revenue per story and bio link

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more, so people who tapped a story and paid later have time to show up.
  2. In the Channels card, open Social, or switch to the Source tab, and find instagram.com, l.instagram.com, facebook.com and lm.facebook.com. Each row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click a row to filter the whole page. The Pages card now shows which landing pages those visitors arrived on.
  4. Switch to the Campaign tab. With one campaign per link, the bio and each story are rows with their own visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

Instagram often introduces a buyer who comes back later through search. Switch the model to first touch in Site settings and compare the Instagram rows under both; history is recomputed, and you can switch back. First-touch or last-touch attribution explains the difference.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Instagram looks small but Direct jumped the day a story went out. Some links are untagged and opened in a browser that did not name the app. Check the bio, the link stickers and any links you sent in messages.
  • A domain you do not recognise shows up in Referral. It is likely your link-in-bio page. Point the bio at your own site instead.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A buyer says they found you on Instagram but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have seen a post without tapping a link and searched for you later. No analytics tool can count a view that never reached your site.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so Instagram's real visitor number can be higher than what any analytics tool shows. Revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from Instagram?

Tag the link in your bio and each story link sticker with utm_source=instagram and a utm_campaign for the place or story. Pass the visitor ID into checkout and use a tool that joins payments to visits. The Campaign tab then shows visitors and revenue per link.

What is l.instagram.com in my referrers?

It is the address Instagram routes outbound links through, so a click from a bio link or story can arrive with l.instagram.com as the referrer. Virling counts it as Social. Facebook does the same with l.facebook.com and lm.facebook.com.

Why do Instagram visits show up as Direct?

Links opened in the Instagram or Facebook app often arrive with no referrer. Virling credits them to the app when its in-app browser names itself in the user agent. If the link opens in a plain browser that does not, and the link has no tag, the visit is Direct. A utm_source tag prevents this.

Can I see which Instagram story sent a paying customer?

Not from the referrer, which only says instagram.com or l.instagram.com. Give each story link sticker its own utm_campaign, or its own tracked link, and the Campaign tab shows visitors and revenue per story.

Does a link-in-bio page break Instagram attribution?

A link page on another domain becomes the referrer, so visits count as Referral from that domain instead of Instagram. Link the bio straight to your site, or to a links page on your own domain, so the Instagram tag is kept.
