How long does a visitor take to become a customer?

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-10-26, updated 2026-11-24.

Time to pay is the number of days between a customer's first visit and their first payment. Measure it per channel, because a channel that pays slowly can look like it is failing when it is only taking longer.

How to measure it

  • Record the date of each visitor's first visit
  • Record the date of their first payment
  • Subtract, then take the median for each channel

Use the median, not the average. One customer who pays after three months pulls an average far from what a normal customer does.

Sorted, the ten are 0, 0, 1, 2, 4, 6, 9, 21, 45 and 112 days.

  • Average: they add up to 200 days, and 200 / 10 = 20 days
  • Median: with ten values it is halfway between the fifth and the sixth, (4 + 6) / 2 = 5 days
  • Paid within 7 days: 6 of 10, 60%
  • Paid within 30 days: 8 of 10, 80%
  • Paid within 90 days: 9 of 10, 90%

The average says 20 days, yet seven of the ten had paid by day 9. Leave out customer D and the average falls from 20 to 9.8 days (88 / 9), while the median only moves from 5 to 4. The shares show what the median hides: one customer in five took more than 30 days.

To copy it in a spreadsheet, put the first visits in column A and the first payments in column B from row 2, and the days in column C with =B2-A2. Then use =AVERAGE(C2:C11), =MEDIAN(C2:C11) and =COUNTIF(C2:C11,"<=7")/COUNT(C2:C11), changing 7 to 30 or 90. The sales cycle calculator gives the average and the median from the same two columns of dates.

Two limits. Only people who paid are in the list, so this is not the share of visitors who buy. And someone who first visited last week cannot have taken 30 days yet, so take customers whose first visit is at least 90 days old before you trust the 90-day share.

What the number tells you

  • Same day: the visitor already knew what they wanted. Make the path to checkout short
  • A few days: they compared options. Make pricing and proof easy to find
  • Weeks: they need reminding. Email and retargeting start to matter

If you offer a free trial, time to pay includes the trial, so a long median may only mean a long trial. Check how many trials pay per channel with the trial to paid calculator.

What changes the time

  • Free trial. If the card is charged when a 14-day trial ends, nobody on the trial can pay before day 14. Compare a median with the length of the trial first, and read trial-to-paid conversion by traffic source to see how many trials pay at all.
  • Price. A small one-off purchase can be decided in one visit. A larger one may need a comparison, a budget or someone else's approval. If you sell several plans, measure each one apart.
  • Sales calls. Booking the call, the call itself and an invoice with payment terms each add days that your website does not control. The same two dates, first visit and first payment, still work.
  • Repeat visits. Each return adds days, and it has to be recognised. If the first visit was on another device, or the cookie was cleared, the clock starts at a later visit and the measured time is shorter than the real one.

Why it changes how you read channels

If you judge a channel after seven days and it takes twenty to pay, you will cut a channel that works. Compare the median time to pay with the date range you are looking at before you decide.

Why it affects the lookback window

The lookback window is how far back a payment can look for a visit to credit. If most customers pay within 30 days, a 30-day window is enough. If your median is longer, widen it, or late payments lose their source. Which visit inside the window gets the credit depends on the model, compared in first-touch or last-touch attribution. Frequently asked questions

What is time to pay?

The number of days between a customer's first visit and their first payment.

Should I use the average or the median?

The median. A few very slow customers distort the average and hide what a typical customer does.

What lookback window should I use?

One longer than most of your customers take to pay. If the median is 12 days, 30 days is usually enough. Widen it if you see many payments with no source.

How long does it take a visitor to convert?

It depends on your price, your trial and whether a sales call is involved, so a number from another business says little about yours. Take the median days from first visit to first payment for your own customers, per channel.

How do I calculate the average time from first visit to first payment?

Subtract the first visit date from the first payment date for each customer, add up the days and divide by the number of customers. Work out the median too, because one very slow customer can pull the average far up.
