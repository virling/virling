How to track sponsorship ROI: newsletters, podcasts and YouTube

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-18.

To track sponsorship ROI, give every paid placement its own link: a UTM-tagged URL with the sponsor in the source and the placement in the campaign, or a tracked short link when the URL is spoken or printed. Carry the visitor ID into checkout, then join each payment to the visit that came before it. The campaign report then shows the revenue each newsletter issue, podcast read or YouTube video brought, and you can set it next to the fee you paid.

Opens, downloads, views and promo code counts do not answer that question on their own. A sponsorship pays back when the buyers it sent pay more than it cost, and only the payment data can tell you that.

Why sponsorship sales land in Direct

Most sponsorships send people to your site in a way that carries no trace of the sponsor.

  • Clicks from email apps often arrive with no referrer. Without tags, a click from a newsletter counts as Direct.
  • Spoken URLs in a podcast read or a video are typed in later. A typed address arrives with no referrer and no tag, so it is Direct too.
  • Printed URLs on a magazine page, a flyer or an event banner work the same way, unless a QR code or a short link adds the tags.
  • Searches for your name after hearing about you arrive from a search engine and count as Organic search.

So the fix is the same everywhere: the link people use must carry the sponsor with it, whether they click it, type it or scan it.

One UTM per sponsor

Tag every placement you pay for. Use the newsletter, show or channel as utm_source, the kind of placement as utm_medium, and the issue, episode or video plus the month as utm_campaign, all lowercase with hyphens. Never reuse one link across two sponsors, or their revenue lands in one row.

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=indie-weekly&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=indie-weekly-2026-11
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=devpod&utm_medium=podcast&utm_campaign=devpod-ep-142
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=jane-codes&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=jane-codes-setup-video

The UTM builder makes each link, and the UTM naming template gives your team one fixed list of sources and mediums to copy from. In Virling, a medium of email counts in the Email channel, a podcast source with no referrer counts in Referral, and a click from youtube.com counts in Social. If you would rather see every paid placement together, use paid as the medium: Virling then counts them all in the Paid channel, and the source still names the sponsor.

Setup per placement type

  • Newsletters. Send the newsletter your tagged link and ask them to use it everywhere your ad appears. If the ad has two links, such as the headline and a button, tell them apart with utm_content. Give each issue its own campaign, so a repeat booking shows whether the second send did as well as the first. Newsletter revenue tracking covers tagging each issue and how email visits are sorted.
  • Podcasts. The host reads the URL out loud, so it has to be short. Use a vanity path on your domain, such as yourdomain.com/devpod, that redirects to the tagged page, or a tracked link in Virling. Put the same link in the show notes. Podcast revenue tracking covers promo codes and listeners who search your name later.
  • YouTube. Put the tagged link first in the description and in a pinned comment, with utm_content to tell them apart. If the creator says a URL in the video, give them a vanity path or a tracked link for that too. YouTube revenue tracking covers tagging every video link and reading revenue per video.
  • Print and events. A printed URL has to be typed, so print a short link or a QR code that opens one.

Tracked short links for spoken or printed URLs

A tracked link is a short URL on your Virling server that redirects to a page on your site with the tags added. You do not have to build the tagged URL yourself, and it is short enough to read out loud or print.

  • Pick the place. Newsletter, YouTube, Podcast and QR code are built in. Pick Other to type the sponsor's name as the source instead.
  • Pick the page it should open. Any page on your site or its subdomains.
  • Share the short URL. The link's id becomes utm_campaign, so its visits show under the Campaign tab. Bot clicks are not counted, and tags already on the page are kept.

For sponsors you work with again and again, the Partners page goes one step further. Add the newsletter, show or creator by name and the page it should open, and Virling makes a short link for them. Partners are ranked by revenue, with visitors, paying customers, revenue net of refunds and refund rate. Copy report link gives the sponsor a read-only page with only their own numbers.

Set it up with Virling

  1. Add the script. Put the Virling script in the head of every page, with your domain in data-site. It reads the tags on the landing page and sets the visitor ID. See script attributes.
  2. Connect a payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. The setup is in link checkout to visits, and every provider is listed under integrations.
  3. Make one link per placement. A tagged URL, a tracked link or a partner link, as above.
  4. Read the numbers. Pick a range that covers the placement and a few weeks after it, then open the Campaign tab in the Channels card. Each placement has its own row with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. Open a payment to see the buyer's journey, with the credited visit marked.

Compare the fee with attributed revenue

Once each placement has its own row, the return on a sponsorship is the revenue credited to it divided by what you paid:

sponsorship ROAS = attributed revenue / placement fee

A result above 1 means the buyers it sent paid more than the fee, but not that it made you money. To pay for itself, the revenue times your gross margin has to cover the fee, so break-even ROAS is 1 divided by your margin. The ROAS calculator works out both from the fee, the revenue and your margin.

  • Wait before you judge. Many buyers come back days or weeks after the first click. With the default model, last non-direct, a buyer who clicked the sponsor's link and later typed your URL is still credited to the sponsor, as long as the click is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. See model and lookback window.
  • Count renewals. A subscription renewal takes the attribution of the customer's first payment, so a sponsorship keeps earning after the month it ran. Look again after a few billing cycles. See renewals.
  • Treat it as a floor. Buyers who heard about you and searched your name later are not in the sponsor's row. The attributed revenue is the least the placement brought, not the most.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • The placement has visits but no revenue. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If much of your revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • The sponsor shows as Direct. The link was edited, the redirect drops the query string, or the sponsor used your plain URL. Open the link they published in a private window and check the address bar.
  • Two sponsors share one row. They use the same source or campaign. Give each sponsor its own source and each placement its own campaign.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track the ROI of a sponsorship?

Give each sponsorship its own tagged link, with the newsletter, show or channel as utm_source and the placement as utm_campaign. Pass the visitor ID into checkout so a tool can join each payment to the visit before it. Then divide the revenue credited to that campaign by the fee you paid.

How do I track a newsletter sponsorship?

Ask the newsletter to use your tagged link instead of your plain URL, with the newsletter as the source, email as the medium and the issue as the campaign. Email apps often send no referrer, so an untagged click lands in Direct. With the tags, Virling counts the click in the Email channel under the newsletter's name.

How do I track a sponsorship read out loud on a podcast or video?

Read out a short URL that adds the tags for the listener: a vanity path on your domain that redirects to a tagged page, or a tracked link in Virling. A listener who types the plain domain arrives as Direct, and one who searches for your name arrives as Organic search.

What UTM medium should I use for sponsorships?

Use the medium of the placement: email for newsletters, podcast for podcasts, and social for YouTube. If you want every paid placement grouped together, use paid as the medium instead; Virling then counts them all in the Paid channel, and the source still names the sponsor.

What is a good ROAS for a sponsorship?

There is no single number. A sponsorship pays for itself when the attributed revenue times your gross margin is more than the fee, so break-even ROAS is 1 divided by your margin. For subscriptions, look again after a few months, because renewals keep adding revenue to the original sponsorship.
