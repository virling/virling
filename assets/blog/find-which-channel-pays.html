How to find which marketing channel pays for your SaaS

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-10-21.

TL;DR: Visitor counts tell you which channel is loud. Revenue per visitor tells you which channel pays. You can get it in an afternoon: give every visitor an ID, pass that ID into checkout, and join each payment back to the visit that came before it.

Why visitor counts lie

Most founders pick channels by traffic. The channel that fills your graph is often the one that pays least. A Reddit post can bring 2,000 visitors and zero customers. A single answer in ChatGPT can bring 40 visitors and 6 customers.

You only see that when revenue sits next to traffic, per channel.

Step 1: give every visitor an ID

Your analytics script should set one first-party ID per visitor (or a daily hash if you run cookieless). Store the first source you saw them from: referrer, UTM tags, or "direct".

Step 2: pass the ID into checkout

This is the step everyone skips. When someone clicks Buy, put the visitor ID into the checkout's metadata. On Stripe that is metadata on the Checkout Session or client_reference_id. Dodo Payments and Polar have the same idea. The full Stripe setup is in tracking Stripe revenue by traffic source.

Now every payment carries the visitor who made it.

Step 3: join payments to visits

For each payment, find that visitor's visits before the payment date. Pick a rule and stick to it:

  • Last non-direct visit: credit the last real source before they paid (the common default)
  • First touch: credit whatever first brought them

Use a lookback window, 30 to 90 days for most SaaS. The trade-offs are in first-touch or last-touch attribution.

Step 4: read revenue per visitor, not visitors

For each channel: revenue divided by visitors. Compare it with your site average. The revenue per visitor calculator does the sums for you.

  • 1.8x average: double down
  • 0.3x average: stop spending time there, or fix the page those visitors land on

The landing page with lots of visitors and a low ratio is usually your cheapest fix.

Step 5: check it weekly

Channels drift. Look at the same four numbers every Monday: revenue, new customers, revenue per visitor, and the channel table sorted by revenue.

When the numbers mislead

  • Few payments. A channel with three customers moves by a third when one more pays. Use a longer date range before you rank it.
  • Long sales cycles. If people take two months to pay, this month's revenue came from earlier visitors, so a channel you just started looks weak. Read a longer range, and use an attribution window longer than your buying cycle.
  • Refunds. Revenue before refunds flatters a channel whose buyers ask for their money back. Use revenue after refunds and check the refund rate by traffic source.
  • Unattributed share. A payment with no visitor ID, or with no visit inside the window, belongs to no channel. If that is a large share of revenue, go back to step 2 before you compare channels. It is not the same as Direct, which is a visit with no known source: see why Direct is your biggest traffic source.

Steps for SaaS, stores and agencies

SaaS

Save the visitor ID when the trial starts, because the payment can come weeks later with no visit right before it. Credit each renewal to the channel of the customer's first payment, then compare which channels' customers are still paying. Revenue attribution for SaaS covers trials, renewals and MRR by channel.

Stores

Save the visitor ID on each order, which revenue attribution for ecommerce covers for Shopify and WooCommerce. Tag every email and SMS link, or those visits land in Direct. Then compare revenue per visitor by channel, net of refunds.

Agencies

Repeat the five steps for each client, with its own script tag and payment connection, and use one UTM naming convention for all of them. Send each client revenue and revenue per visitor by source on one page every week. Virling for agencies shows that report under your brand.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find which marketing channel brings paying customers?

Give every visitor an ID, pass that ID into checkout metadata, and join each payment to the visits before it. Revenue per channel divided by visitors per channel shows which channel pays.

Which attribution model should a SaaS use?

Last non-direct visit is the common default: it credits the last real source before the payment. First touch credits whatever first brought the customer. Pick one and keep it so weeks compare fairly.

How long should the lookback window be?

30 to 90 days for most SaaS. Use the longer end if buyers usually visit several times before they pay.
