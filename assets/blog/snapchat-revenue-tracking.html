Snapchat revenue tracking: which snaps and stories bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-20.

To see which Snapchat visits turn into paying customers, put a UTM-tagged link on every Snap, Story and your Public Profile, with utm_source=snapchat and a campaign for that post. Carry the visitor ID into checkout, or into your app's purchase, then join each payment to the visit that came before it. You then see the revenue and revenue per visitor each Story and Spotlight post brings. Views, swipe-ups and link clicks in Snapchat's own insights do not tell you this.

This works the same for an online store and for an app that sells subscriptions. The only difference is where the payment comes from: a store checkout, or a web paywall connected through RevenueCat.

Where Snapchat clicks come from

Organic Snapchat sends people to your site through a few kinds of links, and each one should be tagged:

  • Snaps and Stories. A link attached to a Snap or a Story opens one page, usually a product page, a landing page or your app's page. Stories disappear after a day, so each one is a short burst of clicks.
  • Spotlight. Spotlight shows your videos to people who do not follow you. Where a Spotlight post cannot carry a link, viewers who want more go to your profile, so the profile link has to be tagged too.
  • Your Public Profile. The website on your profile is a link like any other, and it deserves a fixed campaign such as snap-profile.

Why Snapchat clicks show up as Direct

There are three ways a visit can be credited, and for Snapchat only one of them works reliably.

  • The referrer. Links opened in the Snapchat app often arrive with no referrer at all. When one does arrive, Snapchat is not on Virling's list of known social hosts, so the visit lands in Referral under that host. Either way, a referrer is a host only and cannot tell you which Story the click came from.
  • The in-app browser. Virling credits some apps' in-app browsers, such as Instagram and TikTok, when the app names itself in the user agent, but Snapchat is not on that list. With no referrer and no tag, a click from the Snapchat app counts as Direct, in Virling and in most other tools.
  • A UTM tag. The tag travels in the link itself, so it survives the app, the system browser and a missing referrer. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source. Because snapchat is not a known social source yet, a tagged Snapchat visit counts as Referral, not Social, even with utm_medium=social. It is still out of Direct, and its campaign is still stored.

If your Direct traffic rises the day you post a Story, this is usually why. The full rule order is in channels, and why Direct is your biggest traffic source covers the other causes.

How to set up Snapchat revenue tracking with Virling

1. Tag every Snapchat link

Give each Snap, Story or Spotlight post its own campaign, start every campaign with snap-, and use utm_content to say where the link sat:

https://yourdomain.com/products/linen-apron?utm_source=snapchat&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=snap-apron-restock&utm_content=story
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=snapchat&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=snap-profile&utm_content=profile
  • utm_source=snapchat on every Snapchat link, so they share one source and stay out of Direct.
  • utm_medium=social for organic posts. Use a paid medium such as cpc only for Snapchat ads, so ad clicks land in the Paid channel and organic posts stay apart.
  • utm_campaign names the post: one value per Snap, Story or Spotlight post, lowercase, with hyphens, starting with snap-. The Campaign tab groups visitors and revenue by this value, so this is the tag that gives you revenue per post, and the prefix keeps your Snapchat rows together among other Referral campaigns.
  • utm_content names the spot: story, snap, spotlight or profile. Virling stores it with each visit; keep what you want to compare in the campaign so it shows up in reports.

The UTM builder makes the link for you, and which UTM parameters to use has more naming rules.

If long tagged URLs are awkward to paste into Snapchat, use tracked links instead. In Virling, open the Campaign tab on the Channels card, click + Tracked link, pick Other, type snapchat as the source and pick the page it should open. The short link adds the tags on the click, and the Campaign tab lists it with its visitors and revenue. Make one link per post. See tracked links.

2. Add the script

Paste the Virling script before </head> on every page of your site, and on your web paywall if you have one. It reads the UTM tags on the landing page and sets one first-party cookie, virling_vid, the visitor ID that checkout will carry. Leave cookieless mode off for this site: without the visitor ID, payments cannot be linked to visits. See add the script.

3. Connect a payment provider and pass the visitor ID

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs a connected payment provider and the visitor ID in checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Stores on Shopify or WooCommerce add a small theme snippet that stores the ID on each order. The full list is on the integrations page, and the setup for each provider is in link checkout to visits.

For an app, send Snapchat visitors to your site or web paywall rather than straight to the app store, set the visitor ID as the virling_vid attribute on the RevenueCat customer before the purchase, and connect RevenueCat. Renewals keep the channel of the first payment, so a Story that brought a subscriber keeps the credit for every renewal. See set up Virling for a mobile app. Someone who goes from Snapchat straight to the store and buys in the app has no visit to credit; that purchase counts as Unattributed.

Make a test payment through a tagged Snapchat link, then check that it appears on the Payments page with your snap- campaign in its journey.

See revenue per Snap and Story

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more, so short-lived Stories and slower buyers are both in it.
  2. In the Channels card, open Referral. Tagged Snapchat visits are here, together with any visit that arrived with a snapchat.com referrer.
  3. Click the Referral row to filter the whole page, then switch to the Campaign tab. Each snap- campaign is a row with its own visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  4. The Pages card shows which landing pages those visitors arrived on, so you can tell a product page that sells from one that only gets clicks.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

Snapchat viewers often see a Story, leave, and come back later by typing your address or searching your name. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the payment goes to the tagged Snapchat visit, as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. A later search or other tagged visit does take it, so switch the model to first touch in Site settings to see which posts start journeys; history is recomputed, and you can switch back. What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • No Snapchat campaigns, but Direct jumped the day you posted. Some links are untagged. Check your newest Stories and your profile link first.
  • Snapchat visits are not under Social. That is expected: Snapchat is not a known social source, so tagged visits land in Referral. Find them by their snap- campaigns.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout or RevenueCat. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A buyer says they found you on Snapchat but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have watched without tapping the link and searched for you later. No analytics tool can count a view that never reached your site.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so Snapchat's real visitor number can be higher than what any analytics tool shows. Revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from Snapchat?

Put a UTM-tagged link on each Snap or Story and on your Public Profile, with utm_source=snapchat and a utm_campaign for that post. Pass the visitor ID into checkout or your app's purchase and use a tool that joins payments to visits. The Campaign tab then shows visitors and revenue per post.

Why do Snapchat visits show up as Direct?

Links opened inside the Snapchat app often arrive with no referrer, and Snapchat is not one of the in-app browsers Virling recognizes. With no referrer and no tag, the visit is Direct. A utm_source tag in the link keeps it out of Direct.

Which channel do tagged Snapchat visits land in?

Referral. Snapchat is not on Virling's list of known social sources yet, so a visit tagged utm_source=snapchat counts as Referral, even with utm_medium=social. Start every Snapchat campaign with snap- so you can find them together in the Campaign tab.

Can I see which Snap or Story sent a paying customer?

Yes, if each one has its own utm_campaign or its own tracked link. The Campaign tab shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor for each campaign, and each payment's journey shows the visit it was credited to.

Does this work for app purchases?

For purchases that start on the web, yes. Send Snapchat visitors to your site or web paywall, pass the visitor ID to RevenueCat or your web checkout, and renewals keep the credit. Someone who goes straight from Snapchat to the app store and buys in the app has no visit to credit, so that purchase counts as Unattributed.
