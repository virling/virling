Product Hunt launch revenue: how to measure it

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-16.

Product Hunt launch revenue is the money paid by customers whose credited visit came from producthunt.com or from a link tagged as Product Hunt. To measure it, tag the link on your launch page, carry the visitor ID into checkout, and add up the payments from that source over the days and weeks after the launch. The signup spike on launch day does not tell you this.

A launch day is loud. Visitors and signups jump, then fall back within a day or two. Whether the launch paid off shows up later, when trials end and people decide to pay. Measure that, and compare it with what the rest of your site earns per visitor.

Tag the launch link

There are two ways a visit gets marked as Product Hunt, and you want both working.

  • The referrer. A click from producthunt.com in a browser usually arrives with producthunt.com as the referrer. In Virling, producthunt.com is in the Communities channel and shows as Product Hunt in the Source tab.
  • A tag on the link. Clicks with no referrer, for example from an app or a browser that strips it, land in Direct unless the link says where they came from. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, and when that is missing it reads ref, so ?ref=producthunt works too. producthunt is a known Communities source.

Put your own tag on the website link of your launch page, so it does not depend on anything Product Hunt adds:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=producthunt&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=launch-2026-11
  • utm_source=producthunt groups every Product Hunt link under one source.
  • utm_medium=community keeps the visit in Communities. A paid medium such as cpc would move it to Paid.
  • utm_campaign names the launch. If you launch again later, or link from the maker comment and a follow-up post, give each its own campaign and the Campaign tab keeps them apart.

Prefer a short link? In Virling, open the Campaign tab on the Channels card, click + Tracked link, pick Product Hunt and the page it should open. The link adds the source, a community medium and its own campaign on the click. See tracked links and which UTM parameters to use, or build one with the UTM builder.

Make sure payments can be credited

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs the visitor ID in checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Every provider is covered in link checkout to visits. Set this up before launch day. The product launch tracking checklist covers the rest of the preparation, and if you built the app with an AI coding tool and sell through Stripe Checkout, one prompt adds the script and the visitor ID.

Follow payments for weeks, not the day

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range from launch day to four to six weeks after it.
  2. In the Channels card, switch to the Source tab and find Product Hunt. The row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click the Product Hunt row to filter the whole page. The revenue chart now shows when Product Hunt money arrived, day by day, and the Pages card shows where those visitors landed.
  4. Switch to the Campaign tab to see the launch campaign on its own, if you tagged it.
  5. Change the range to launch week only, then to the weeks after, and compare. The difference is the revenue that arrived late.
  6. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

You can also log the launch on the Launches page with its name, link, channel and day. Virling shows the visitors, new customers and revenue of that day and the day after, and marks the launch on the revenue chart. Those two days count all traffic, not only Product Hunt, so use them as context and the Source and Campaign numbers for the real credit.

Late conversions: trials that pay after launch week

Most launch visitors who end up paying do not pay on launch day. They sign up, try the product, and pay when the trial ends or later. If you judge the launch after a week, you miss them. The Time to pay card shows the median days from first visit to first payment per channel; keep watching the launch at least that long past the end of your trial. See how long a visitor takes to become a customer.

  • The lookback window. A payment is credited only to visits inside the window before it, 90 days by default. If your trial plus your typical decision time is longer, widen it in Site settings, or late Product Hunt payments lose their source. See the model and lookback window.
  • Card-up-front trials. The first real charge happens without a visit, when the provider charges the card. For Stripe, Polar and Dodo Payments, Virling reads the trial start from the $0 checkout and credits the later charge to the visits before the trial began. See renewals and free trials.
  • Trials with no card. The user pays through checkout when they upgrade, and that checkout carries the visitor ID like any other payment, as long as you pass it.
  • Later visits from other channels. With the default model, last non-direct, a visitor who found you on Product Hunt and came back weeks later through a search result is credited to search. To see which channel introduced your buyers, switch the model to first touch in Site settings and compare. History is recomputed, and you can switch back.

To compare how well launch trials convert against your other channels, send a goal when the trial starts and divide payers by trials per channel, as in trial-to-paid conversion by traffic source. Then check the Retention page after a month or two: it groups customers by the channel of their first payment, so you can see whether Product Hunt customers keep paying.

An illustration

Made-up numbers to show the method; they are not Virling's own launch. Say a launch sends 3,000 visitors from Product Hunt in its first week, and the product has a 14-day trial at $29 a month. In launch week, 5 customers pay, $145. Over the next five weeks, as trials end, 11 more pay, for 16 customers and $464 in first payments. Judged at the end of launch week, the launch made $145, or about $0.05 per visitor. Judged at six weeks, it made $464, about $0.15 per visitor, and more than two thirds of it arrived after launch week. Compare that with your site average over the same range, and keep an eye on renewals: if those customers stay, every renewal keeps counting toward Product Hunt.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Product Hunt looks small but Direct jumped on launch day. Clicks with no referrer and no tag. Tag the link on the launch page.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • Trial payments show as Unattributed. The trial started before Stripe was connected, or the provider is not one of the three with trial-start credit. The trial-to-paid post covers the workarounds.
  • Revenue keeps arriving weeks later. Normal for trials and considered purchases. Use a longer range before you decide.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so Product Hunt's real visitor number can be higher than any analytics tool shows. Their revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Most launches go out in more than one place. Measure the others the same way: a Show HN or front-page post, posts and milestones on Indie Hackers, and the directories you list in after the launch.

Frequently asked questions

How do I measure revenue from a Product Hunt launch?

Tag the link on your launch page with utm_source=producthunt and a campaign for the launch, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and use a tool that joins payments to visits. Then add up the payments credited to Product Hunt over the weeks after the launch, not only on launch day.

How long after a Product Hunt launch should I keep measuring?

At least as long as your trial plus the median time your customers take to pay. For a 14-day trial that is usually four to six weeks. Most launch traffic arrives in the first two days, but the payments it leads to can arrive much later.

Why do Product Hunt visits show up as Direct?

Some clicks arrive with no referrer, for example from an app or a browser that strips it. With no referrer and no tag the visit is Direct. A utm_source=producthunt or ref=producthunt on the link keeps it credited to Product Hunt.

Do trials that convert after launch week count toward the launch?

Yes, if the Product Hunt visit is inside your lookback window, 90 days by default in Virling, and no later non-direct visit took the credit. For card-up-front trials on Stripe, Polar and Dodo Payments, Virling credits the later charge to the visits before the trial started.

Is the signup spike a good measure of a launch?

No. Launch day brings curious visitors who sign up to look around. Compare paying customers and revenue per visitor from Product Hunt with your site average over the following weeks instead.
