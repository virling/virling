Microsoft Copilot traffic: which Copilot visits pay

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-18.

To see which Microsoft Copilot visits turn into paying customers, count every visit that arrives from copilot.microsoft.com or carries a Copilot utm_source, keep it apart from Bing search, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and join each payment to the visit before it. You then see the revenue Copilot referral traffic brings and what each Copilot visitor is worth next to Bing, Google, social and email. A visit count alone cannot tell you that, because Copilot usually sends few visitors and the question is how many of them pay.

This post covers Microsoft Copilot and how it differs from Bing. For ChatGPT, see ChatGPT revenue tracking; for search engines, see organic search revenue tracking; for AI assistants in general, see is AI search traffic worth anything.

Copilot and Bing are two sources

Both are Microsoft products, so they are easy to mix up in a report. Virling reads the referrer host of each visit, and the two hosts go to different channels.

  • The copilot.microsoft.com referrer. A click on a link in Copilot in the browser usually arrives with copilot.microsoft.com as the referrer. Virling puts that host in the AI channel and lists it as Copilot. The referrer is a host only, so it never says which answer or question the click came from.
  • The bing.com referrer. A click on a Bing results page arrives from bing.com or www.bing.com. Virling puts that host in Organic search and lists it as Bing, next to Google and the other search engines. That includes any AI answer shown on a Bing results page: the click still comes from bing.com, so it counts as Bing search, not as Copilot.
  • A utm_source tag. When the referrer is missing, Virling reads utm_source. A visit tagged utm_source=copilot.microsoft.com is credited to Copilot, in the AI channel, exactly as if the referrer had been there, and a bare utm_source=copilot also counts as AI. utm_source=bing or utm_source=bing.com counts as Organic search. A referrer always wins over the tag, so a visit from bing.com stays in search whatever its utm_source says. You only see these tags on links that carry them; Virling does not add them.
  • No referrer and no tag. Links opened from an app, or anywhere else the referrer is dropped, can arrive with neither. Nothing in the visit names Copilot or Bing, so it counts as Direct, in Virling and in most other tools.

If a Copilot link reaches you from a Microsoft address other than copilot.microsoft.com, that host is not on Virling's AI list: the visit counts as Referral and shows under its own host name in the Source tab. So the Copilot row is a floor, not a full count. People who read an answer and later type your name into a browser or a search engine arrive as Direct or Organic search; no analytics tool can tie that visit to the answer. The full rule order is in channels, and the traffic source checker shows how a given landing URL and referrer are credited: try copilot.microsoft.com and bing.com as the referrer to see one land in AI and the other in Organic search.

One exception to the order: a paid medium wins over everything. A Microsoft Advertising link with utm_medium=cpc counts as Paid even though it arrives from bing.com, so ads you run stay apart from organic Bing visits. Without that tag, an ad click from bing.com looks the same as an organic one.

Let Copilot read your pages

Microsoft says Copilot grounds its web answers in Bing search, so Copilot can only link to pages Bing can read. Bingbot is the crawler that fetches them. Check that robots.txt does not block it, and that your pages are in the Bing index.

Bot fetches are never counted as visitors in Virling. Virling recognizes live answer fetches from ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity, but no Copilot bot of that kind, so Copilot answers do not show as fetches on the AI card. The AI crawler checker reads your robots.txt for the AI bots that have their own names.

Set up Copilot revenue tracking in Virling

  1. Add the tracking script to every page. See the tracking script. Nothing Copilot-specific is needed: the referrer and the tag are read on every visit.
  2. Connect your payment provider: Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify or WooCommerce.
  3. Pass the visitor ID into checkout. Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links and Dodo Payments links get it from the script on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. See link checkout to visits. Without this step Copilot shows visitors and no revenue.
  4. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more. Copilot usually sends fewer visitors than search, so short ranges are noisy.
  5. In the Channels card, click AI, or switch to the Source tab, and find Copilot. The row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  6. To compare it with Bing, click Organic search and find the Bing row. Each row has its own visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  7. Click the Copilot row to filter the whole page. The Pages card now shows which landing pages Copilot sends people to, and which of them lead to payments.
  8. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

How Virling credits the payment

People often click a link in Copilot, leave, and come back days later by typing your URL. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the payment goes to the Copilot visit, as long as it started inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. If the buyer subscribes, every renewal keeps counting toward Copilot. If they come back through a Bing search or a newsletter before paying, that later visit takes the credit instead.

To see how often Copilot starts journeys that another channel finishes, switch the model to first touch in Site settings. First-touch or last-touch attribution explains the difference. Changing the model recalculates every past payment, so you can switch back.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Copilot has visitors but no revenue. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A buyer says they found you in Copilot but the payment is credited to Bing. They may have seen the answer on a Bing results page, which counts as Bing search, or come back through a Bing search before paying.
  • A buyer says they found you in Copilot but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have read the answer without clicking, or opened the link from an app that dropped the referrer. That visit has no trace of Copilot.
  • Copilot shows no visits at all. Check that robots.txt does not block Bingbot and that your pages are in the Bing index.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted, so Copilot's real visitor number can be higher than any analytics tool shows. Their revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

For the search side of the split, including engines that use Bing's results, see which Bing search visitors pay. Google has the same kind of split: Gemini visits count as AI, while clicks from AI Overviews count as Organic search.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which Copilot visits turn into paying customers?

Use a tool that groups copilot.microsoft.com visits into their own source and joins each payment to the visit before it. In Virling, pass the visitor ID into checkout, then open the AI channel and the Copilot row: it shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, and the Payments page shows each buyer's visits.

Is Copilot traffic counted as Bing search?

No. Virling puts copilot.microsoft.com in the AI channel and bing.com in Organic search, as two separate rows. A click on a result or an answer shown on a bing.com page arrives from bing.com, so it counts as Bing search, even when that page shows Copilot answers.

How do I track traffic from Microsoft Copilot?

Look for copilot.microsoft.com in your referrers. Virling lists every visit with that referrer as Copilot in the AI channel. A visit with no referrer but utm_source=copilot.microsoft.com or utm_source=copilot also counts as AI, while utm_source=bing counts as Organic search.

Why does some Copilot traffic show up as Direct?

A visit with no referrer and no utm_source has nothing that names Copilot, so it is Direct in Virling and in most other tools. That can happen when a link is opened from an app that drops the referrer. Treat the Copilot number as a floor.

Does a Copilot visit still get credit if the buyer pays a week later?

With the default model, last non-direct, yes. If the buyer comes back by typing your URL, that Direct visit does not take the credit; the payment goes to the Copilot visit as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default.
