DuckDuckGo, Brave and privacy search revenue tracking

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-20.

To see which privacy search visits turn into payments, record the referrer on each visit, carry the visitor ID into checkout, then join each payment to the visit that came before it. Virling counts duckduckgo.com, search.brave.com, ecosia.org, kagi.com, startpage.com and qwant.com as known search hosts, so a click from any of them is Organic search, with its own row next to google.com and bing.com. You then see the visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor each engine brings.

Buyers who pick a privacy search engine often pick a privacy browser and blockers too. That changes what reaches your analytics, so this post covers what these engines send, why some of their visits show as Direct, and where cookies fit.

How Virling classifies these engines

Every session gets one channel, decided by its first pageview. The rules run in order and the first match wins: a paid or email utm_medium, then the referrer host, then utm_source, and Direct only when none of them is there.

  • By referrer. duckduckgo.com, search.brave.com, ecosia.org, kagi.com, startpage.com and qwant.com are known search hosts. A leading www. is ignored and subdomains match, so html.duckduckgo.com counts as DuckDuckGo. Only Brave's search host counts: a link from the brave.com website itself is Referral.
  • By utm_source, when there is no referrer. duckduckgo, brave and ecosia are known search sources. kagi, startpage and qwant are not on that list, so use the host instead, such as utm_source=kagi.com, or the visit counts as Referral.
  • Each engine is its own row. Some of these engines draw on other engines' results, but the click comes from their own site, so DuckDuckGo is never folded into Bing. Bing revenue tracking covers that side.

The full rule order and host lists are in channels.

What referrer data these engines send

When someone clicks a result, the browser tells your site where the click came from in the referrer. Browsers send only the origin by default when the click crosses to another site, such as https://duckduckgo.com/, and Virling stores only the host. The search query is not part of it.

  • You get the engine, not the keyword. No script on your site can recover what the visitor typed, so no tool that runs on your pages can show revenue by privacy search keyword. The landing page is the closest stand-in: it is the page that ranked.
  • You may get nothing at all. A browser setting, an extension or the engine's own referrer policy can remove the referrer. Then the visit has no sign of where it came from.
  • Untagged ads look organic. Ads on some of these engines that arrive with no utm_medium look like organic clicks. Tag ad links with utm_medium=cpc so they move to Paid.

Why some visits show as Direct

A visit with no referrer, no UTM tag and no known in-app browser is Direct. So a privacy search click whose referrer was removed lands in Direct, next to typed addresses and bookmarks. Why Direct is your biggest traffic source covers everything else that ends up there.

Two things keep this from costing you the revenue:

  • Last non-direct. The default model credits the payment to the latest visit in the lookback window, 90 days by default, that was not Direct. If the buyer found you through Brave Search last week and came back with no referrer today, the Brave visit gets the credit. See the attribution model.
  • Tag what you control. Organic results cannot be tagged, but links you place yourself can. A utm_source of duckduckgo, brave, ecosia or kagi.com puts a visit with no referrer back under Organic search. The UTM builder makes the link.

Some privacy visits are not counted at all. An ad blocker that stops the script sends nothing. If your tag has data-respect-gpc, a browser with Global Privacy Control turned on sends nothing either. Payments from those visitors still count in your total, as Unattributed. See Unattributed revenue.

How cookieless tracking fits

Revenue attribution needs a stable visitor ID that reaches checkout. Virling keeps it in a first-party cookie, virling_vid, which holds a random ID and is set on your own domain.

  • Cookieless mode counts visitors with a daily salted hash instead. Visits, referrers and the Organic search channel still work, but there is no ID to pass into checkout, so new payments show as Unattributed.
  • Cookieless until consent counts every visit without a cookie and sets the cookie once the visitor accepts it. Payments from visitors who accepted are attributed to their search visit.

Cookieless analytics and revenue explains the trade, cookie consent and revenue tracking shows what a banner does to it, and cookieless mode has the setup.

Set it up in Virling

  1. Put the tag in the <head> of every page. It records the referrer and any UTM tags on the first pageview. See add the script.
  2. Connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links and Dodo Payments links get it on click; for a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie. See link checkout to visits and the integrations.
  3. Pick a range of a few months, since search visitors often take days or weeks to pay.
  4. On the Channels card, click Organic search, then open the Source tab. duckduckgo.com, search.brave.com, ecosia.org and kagi.com are separate rows, each with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  5. Click a row to filter the page to that engine, then read the Landing page tab of the Pages card to see which pages its searchers arrived on and the revenue credited to each.

Judge revenue per visitor, not totals, and wait until a row has several payments. A small engine with a handful of visitors and one payment says little either way. Organic search revenue tracking covers reading the search channel as a whole.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from DuckDuckGo?

Add the tracking script to every page, connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout, so each payment joins the visit before it. Visits that arrive from duckduckgo.com count as Organic search, and the Source tab shows DuckDuckGo's visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.

Are Brave Search, Ecosia and Kagi counted as organic search?

Yes. search.brave.com, ecosia.org and kagi.com are known search hosts, as are duckduckgo.com, startpage.com and qwant.com. A visit from any of them counts as Organic search and shows as its own row, not as Bing or Google.

Why do some privacy search visits show as Direct?

A visit is Direct when it arrives with no referrer and no UTM tag. If a browser setting or an extension removes the referrer, the click from the search engine carries no sign of where it came from. A payment still goes to an earlier search visit by the same visitor, if there is one in the lookback window.

Can I see which DuckDuckGo keywords bring paying customers?

No. The referrer tells your site which engine sent the visitor, not what they searched, so no script on your site can record the query. The landing page is the closest stand-in, since it is the page that ranked.

Can I track privacy search revenue without cookies?

You can count the visits without cookies, but not join them to payments. Cookieless mode has no stable visitor ID to pass into checkout, so new payments show as Unattributed. Cookieless until consent counts every visit and attributes payments from visitors who accept the cookie.
