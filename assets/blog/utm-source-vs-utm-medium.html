utm_source vs utm_medium: the difference, with examples

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-24.

utm_source says where the link is: the site, app, list or partner, such as linkedin or newsletter. utm_medium says what kind of channel that is, such as social, email or cpc. A LinkedIn post and a LinkedIn ad share the source linkedin; the medium, social or paidsocial, tells them apart. If a value is a name, it is a source. If many places could share it, it is a medium.

Google describes them the same way in its campaign URL guide: the source is the referrer, for example google, newsletter4 or billboard, and the medium is the marketing medium, for example cpc, banner or email. For the other three tags, see which UTM parameters to use to trace revenue and utm_content vs utm_term.

How Virling uses each one

Every visit gets one channel from its first page. The rules run in this order and the first match wins:

  • The medium, for Paid and Email only. cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social or paidsocial is Paid. email, newsletter or e-mail is Email. The referrer and the source cannot change that.
  • The referrer, when there is one. With any other medium, the site the click came from decides: AI, Organic search, Communities, Social or Referral.
  • The source, when there is no referrer. A known name such as chatgpt, google, reddit or linkedin gives its channel. Any other value is Referral. When utm_source is missing, ref is used.
  • With no referrer and no source, a known in-app browser gives the visit to its app. Otherwise any other medium is Referral, and no medium is Direct.

So utm_medium=social does not make a visit Social by itself: the referrer or the source does. The known hosts and source names are listed in how channels are decided.

Neither tag has a tab of its own. The Source tab lists the sites that sent the visit. With no referrer, a source typed as a known domain, such as linkedin.com, shows there, and a bare name such as linkedin sets the channel but no row. To get a row with visitors and revenue for each link, give it its own utm_campaign, which fills the Campaign tab.

Common mix-ups

  • The two are swapped. utm_source=email&utm_medium=newsletter still lands in Email in Virling, because newsletter is an email medium, but the source no longer says which list it was.
  • The same word in both. linkedin / linkedin says nothing about whether it was a post or an ad.
  • The payment type is in the source. linkedin-ads splits one platform into two sources. Keep linkedin and let the medium say it is paid.
  • The campaign is in the medium. utm_medium=black-friday belongs in utm_campaign. A medium should still make sense next year.
  • A new spelling of a medium. Paid Social, paid-social and social-paid are three values, and none of them is a paid medium in Virling.
  • The email tool is the source. Your email software is not where the reader is. Name the list or the series, so the source survives a change of tool.
  • Another newsletter, sponsored. Its name is the source. Choose email or cpc as the medium once, depending on whether you count it with your emails or your ads, and keep to it.

How to fix links you already shared

A tag is read when the page loads, so a fix changes only the visits that come after it. Start with the links that still get clicks.

  • List the links. Put each URL in a sheet with one column for the source and one for the medium. Read down the medium column: every platform, list or partner name in it is a mix-up.
  • Check the spelling. Paste the links into the UTM checker to find capitals, spaces, duplicates and missing tags. It does not know which of your names is a source, so swapped pairs need your own eyes.
  • Edit what you can. Profile links, ad URLs, email templates and automations, scheduled posts and pages a partner can change for you. Keep utm_campaign as it was, so old and new visits stay in one Campaign row.
  • Redirect what you cannot edit. A sent email or a printed code keeps its link. If it goes through a short link whose target you can change, change that. Otherwise add a rule on your own site that redirects the exact wrong pair to the right one and keeps the rest of the query string. A Virling tracked link cannot be changed after you make it.
  • Write down the date. Compare channels before and after it with care. In Virling, a visit keeps the channel it was given, and a payment can still be credited to an older visit inside the lookback window, 90 days by default.
  • Test one link of each kind. Open it in a private window and see that both tags are still in the address bar after any redirect.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between utm_source and utm_medium?

utm_source names the exact place the link is, such as linkedin or newsletter. utm_medium names the kind of channel, such as social, email or cpc. One source can have several mediums, and one medium covers many sources.

Is Google a source or a medium?

A source. For a Google Ads link, the source is google and the medium is cpc. Do not tag organic search results at all: the search engine already sends a referrer.

Is email a source or a medium?

A medium. Use utm_medium=email and put the list or the sender in the source, such as utm_source=newsletter.

Do I need both utm_source and utm_medium?

Put both on every link. Without a medium, a paid or email link cannot be told apart from an ordinary one. Without a source, you know the kind of channel but not the place.

Are utm_source and utm_medium case sensitive?

In Google Analytics, yes: Google says utm_source=google is different from utm_source=Google. Virling ignores case when it decides the channel, but lowercase keeps the values the same in every tool.

Does changing a UTM tag fix old visits?

No. A visit keeps the tags it landed with, and in Virling the channel it was given then. A corrected link changes only the visits that come after it.
