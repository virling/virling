LinkedIn revenue tracking for B2B SaaS

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-16.

LinkedIn revenue tracking means counting the money paid by customers whose credited visit came from LinkedIn: a post, a comment, your profile, a company page or a message. For B2B that money often arrives weeks after the click, from a buyer who read several posts first, so the setup has to survive a long gap between visit and payment. Reactions and profile views show none of this.

B2B founders and their teams post on LinkedIn to reach the people who sign off on budgets. Whether the hours pay comes down to which posts, and which accounts, bring visitors who later pay. To put the hours in the sum as well, see how to measure social media ROI.

The LinkedIn app's in-app browser

Many people read LinkedIn in its mobile app, which opens links in its own browser. Those visits often arrive with no referrer, the case most analytics tools count as Direct. The LinkedIn app's browser names itself in the user agent, and when it does, Virling credits the visit to linkedin.com in the Social channel instead. If the link opens in a plain system browser that does not name the app, the visit stays Direct unless the link is tagged.

From a desktop browser, clicks usually arrive with linkedin.com as the referrer, and links LinkedIn has shortened arrive from lnkd.in. Virling puts both hosts in Social and shows them as one row, linkedin.com. A referrer or a utm_source always wins over the user agent; the full rule order is in channels.

Company page or personal profile

A post from your personal profile and the same post from the company page reach different people, but both arrive as linkedin.com or the app. Put the account in the campaign name:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=pricing-teardown-founder
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=pricing-teardown-company

When teammates post too, give each person a suffix of their own. The Campaign tab then shows which accounts bring buyers, not only which topics. linkedin is a known Social source, so these visits land in Social even with no referrer.

Comments, document posts and profile links

  • Links in comments. Many people put the link in the first comment, because they believe posts with outside links reach fewer people. A comment link arrives the same way as a post link, so only a tag tells them apart. Use the post's campaign with a -comment suffix.
  • Document posts. Readers page through the slides of a document post in the feed, and a URL printed on a slide may not be a link they can tap. Whoever types it arrives as Direct. Put the tagged link in the post text or a comment, and if a slide must show an address, use a short page of its own, such as /linkedin, so its visits are still visible in the Pages card.
  • Your profile and company page. The website link on your profile, the Featured section and the company page's website button stay up for months. Give each a fixed campaign, such as profile-featured.
  • Messages. If you sell through outreach, tag the links you send in messages with a campaign of their own.

To keep tags out of sight, use a tracked link: open the Campaign tab of the Channels card, click + Tracked link, and pick LinkedIn and the page it should open. It adds utm_source=linkedin.com, a social medium and its own campaign on the click; see tracked links. The UTM builder and the social UTM link generator make tagged links, which UTM parameters to use covers naming, and paid campaigns are in LinkedIn Ads revenue tracking.

Longer B2B sales cycles

A reader might book a demo a month after a post, start a trial, and pay once a manager approves. Three things decide whether that payment still reaches LinkedIn:

  • Lookback window. Only visits inside the window before the payment count. The default is 90 days; choose 365 in Site settings if your deals take longer.
  • Model. Last non-direct, the default, ignores the typed-URL visit before a sales call. First touch credits the visit that introduced the buyer, which for many B2B teams is a post. Compare the linkedin.com row under both; history is recomputed when you switch.
  • Identify. The person who clicked is not always the one who pays. Send the email from your demo or signup form so the buyer's visits join the payment, as set out in sales-led B2B setup.

The Time to pay card shows the median days from first visit to first payment per channel; how long a visitor takes to become a customer explains how to read it, and attribution for B2B SaaS teams covers invoices and sales calls. The visitor ID still has to reach checkout or the customer record; each payment provider is covered in link checkout to visits.

Is LinkedIn worth it for your SaaS?

Use a range longer than your usual sales cycle, often a quarter, or the newest posts look worse than they are. Find linkedin.com in the Source tab and read its revenue per visitor against your site average, then open the Campaign tab for the post, comment, profile and company page rows. On the Payments page, open a deal to see the buyer's visits with the credited one marked; an early LinkedIn visit shows there even when another channel got the credit.

If LinkedIn brings visitors but little revenue under both models after a full sales cycle, write fewer posts or point them at a different page. The measure is the one in what is revenue per visitor. If you post on X too, revenue from posts on X deals with t.co and threads, and if you answer buyers' questions, which Quora answers bring customers shows how to tag each one. If you sell sessions, packages or retainers instead of software, see revenue attribution for coaches and consultants.

When the numbers look odd

  • Direct rose the day you posted. Some app opens did not name the app. Tag the next post.
  • The founder's posts and the company page share one row. They used the same campaign. Add a suffix per account.
  • A deal you know came from LinkedIn is Unattributed. Often the invoice was paid by someone else, such as an accounts team, so the emails do not match. See Unattributed revenue.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from LinkedIn?

Tag posts, comments, profile links and company page links with utm_source=linkedin and a campaign for each, pass the visitor ID into checkout or send the buyer's email from your demo form, and read revenue per campaign over a range longer than your sales cycle.

Why do LinkedIn visits show up as Direct?

The LinkedIn app opens links in its own browser, often with no referrer. Virling credits those visits to linkedin.com when the browser names the app in its user agent; when it does not, they stay Direct. A utm_source=linkedin tag keeps them credited either way.

Does a link in the first comment get credited to LinkedIn?

Yes. A comment link arrives with the same referrer or in-app browser as a post link, so it counts as LinkedIn. To tell the two apart, give the comment link its own campaign, such as the post's name with -comment added.

Is LinkedIn worth it for a SaaS?

Judge it over a range longer than your sales cycle, under both last non-direct and first touch. If its revenue per visitor is at or above your site average, keep posting; if not, change the posts or the page they link to.

Should company page and personal profile posts be tagged differently?

Yes. Both arrive as linkedin.com, so put the account in the campaign, such as pricing-teardown-founder and pricing-teardown-company, and the Campaign tab shows which account brings buyers.
