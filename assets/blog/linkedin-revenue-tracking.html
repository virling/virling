LinkedIn revenue tracking for B2B SaaS

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-16.

LinkedIn revenue tracking means counting the money paid by customers whose credited visit came from LinkedIn: a click on a post, a comment, your profile or a message. To measure it you need each LinkedIn visit stored with a visitor ID, that ID carried into checkout, and payments joined back to the visit. Impressions, reactions and profile views do not tell you this.

B2B founders spend hours a week writing posts. Whether that is worth it comes down to one question: did anyone who clicked through from LinkedIn pay, and how much was each LinkedIn visitor worth compared with your other channels?

How LinkedIn visits get counted

There are three ways a visit gets marked as LinkedIn, and you want all of them working.

  • The referrer. A click in a browser usually arrives with linkedin.com, or its lnkd.in short link host, as the referrer. In Virling both are in the Social channel and show as one row, linkedin.com, in the Source tab.
  • The in-app browser. Links opened inside the LinkedIn mobile app often arrive with no referrer. With no referrer and no tag, most tools count that visit as Direct. When the app's browser names itself in the user agent, Virling credits the visit to linkedin.com in Social instead. If the link opens in a plain system browser that does not name the app, the visit stays Direct.
  • A UTM tag. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, and linkedin is a known Social source. A tag also works when the app detection does not.

A referrer or a utm_source always wins over the in-app browser guess. The full rule order is in channels.

Tag your posts and profile links

The referrer only says linkedin.com. It cannot tell a post from your profile, a comment or a direct message, because Virling stores the referrer host, not the full URL. Tag each place with its own campaign:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=post-pricing-teardown
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=profile-featured
  • utm_source=linkedin for every LinkedIn link, so they group under one source.
  • utm_medium=social for posts, comments, profiles and messages. Anything except a paid or email medium keeps the visit in Social.
  • utm_campaign names the place: one per post, and fixed ones for the links that live on your profile, such as the website link, the Featured section and your company page.

Profile links are worth tagging even if you never post. People who read a post often open your profile before they visit your site, so a profile campaign shows what that path earns. More naming rules are in which UTM parameters to use, and the UTM builder makes the link for you. If a post also goes out on X or Threads, make all the social links at once. For campaigns you pay for, see LinkedIn Ads revenue tracking.

If a long tagged URL looks messy in a post, use a tracked link instead. In Virling, open the Campaign tab on the Channels card, click + Tracked link, pick LinkedIn and the page it should open. The short link adds the source, a social medium and its own campaign on the click, and the Campaign tab then lists it by name with its visitors and revenue. See tracked links.

Make sure payments can be credited

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs the visitor ID in checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Every provider is covered in link checkout to visits.

B2B buyers rarely pay on the first visit; attribution for B2B SaaS teams covers long trials, invoices and sales calls. With the default model, last non-direct, someone who clicked your post on Monday and came back on Friday by typing your URL is still credited to LinkedIn, as long as the LinkedIn visit is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. If they subscribe, every renewal keeps counting toward LinkedIn.

Is LinkedIn worth it for your SaaS?

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more, so slow buyers have time to pay.
  2. In the Channels card, open Social, or switch to the Source tab, and find linkedin.com. The row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click the row to filter the whole page. The Pages card now shows which landing pages LinkedIn visitors arrived on.
  4. Switch to the Campaign tab to split LinkedIn by post and by profile link, if you tagged them.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.
  6. Check the Retention page after a month or two. It groups customers by the channel and referrer of their first payment, so you can see whether LinkedIn customers keep paying.

LinkedIn often introduces a buyer who later comes back through search or a typed URL. Last-touch models can undercount that. Switch the model to first touch in Site settings and compare the LinkedIn row under both; history is recomputed, and you can switch back. First-touch or last-touch attribution for a SaaS explains when each one fits.

There is no universal answer to whether LinkedIn pays. If its revenue per visitor is at or above your site average, the time is likely well spent; if it brings visitors but little revenue under both models, write fewer posts or point them at a different page. If you post on X too, revenue from posts on X follows the same steps, and if you answer questions from buyers, which Quora answers bring customers shows how to tag each one. If you sell sessions, packages or retainers instead of software, see revenue attribution for coaches and consultants.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • LinkedIn looks small but Direct jumped the day you posted. Some app opens did not name the app. Tag the next post.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • Revenue shows up weeks later. Normal for B2B. The Time to pay card shows the median days from first visit to first payment per channel. See how long a visitor takes to become a customer.
  • A buyer says they found you on LinkedIn but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have seen a post without clicking. No analytics tool can count a view that never reached your site.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so LinkedIn's real visitor number can be higher than what any analytics tool shows. Revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from LinkedIn?

Tag each LinkedIn link with utm_source=linkedin and a campaign per post or profile link, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and use a tool that joins payments to visits. Then compare LinkedIn's revenue and revenue per visitor with your site average.

Why do LinkedIn visits show up as Direct?

Links opened inside the LinkedIn app often arrive with no referrer. Virling credits them to LinkedIn when the app's browser names itself in the user agent; otherwise they stay Direct. Adding utm_source=linkedin to the link keeps them credited to LinkedIn either way.

Can I see which LinkedIn post sent a paying customer?

Not from the referrer alone, because Virling stores the referrer host, not the full URL. Put the post in utm_campaign, or use a tracked link per post, and the Campaign tab shows revenue per post.

Is LinkedIn worth it for a SaaS?

Compare LinkedIn's revenue per visitor with your site average over a few weeks, under both last-touch and first-touch models. If it pays at or above average, keep posting; if it brings visitors but little revenue, change the posts or the page they link to.

Should I tag my LinkedIn profile links?

Yes. Give the website link, the Featured section and your company page each their own utm_campaign, so you can tell profile visits from post clicks.
