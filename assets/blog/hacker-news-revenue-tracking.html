Hacker News launch revenue: which HN visits turn into paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

Hacker News launch revenue is the money paid by customers whose credited visit came from news.ycombinator.com or from a link tagged hn. Points, rank and the height of the traffic spike do not tell you this. A Show HN can fill your visitor chart for an afternoon, and the payments it leads to can arrive a month later from readers who came back on their own.

What a Show HN day looks like

A Show HN starts on the new and show pages, where few people look. Until it collects upvotes, it sends a small, steady trickle. If it reaches the front page, the visits jump within minutes and stay high while it holds a spot there. When it slides off, usually later that day or the next, the traffic drops almost as fast as it came. A post that never leaves the new page looks like a small bump instead of a spike.

  • Front page vs new. Both send the same referrer, news.ycombinator.com, and Virling keeps the referrer host, not the page on Hacker News the click came from. You cannot split front page clicks from new page clicks in the Source tab. The shape of the visitor chart for that day tells you when the post reached the front page.
  • Links in comments. Readers ask about pricing, self-hosting or how something works, and you answer with a link. Those clicks come from news.ycombinator.com too, so they land on the same Hacker News row. The Pages card, filtered to Hacker News, shows them as landings on /pricing or your docs instead of the page you submitted.
  • Mirrors and digests. Sites and newsletters that copy the front page send their own referrer or tags, so they show up as separate rows, not as Hacker News.

news.ycombinator.com and the Communities channel

In Virling, news.ycombinator.com is a Communities host, and its row is named Hacker News. A clean URL in your Show HN is enough for clicks from a browser. Clicks from reader apps and some clients arrive with no referrer; for those Virling reads utm_source, then ref, and hn, hackernews and ycombinator all map to the same Hacker News row. When a referrer is present, it wins over the tag. If you share the launch on other sites as well, give that link its own utm_campaign, such as show-hn-2026-11, so a second Show HN next year stays apart in the Campaign tab:

https://yourdomain.com/?ref=hn
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=hn&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=show-hn-2026-11

The UTM builder makes the second link for you, and channels has the full rule order.

Why a spike of signups pays weeks later

Many Hacker News readers sign up to try things. On launch day that shows up as trial starts and free accounts, not payments. The money comes when a trial ends, when a team gets budget approved, or when someone hits the limit of a free plan, which can be weeks after the post. By then the reader comes back by typing your URL, and that visit is Direct.

The default model, last non-direct, keeps the credit on the Hacker News visit through a Direct return, but a later search for your name takes it. First touch keeps it on the Hacker News visit whatever came after, which is the view that shows what the launch really earned. Either way the visit must be inside the lookback window, 90 days by default; see the model and lookback window. The Time to pay card shows the median days from first visit to first payment per channel, which is how long to wait before you judge the post.

Measuring any of this needs the usual setup: the script on every page, a connected payment provider and the visitor ID passed into checkout, as in the quickstart. Do it a few days before you submit, since a post can reach the front page within an hour and visits from before the script are gone; the launch tracking checklist lists what to test.

Read the launch a month later

  1. Log the post on the Launches page with its name, link, channel and day. Virling marks it on the revenue chart and shows the visitors, new customers and revenue of that day and the day after, counting all traffic. Expect few new customers there; it is the spike, not the result.
  2. Pick a range from the day of the post to four to six weeks after it, and find Hacker News in the Source tab of the Channels card.
  3. Click the row to filter the page. The revenue chart now shows Hacker News money arriving in the weeks after the spike, not on the day.
  4. Switch the model to first touch in Site settings and compare. If the row grows, readers found you on Hacker News and paid after a later search or link. History is recomputed, and you can switch back.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see its journey: the Hacker News visit, then the Direct returns before the payment.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Direct jumped in the same hour the post hit the front page. Clicks from apps with no referrer. Add ?ref=hn to the link you submit next time.
  • Nothing paid in the first week. Normal for a launch. Wait at least your trial length plus the Time to pay median before you decide.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. The visitor ID is not reaching checkout; see Unattributed revenue.
  • Fewer visits than the server logs show. Readers who block scripts are not counted by any analytics tool. Their payments still count in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see revenue from a Show HN post?

Open the Source tab of the Channels card and find Hacker News, which is where visits from news.ycombinator.com land. Pick a range that runs four to six weeks past the post, since most of the payments arrive after the spike, not during it.

Should I add UTM tags to my Show HN link?

You do not have to. A click from the Hacker News site usually arrives with news.ycombinator.com as the referrer, which Virling already credits to Hacker News. A short tag such as ref=hn also catches clicks that arrive with no referrer, for example from reader apps. When a referrer is present it wins over the tag.

Can I tell front page clicks from new page or comment clicks?

Not from the referrer. Clicks from the front page, the new and show pages, and links in comments all arrive as news.ycombinator.com, and Virling keeps the host only. The visitor chart shows when the post reached the front page, and the Pages card shows comment links as landings on the pages you linked.

Why do Show HN signups pay weeks later?

Readers sign up to try the product, and pay when a trial ends or a free plan runs out. When they come back by typing your URL, the default model keeps the credit on the Hacker News visit; first touch keeps it even after a later search, as long as the visit is inside the 90-day lookback window.
