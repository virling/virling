Hacker News launch revenue: which HN visits turn into paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

Hacker News launch revenue is the money paid by customers whose credited visit came from news.ycombinator.com or from a link tagged as Hacker News. To see which Hacker News visits turn into paying customers, add a tracking script that keeps the referrer, pass the visitor ID into checkout, connect your payment provider, and follow the payments credited to Hacker News for weeks after the post. The points and the traffic spike do not tell you this.

A Show HN or a front-page post brings a lot of visitors in a few hours, then falls away within a day or two. Many readers are curious, not buying. The few who pay often do it later, after they come back on their own. Measuring a launch means following those people, not the spike.

How a Hacker News visit is recognized

  • The referrer. A click on a link on news.ycombinator.com in a browser usually arrives with news.ycombinator.com as the referrer. In Virling that host is in the Communities channel and shows as Hacker News. This covers the post link, links in comments and your own replies, since they are all on the same site.
  • A tag on the link. Some clicks arrive with no referrer, for example from reader apps or browsers that strip it. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, and when that is missing it reads ref. hn, hackernews and ycombinator are known Communities sources. When a referrer is present, it wins over the tag.
  • Everything else. A click with no referrer and no tag is Direct. Sites that copy or mirror the front page send their own referrer, so they show as separate Referral rows.

Why most Hacker News visitors come back as Direct

A reader opens your page from the front page, skims it, and closes the tab. Days later they remember the name, type your URL or open a bookmark, and sign up. That second visit has no referrer, so on its own it is Direct. If you only look at the visit right before the payment, Hacker News looks like it sent traffic and nothing else, and Direct looks like your best channel.

Virling keeps every visit of a visitor, tied together by the virling_vid cookie, and credits the payment with your attribution model:

  • Last non-direct, the default. The latest visit that was not Direct gets the credit. The Hacker News visit keeps it when the visitor comes back by typing your URL, but a later visit from search or another site takes it.
  • First touch. The earliest visit in the window gets the credit, so the Hacker News visit keeps it whatever came after. This is the view that shows which launches introduce your buyers. Switch it in Site settings; history is recomputed, and you can switch back.
  • The lookback window. Only visits inside the window before the payment count, 90 days by default. If your trial plus decision time is longer, widen it. See the model and lookback window.

One limit: the cookie lives in one browser. A reader who sees the post at work and pays from their phone a week later is a new visitor to any analytics tool, and that payment cannot be tied to Hacker News. First-touch or last-touch attribution and why Direct is your biggest traffic source go deeper.

Set it up before you post

  1. Decide on the link. The referrer already credits clicks from the site, so a clean URL works for a Show HN. To also catch clicks with no referrer, add a short tag such as ?ref=hn, or full UTM tags for links you share about the launch elsewhere: 
    https://yourdomain.com/?ref=hn
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=hn&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=show-hn-2026-11
    Use utm_medium=community to stay in Communities, and one utm_campaign per post so the Campaign tab keeps a second launch apart from the first. The UTM builder makes the link for you, and which UTM parameters to use covers naming. For a short link, open Links in Virling, pick Other as the place, type hn as the source and Communities as the channel; see tracked links.
  2. Add the script. Put the Virling tag in the <head> of every page, including the page you submit. It records the referrer and tags of the first pageview and sets the visitor cookie. See add the script, and framework guides for Next.js, Astro, WordPress, Framer and Webflow.
  3. Connect a payment provider. Virling reads payments from Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce; all of them are on the integrations page, with setup in the payments docs.
  4. Pass the visitor ID into checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. See link checkout to visits. Without this step you get visits but no revenue.

Set this up a few days early and check a test visit and a test payment on the dashboard. A post can reach the front page within an hour, and visits recorded before the script was on the page are gone. The launch tracking checklist lists what to tag and test beforehand.

Read the results

  1. Pick a range from the day of the post to four to six weeks after it.
  2. In the Channels card, switch to the Source tab and find Hacker News. The row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click the row to filter the whole page. The revenue chart now shows when Hacker News money arrived, and the Pages card shows where those visitors landed.
  4. Switch to the Campaign tab to see a tagged post on its own.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked. This is where you see the Hacker News visit followed by a Direct return.
  6. Log the post on the Launches page with its name, link, channel and day. Virling marks it on the revenue chart and shows the visitors, new customers and revenue of that day and the day after. Those two days count all traffic, not only Hacker News, so use them as context and the Source row for the credit.

Compare the default model with first touch over the same range. If Hacker News revenue grows under first touch, readers found you there and paid after coming back through another channel. The Time to pay card shows the median days from first visit to first payment per channel; keep watching at least that long past the end of your trial. What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Hacker News looks small but Direct jumped the same day. Clicks with no referrer and no tag. Add ?ref=hn to links you control next time.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A buyer says they found you on Hacker News but the payment is credited elsewhere. A later non-direct visit took the credit under the default model, the visit is outside the lookback window, or they paid from another device. Try first touch.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so Hacker News's real visitor number can be higher than any analytics tool shows. Their revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see revenue from a Hacker News launch?

Add the tracking script, pass the visitor ID into checkout and connect your payment provider. Visits from news.ycombinator.com land in the Communities channel as Hacker News, and each payment is credited to the visit before it, so the Hacker News row shows the visitors and revenue it brought over the weeks after the post.

Should I add UTM tags to my Show HN link?

You do not have to. A click from the Hacker News site usually arrives with news.ycombinator.com as the referrer, which Virling already credits to Hacker News. A short tag such as ref=hn also catches clicks that arrive with no referrer, for example from reader apps. When a referrer is present it wins over the tag.

Why does Direct traffic go up after a Hacker News post?

Many readers close the tab and come back later by typing your URL or from a bookmark, and some clicks arrive with no referrer at all. Both count as Direct. With the default model, last non-direct, a later Direct visit does not take the credit from the Hacker News visit.

Does Hacker News get credit if the visitor pays weeks later?

Yes, if the Hacker News visit is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default, and no later non-direct visit such as a search took the credit. Switch the model to first touch to credit the visit that introduced the buyer, whatever came after.

How long after a Hacker News post should I keep measuring?

At least as long as your trial plus the median time your customers take to pay, which the Time to pay card shows per channel. Most of the traffic arrives in a day or two, but the payments it leads to can arrive weeks later.
