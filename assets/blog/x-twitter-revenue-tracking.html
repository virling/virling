X (Twitter) revenue tracking for founders who build in public

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-16.

To track revenue from X (Twitter), you need three things: clicks that arrive with t.co or x.com as the referrer counted as X, UTM-tagged links in your bio and posts so clicks without a referrer still count, and each visit joined to the payment that followed by a visitor ID carried into checkout. With those in place you can read the revenue, and the revenue per visitor, that your posts bring. Impressions, likes and follower counts do not tell you this.

If you build in public, you post progress, numbers and launches on X most days. Whether that brings paying customers comes down to one question: did anyone who clicked through from X pay, and how much was each X visitor worth compared with your other channels?

How X visits get counted

There are two ways a visit gets marked as X, and you want both working.

  • The referrer. X wraps links in posts with its t.co short link, so a click in a browser usually arrives with t.co as the referrer, and some arrive from x.com or twitter.com. In Virling all three hosts are in the Social channel and show as one row, x.com, in the Source tab.
  • A UTM tag. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, or ref when it is missing. x and twitter are known Social sources.

The gap is the X mobile app. Links opened inside an app often arrive with no referrer. Virling can credit some apps from the user agent of their in-app browser, such as Instagram or LinkedIn, but X is not one of them. So an untagged click from the X app with no referrer is counted as Direct. A tag on the link fixes that, because a utm_source is read whenever there is no referrer. The full rule order is in channels.

Virling can credit payments by first touch or last non-direct, so you can check whether x.com earns more as the visit that introduced a buyer or as the last one before they paid.

Tag your bio and post links

The referrer only says t.co. It cannot tell your bio from a thread, a reply or a launch post, because Virling stores the referrer host, not the full URL. Tag each place with its own campaign:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=x&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=bio
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=x&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=post-launch-v2
  • utm_source=x for every X link, so they group under one source.
  • utm_medium=social for posts, replies, your bio and DMs. Anything except a paid or email medium keeps the visit in Social.
  • utm_campaign names the place: one per post you want to measure, and fixed ones for the links that stay put, such as the bio link and the pinned post.

The bio link is worth tagging first. People who like a post often open your profile before they visit your site, and app clicks on an untagged bio link land as Direct. More naming rules are in which UTM parameters to use, and the UTM builder makes the link for you. To share one page on several networks, the social UTM link generator makes a tagged link for each. For posts you pay to promote, see X Ads revenue tracking.

If a long tagged URL looks messy in a post, use a tracked link instead. In Virling, open Links, pick x.com as the source and the page it should open. The short link adds the source, a social medium and its own campaign on the click, and the Campaign tab lists it with its visitors and revenue. See tracked links.

Make sure payments can be credited

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs the visitor ID in checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Every provider is covered in link checkout to visits.

Few people buy on the first click from a post. With the default model, last non-direct, someone who clicked your post on Monday and came back on Friday by typing your URL is still credited to X, as long as the X visit is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. If they subscribe, every renewal keeps counting toward X.

Does posting on X bring paying customers?

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more, so slow buyers have time to pay.
  2. In the Channels card, open Social, or switch to the Source tab, and find x.com. The row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click the row to filter the whole page. The Pages card now shows which landing pages X visitors arrived on.
  4. Switch to the Campaign tab to split X by post and by bio link, if you tagged them.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.
  6. Check the Retention page after a month or two. It groups customers by the channel and referrer of their first payment, so you can see whether X customers keep paying.

Building in public often introduces a buyer who later comes back through search or a typed URL. Last-touch models can undercount that. Switch the model to first touch in Site settings and compare the X row under both; history is recomputed, and you can switch back. First-touch or last-touch attribution for a SaaS explains when each one fits.

There is no universal answer to whether X pays. If its revenue per visitor is at or above your site average, the time is likely well spent; if it brings visitors but little revenue under both models, change what you post or the page you link to. The same method works for communities, as in how much revenue Reddit brings. If you post the same updates elsewhere, see what your Bluesky posts earn, counting visits from Mastodon instances and whether LinkedIn brings B2B customers. What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • X looks small but Direct jumped the day a post took off. App clicks on untagged links arrived with no referrer. Tag the bio and the next post.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • Revenue shows up weeks later. The Time to pay card shows the median days from first visit to first payment per channel. See how long a visitor takes to become a customer.
  • A buyer says they found you on X but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have read your posts without clicking. No analytics tool can count a view that never reached your site.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so X's real visitor number can be higher than what any analytics tool shows. Revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from X (Twitter)?

Count clicks that arrive with t.co or x.com as the referrer, tag your bio and post links with utm_source=x and a campaign per post, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and use a tool that joins payments to visits. Then compare X's revenue per visitor with your site average.

Why do clicks from X show up as Direct?

Links opened inside an app often arrive with no referrer. The X app is not one of the in-app browsers Virling can recognize, so an untagged click with no referrer is Direct. Adding utm_source=x to the link keeps it credited to X.

What is t.co in my referrers?

X wraps every link in a post with its t.co short link, so browser clicks usually arrive with t.co as the referrer. Virling puts t.co, x.com and twitter.com in the Social channel and shows them as one row, x.com.

Can I see which post on X sent a paying customer?

Not from the referrer, which is only a host such as t.co. Give each post its own utm_campaign, or its own tracked link, and the Campaign tab shows visitors and revenue per post.

Should I tag the link in my X bio?

Yes. Give the bio link and the pinned post fixed campaigns of their own, so you can tell profile visits from clicks on a single post.
