Podcast revenue tracking: which shows bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

Podcast revenue tracking means giving every show its own link, so you can see which podcast visits turn into paying customers. Use a tracked link or a vanity URL that redirects to a UTM-tagged page, with the show in the source and the episode in the campaign. Carry the visitor ID into checkout, then join each payment to the visit that came before it. Download counts and promo code redemptions do not tell you this on their own.

A sponsorship read or a guest spot is spoken, not clicked. Listeners hear a URL while driving or cooking, and some of them type it in days later. That makes podcasts one of the hardest channels to credit, and the easiest to undercount.

Why podcast sales land in Direct

There are three ways a podcast listener reaches your site, and only one of them carries the show with it.

  • The show notes link. Podcast apps often open links with no referrer, and none of them is on the list of known hosts. Without a tag, a click from the show notes counts as Direct, or as Referral when a referrer does come through.
  • The spoken URL. A listener who types the address they heard arrives with no referrer and no tag. That is Direct, in Virling and in most other tools, unless the address they typed adds the tags for them.
  • A search for your name. Many listeners only remember the product name and search for it. That visit arrives from a search engine and counts as Organic search.

So a podcast that works often shows up as a bump in Direct and branded search the week the episode airs, with nothing under its own name. Why Direct is your biggest traffic source covers the other causes.

Vanity URLs and tracked links

The fix is a link short enough to say out loud that still carries the show's tags. There are two ways to get one.

  • A vanity URL on your domain, such as yourdomain.com/devpod, set up as a redirect to a tagged page. It is easy to remember after hearing it once, and the tags travel in the redirect, so typed visits are credited too. Use one path per show, and set it up in your web server or hosting redirects.
  • A tracked link in Virling, a short URL on your Virling server that adds the tags on each click. Pick Podcast as the place and the page it should open. The link adds utm_source=podcast, utm_medium=referral and its own id as the campaign, and bot clicks are not counted. Use it in show notes and episode pages, where nobody has to say it out loud. See tracked links.

For paid sponsorships, the Partners page goes one step further. Add the show by name and the page it should open, and Virling makes a short link whose source is the show's name in dashed words, such as jane-doe-s-podcast. Partners are ranked by revenue, and Copy report link gives the host a read-only page with only their own visitors, paying customers and revenue.

Promo codes vs tracked links

Sponsors often get a discount code to read on air. A code and a link answer different questions, so use both.

  • A promo code is counted by your payment provider when it is used, however the buyer got to checkout. It catches listeners who typed your plain URL or searched for you. It cannot tell you how many listeners visited and did not buy, and a code that leaks to a coupon site gets credited to the show anyway.
  • A tracked link or vanity URL counts visits, paying customers and revenue per visitor, and keeps working for renewals. It misses listeners who never use the link.

Virling credits a payment by the visit that came before it, not by the code. It does not read discount codes, so code redemptions stay in your payment provider. Comparing the two for one show is a fair check: if the code is used far more often than the link brings buyers, most listeners are arriving another way.

Set it up with Virling

  1. Tag each show's link. If you build the URL yourself, use the show as the source, podcast as the medium and the episode or sponsorship as the campaign, lowercase with hyphens. Point the vanity URL at it. The UTM builder makes the link for you, and which UTM parameters to use has more naming rules.
    https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=devpod&utm_medium=podcast&utm_campaign=devpod-ep-142&utm_content=host-read
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=devpod&utm_medium=podcast&utm_campaign=devpod-ep-142&utm_content=show-notes
    A show name is not a known source, so these visits count in the Referral channel, with the show as their own source row.
  2. Add the script. Put the Virling script in the head of every page, with your domain in data-site. It reads the tags on the landing page and sets the visitor ID. See script attributes.
  3. Connect a payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. The setup is in link checkout to visits, and each provider has its own page: Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce. All of them are listed under integrations.
  4. Read the numbers. Pick a range of a few weeks, open the Source tab in the Channels card to find the show, then switch to the Campaign tab to see visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor per episode or link. Open a payment to see the buyer's journey, with the credited visit marked.

Listeners who search your name later

No tool can count a listen that never reached your site, and a listener who heard the ad and later searched for you leaves no trace of the show. What you can do:

  • Make the vanity URL the thing you say. A short path on your own domain is easier to remember than a code, and typing it carries the tags.
  • Know how the model credits returns. With the default model, last non-direct, a listener who clicked the show's link and later typed your URL is still credited to the podcast, as long as the click is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. A later search visit is not Direct, so it takes the credit instead. Switch to first touch in Site settings to see which shows start journeys; model and lookback window explains both.
  • Watch branded search and Direct around air dates. A rise in both the week an episode airs is a hint, not proof, since anything else that week can cause it.
  • Ask buyers. A "where did you hear about us" field at signup catches listeners no link can. Keep it next to the attributed numbers rather than in place of them.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • The show has visits but no revenue. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If much of your revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • The vanity URL shows as Direct. The redirect is dropping the query string, or points at a page without tags. Open it in a private window and check the address bar.
  • Two shows share one row. They use the same source or campaign. Give each show its own source and each episode its own campaign.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from a podcast?

Give each show its own link: a tracked link, or a vanity URL on your domain that redirects to a UTM-tagged page. Pass the visitor ID into checkout and use a tool that joins payments to visits. The campaign report then shows visitors and revenue per show or episode.

What UTM tags should I use for a podcast?

Use the show's name as utm_source, podcast as utm_medium and the episode or sponsorship as utm_campaign, all lowercase with hyphens. In Virling a podcast source counts in the Referral channel.

Are promo codes or tracked links better for podcast sponsorships?

Use both. A promo code counts sales in your payment provider even when the listener never types the link, but it says nothing about visits or how many people came and did not buy. A tracked link counts visits and revenue per visitor. Virling credits payments by the visit, not by the code.

Why do podcast listeners show up as Direct or Google?

Many listeners hear your name, then type your URL or search for it later. A typed visit with no tag is Direct, and a search visit is Organic search. If they clicked the show's link earlier, last non-direct still credits the podcast over a later Direct visit, but a later search visit takes the credit.

Can a podcast host see the revenue their episode brought?

Yes, if you add the show as a partner in Virling. Copy report link gives the host a read-only page with only their own visitors, paying customers and revenue for the last 7, 30 or 90 days.
