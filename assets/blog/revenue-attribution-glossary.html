Revenue attribution glossary: 40 terms in plain words

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-24.

A revenue attribution glossary explains the words used when you trace a payment back to the visit that brought it. The short version: a visit arrives with a referrer or UTM tags, which set its channel. A visitor ID goes into checkout, so the payment can be joined to the visits before it. A window decides which visits are old enough to ignore, and a model picks the one that gets the credit. What cannot be joined is Unattributed. The 40 terms below are in alphabetical order, with the metrics that use the result, such as CAC, LTV, ROAS, MER and MRR.

Jump to a term

  • A to C: Attribution model, Attribution window, Average order value (AOV), CAC payback, Channel, Churn rate, Click ID, Cross-device attribution, Customer acquisition cost (CAC)
  • D to F: Dark social, Direct, First touch
  • L: Last non-direct, Last touch, Lifetime value (LTV), Linear attribution, LTV to CAC ratio
  • M to N: Marketing efficiency ratio (MER), Matched revenue, Monthly recurring revenue (MRR), Multi-touch attribution, Net revenue retention (NRR), New customer revenue
  • P to R: Position-based attribution, Referrer, Refund, Refund rate, Renewal, Return on ad spend (ROAS), Revenue attribution, Revenue per visitor (RPV)
  • S to T: Self-reported attribution, Session, Touch
  • U to V: Unattributed revenue, UTM parameters, utm_campaign, utm_content and utm_term, utm_source and utm_medium, Visitor ID

A to C

Attribution model

The rule that decides which visits get the credit for a payment when the buyer visited more than once. Virling has three: last non-direct, first touch and last touch. The attribution model calculator shows how five models credit the same payment.

Attribution window

The number of days before a payment in which a visit can earn credit for it, also called a lookback window. Older visits are ignored under every model. Virling offers 30, 60, 90 or 365 days and uses 90 by default; see how long a visit should get credit.

Average order value (AOV)

Revenue divided by the number of orders in the same period. The AOV calculator works it out per channel.

CAC payback

The months a new customer takes to pay back what it cost to win them, counting only gross profit: CAC / (monthly revenue per customer × gross margin). Work it out with the CAC payback calculator.

Channel

The group a visit belongs to by where it came from. Virling uses eight: Paid, Email, AI, Organic search, Communities, Social, Referral and Direct. The first pageview of a session decides it, by the channel rules.

Churn rate

Customer churn is customers lost in a period divided by customers at its start. Revenue churn does the same with MRR and counts downgrades too. Leave out customers who joined during the period, as the churn rate calculator does.

Click ID

A code that an ad platform adds to a link so it can recognise the click later, such as gclid from Google Ads, fbclid from Meta and msclkid from Microsoft Advertising. Only that platform can turn it into a campaign, so it does not replace tags; see click IDs and UTM tags.

Cross-device attribution

Crediting a sale to the visit that brought the buyer when they found you on one device and paid on another. Each browser has its own visitor ID, so without fingerprinting cross-device attribution works only where the buyer identifies themselves on both.

Customer acquisition cost (CAC)

What you spent to win customers divided by the new paying customers you won in the same period. Trials, signups and renewals do not count as new customers. Blended CAC uses the totals, and CAC by channel uses one channel’s spend and customers.

D to F

Dark social

Visits from links shared in chat apps, direct messages and email that arrive with no referrer, so they look like Direct. See how to track dark social.

Direct

The channel of a visit that carries no sign of where it came from: no referrer, no source or medium tag, and no in-app browser that names itself. Typed addresses and bookmarks are Direct, and so are many untagged links opened in apps. See why Direct is so big.

First touch

A model that gives the whole payment to the earliest visit in the window, Direct or not. It shows which channels introduce people who later pay; see first touch against last touch.

L

Last non-direct

A model that gives the whole payment to the latest visit in the window that was not Direct. If every visit was Direct, the latest Direct one gets it. It is the default in Virling’s model setting.

Last touch

A model that gives the whole payment to the latest visit in the window, Direct included. It shows how people arrive when they are ready to buy.

Lifetime value (LTV)

The gross profit a customer brings over the whole time they buy from you. For a subscription, the LTV calculator uses ARPU × gross margin / monthly churn, where ARPU is the monthly revenue per customer. Net LTV in Virling is simpler: every payment made so far, net of refunds and before costs.

Linear attribution

A multi-touch model that splits a payment equally between all the visits. Virling does not offer it.

LTV to CAC ratio

Lifetime value divided by customer acquisition cost. Above 1, the average customer returns more gross profit than they cost to win. Compare channels with the LTV to CAC calculator.

M to N

Marketing efficiency ratio (MER)

Total revenue divided by total marketing cost for the same period. It counts no sale twice, but it cannot say which channel earned what. Work it out with the MER calculator.

Matched revenue

The share of revenue tied to a visit. Virling shows it under the Overview numbers, with the reasons for the rest.

Monthly recurring revenue (MRR)

The subscription revenue you can expect every month, with a yearly plan counted as one twelfth of its price. The MRR calculator uses prices after discounts. Virling’s own MRR uses list prices before discounts and tax.

Multi-touch attribution

Splitting the credit for one payment across several visits instead of giving it all to one. Linear and position-based are two common rules for multi-touch attribution.

Net revenue retention (NRR)

Starting MRR plus upgrades, minus downgrades and cancellations, divided by starting MRR. New customers are left out. Work it out with the NRR calculator.

New customer revenue

The first payment of each customer, where new means new ever, not new in the date range. Every payment after it is returning customer revenue.

P to R

Position-based attribution

A multi-touch model that gives 40% of a payment to the first visit, 40% to the last, and splits the other 20% between the ones in the middle. With only two visits, each gets 50%. Virling does not offer it.

Referrer

The address of the page a visitor came from, as the browser reports it. Between two sites, browsers send only the domain by default, and sometimes nothing; see why the referrer is empty.

Refund

Money given back to a buyer, in whole or in part. Virling credits each payment after refunds, so a refund lowers the revenue of the channel that brought the sale, however late it comes.

Refund rate

The share of orders refunded, or the share of the money taken that went back to buyers. Count a refund against the order it belongs to, not the day it happened. To compare channels, see refund rate by traffic source.

Renewal

A later charge on a subscription, made with no visit of its own. In Virling a renewal takes the attribution of the customer’s first payment; see subscription renewal attribution.

Return on ad spend (ROAS)

Revenue from ads divided by ad spend. It uses revenue, not profit, so break-even ROAS is 1 / gross margin. Work it out with the ROAS calculator.

Revenue attribution

Joining each payment to the website visits before it, so you can see which channel, campaign and page brought paying customers. Start with what revenue attribution is and how it works.

Revenue per visitor (RPV)

Revenue divided by unique visitors for the same period. It equals conversion rate × average order value, and it is the fair way to compare a big channel with a small one; see what revenue per visitor is.

S to T

Self-reported attribution

Asking buyers how they heard about you and crediting the source they name. Self-reported attribution finds sources that leave no click, such as podcasts and word of mouth.

Session

One visit: the pageviews and goals of one visitor until 30 minutes pass with no event. In Virling it also ends when a page loads with different UTM tags, or when the visitor leaves and returns after a set time; see sessions in the docs.

Touch

A visit that can get credit for a payment, also called a touchpoint. In Virling a session’s touch is its first pageview.

U to V

Unattributed revenue

Payments that count toward revenue but are not credited to any visit, most often because no visitor ID reached checkout. The causes and fixes are in unattributed revenue.

UTM parameters

Tags you add to a link to say where it is and what it belongs to: utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_content and utm_term. Make one with the UTM builder.

utm_campaign

The tag that names the push a link belongs to, such as launch-oct. Use the same name on every link of one push to read revenue by campaign.

utm_content and utm_term

utm_content says which link or ad was clicked, and utm_term says which paid search keyword the ad was bought on. Both are optional; see when to use each.

utm_source and utm_medium

utm_source says where the link is, such as linkedin, and utm_medium says what kind of channel that is, such as social or cpc. In Virling a medium for Paid or Email decides the channel before anything else; see source against medium.

Visitor ID

The random ID a tracking script gives a browser so visits can be joined to each other and to a payment. Virling keeps it in a first-party cookie named virling_vid, and checkout has to carry it.

Frequently asked questions

What is revenue attribution in simple terms?

It is joining each payment to the website visits before it, so you can see which channel brought the paying customer. It needs a visitor ID on every visit, that ID passed into checkout, and a model that picks which visit gets the credit.

What is the difference between Direct and Unattributed?

Direct is a channel: the payment was joined to a visit, and that visit had no sign of where it came from. Unattributed means the payment was not joined to any visit, most often because no visitor ID reached checkout. Both count toward revenue.

What is the difference between first touch, last touch and last non-direct?

All three give the whole payment to one visit in the window. First touch picks the earliest visit, last touch picks the latest, Direct included, and last non-direct picks the latest that was not Direct.

What is the difference between a UTM tag and a click ID?

You write UTM tags, and any analytics tool can read them. An ad platform adds a click ID, such as gclid, and only that platform can turn it into a campaign. They work together on one link.

What is the difference between ROAS and MER?

ROAS divides the revenue from ads by what you spent on them, so it depends on which revenue is credited to the ads. MER divides all revenue by all marketing cost, so nothing is counted twice, but it cannot say which channel earned what.

What is the difference between an attribution model and an attribution window?

The window decides which visits can get credit, by how many days before the payment they happened. The model then decides which of those visits gets it.
