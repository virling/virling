Google Business Profile UTM tracking: which Maps clicks pay

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-22.

To see which Google Business Profile visits turn into paying customers, add UTM tags to the website link on your profile, with utm_source=google and a campaign of its own, store the visitor ID on each payment or order, and join each payment to the visit before it. Without tags, a click on the Website button on Google Search or Google Maps looks like any other Google click: it arrives as Organic search on the google.com row, or as Direct when the Maps app sends no referrer. Your own utm_campaign is what separates profile visits.

This guide is for stores and service businesses with a Google Business Profile. The profile's own performance report counts website clicks; it does not tell you which of those clicks became paying customers.

How Google Business Profile visits are classified

Virling gives every session one channel, decided by its first pageview. The rules run in order and the first match wins:

  • A paid or email medium. A utm_medium of cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social or paidsocial is Paid, and email, newsletter or e-mail is Email. Any other medium, such as organic, does not decide the channel.
  • A Google referrer. Any host that starts with google. or contains .google., such as www.google.com or maps.google.com, is a known search host, so the visit is Organic search. Every Google domain shares one google.com row. The referrer only names the host, so a profile click and a search result click look the same.
  • No referrer, with a tag. Apps often open links with no referrer. Then Virling reads utm_source, or ref if there is no utm_source. google is a known search source, so the visit is still Organic search. A source Virling does not know, such as gbp or google_maps, counts as Referral.
  • No referrer, no tag. The visit is Direct. If it was opened in the Google app, the Direct row is labelled Google app, but that label covers anything opened in the app, not only your profile.

So untagged profile clicks are split between Organic search and Direct, mixed in with search clicks, typed visits and bookmarks. With utm_source=google and a medium that is not paid or email, every profile click lands in Organic search and carries your campaign, and the campaign is what you filter on. The full rule order is in channels.

Step 1: add the script

Add the Virling script to the <head> of every page of your site. It records each visit with its referrer and UTM tags, and sets the first-party visitor ID that payments need later. On Shopify, it goes in your theme's theme.liquid; on WordPress and WooCommerce, the Virling WordPress plugin adds it for you. The app's setup screen shows the exact tag for your site, and add the script covers where it goes.

Step 2: tag your website link with UTM parameters

In your Business Profile, edit the website under your contact details and replace the plain address with a tagged one. If you use the appointment or menu links, tag those too, with the same campaign:

https://yourbusiness.com/?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=business-profile
https://yourbusiness.com/book?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=business-profile&utm_content=appointment
  • utm_source=google, so a click that arrives with no referrer, as many Maps app clicks do, still counts as Organic search instead of Direct.
  • utm_medium=organic, or any value that is not a paid or email medium. It keeps the visit out of Paid.
  • utm_campaign=business-profile, one fixed value. The Campaign tab groups visitors and revenue by it, which is how you see profile visits apart from search.
  • utm_content, if you want it, can name the link or the location. Virling stores it with each visit; keep what you want to compare in the campaign so it shows up in reports.

If you run more than one location, give each profile the same campaign and its own utm_content, such as the town. If you also run local ads, tag those links with utm_medium=cpc and a different campaign so they stay in Paid; tracking Google Ads by campaign covers that setup.

The UTM builder makes the tagged URL for you. Once the profile is updated, open it on Google, click Website and paste the URL you land on into the UTM checker. It lists the tags on the link and flags a missing source or medium, capitals, spaces and duplicate parameters. More naming rules are in which UTM parameters to use.

Step 3: connect your payment provider

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs the payments and the visitor ID on each one. In the app, open your site, then Setup, then Connect payments, paste the read-only key your provider needs and click Connect and backfill.

  • Shopify. Create a custom app with only the read_orders scope and paste its token, then add the short theme snippet that stores the visitor ID on the cart, which Shopify copies onto the order. See Shopify.
  • WooCommerce. Create a REST API key with Read permission and paste it, then add the short snippet that stores the virling_vid cookie on each new order. See WooCommerce.
  • Stripe and other checkouts. Service businesses that take deposits or bookings through Stripe Payment Links, Polar or Dodo Payments links on their site need no code: the script adds the visitor ID when a visitor clicks the link. Your own checkout passes the virling_vid cookie, as link checkout to visits shows.

Other stores and checkouts are on ecommerce integrations. Make a test purchase or booking through your profile's Website button, then check that it appears on the Payments page with google.com as its source.

Local customers often find you on Maps, then come back days later by typing your address. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the payment goes to the profile visit, as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. See Google Business Profile revenue

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more.
  2. On the Channels card, open the Campaign tab and find business-profile. The row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click the row to filter the whole page to profile visits, then open the Landing page tab of the Pages card to see which links they used.
  4. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

To compare profile visits with the rest of your search traffic, clear the filter and look at the google.com row under Organic search. Organic search revenue tracking covers that side, and Google Shopping revenue tracking uses the same approach for free product listings. The revenue per visitor calculator does the sum for any channel.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • The business-profile campaign is missing. The tags are not on the link Google shows. Check the website field in your profile, and paste a live landing URL into the UTM checker.
  • Profile visits show as Referral. The utm_source is a value Virling does not know, such as gbp, and the referrer was missing. Change the source to google.
  • Profile visits show as Paid. The medium is a paid one such as cpc. Keep paid mediums for ads only.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching the payment; check the theme, order snippet or checkout code. See Unattributed revenue.
  • Customers who call or visit in person never reach your site, so they are not counted. A payment link you email after a call carries no visitor ID; it still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which Google Business Profile visits turn into paying customers?

Tag the website link on your profile with utm_source=google, a medium that is not paid or email, and a campaign of its own such as business-profile. Then store the visitor ID on each payment or order and use a tool that joins payments to visits. In Virling, the Campaign tab shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor for that campaign, apart from other Google traffic.

Why do Google Business Profile clicks show up as organic search or Direct?

A click on the website button usually arrives with a Google referrer such as www.google.com, which is a host only and looks like any search click, so Virling counts it as Organic search. Clicks from the Google Maps app often arrive with no referrer at all, and without a tag those count as Direct.

What UTM tags should I put on my Google Business Profile website link?

Use utm_source=google so the visit counts as Organic search even when the referrer is missing, a medium such as organic that is not a paid or email medium, and a fixed utm_campaign such as business-profile. Avoid a source like gbp or google_maps: Virling does not know it, so a visit with that tag and no referrer counts as Referral.

Can I track Google Maps website clicks for more than one location?

Yes. Give each location's profile the same campaign and its own utm_content, such as the town or branch name. The campaign row shows the total for all your profiles, and Virling stores utm_content with each visit.

Does Virling track phone calls or direction requests from my profile?

No. Virling records visits to your website and the payments that follow them. Calls, direction requests and messages that happen on Google do not reach your site, so they are not counted. A payment link you send after a call carries no visitor ID and counts as Unattributed.
