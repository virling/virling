Stack Overflow traffic revenue: do your answers bring paying customers?

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-21.

Stack Overflow traffic revenue is the money paid by people who first reached your site from an answer, a question or a profile on stackoverflow.com. To see which Stack Overflow visits turn into paying customers, tag the profile and answer links you control where the rules allow it, add a tracking script that keeps the referrer, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and connect your payment provider. Each payment is then joined to the visit before it. Reputation, answer views and upvotes do not tell you this.

For a developer tool, an answer that solves a real problem with your library or API can be read by developers for a long time. Some of them click through to your docs, try the product, and pay. Stack Overflow shows how often an answer was viewed, not who bought afterwards, so the revenue has to be measured on your side.

How a Stack Overflow visit is recognized

  • The referrer. A click on a link in an answer, a question or a profile usually arrives with stackoverflow.com as the referrer. stackoverflow.com is not on Virling's list of search, AI, community or social hosts, so it is in the Referral channel and shows as stackoverflow.com in the Source tab.
  • Usually only the origin. Browsers trim the referrer of a click to another site down to its origin by default, so most visits carry https://stackoverflow.com/, not the address of the answer. A click from your top answer, a comment and your profile look the same. This is why the links you control should carry tags.
  • A tag on the link. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, and when that is missing it reads ref. Developers often copy a link out of an answer into a chat, an issue or a code comment, and those clicks arrive with no referrer. A tagged one lands in Referral with its campaign; an untagged one is Direct. When a referrer is present it decides the channel and source, and the utm_campaign on the link is still recorded.
  • Other Stack Exchange sites. Each site sends its own host, such as superuser.com, serverfault.com or unix.stackexchange.com. Each is its own Referral row. A language site such as ru.stackoverflow.com is also its own row, apart from stackoverflow.com.

Why answers keep sending visits for years

An accepted or highly voted answer stays on the question page, and the question page keeps ranking in search for the error message or the task it describes. Developers who hit the same problem next year land on the same answer and click the same link. So a single answer can bring a slow, steady stream of visits long after you wrote it, unlike a launch post that peaks in a day.

That matters for attribution in two ways. Every new click starts a fresh visit, so an answer from years ago can still be credited for a payment made this month. And the payment often comes weeks after the click: a developer finds your library while fixing a bug, uses the free tier or the open-source version, and only pays when their project grows or their team needs the hosted plan. Virling credits a payment to a visit inside the lookback window, 90 days by default, and you can widen it to 365 days in site settings. See the model and lookback window.

Set up Stack Overflow revenue tracking

  1. Tag the links you control, where allowed. Your profile is yours: the website field and the About me text can carry a tagged link. In answers, link to your product only when it answers the question, and say that you work on it, as Stack Overflow's rules on self-promotion require. Other users can edit your answers, and a tracking parameter may be removed; a plain link still counts through the referrer. Use one utm_source for everything on Stack Overflow and one utm_campaign per placement: 
    Profile:   https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=stackoverflow&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=so-profile
Answers:   https://yourdomain.com/docs/?utm_source=stackoverflow&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=so-answers
    Keep the values lowercase and the same everywhere. Do not use utm_medium=email or cpc, which move the visit into Email or Paid. One shared campaign for answers is enough for most teams; a campaign per answer gets noisy and is more likely to be edited out. The UTM builder makes the links, and which UTM parameters to use covers naming.
  2. Add the script. Put the Virling tag in the <head> of every page your links point to, including docs pages, since answers usually link to docs. Docs on a subdomain such as docs.yourdomain.com count for the same site. It records the referrer and tags of the first pageview and sets the visitor cookie. See add the script, and framework guides for Next.js, Astro, WordPress, Framer and Webflow.
  3. Connect a payment provider. Virling reads payments from Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce; all of them are on the integrations page, with setup in the payments docs.
  4. Pass the visitor ID into checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. See link checkout to visits. Without this step you get visits but no revenue.

Then click the link on your own profile in a private window and make a test payment, and check that the visit shows as stackoverflow.com and the payment is matched. How channels are decided lists every rule and known source.

Compare Stack Overflow customers with other channels

  1. Pick a range of a few months at least, since the payment often comes weeks after the first visit.
  2. In the Channels card, switch to the Source tab and find stackoverflow.com. The row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average. Compare it with GitHub, search and community rows over the same range.
  3. Click the row to filter the whole page. The Pages card shows which docs or landing pages Stack Overflow visitors reached, and the revenue chart shows when their money arrived.
  4. Switch to the Campaign tab to split profile and answer links. Tagged clicks that arrived with no referrer are counted here too.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

Compare the default model, last non-direct, with first touch over the same range. If Stack Overflow revenue grows under first touch, developers found you through an answer and paid after coming back through search, docs or a typed URL. The Time to pay card shows the median days from first visit to first payment per channel. For the repository side of the same buyers, see GitHub revenue tracking; for the pages answers link to, see whether your docs bring paying customers; and for every developer channel together, see revenue attribution for developer tools.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Stack Overflow looks small but Direct is large. Links copied out of answers into chats, issues and code arrive with no referrer and no tag. Tag the links you control.
  • The Campaign tab has rows the Source tab does not. Tagged clicks with no referrer count in Referral and in their campaign, but not in the stackoverflow.com source row. Read the campaigns for placements and the source row for clicks from the site itself.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A customer says they found you on Stack Overflow but the payment is credited elsewhere. A later non-direct visit took the credit under the default model, the visit is outside the lookback window, or they paid from another device. Try first touch or a longer window.

Frequently asked questions

Do Stack Overflow answers bring paying customers?

They can, and you can check. Add the tracking script, pass the visitor ID into checkout and connect your payment provider. Visits from stackoverflow.com land in the Referral channel and show as stackoverflow.com in the Source tab, and each payment is credited to a visit before it, so that row shows the visitors, customers and revenue Stack Overflow brought.

Why can I not see which answer sent a visitor?

Browsers usually send only the origin, https://stackoverflow.com/, as the referrer of a click to another site, not the address of the answer. Every click from an answer, a comment or a profile looks the same. Give your profile and answer links their own utm_campaign to tell them apart in the Campaign tab.

Can I put UTM tags on links in my Stack Overflow answers?

Link to your product only when it answers the question, and disclose that you work on it. Other users can edit answers and may remove tracking parameters, so keep tags short and expect some to go. A plain link still counts through the referrer. Your profile website field is the safest place for a tagged link.

Does an old answer still get credit for new customers?

Yes. Each new click from an old answer starts a fresh visit, and a payment is credited to a visit inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. The age of the answer does not matter, only the time between the click and the payment.

Do Super User, Server Fault and other Stack Exchange sites count as Stack Overflow?

No. Each site sends its own host as the referrer, such as superuser.com or serverfault.com, and shows as its own Referral row in the Source tab. Give links there the same utm_campaign as your Stack Overflow links if you want one total in the Campaign tab.
