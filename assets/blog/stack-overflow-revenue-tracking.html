Stack Overflow traffic revenue: do your answers bring paying customers?

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-21.

Stack Overflow traffic revenue is the money paid by people who first reached your site from an answer, a question or a profile on stackoverflow.com. Reputation, votes and view counts stop at the question page. To learn which readers went on to pay, the click has to be joined to the payment on your own site.

For a developer tool, an answer that solves a real problem with your library or API can be read for years. Some readers click through to your docs, try the product and pay. This post covers what is particular to Stack Overflow: how its referrer arrives, what the network's rules let you link, why old answers keep working, and why tags in answers rarely survive.

How a Stack Overflow visit is recognized

  • The referrer. stackoverflow.com is not on Virling's lists of search, AI, community or social hosts, so a click from it lands in the Referral channel. The Source tab shows the bare host, stackoverflow.com.
  • Origin only. Browsers trim the referrer of a click to another site down to https://stackoverflow.com/ by default, not the question URL. Your top answer, a comment under someone else's answer and your profile all arrive alike.
  • Copied links. Developers paste a link from an answer into a chat, an issue or a code comment, and the later click carries no referrer. Without a tag it is counted as Direct.
  • The rest of the network. Every Stack Exchange site sends its own host, such as superuser.com, serverfault.com or unix.stackexchange.com, and gets its own Referral row. Language sites such as ru.stackoverflow.com are separate rows too.

What the Stack Exchange rules allow

The help center page on how not to be a spammer sets the limits. You may link to your own product or docs when it answers the question, but you must say in the answer that you made it or work on it. An answer that only points to your product, or the same link posted across many questions, is likely to be flagged as spam and deleted, and a deleted answer stops sending visits. Solve the problem on the page and use the link as the place to read more.

Your profile is different. The website field and the About me text are yours, and they are the one place on the site where a tagged link is not edited by anyone else.

Why answers keep sending visits for years

An accepted or highly voted answer stays on the question page, and the question page keeps ranking in search for the error message or the task it describes. Developers who hit the same problem next year land on the same answer and click the same link. So a single answer can bring a slow, steady stream of visits long after you wrote it, unlike a launch post that peaks in a day.

That matters for attribution in two ways. Every new click starts a fresh visit, so an answer from years ago can still be credited for a payment made this month. And the payment often comes weeks after the click: a developer finds your library while fixing a bug, uses the free tier or the open-source version, and only pays when their project grows or their team needs the hosted plan. Virling credits a payment to a visit inside the lookback window, 90 days by default, and you can widen it to 365 days in site settings. See the model and lookback window.

Why UTM tags in answers usually get lost

Answers on Stack Overflow are edited by other users. An editor tidying a post often rewrites a link to its plain address and drops the query string, and reviewers tend to approve that as cleanup. A link full of utm_ parameters also looks promotional, which invites the edit. Expect tagged links in answers to lose their tags over time, while the referrer keeps counting clicks that come straight from the page.

Two habits work better than tags there. Link an answer to a page few other sources link to, such as the docs page for that exact error, and read the Pages card with the stackoverflow.com row selected. And keep one tagged link where only you edit it, in your profile:

Profile:   https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=stackoverflow&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=so-profile

The tag matters most for copied links. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, or ref when it is missing, so a tagged link pasted into a chat lands in Referral with its campaign instead of Direct. The UTM builder makes the link.

Reading the stackoverflow.com row

  • Use a long range. Answer traffic is slow and steady, so a week says little. Six months or a year shows whether answers pay.
  • Try first touch. A developer often finds you in an answer, then returns through search or a typed URL to pay. The default model, last non-direct, gives that later search visit the credit; first touch gives it to the answer.
  • Check the landing pages. Select the row, and the Pages card lists the docs pages Stack Overflow readers reached, which shows which answers are working.
  • Add up the network. Super User, Server Fault and the other sites are separate rows. Give your profile link on each site the same utm_campaign to see one total in the Campaign tab.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Visits from one answer stopped. The question may have been closed or deleted, your answer removed as promotion, or the link edited out. The answer's revision history on Stack Overflow shows which.
  • The answer campaign shrinks but the source row does not. Edits removed the tags. The referrer still counts those clicks under stackoverflow.com.
  • Direct grows when a popular answer does. Links copied out of answers arrive with no referrer and no tag. Point copied links at a tagged profile link or a distinct docs page.
  • A customer says they found you on Stack Overflow but the payment is credited elsewhere. A later non-direct visit took the credit, the answer visit is outside the lookback window, or they paid from another device. Try first touch or a longer window.

Frequently asked questions

Do Stack Overflow answers bring paying customers?

Some do, and the stackoverflow.com row in the Referral channel shows how many. Once the script runs on the pages your answers link to and the visitor ID reaches checkout, that row lists the visitors, customers and revenue that came from Stack Overflow.

Why can I not see which answer sent a visitor?

Browsers usually send only the origin, https://stackoverflow.com/, as the referrer of a click to another site, not the address of the answer. Every click from an answer, a comment or a profile looks the same. Link each answer to a distinct docs page and read the Pages card, or tag your profile link with its own utm_campaign.

Can I put UTM tags on links in my Stack Overflow answers?

You can, but other users edit answers and often strip the query string from links, so tags in answers rarely last. A plain link still counts through the referrer. Your profile website field is the safest place for a tagged link.

Will an answer that links to my product be deleted?

Not if it answers the question on its own and says that you work on the product. The Stack Exchange help center asks you to disclose your affiliation and not to post mainly to promote; an answer that is only a link, or the same link across many questions, is often deleted as spam.

Does an old answer still get credit for new customers?

Yes. Each new click from an old answer starts a fresh visit, and a payment is credited to a visit inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. The age of the answer does not matter, only the time between the click and the payment.

Do Super User, Server Fault and other Stack Exchange sites count as Stack Overflow?

No. Each site sends its own host as the referrer, such as superuser.com or serverfault.com, and shows as its own Referral row in the Source tab. Give links there the same utm_campaign as your Stack Overflow links if you want one total in the Campaign tab.
