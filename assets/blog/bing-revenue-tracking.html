Bing revenue tracking: which Bing search visitors pay

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-20.

To track revenue from Bing, add a tracking script that records the bing.com referrer on each visit, carry the visitor ID into checkout, then join each payment to the visit that came before it. You then see which Bing visitors turned into paying customers, the revenue they brought and what each one is worth next to Google. Clicks and impressions in Bing Webmaster Tools do not tell you this.

Bing is usually a smaller source than Google, so it is easy to ignore. The question that matters is not how many visitors it sends but whether they pay. This post covers organic Bing search only, and the engines that use Bing results. It does not cover paid clicks; for those, see Microsoft Ads revenue tracking.

How Bing visits show up

Virling gives every session one channel, decided by its first pageview. A click on a Bing results page arrives with bing.com as the referrer. bing.com is a known search host, so the visit counts as Organic search and shows as a Bing row next to google.com, duckduckgo.com and the other engines. Subdomains match too, so a regional host such as cn.bing.com is also Bing.

  • The referrer is a host only. Bing tells your site the visitor came from bing.com, not what they typed. No script on your site can recover the query, so no tool that runs on your pages can show revenue by Bing keyword. The landing page is the closest stand-in: it is the page that ranked.
  • Copilot is not Bing search. Visits from copilot.microsoft.com count as AI, because AI hosts are checked before search hosts. A click on a bing.com page counts as Bing search, even when that page shows an AI answer, because the referrer is still bing.com. Copilot revenue tracking covers the AI side.
  • Untagged ads look organic. A Microsoft Ads click that arrives from bing.com with no utm_medium looks like an organic click. Tag ad links with utm_medium=cpc so they move to Paid and stay out of your organic numbers.

The full rule order is in channels.

Engines that use Bing results

Some search engines, such as DuckDuckGo, Yahoo and Ecosia, draw on Bing's results for some or all of their pages. The click still comes from their own site, so the referrer is duckduckgo.com, a Yahoo domain or ecosia.org, and each one is its own row under Organic search, not part of the Bing row. All of them are known search hosts, so none of these visits fall into Referral or Direct. For reading their rows, see revenue from privacy search engines.

If you want a rough view of everything that ranks you through Bing's index, add the Bing row to the rows of the engines that use it. Keep them apart when you judge each engine, since their visitors differ.

Set it up in Virling

1. Add the script

Put the tag in the <head> of every page. It records the referrer and any UTM tags on the first pageview, which is all Bing attribution needs. The setup screen in the app shows the exact tag for your site. See add the script.

<script defer src="https://app.virling.com/v.js" data-site="yourdomain.com"></script>

2. Tag the links you control

Bing organic results are not links you can tag, and they do not need tags: the referrer already credits them. Tags matter for links to your site that you place yourself, such as the website link on a business listing. For those, use:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=bing&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=business-listing
  • utm_source=bing is a known search source. When a visit arrives with no referrer, this tag puts it in Organic search as Bing.
  • utm_medium must not be a paid or email value such as cpc or newsletter, or the visit moves to Paid or Email.
  • utm_campaign names the link, so it gets its own row in the Campaign tab.

The UTM builder makes the link for you, and which UTM parameters to use has more naming rules. A tracked link also works if you want a short URL with its own click count.

3. Connect your payment provider

Tags and referrers only get you visits. Revenue needs the visitor ID in checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Each provider has its own page under integrations: Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce. The setup for all of them is in link checkout to visits.

Searchers often read, leave and come back later by typing your address. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the payment goes to the Bing visit, as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. See the attribution model.

Compare Bing with Google by revenue

Clicks say which engine sends more people. Revenue per visitor says which one sends people who pay. A smaller engine can be worth more per visitor than a larger one, and you only see that once payments are joined to visits.

  1. Pick a range of a few months, since search visitors often take days or weeks to pay.
  2. On the Channels card, click Organic search to filter the whole dashboard to search visitors.
  3. Open the Source tab. bing.com and google.com are separate rows, each with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, and a chip that compares it with your site average.
  4. Click the Bing row to filter the page to Bing. The Landing page tab of the Pages card now shows which pages Bing searchers arrived on and the revenue credited to each. Do the same for Google and compare the two lists.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

Judge revenue per visitor, not totals, and wait until both rows have several payments. A Bing row with a handful of visitors and one payment says little either way. Organic search revenue tracking covers reading the search channel as a whole.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Bing shows visits but no revenue. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • Bing revenue looks too low. A customer who found you on Bing and came back through a newsletter is credited to Email under the default model. Switch to first touch in Site settings to see which searches start journeys; first-touch or last-touch attribution explains the difference.
  • Bing organic looks high after you started Microsoft Ads. Some ad links are missing utm_medium=cpc.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so Bing's real visitor number can be higher than any analytics tool shows. Revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from Bing?

Add the tracking script to every page, connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout, so each payment joins the visit before it. Visits that arrive from bing.com count as Organic search, and the Source tab shows Bing's visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.

Do I need UTM tags for Bing organic search?

No. Bing organic results are not links you can tag, and a click from a Bing results page arrives with bing.com as the referrer, which Virling already counts as Organic search. Tag only the links you place yourself, such as a business listing, with utm_source=bing.

Can I see which Bing keywords bring paying customers?

No. Bing tells your site that the visitor came from bing.com, not what they searched, so no script on your site can record the query. The landing page is the closest stand-in, since it is the page that ranked.

Are DuckDuckGo and Yahoo visits counted as Bing?

No. Some engines draw on Bing's results, but the click comes from their own site, so the referrer is duckduckgo.com or a Yahoo domain. Each shows as its own row under Organic search.

Is Microsoft Copilot traffic counted as Bing?

Visits from copilot.microsoft.com count as AI, not search. A click on a bing.com results page counts as Bing search, even when that page shows an AI answer, because the referrer is bing.com.

How do I compare Bing and Google?

Filter to Organic search and open the Source tab. bing.com and google.com are separate rows, each with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. Compare revenue per visitor, not visitors, and use a range long enough for both rows to have several payments.
