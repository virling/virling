Churn by acquisition channel: which channels lose customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-24.

Churn by acquisition channel is the share of the customers each marketing channel brought who then left. To get it, credit every customer to the channel of their first payment and keep it fixed, then divide the customers a channel lost in a period by the customers it had at the start. One churn rate for the whole business tells you how many left. Split by channel, it tells you where the leavers came from.

Most SaaS founders have the first number, from their billing page. The second is harder, because billing knows who cancelled and analytics knows where visitors came from, and nothing joins the two.

The formula for churn by channel

channel churn = channel customers lost in the period / channel customers at the start

  • Fix the channel at the first payment. A customer who came from a launch post and later returns through a search still belongs to the launch post. If the channel can move, leavers drift into whichever channel they touched last.
  • Leave out customers who joined during the period, from both numbers. Counting them makes the base larger and churn look lower, most of all in the channel that is growing fastest.
  • Pick one definition of lost. Cancelled, or stopped paying. Failed cards also end subscriptions, so decide whether they count, and apply it to every channel.
  • Use one period. Monthly plans suit monthly churn. Annual customers cannot leave until their year ends, so a channel that sells mostly annual plans shows low monthly churn until renewals come due.

The churn rate calculator does the sum. Run it once per channel, with the customers that channel brought.

Why a channel can win on signups and lose on churn

  • Curiosity is not need. A launch or a popular post reaches many people at once. Some pay to try it, and the reason to keep paying was never there.
  • The promise was too big. When an ad or a post promises more than the product does, people find out in their first month, which is when they cancel.
  • The discount did the selling. Customers who came for a first-month price meet the full price on the first renewal.
  • The fit is wrong. A channel can reach people outside your market: too small a team, a country you do not support, a need you cover only in part.

A signup report cannot show any of this, because the channel has already been counted as a win. It keeps looking like one for as long as new signups replace the people leaving.

A worked example

Communities brought the most new customers and added the fewest in the end, because 12 of its 80 left. The blended 7.5% describes none of the three channels: search is at less than half of it and communities at twice it. Communities had a third of the customers at the start (80 of 240) and two thirds of the leavers (12 of 18), so a fix to what those posts promise would help more than a fix to the whole product.

If each rate held, a communities customer would stay about 1 / 0.15 = 6.7 months and a search customer about 30. Treat that as a rough guide, since churn is rarely steady. What those months are worth in money is a separate question, answered in LTV by acquisition channel.

Small samples: how much to trust a channel's churn

Splitting customers by channel makes every group smaller, and churn counts an event that is rare to begin with.

  • Read the counts, not only the rate. In the example, Email lost 2 of 40. One more cancellation and its churn is 7.5% instead of 5%. With 5 customers, one cancellation is 20 points.
  • Put a range on it. By a standard 95% interval (Wilson), 2 of 40 fits a true rate anywhere from about 1% to 17%, 4 of 120 from about 1% to 8%, and 12 of 80 from about 9% to 24%. The first two overlap, so the example cannot rank email and search. It does put communities above search.
  • Zero is not proof. None of 10 customers leaving still fits a true monthly rate of up to about 26%, with 95% confidence.
  • Compare customers of the same age. Churn is often highest in the first months. A channel you started last month holds only new customers, so it can look worse than an old one without being worse. Group by first month, as in the cohort retention template, and compare groups at the same age.
  • Add months together before you act. Three months that point the same way say more than one large gap. Do not split by campaign until the channel itself has enough customers.

Read churn by channel in Virling

Virling joins each payment to the visits before it, and the Retention page groups customers by the channel, referrer and campaign credited for their first payment. Renewals keep that first channel.

  1. Open Retention and stay on the channel tab, or switch to referrers or campaigns.
  2. Read 30-day churn. Of the customers whose first payment was more than 30 and at most 60 days ago, it is the share with no payment (not fully refunded) and no live subscription in the last 30 days. The first payment itself does not count as activity. Every customer in it is of a similar age, so channels are compared fairly.
  3. Hover the percentage to see the counts behind it.
  4. The date range does not change 30-day churn. It does change Customers, the customers whose first payment falls in the range, and under it how many are still paying today. For an older group, set the range to one month and compare the two counts per channel.
  5. Check Refund rate in the same row, the share of customers acquired with any refund.

Until a site has 60 days of payments, the page leads with refund rate and 30-day churn, and says how many days of payments there are. LTV needs longer. When there are at least 20 customers and 5 still paying, the page can lead with a gap, such as a source that sent 60% of your customers but only 25% of the ones still paying. It needs at least 5 customers from that source and a gap of 15 points or more.

What counts as still paying

The provider's subscription status decides where Virling can read it: a Stripe subscription that is active, trialing or past_due is live, and one cancelled at the end of its period stays live until the period ends. Otherwise, a customer is still paying when they have a payment, not fully refunded, within their last billing interval plus 7 days. Polar and Creem need the optional subscriptions:read scope and Paddle subscription.read. Without it their customers use payment recency and the page says so. The status list per provider is in retention by channel.

30-day churn is not the monthly rate in the formula above. The formula takes every customer a channel had, of any age. 30-day churn looks only at new customers and asks whether they paid again or still have a live subscription. Expect the two to differ.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Everyone churned. If you sell one-off products, a customer with one payment and no subscription counts as churned after 30 days. Read refund rate and revenue per channel instead.
  • Annual customers show as churned. With no subscription status to read, an annual customer has no payment in the last 30 days. With Polar, Creem or Paddle, give the key the read scope above.
  • Long-standing customers are missing. Virling imports the last 90 days, and a customer whose earliest payment there is a renewal started before the import, so they are left out.
  • Unattributed has the most churn. Those first payments were not joined to a visit. See Unattributed revenue and link checkout to visits.

For the money that leaves with those customers, read MRR by acquisition channel.

Frequently asked questions

What is churn by acquisition channel?

It is your customer churn rate split by the marketing channel that brought each customer. Each customer stays in the channel credited for their first payment, so a cancellation counts against the channel that won them.

How do I calculate churn by acquisition channel?

Take the customers a channel had at the start of the month, count how many of those cancelled or stopped paying during it, and divide the second by the first. Leave out customers who joined during the month. Use the same period and the same definition of lost for every channel.

Why do some channels have higher churn than others?

Channels bring people with different intent. A launch post reaches curious people who try and leave, while someone who searched for the problem may well still have it next month. Promises in the ad or post, discounts and which plan people pick also differ by channel.

How many customers do I need before I trust a channel's churn?

There is no fixed number, but with 5 customers one cancellation is 20 points, and with 40 it is 2.5. Read the counts behind every percentage, and wait for several months that agree before you move budget.

What is the difference between churn by channel and LTV by channel?

Churn is how fast a channel's customers leave. LTV is how much they have paid. Churn can be read after a month or two, while LTV needs several billing cycles. A channel can have higher churn and still a higher LTV when its customers pick a bigger plan.

How does Virling measure 30-day churn?

Of the customers whose first payment was more than 30 and at most 60 days ago, it is the share with no payment that was not fully refunded and no live subscription in the last 30 days. The first payment itself does not count as activity, and the date range does not change it.
