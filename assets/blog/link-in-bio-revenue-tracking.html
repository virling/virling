Link in bio UTM tracking: which link-in-bio clicks make sales

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-22.

To see which link-in-bio clicks turn into paying customers, give every link in your Instagram, TikTok and YouTube profiles its own UTM tags: the platform as utm_source and the link's name as utm_campaign. Carry the visitor ID into checkout, then join each payment to the visit that came before it. You then see visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor for each platform and each link. Link taps in the apps' own insights stop at the click and do not tell you this.

This post covers organic profile links: the bio link on Instagram and TikTok, the links on a YouTube channel and in video descriptions, and the links page many creators and small stores put behind them.

Why link-in-bio visits arrive as Direct

A browser sends a referrer when someone clicks a link on a web page. Profile links are mostly tapped inside mobile apps, and links opened there often arrive with no referrer at all. With no referrer and no tag, a visit has nothing that says where it came from, so most tools count it as Direct, next to typed addresses and bookmarks.

  • Instagram. Outbound clicks often arrive with l.instagram.com as the referrer, or with none when opened in the app. When the in-app browser names Instagram in its user agent, Virling credits the visit to instagram.com in Social. Instagram revenue tracking covers stories and the rest in detail.
  • TikTok. The bio link opens in TikTok's in-app browser, which often sends no referrer. Virling credits the visit to tiktok.com in Social when the user agent names TikTok. For ads there, see TikTok Ads revenue tracking.
  • YouTube. Clicks from a description or channel link can arrive with youtube.com as the referrer, which is Social. Virling has no in-app browser rule for YouTube, so a click with no referrer and no tag stays Direct. YouTube revenue tracking covers descriptions and pinned comments.

In every case the referrer is a host only. Even when it is present it says instagram.com or tiktok.com, never which link in your bio was tapped. If the link opens in a plain system browser that does not name the app, the visit is Direct. The full rule order is in channels; why Direct is your biggest traffic source and dark social traffic cover the other ways a visit loses its source.

The fix is the same everywhere: one tagged link per profile and per link. The tag travels inside the URL, so it survives the app, the system browser and a missing referrer.

Step 1: pick a naming scheme for bio links

Write the scheme down once and use it on every platform, so each link always lands in the same row:

  • utm_source is the platform, lowercase: instagram, tiktok or youtube. All three are known Social sources in Virling.
  • utm_medium=social on every organic profile link.
  • utm_campaign names the link: bio for a single bio link, or bio- plus the link's job when you have several, such as bio-shop, bio-pricing or bio-free-guide. Lowercase, with hyphens. The Campaign tab groups visitors and revenue by this value, so this is the tag that tells your links apart.
  • utm_content is optional. Use it for the spot, such as bio or description, and keep the link itself in the campaign.
https://yourdomain.com/shop?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=bio-shop
https://yourdomain.com/shop?utm_source=tiktok&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=bio-shop
https://yourdomain.com/free-guide?utm_source=youtube&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=bio-free-guide&utm_content=description

Keeping the same campaign name across platforms lets you compare one link on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube; the source tells the platforms apart. When you swap what a link points to, change the campaign too, for example bio-pricing then bio-webinar, so each version gets its own row. The social UTM link generator builds one tagged link per platform, and the UTM naming template gives you a table of values to share with your team.

Step 2: decide where the links page lives

Many profiles point to one links page that lists several links. Where that page lives changes what you see:

  • A links page on your own domain, such as yourdomain.com/links. Tag the profile link that points to it, for example ?utm_source=tiktok&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=bio. Clicks between your own pages never change where a visit came from, so the visit stays TikTok, and the page each visitor opens next shows in the Pages card.
  • A links page on another domain. That domain becomes the referrer for every click on it, and a referrer wins over utm_source, so the visit's source and channel are that domain under Referral, not Instagram, TikTok or YouTube. The campaign tag is still kept, so tagging each link on that page with its own utm_campaign still gives you revenue per link in the Campaign tab. Use a separate links page, or separate campaign names, per platform if you want the platforms apart.
  • No links page. Point the bio straight at one tagged page on your site. Simplest, and the source stays the platform.

Step 3: add the script

Add the Virling script to the <head> of every page. It records each visit with its referrer and UTM tags, and sets the first-party visitor ID that checkout needs later. The app's setup screen shows the exact tag for your site, and add the script covers where it goes, with guides for common frameworks and site builders.

If a long tagged URL is awkward in a profile field, use a tracked link instead. In Virling, open the Campaign tab on the Channels card, click + Tracked link, pick YouTube, or pick Other and type instagram.com or tiktok.com as the source, then pick the page it should open. The short link adds the tags on the click, and the Campaign tab lists it with its visitors and revenue. See tracked links.

Step 4: connect Shopify, Stripe or another provider

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs the payments and the visitor ID in checkout. In the app, open your site, then Setup, then Connect payments, paste the key your provider needs and click Connect and backfill; see payments. Each provider has its own page: Shopify, Stripe, WooCommerce, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem and RevenueCat, all listed on integrations.

If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the visitor ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Every case is covered in link checkout to visits.

People often tap a bio link, leave, and come back later by typing your URL or searching your name. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the payment goes to the tagged bio visit, as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. If they subscribe, every renewal keeps counting toward that link.

See revenue per bio link

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more, so people who tapped a link and paid later have time to show up.
  2. In the Channels card, open Social, or switch to the Source tab, and find instagram.com, l.instagram.com, tiktok.com and youtube.com. Each row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  3. Switch to the Campaign tab. With one campaign per link, bio-shop, bio-pricing and the rest are rows with their own visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  4. Click a row to filter the whole page, then check the Pages card for where those visitors went.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Direct jumped the day you posted. Some profile link is untagged and opened in a browser that did not name the app. Check every bio, channel link and description.
  • A domain you do not recognise shows up in Referral. It is likely a links page on another domain. Read it by campaign, or move the links page to your own domain.
  • One link splits into several campaign rows. The same link was tagged with different spellings. Pick one name from your scheme and fix the others.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.

The same idea works for Pinterest pins and profile links, a Snapchat Public Profile and Twitch panels. Tagged Snapchat and Twitch visits count as Referral rather than Social, so read those by campaign.

Frequently asked questions

How do I add UTM tracking to my link in bio?

Add utm_source for the platform, utm_medium=social and a utm_campaign that names the link to every URL in your bio and on your links page, for example ?utm_source=tiktok&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=bio-pricing. Use one campaign per link so each gets its own row in reports.

Why do link-in-bio clicks show up as Direct?

Links opened inside the Instagram, TikTok or YouTube apps often arrive with no referrer. With no referrer and no UTM tag, a visit has nothing that says where it came from, so it is counted as Direct. A utm_source tag travels in the link itself and prevents this.

How do I see which link-in-bio clicks make sales?

Tag each link with its own utm_campaign, add a tracking script that sets a visitor ID, pass that ID into checkout and connect your payment provider. Virling joins each payment to the visit before it, so the Campaign tab shows visitors and revenue per link.

Should I use a links page on another domain?

It works for the Campaign tab, because the campaign tag is kept. But a links page on another domain becomes the referrer, so the visit's source and channel are that domain under Referral, not Instagram, TikTok or YouTube. A links page on your own domain avoids this.

Do I need a different link for each platform?

Yes. Use the same page with a different utm_source for Instagram, TikTok and YouTube, so each platform is its own source. Keep the campaign names the same across platforms if you want to compare one link on all three.
