Community revenue tracking: which Discord, Slack and forum posts bring customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

To see which community visits turn into paying customers, tag every link you share in a Discord server, Slack group or forum with the platform in utm_source and the community's name in utm_campaign. Carry the visitor ID into checkout, then join each payment to the visit that came before it. You then see visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor for each community, month after month. Member counts, reactions and replies do not tell you this.

Answering questions in a founders' Discord or a niche forum can take hours a week. Whether that time pays comes down to one question: did anyone who clicked your link in this community become a customer, and how does that compare with the other places you post?

Why links from chat apps lose the referrer

There are three ways a visit gets credited to a community, and only one of them is under your control.

  • The referrer. A click on a forum in a web browser usually arrives with the forum's host as the referrer. Public communities Virling knows, such as reddit.com, news.ycombinator.com, indiehackers.com and dev.to, land in the Communities channel. Any other forum host, such as a Discourse or Circle site, lands in Referral under its own name. Either way the referrer is a host only, so it cannot tell you which thread or post the click came from.
  • The in-app browser. Most Discord and Slack reading happens in the desktop and mobile apps, and links opened from them often arrive with no referrer. When the app opens the link in its own browser and names itself in the user agent, Virling credits the visit to discord.com or slack.com, in Social. Many clicks open in the system browser instead, which carries no sign of the app. With no referrer and no tag, those count as Direct, in Virling and in most other tools.
  • A UTM tag. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source. discord and slack are known Social sources, and reddit, indiehackers, hn and devto are known Communities sources. The tag travels in the link itself, so it survives the app, the system browser and a missing referrer.

If your Direct traffic rises the day you share something in a busy server, this is usually why. The full rule order is in channels, and why Direct is your biggest traffic source covers the other causes.

Tag links by community

The channel only says Social, Communities or Referral. What you want is one row per community, so the community goes in the campaign:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=discord&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=saas-founders-discord&utm_content=intro-channel
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=slack&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=bootstrappers-slack&utm_content=show-and-tell
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=indiehackers&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=indiehackers-forum&utm_content=pricing-thread
  • utm_source names the platform: discord, slack, or the forum's name. Use the known values above where they fit, so the visit lands in the right channel. A forum Virling does not know counts as Referral, which is fine as long as the campaign is set.
  • utm_medium=community on all of them. Any medium except a paid or email one leaves the channel to the source, so keep it the same everywhere and never use cpc or email for community links.
  • utm_campaign names the community: one value per server, group or forum, lowercase, with hyphens, and the same value every time you post there. The Campaign tab groups visitors and revenue by this value, so this is the tag that gives you revenue per community over months.
  • utm_content names the spot: the channel, thread or post. Virling stores it with each visit; keep the community in the campaign so it shows up in reports.

Write the names down once and reuse them. The UTM naming template gives you a sheet for that, the UTM builder makes each link, and which UTM parameters to use covers the naming rules.

If a long tagged URL looks out of place in a chat, use a tracked link instead. In Virling, open Links, pick Discord as the place, or Other with slack or the forum's name as the source, and the page it should open. The short link adds the tags on the click, and its id becomes the campaign, so make one link per community and keep sharing that same link. See tracked links.

Set up Virling

  1. Add the script. Put the Virling tag in the <head> of every page, including the pages you link to from communities. It records the referrer and tags of the first pageview and sets the visitor cookie. See add the script, and framework guides for Next.js, Astro, WordPress, Framer and Webflow.
  2. Connect a payment provider. Virling reads payments from Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce; all of them are on the integrations page, with setup in the payments docs.
  3. Pass the visitor ID into checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. See link checkout to visits. Without this step you get visits but no revenue.
  4. Tag before you post. Make the links for each community you are active in, then open one yourself and check that the visit shows on the dashboard with the right source and campaign.

Measure paid customers per community over months

Community members rarely click once and buy. They see your name in a few threads, visit, leave, and come back later by typing your URL. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the payment goes to the tagged community visit, as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. If your trial plus decision time is longer, widen the window in Site settings; see the model and lookback window. If they subscribe, every renewal keeps counting toward that community.

  1. Pick a range of a few months, since the value of a community builds up over many posts.
  2. Switch the Channels card to the Campaign tab. With one campaign per community, each community is a row with its own visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click a row to filter the whole page. The revenue chart shows when that community's money arrived, and the Pages card shows where its visitors landed.
  4. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.
  5. Switch the model to first touch in Site settings and compare. If a community's revenue grows under first touch, it introduces buyers who later pay through another channel. History is recomputed, and you can switch back.

The Time to pay card shows the median days from first visit to first payment per channel; wait at least that long before you judge a community you just joined. First-touch or last-touch attribution and how long a visitor takes to become a customer go deeper.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • The community looks small but Direct jumped the day you posted. Some links went out untagged, or someone reshared your plain URL. Use the tagged link or tracked link every time.
  • One community is split across rows. The campaign was spelled differently in different posts, such as SaaS-Founders and saas-founders-discord. Pick one value and keep it.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A buyer says they found you in a community but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have read your posts without clicking, a later non-direct visit took the credit, or they paid from another device. The cookie lives in one browser, so no analytics tool can join those. Try first touch.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so a community's real visitor number can be higher than what any analytics tool shows. Revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from a Discord or Slack community?

Tag every link you share with utm_source set to the platform and utm_campaign set to the community, add the tracking script to your site, pass the visitor ID into checkout and connect your payment provider. The Campaign tab then shows visitors and revenue for each community.

Why do clicks from Discord and Slack show up as Direct?

Links opened from the desktop and mobile apps often arrive with no referrer. When the app opens the link in its own browser and names itself in the user agent, Virling credits Discord or Slack, but a link opened in the system browser carries no such sign. With no referrer and no tag, the visit is Direct. A utm_source on the link keeps it credited.

Which channel do Discord, Slack and forum visits land in?

In Virling, discord and slack are known Social sources. Public communities such as Reddit, Hacker News, Indie Hackers and dev.to are in Communities. A forum Virling does not know, such as a private Discourse or Circle site, counts as Referral and shows under its own name.

How do I compare one community with another?

Give each community its own utm_campaign, or its own tracked link, and keep using it for every post there. The Campaign tab then has one row per community with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, over any date range you pick.

How long should I measure a community before judging it?

Months rather than days. Community members often click several times before they buy, so pick a range of a few months, check that the lookback window, 90 days by default, is longer than your trial plus decision time, and compare first touch with the default model.
