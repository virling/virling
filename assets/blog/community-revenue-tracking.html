Community revenue tracking: which Discord, Slack and forum posts bring customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

To see which Slack workspaces, forums and paid communities bring paying customers, give each community one utm_campaign value, put the platform in utm_source, and join every payment to the visit before it. Each month you then get a row per community with its visitors and revenue, and the new customers it brought when you filter by it.

This post is the hub for groups that are not one big public site: a founders' Slack, a niche Discourse forum, a paid group on Circle or a similar platform. Replies, member counts and likes show that people saw you. They do not show who paid, or whether one group is worth more of your week than another.

Where community clicks land

Slack and most community apps often open links with no referrer, so an untagged click counts as Direct, while a forum read in a web browser usually sends its host, which lands in Communities for public sites Virling knows, such as Reddit or Indie Hackers, and in Referral under its own name for the rest. Either way the host never names the workspace, thread or space, so the tag has to. The full rule order is in channels.

UTM naming for each kind of community

Use utm_medium=community on all of these links, never a paid or email medium, and pick a campaign value per community that you never change. The source and the content depend on the kind of group:

  • Slack workspaces. Many founders sit in several workspaces, and a link pinned in a channel or a workspace canvas keeps getting clicks for months. One campaign per workspace keeps a quiet workspace with paying members from hiding behind a loud one.
  • Forums. A thread is read long after you post, and clicks from it send the forum's host as the referrer, which then decides the channel and the source. The campaign still comes from your tag, so the forum keeps its own row. For single public sites, see Indie Hackers posts and milestones and a Hacker News post.
  • Paid communities. Posts sit behind a login, so a referrer can only ever name the platform's host, and members reading in its mobile app may send no referrer at all. Tag every link there, including event descriptions, course lessons and welcome posts.

Keep the values in one place: the UTM naming template is a sheet for them and the UTM builder turns them into links; which UTM parameters to use explains the rules. Where a long URL looks odd, make a tracked link instead: on the Campaign tab click + Tracked link, pick Other and type the source, such as slack.com or the forum's domain. Its id becomes the campaign, so make one per community and keep sharing it. See tracked links.

A monthly view of paying customers per community

A community's value builds over many posts, so read it once a month rather than after each one.

  1. Set the date picker to This month, or use Custom range for a month that has ended.
  2. Switch the Channels card to the Campaign tab. Each community is a row with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, and a chip against your site average.
  3. Click a community's row to filter the page. The numbers at the top then show that community's Revenue and New customers for the month, and the revenue chart shows the days its money came in.
  4. Turn on Compare to other dates and pick the month before, to see whether the community is growing or fading.
  5. Copy new customers and revenue per community into your sheet. After a few months the rows show which groups to keep and which to leave.

Payments are credited under the default model, last non-direct, within the lookback window, 90 days by default, so a member who clicks in one month and pays in a later one still counts for that community if no other non-direct visit came in between, and renewals keep counting too. If you pay a monthly fee to belong to a group, set that fee against its revenue by hand, since the Profit by channel card works per channel, not per campaign. See the model and lookback window.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • A Slack workspace shows almost nothing, but Direct rose that week. Links were pasted untagged, or members copied your plain URL into other channels. Pin the tagged or tracked link and point people to it.
  • A forum shows in Referral under its host, not as your campaign. Someone linked to you there without your tags, or the post went up before you tagged it. The visits still count; tag new posts and edit old ones where the forum allows it.
  • Two names for one community. Bootstrappers and bootstrappers-slack are two rows. Fix the spelling in your sheet and use only one from now on.
  • New customers is zero for every community. If the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers shows most revenue unmatched, checkout is not getting the visitor ID. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A member says the community sent them, but the payment sits elsewhere. Reading without clicking leaves no trace, and a click on a phone followed by a purchase on a laptop cannot be joined, since the cookie lives in one browser. Compare with first touch in Site settings.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from Slack groups and forums?

Give each community its own utm_campaign, put the platform in utm_source (slack, a known forum name, or the forum's domain) and use utm_medium=community. With the script, payments and visitor ID set up as in the quickstart, each community gets a row of visitors and revenue on the Campaign tab.

Which channel do Slack, forum and paid community visits land in?

In Virling, slack is a known Social source. Reddit, Hacker News, Indie Hackers and dev.to are in Communities. A forum or paid community Virling does not know, such as a self-hosted Discourse or a Circle site, counts as Referral; its campaign still gives it a row of its own.

How do I see paying customers per community each month?

Set the range to one month, open the Campaign tab of the Channels card and click the community's row. The Revenue and New customers numbers at the top then cover only that community, and Compare to other dates sets them against the month before.

How should I tag links in a paid community?

Use the community's domain as utm_source, its name as utm_campaign, the space, event or lesson as utm_content, and utm_medium=community. Posts there sit behind a login, so the tag is the only thing that says which space or lesson a click came from.

How long should I measure a community before judging it?

At least a few months. Members usually see you several times before they click and buy, so check that the lookback window, 90 days by default, covers your trial plus decision time, and read the Time to pay card before you drop a community.
