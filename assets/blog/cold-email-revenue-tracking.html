Cold email revenue tracking: which sequences bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

To see which cold email visits turn into paying customers, tag every link in each sequence with utm_medium=email and a utm_campaign that names the sequence and the step. Carry the visitor ID into checkout, then join each payment to the visit that came before it. You then compare sequences by paying customers and revenue, not by opens, clicks or replies.

This is written for B2B SaaS founders who send outbound themselves or with a small team. A sequence takes hours to research and write, and a sending tool can tell you who replied. It cannot tell you who paid, because the payment happens weeks later on your site and in your payment provider.

Why cold email clicks arrive with no referrer

A visit gets its channel from its first pageview. For a click in an email, the usual clues are missing:

  • Mail apps send no referrer. Desktop and mobile mail apps open the link in a browser with nothing saying where it came from. With no referrer and no tag, the visit counts as Direct, in Virling and in most other tools.
  • Webmail names the mail service, not the email. When webmail does pass a referrer, it is the mail host. That says nothing about which sequence or step sent the click, and a Google host matches the Organic search list, so an untagged click can even land there.
  • Click tracking adds a hop. Sending tools that track clicks send the reader through a redirect on their own domain first. Some of these pass no referrer at all; others show up as the tracking domain, so the visit lands in Referral under a host you do not recognise.

A utm_medium of email, newsletter or e-mail is checked before the referrer, so a tagged click is in the Email channel whatever the mail client did. The full rule order is in channels, and why Direct is your biggest traffic source covers the other causes.

Judge a sequence by paid customers, not replies

Sending tools report opens, clicks and replies because those are what they can see. None of them is a customer.

  • Opens are unreliable, since many mail apps load images on their own, and a loaded image is how an open is counted.
  • Clicks include link scanners at the recipient's company, which open links before the person does. A scanner that does not run JavaScript never reaches Virling as a visit.
  • Replies include objections, out-of-office notes and requests to stop. A positive reply is interest, and many people who say "send me more" never start a trial.

A sequence with a lower reply rate can still be the better one if the people it sends to your site pay. Compare sequences by paying customers, revenue and revenue per visitor, so a sequence sent to 200 people and one sent to 2,000 sit on the same scale.

Trial to paid takes time

Most B2B SaaS sells through a trial, so a click on Tuesday turns into a payment two weeks later, or longer if a team has to agree. Judge a sequence too early and every sequence looks like it brought nothing.

  • Wait one full trial length after the last email in a sequence before you compare it, and leave out trials that have not had their chance yet.
  • Credit survives the wait. With the default model, last non-direct, a prospect who clicks your email and later comes back by typing your URL is still credited to the email visit, as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. For long B2B cycles, choose 365 days in Site settings; see model and lookback window.
  • Card-up-front trials are charged by the provider when the trial ends, so that first charge has no visit of its own. Virling credits it from the visits before the trial started for Stripe, Polar and Dodo Payments; see renewals and free trials.
  • Renewals count too. A subscription credited to a sequence keeps counting toward it every time it renews.

Set it up with Virling

  1. Tag every link in every step. Use one source for all outbound, email as the medium, and a campaign that names the sequence and the step, all lowercase with hyphens:
    https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=outbound&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=ops-leaders-q4-step-1
https://yourdomain.com/pricing?utm_source=outbound&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=ops-leaders-q4-step-2&utm_content=pricing-link
    utm_source=outbound keeps cold email apart from a newsletter in the Source tab, while both stay in the Email channel. Virling's reports group revenue by campaign, so the sequence and step both go in utm_campaign: each step is its own row, and adding the rows that share a prefix gives the whole sequence. utm_content is optional, to tell two links in one email apart; it is stored with the visit but is not a report row. Never put the prospect's name, email or company in a tag. The UTM builder makes the links, and the UTM naming template keeps the values the same across sequences.
  2. Add the script. Put the Virling script in the head of every page of your marketing site and app, with your domain in data-site. Pages on subdomains such as app.yourdomain.com count too. It reads the tags on the landing page and sets the visitor ID. See add the script.
  3. Connect your payment provider. In the app, open Setup, then Connect payments, pick the provider and paste a read-only key. Virling backfills recent payments and keeps reading new ones. See payments, and the page for Stripe, Paddle, Polar, Lemon Squeezy, Dodo Payments or Creem. All of them are listed under integrations, and revenue attribution for SaaS covers the rest.
  4. Pass the visitor ID into checkout. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links and Dodo Payments links get it from the script on click. See link checkout to visits.
  5. Read the numbers. Pick a range that covers the sequence plus one trial length. In the Channels card, click Email, switch to the Source tab to find outbound, then the Campaign tab to see each step with its visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey, with the credited visit marked.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Direct or Referral jumped the day a sequence went out, but its campaign is small. A step went out with an untagged link, or a link in the signature has no tags. Click a link from a sent email and check the address bar for the tags.
  • Steps are split across rows. Values differ in case or spelling, such as Step-2 and step-2. Pick one format and keep it.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If much of your revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A customer says they came from your email but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have read it on their phone and signed up later on a laptop. Those are two visitors, and no tool that runs on cookies can join them. A later search visit also takes the credit under the default model; switch to first touch in Site settings to see which sequences start journeys.

Once a prospect starts a trial, your onboarding and trial emails take over; revenue from onboarding, trial and upgrade emails covers how they get credit, and Loops email revenue shows the tags if you send them from Loops.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from cold email?

Tag every link in each sequence with utm_source, utm_medium=email and a utm_campaign that names the sequence and step. Add the script to your site, connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Each payment is then credited to the tagged visit, so you can read paying customers and revenue per sequence.

Why do cold email clicks show up as Direct?

Desktop and mobile mail apps usually send no referrer when a link is clicked, and some click-tracking redirects drop it too. With no referrer and no tag, the visit is Direct, and so is the payment that follows. A utm_medium of email puts the visit in the Email channel whatever the referrer says.

What UTM tags should I use for cold email?

Use one utm_source for all outbound, such as outbound, utm_medium=email, and a utm_campaign per sequence and step, such as ops-leaders-q4-step-2, all lowercase with hyphens. Do not put the prospect's name, email or company in the tags.

Should I judge a cold email sequence by reply rate?

Replies include objections, out-of-office notes and requests to stop, and a positive reply is still not a customer. Judge a sequence by the paying customers and revenue its visits bring, and wait until its trials have had time to convert.

How long should I wait before judging a sequence?

At least one full trial length after the last email, plus the time your buyers usually take to decide. The Time to pay card shows the median time from first visit to first payment per channel. For long B2B cycles, a 365-day lookback window keeps early visits in the credit.
