Dark social traffic: what it is and what revenue it brings

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

TL;DR: Dark social traffic is visits from links shared in chat apps, direct messages and email that arrive with no referrer, so analytics files them as Direct. It is real word of mouth, not people typing your address. You cannot measure all of it, but you can spot it by landing page, tag the links you share, recognise some in-app browsers, and credit each payment to the last visit that had a real source.

Where dark social traffic comes from

A browser sends a referrer when someone clicks a link on a web page. Many private places do not: a link opened from a chat app, a direct message, an email client or a PDF often arrives with nothing that says where it was clicked; why document.referrer is empty lists the usual causes. With no referrer and no UTM tag, the visit has no source, and it lands in Direct with typed addresses and bookmarks.

How to tell dark social from true direct visits

The landing page is the best clue. Someone who types your address or opens a bookmark nearly always lands on the homepage, a login page or another short URL. Nobody types the address of a long blog post or a deep docs page, so Direct visits that land there almost certainly came from a link someone shared.

  1. Click Direct on the Channels card to filter the page to it, then open the Landing page tab under Pages.
  2. Treat the homepage, login and pricing as mostly typed or bookmarked.
  3. Treat posts, docs, changelog entries and other deep pages as mostly shared links.
  4. Watch for spikes: a deep page that suddenly gets Direct visits on one day was probably dropped in a group chat.

To check how one particular URL will be classified, paste it into the traffic source checker.

What Virling can and cannot recover

Virling gives each session one channel from its first pageview. A utm_medium of email or newsletter makes it Email, a referrer or a utm_source decides the channel next, and only a visit with none of these is Direct. Before falling back to Direct, it checks the user agent: when it names Instagram, Facebook or Messenger, TikTok, LinkedIn, Telegram, WhatsApp, Slack or Discord, the visit is credited to that app as Social. The rules are in the channel docs.

What is left in Direct is split on the Channels card by how visitors arrived: typed or bookmarked, a shared link, or a return from checkout. A visit counts as a shared link when its URL carries a share or click ID that some apps add.

What it cannot do: some apps open links in a plain system browser that does not name them, and many chat and mail apps strip everything. Those visits stay Direct, and nothing in them says which chat or person shared the link. The landing page breakdown above is how you read them.

How to see what revenue dark social brings

  1. Tag every link you share on purpose: utm_source=whatsapp, utm_source=slack or utm_medium=email, with a utm_campaign for the post or message. The rules are in which UTM parameters to use. For posts that people may pass on, the social UTM link generator makes a tagged link per platform.
  2. Give people a tagged link to share, such as a share button or a copy link on your posts, so the links they pass on keep the tag.
  3. Connect your payment provider so each payment is joined to the visits before it.
  4. Use a last non-direct model. It credits a payment to the latest visit in the lookback window that was not Direct, so a buyer who first clicked a tagged link in a chat and later typed your address is credited to that link. If every visit was Direct, the payment stays Direct.
  5. Read revenue by landing page for Direct. Revenue on deep pages under Direct is a fair estimate of what untagged shared links earn. Asking buyers helps too; see self-reported attribution.

Virling uses last non-direct by default with a 90-day lookback, and you can switch to first touch or last touch in site settings. See the model docs.

Frequently asked questions

What is dark social traffic?

Visits from links shared in private places, such as chat apps, direct messages and email, that arrive with no referrer. Analytics tools cannot see where they came from, so they usually file them as Direct.

Why does dark social show up as Direct?

Chat and mail apps often pass no referrer when a link is opened. With no referrer and no UTM tag, a visit has nothing to say where it came from, so it falls into Direct.

Can any tool fully measure dark social?

No. A visit with no referrer, no tag and no app name in its user agent carries no record of where the link was shared. You can shrink dark social by tagging links and recognising in-app browsers, but not remove it.

How do I tell dark social apart from people typing my URL?

Look at landing pages. People who type your address or use a bookmark mostly land on the homepage, while a deep blog post or docs page in Direct was almost certainly reached through a shared link.

Does dark social traffic bring revenue?

It can. Read revenue per channel and per landing page, not visits, and credit payments with a last non-direct model so a buyer who first came from a tagged link is credited to it.
