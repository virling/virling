LTV by acquisition channel: which channels bring customers worth the most

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-20.

To see LTV by acquisition channel, first find out which visits turned into paying customers, not only clicks: pass the visitor ID into checkout so each first payment is joined to the visit that came before it and credited to a channel. Then let every renewal and upgrade keep that first channel, and divide what each channel's customers have paid, net of refunds, by how many customers it brought. That is revenue per customer by channel, and it keeps growing for as long as they pay.

For a subscription product the first payment is a small part of the story. A customer on a monthly plan can pay twelve times in a year, or once. Which channel they came from often decides which of the two it is, and a dashboard that counts signups per channel cannot show it.

Why lifetime value differs by channel

  • Intent differs. Someone who searched for a tool that does exactly what yours does arrives ready to use it. Someone who clicked a launch post may be curious and nothing more. Both can pay once; fewer of the curious ones renew.
  • Plans differ. A channel that reaches teams may bring customers on your top plan, while one that reaches hobbyists brings the cheapest. The first payment already differs, and every renewal repeats the gap.
  • Expectations differ. An ad or a post that promises more than the product does brings customers who ask for refunds or cancel after the first month. Refund rate by traffic source and churn by acquisition channel cover each.
  • Upgrades differ. Customers who grow into a bigger plan add revenue long after they signed up. If upgrades are not credited to the channel that won the customer, that channel looks smaller than it is.

None of this shows up in clicks, signups or even first-payment revenue. It shows up only when you follow each customer's payments over months and keep them tied to where the customer came from.

How renewals are credited to the original channel

A renewal never goes through your site, so it has no visit to credit. A tool that credits each payment to the visit before it puts renewals in Unattributed or Direct, and the channel that won the customer gets credit only for the first month. Virling uses one rule instead: a renewal takes the attribution of the customer's first payment.

  1. The first payment is credited to a visit. By default, the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct. See model and lookback window.
  2. Each renewal is matched to that first payment by the provider's customer ID, and copies its channel, referrer, campaign, landing page, country and device. If someone subscribed after a visit from Reddit, every renewal counts toward that Reddit visit, even years later.
  3. Upgrades and other plan changes count as renewals too, so expansion stays with the same channel.
  4. Free trials are handled. A trial charges nothing at checkout, so Virling keeps the trial start apart, never counts it as revenue, and credits the customer's later payment to the visits before the trial.

How each provider marks a renewal, and the limits of the rule, are in renewals. The short version: payments without a customer ID cannot be linked to a first payment, and customers are matched per provider.

How to compare channels on revenue per customer over months

Average LTV per channel is net revenue from the channel's customers divided by the number of those customers. The catch is time: customers acquired last week have paid once, customers from last year have paid many times. Comparing the two tells you about their age, not their channel.

  1. Group customers by when they first paid. Pick one month, such as January, and look only at customers whose first payment fell in it.
  2. Read average LTV per channel for that group. Every payment they have made since, net of refunds, divided by the customers each channel brought.
  3. Repeat for later months. Line up the January, February and March groups. A channel whose customers keep adding revenue month after month is worth more than one whose customers stop after the first payment, even if it brings fewer of them.
  4. Project forward with care. Paid-so-far LTV is a fact; lifetime LTV is a guess. The LTV calculator estimates it from revenue per customer per month, churn and gross margin, with a table per channel, and the LTV to CAC calculator sets it against what each channel costs.

To see how monthly recurring revenue splits by channel instead of lifetime revenue, read MRR by acquisition channel.

A worked example

Example only: made-up numbers for an imaginary product on monthly plans, not results from a real business. Customers whose first payment was in January, and their average LTV so far, read at three points:

ChannelCustomers acquiredAvg LTV after 1 monthAfter 3 monthsAfter 6 months
Communities20$30$55$70
Organic search10$30$85$160
Paid8$50$120$210

After one month the three channels look close, and communities brought twice as many customers as search. After six months, a search customer is worth more than twice a community customer, because most community customers stopped paying after a month or two. Paid customers started on a bigger plan and kept paying, so whether Paid is worth its cost depends on what each of those eight customers cost to win. With counts this small, one customer moves a row a lot, so read several monthly groups before you move budget.

Set it up with Virling

1. Tag your links

Untagged clicks from apps and emails often arrive with no referrer and land in Direct, which hides the channel for the life of the customer. Tag every link you control:

https://yourproduct.com/?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=launch-post
https://yourproduct.com/?utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=brand
https://yourproduct.com/?utm_source=weekly-digest&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=issue-42
  • utm_source is the place, such as google, linkedin, reddit or the newsletter's name. Known sources go straight to their channel: reddit to Communities, linkedin to Social.
  • utm_medium decides Paid and Email first: cpc, ppc, paid or paidsocial is Paid; email or newsletter is Email. Use another value, such as social, for organic posts.
  • utm_campaign names the post, ad or issue, and the Campaign tab groups visitors and revenue by it.

The UTM builder makes the link, and tracked links give you a short link with the tags added on each click. The full rule order is in channels.

2. Add the script

Paste the tag from your site's setup page before </head> on your marketing site and your app. It sets the first-party virling_vid cookie that links a payment to the visits before it, shared across subdomains so yourproduct.com and app.yourproduct.com see the same visitor. See add the script.

<script defer src="https://app.virling.com/v.js" data-site="yourproduct.com"></script>

3. Connect your payment provider

Connect Stripe, Paddle, Polar, Lemon Squeezy, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify or WooCommerce with a read-only key. Virling imports the last 90 days and never creates, changes or refunds anything. For retention, give it read access to subscriptions where the provider asks for it: Polar and Creem need subscriptions:read, Paddle subscription.read. Each provider has its own page under integrations, and the keys are listed in payments.

4. Pass the visitor ID into checkout

Stripe Payment Links, Polar, Dodo Payments and Lemon Squeezy checkout links need nothing: the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Renewals show as Unattributed. The first payment was not attributed, or it is older than the 90-day import, so long-running subscriptions fill in as new customers arrive. See Unattributed revenue.
  • Old customers are missing. A customer whose earliest payment in Virling is a renewal started before the import, so they are left out of the Retention page.
  • A customer counts twice. Customers are matched per provider, so someone who moved from one provider to another counts as two.
  • Direct has the most customers. Some links are untagged, or the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers, and read why Direct is your biggest traffic source.

Frequently asked questions

What is LTV by acquisition channel?

It is the lifetime value of your customers split by the marketing channel that brought each one. Every payment a customer makes, including renewals and upgrades, counts toward the channel credited for their first payment, so you can compare what a customer from each channel is worth.

Why does lifetime value differ by channel?

Customers from different channels arrive with different intent and expectations. Some pick a bigger plan, some stay longer, some ask for refunds more often. Two channels can bring the same number of customers and very different revenue over the following months.

Which channel gets the credit for a renewal?

The channel of the customer's first payment. A renewal has no visit of its own, so Virling copies the channel, referrer and campaign of the first payment, matched by the provider's customer ID. Upgrades and plan changes keep that channel too.

How do I compare LTV across channels fairly?

Compare groups of customers of the same age. Pick the customers whose first payment fell in one month and read their average LTV per channel, then repeat for later months. Customers acquired last week have had less time to pay than customers from last year.

How soon can I judge a channel's LTV?

LTV needs months. In the first 60 days, lean on refund rate and 30-day churn per channel, and read average LTV once customers have had several billing cycles. With only a handful of customers per channel, one customer can move the number a lot.
