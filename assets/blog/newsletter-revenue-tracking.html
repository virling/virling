Newsletter revenue tracking: see which issue brings sales

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-16, updated 2026-11-25.

To track sales from an email newsletter, tag every link in each issue with the same utm_medium and a utm_campaign named after the issue, then credit each payment to the visit that carried those tags. Opens and clicks only tell you who read. Payments joined to tagged visits tell you which issue paid.

Why newsletter sales land in Direct

Most email clients and webmail apps send no referrer when a reader clicks a link. Without UTM tags, that visit has no source at all and is counted as Direct, and so is any payment that follows. The newsletter did the work, and the report gives the credit to nobody.

Virling puts a visit tagged utm_medium=email in the Email channel even with no referrer, so payments credited to your newsletter show there and not under Direct.

Tag each issue

Use one source and one medium for every issue, and change only the campaign:

https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=issue-42

  • utm_source=newsletter: the same value in every issue, so all of it adds up in one row
  • utm_medium=email: puts the visit in the Email channel
  • utm_campaign=issue-42: one value per issue, lowercase, with hyphens
  • utm_content: optional, to tell two links in the same issue apart, such as top-link and footer

Tag every link that points to your site, not only the main one. Readers often click the link you least expected. The free UTM builder makes the tagged URLs for you, and which UTM parameters to use covers the naming rules.

How Virling sorts a newsletter visit

Every session gets one channel from its first pageview, and utm_medium is checked first. A medium of email, newsletter or e-mail is the Email channel, whatever the referrer says. The source and campaign are stored with the visit too. See how channels are decided.

When a payment comes in, it is joined to the visits before it. Under the default model the whole payment, after refunds, is credited to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct, and to that visit’s channel, source, campaign and landing page. A reader who clicks issue 42 on Tuesday and comes back on Friday by typing your address is still credited to issue 42. See the attribution model.

See payments per issue

1. Open the Email channel

On the dashboard, the Channels card lists each channel with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. Click Email and the whole page filters to visits from your newsletter.

2. Switch to the Campaign tab

The Campaign tab on the same card has one row per utm_campaign, so each issue shows its own visitors and revenue. Compare issues by revenue per visitor, not by visitors: a short issue that sends few readers who buy is worth more than a long one that sends many who do not.

3. Open the payments

Click an issue to filter the dashboard to it and see its revenue over time. The Payments page lists every payment with its source; open one to see the journey: the sessions before the payment and which one got the credit.

Example: one month of four issues

Example only, with made-up numbers for an imaginary product that costs $50 a month or $300 a year. Each customer pays once in the month and is credited to one issue. Issue 42 has five monthly buyers and one yearly buyer, and the only buyer from issue 44 chose the yearly plan:

Clicks come from your newsletter tool or a tracked link. Customers and revenue are the payments credited to that issue’s campaign. Issue 43 has the most clicks and the lowest revenue per click.

With few payments, look at the customers before the ranking. Issue 44 is second only because of one yearly payment: had that buyer chosen the monthly plan, it would be at $0.20 and last. Had issue 42’s yearly buyer also chosen the monthly plan, it would still lead at $1.20 per click from six customers, so its lead does not rest on one sale. Four issues and 14 customers are still too few to set a rule. Compare issues on the same topic over several months, and leave a recent issue time for late payments before you judge it.

Or use a tracked link per issue

Instead of writing UTM tags by hand, open Links in the app and make a short link for the issue. Set the destination, a source such as newsletter, and the channel Email. A click redirects to your page with utm_source, utm_medium=email and utm_campaign added, and Links shows the clicks, visitors and revenue each link brought. The link’s id becomes the campaign, so give each link a name you will recognise, such as “Issue 42, pricing link”. See tracked links.

What to check if the numbers look off

  • Newsletter revenue still shows as Direct. A link was left untagged, or the tags were stripped by a redirect before your page. Click a link from a sent issue and check the address bar for the tags.
  • Issues are split across rows. Values differ in case or spelling, such as Issue-42 and issue-42. Pick one format and keep it.
  • Revenue shows no channel at all. The payment was not matched to a visit. Pass the visitor ID into checkout; see link checkout to visits.
  • A recent issue looks weak. Readers often pay days later. Give it time, and check the date range, since a sale is credited on the day of the payment.

By newsletter tool

Each tool handles links and checkout a little differently:

  • Kit broadcasts and sequences: one campaign per broadcast, and one per email in a sequence.
  • beehiiv issues: beehiiv adds UTM tags to post links by default, so check the settings before you write your own.
  • Substack posts and notes: web clicks and email clicks arrive differently, and a tag joins them.
  • Paid Ghost memberships: Ghost’s checkout cannot carry a visitor ID, so payments are matched by email.
  • Mailchimp campaigns: tag links by hand, or use its Google Analytics link tracking, but not both.
  • Loops campaigns and automated emails: one campaign per send, and one per email in a sequence.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track sales from an email newsletter?

Add utm_source, utm_medium=email and a utm_campaign per issue to every link, connect your payment provider, and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Each payment is then credited to the tagged visit, so you can read revenue per issue.

Should utm_medium be email or newsletter?

Either works in Virling: email, newsletter and e-mail all count as the Email channel. Pick one and use it in every issue so your reports stay in one row.

Why does my newsletter traffic show as Direct?

Many email clients send no referrer when a link is clicked. Without UTM tags the visit has no source, so it is counted as Direct, and so is the payment that follows.

Does Virling send my newsletter?

No. Virling reads the tagged visits and the payments they bring. Keep sending with the tool you use today.

Why are newsletter clicks higher than visitors?

They count different things. A tracked link counts a click every time it is opened, so the same person twice is two clicks. A visitor is counted by the script on your page, so a blocked script or an app that opened the link for a preview leaves a click with no visitor.

Which issue gets the credit if a reader clicks several?

Under the default model, the latest visit that was not Direct in the 90 days before the payment, so the last issue clicked unless the reader came from another source after it. First touch credits the earliest visit in the window instead. You can change the model and the window in site settings.
