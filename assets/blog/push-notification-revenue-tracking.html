Push notification revenue tracking for online stores

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-21.

To see which push notification campaigns bring orders, tag the link in every web or mobile push with utm_source=push, utm_medium=push and a utm_campaign that starts with push-, make sure the tap opens a page of your store rather than your app, then credit each Shopify or WooCommerce order to the tagged visit before it. A tap on a notification has no web page behind it, so an untagged push arrives with no referrer and is counted as Direct.

Web push and app push are tracked differently

A web push comes from your website. A shopper allows notifications in the browser, your push tool sends through that browser, and a tap opens the URL you set in a browser tab. That visit runs your pages and the Virling script, so a tagged web push can always be credited.

An app push comes from your native app, if you have one. A tap opens the app, and what happens next is up to the app: it may show a screen of its own, or load a page of your site inside the app. Only the second case is a web visit that Virling can see, and only if the page that loads runs the script.

If you send both, keep the source as push and put the kind in the campaign, such as push-web-winter-sale-2026-12 and push-app-winter-sale-2026-12. The two then sit on separate rows, and you can see how much of the app side ever reached your website.

When a notification opens your app instead of your site

A store that has an app can claim its own web addresses, with Universal Links on iOS and App Links on Android. When it does, a tap on a link to yourstore.com in an app push can open the app on a phone that has it installed, instead of the browser. The tags are still on the URL, but no web page loads, so nothing is recorded.

  • The push is never credited. There is no visit to give the order to.
  • An order placed in the app has no visitor ID. It shows as Unattributed. It still counts toward revenue, but no campaign gets it.
  • The same notification behaves differently per phone. Shoppers without the app get your website and are tracked; shoppers with it are not. Your push revenue in Virling is the website part only.

If most push orders happen inside your app, read them in your push tool or app store reports. Virling answers a narrower question: which pushes sent shoppers to your website before they ordered there.

Abandoned cart and back in stock pushes

Automated pushes are set up once and then run for months, so they are the ones most often left untagged. Two of them need extra care.

  • Abandoned cart. The link often goes to a cart recovery URL that rebuilds the cart and then redirects to the cart or checkout. If that redirect keeps only the path, the tags are lost and the order shows as Direct. Tap a real cart reminder on your own device and look for utm_source=push in the address bar of the page that opens.
  • Back in stock. Each send goes to the few shoppers who asked about one product, so the counts are small and the intent is high. Keep one campaign for the whole automation, push-flow-back-in-stock, rather than one per product. Filter by that campaign and the Landing page tab of the Pages card shows which product pages the taps opened.

Opt-in limits on iOS Safari

On iPhone and iPad, web push works only from iOS and iPadOS 16.4, and only after the shopper adds your site to the Home Screen and opens it from there. Safari will not ask for permission from a normal tab, and the request has to follow a tap on a button of yours. Most iPhone shoppers never take those steps, so expect far fewer web push subscribers on iOS than on Android or desktop browsers.

A tap on such a notification opens the Home Screen web app, not Safari. That web app runs your pages and the script, so the visit is recorded, but it keeps its own cookies apart from Safari. A shopper who taps the push there and later orders in Safari is two visitors to Virling, and the order goes to the Safari visits. Treat iOS web push revenue as a floor.

Set up push revenue tracking

Use these values on every notification link, lowercase:

  • Campaign: https://yourstore.com/collections/sale?utm_source=push&utm_medium=push&utm_campaign=push-winter-sale-2026-12
  • Abandoned cart: utm_campaign=push-flow-abandoned-cart
  • Back in stock: utm_campaign=push-flow-back-in-stock
  • Price drop: utm_campaign=push-flow-price-drop

Set the tags in your push tool if it can add them to every notification, or paste the full tagged URL as the link. Build them with the free UTM builder. Do not tag pushes as email: that medium moves a visit into the Email channel and you would lose the split.

With the tags on, push is not a source Virling knows, so the visit counts as Referral, and the order is joined to it through the visitor ID a short snippet stores on the order. The script, the read-only store key and that snippet are covered in set up Virling for an online store; the rule order is in how channels are decided.

Read push revenue

On the dashboard, click Referral in the Channels card and switch to the Campaign tab. Every row that starts with push- is one push campaign or automation, with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. The Source tab will not split push out: a visit with no referrer and a source Virling does not know is listed under the channel's name, together with your other tagged links. Compare automations by revenue per visitor, and open an order on the Payments page to see the visits before it.

What to check if the numbers look off

  • Push orders show as Direct. An automation was left untagged, or a cart recovery redirect dropped the tags.
  • Mobile push sends few visits. The links open your app on phones that have it installed. Test on a phone with the app and one without.
  • Push revenue is lower than in your push tool. Push tools can credit an order to a notification that was delivered or opened within a window, and can count orders placed in your app. Virling credits each order once, to a web visit.
  • iOS shows almost nothing. Few iPhone shoppers add your site to the Home Screen, and orders in Safari after a tap go to the Safari visits.

Frequently asked questions

Why do push notification clicks show up as Direct?

A notification is not a web page, so a tap on it opens your store with no referrer. Unless the link carries UTM tags, the visit and the order after it are counted as Direct.

What UTM parameters should I use for push notifications?

utm_source=push and utm_medium=push on every notification, with a utm_campaign that starts with push-, such as push-winter-sale-2026-12 or push-flow-abandoned-cart. Add web or app to the campaign if you send both.

Can Virling track a push that opens my app instead of my website?

No. Virling counts visits to web pages that run its script. A push that opens your native app makes no web visit, so it is never credited, and an order placed in the app without your web pages has no visitor ID and shows as Unattributed. It still counts toward revenue.

Are web push notifications on iPhone tracked?

Yes, when the tap opens your site. iOS sends web push only to sites added to the Home Screen, from iOS 16.4. The tap opens that Home Screen web app, which runs the Virling script but keeps its own cookies apart from Safari, so an order placed later in Safari is credited to the Safari visits.

Where do I see push revenue in Virling?

Click Referral in the Channels card, then the Campaign tab. Push has no channel of its own, so each row that starts with push- is one push campaign or automation.

Does Virling send push notifications?

No. Virling reads tagged visits and your Shopify or WooCommerce orders. Keep sending with the push tool you use today.
