Google AI Mode traffic tracking: where the clicks and revenue go

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-26.

Google AI Mode traffic tracking comes down to one fact: AI Mode is part of Google Search, so a click on a link in an AI Mode answer reaches your site from google.com, like any other Google result, and Virling counts it as Organic search. Google does not document any referrer or URL parameter that marks a click as coming from AI Mode, so no analytics tool on your site can split AI Mode clicks from classic results or AI Overviews. What you can see is the revenue Google search brings as a whole, by landing page, and you can check one click and one payment yourself in ten minutes.

This post is for founders and store owners who see their site cited in AI Mode and want to know whether those visits turn into customers. For the answer box on the main results page, see Google AI Overviews revenue tracking; for Google search in general, see organic search revenue tracking; for AI assistants in general, see is AI search traffic worth anything.

What Google AI Mode is

Google's AI Mode help page (read 2026-11-26) calls it Google's most powerful AI search experience: you ask a question, get an AI answer with links to the web, and go deeper with follow-up questions. It splits your question into subtopics and searches for each one at the same time, which Google calls query fan-out. When Google is not confident enough in an AI answer, AI Mode shows a set of web links instead.

Google's page for site owners, AI features and your website (read 2026-11-26), says AI Mode is meant for questions that need further exploration, reasoning or comparisons, and that AI Overviews and AI Mode may both use query fan-out. To be shown as a supporting link, a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet; Google says there are no additional technical requirements and no special files or markup needed.

How AI Mode clicks arrive

Neither Google page says anything about referrers, UTM tags or other URL parameters on AI Mode links. What the developer page does say is that sites appearing in AI features, AI Overviews and AI Mode included, are counted in the overall search traffic in Search Console, in the Performance report under the Web search type. Google's own report does not split them out.

So treat an AI Mode click as a Google search click. The link is followed from a Google Search page, so the browser sends google.com as the referrer, with no search query and no mark of AI Mode. You cannot add your own utm_source either, because Google picks the URL it links to. The test below shows what your own browser sends.

What cannot be told apart from normal Google search: a click from AI Mode, a click from an AI Overview, and a click on a classic blue link all look the same to your site.

Which channel AI Mode visits get in Virling

  • Organic search. Virling puts any Google search host, google.com or a country domain such as google.co.in, in the Organic search channel, and every Google country domain shares one google.com row. An AI Mode click lands in that row.
  • Not AI. The AI channel is for assistants with their own address, such as chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai and claude.ai. The only Google host in it is gemini.google.com, the Gemini app, which is checked before the search rules, so Gemini app visits never merge into the google.com row.
  • Ads stay apart. A paid medium wins over everything. A Google Ads link tagged utm_medium=cpc counts as Paid, even though it also arrives from google.com.

The full rule order is in channels. The traffic source checker shows how a landing URL and referrer are credited: enter a google.com referrer to see Organic search, then gemini.google.com to see AI.

How a payment days later is credited

AI Mode is built for research, so many people read an answer, click through, leave, and pay days later after typing your URL. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the payment goes to the latest visit that was not Direct, as long as it started inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. If that was the Google visit, Google gets the revenue. If the buyer came back through a newsletter or an ad before paying, that later visit takes the credit instead.

To see how often Google starts journeys that another channel finishes, switch to first touch in Site settings. Changing the model or window recalculates every past payment, and changing it back restores the earlier credit. See model and lookback window.

Check it yourself with one click and one payment

Before this works, add the tracking script to every page, connect your payment provider, and pass the visitor ID into checkout, as in link checkout to visits. Then:

  1. Find an answer that cites you. In a private window, open AI Mode in Google Search and ask a question your site answers well.
  2. Click the link to your site in the same private window.
  3. Find the visit. Open the Visitors list in Virling. The visit shows up right away, with google.com as its first source. Open it to see its landing page and visitor ID. In the Channels card, it is under Organic search.
  4. Make a test payment. In the same window, go to checkout and pay in your provider's test mode, for example a Stripe test card on a key that starts with rk_test_. See test your setup for each provider.
  5. Find the payment. Wait a minute for the next sync and open the Payments page. The payment shows google.com as its source. Open it to see the journey, with the AI Mode click marked as the credited visit.

Payments imported with a test key stay in your reports. If you want only real payments in your numbers, make one small live payment instead and refund it.

If the visit shows as Direct, your browser or an extension stripped the referrer. Try another browser. If the payment shows as Unattributed, the visitor ID did not reach checkout; see unattributed revenue.

What you can learn anyway

  • Revenue by landing page. Click the google.com row to filter the dashboard. The Pages card then shows which landing pages Google sends people to and which of them lead to payments. A page you have seen cited in AI Mode that earns more from Google after it started being cited is a lead worth following, but not proof: classic rankings move too.
  • Revenue per visitor. The Channels card shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor for Organic search, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  • The buyer's path. Open a payment to see every visit before it, with the credited one marked.
  • Whether Google's AI names you. The Answers tab of the AI card asks your buyer questions to Gemini with Google Search grounding each week. It does not cover AI Mode or AI Overviews, since Google has no API for them, but it is the closest signal for whether Google's AI finds you. See AI answers.
  • What you cannot see: the search query, which Google does not send, and anyone who read an AI Mode answer without clicking. If they later search your name or type your URL, that visit has no trace of AI Mode.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track Google AI Mode traffic?

Track Google organic search as a whole. AI Mode is part of Google Search, so a click from it reaches your site from google.com and Virling counts it as Organic search, in the google.com row. Pass the visitor ID into checkout and each payment is credited to the Google visit and landing page before it.

Can I separate AI Mode clicks from normal Google results?

No. Google's documentation does not describe a referrer or URL parameter that marks an AI Mode click, and Search Console includes AI Mode in the overall Web search traffic. No script on your site can tell an AI Mode click from a classic result or an AI Overview click.

Is AI Mode the same as the Gemini app?

No. AI Mode is a mode of Google Search, so its clicks count as Organic search. The Gemini app at gemini.google.com is a separate assistant, and Virling counts its visits in the AI channel.

How is a payment made days after an AI Mode click credited?

With the default model, last non-direct, the payment goes to the latest visit in the 90-day window that was not Direct. If the buyer clicked from AI Mode, left, and came back by typing your URL, the Google visit gets the credit.

How can I check where an AI Mode click lands?

Search for something your site answers in AI Mode, click a link to your site in a private window, and open the Visitors list in Virling. The visit shows its source as google.com in Organic search. Pay in your provider's test mode in the same window to see the payment credited to that visit.
