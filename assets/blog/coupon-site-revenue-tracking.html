Do coupon sites bring new customers? How to check your orders

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-24.

Do coupon sites bring new customers? Some do, but many coupon clicks can come from people who were already at your checkout and left to look for a code. A coupon site brought the buyer when its click was their first recorded visit and the order is their first. It took credit at checkout when the cart came before the click. To tell them apart, tag every coupon partner's link, then compare the first and last visit of each order it is credited with. A code report or a last-click total cannot show the difference.

This is written for stores that pay coupon sites a commission, or that see one near the top of a sales report.

Why the last click goes to the coupon site

A buyer finds you through search, fills a cart and reaches a checkout page with an empty box for a code. They open a new tab, search for your store's name and “coupon”, and click through a coupon site back to your store. The order was close before the coupon site was involved, yet its click is now the last one.

Which channel gets the order depends on the attribution model. In Virling there are three:

  • Last non-direct, the default, credits the latest visit in the window that was not Direct. A tagged coupon click is not Direct, so it wins.
  • Last touch credits the latest visit, Direct included. The coupon click wins again when it comes last.
  • First touch credits the earliest visit in the window. Search wins, and the coupon site is credited only for journeys it started.

Four journeys, with a tagged coupon link and every visit inside the window:

JourneyFirst touchLast touchLast non-direct
Search, then coupon click at checkout, orderSearchCoupon siteCoupon site
Coupon site, order in the same visitCoupon siteCoupon siteCoupon site
Coupon site, then your email, orderCoupon siteEmailEmail

The third row is why last touch is not simply too generous to coupon sites. It can also miss buyers they did bring. The fourth shows up only in your code report. For more on the choice, see first-touch or last-touch attribution.

How to tag partner links

Give each coupon partner one link of its own, with the same lowercase name in the source and the campaign:

https://yourstore.com/?utm_source=coupon-partner-a&utm_medium=affiliate&utm_campaign=coupon-partner-a
  • One campaign per partner. Virling groups revenue by campaign, so this is the row you will read. Keep coupon partners apart from content affiliates, who send a different kind of visit. The UTM builder makes the link, or create one under Partners.
  • Not cpc or email as the medium. Those move the visit into the Paid or Email channel.
  • Land on a store page, not on checkout. The visit is recorded by the script on the page the link opens. A link that opens a checkout page without the script leaves no visit.
  • Test the redirect. If the link passes through an affiliate network, click it in a private window and check that the tags are still in the address bar.

The tag matters most at the last step. In Virling, a page that loads with tags different from the visit's own starts a new visit, so a tagged coupon click always gets its own line in the journey. Without tags, a buyer who comes back within a few minutes, or who kept a store tab open, stays in the visit they were in, and the coupon site does not appear at all. The rules are in how attribution works, and affiliate revenue tracking covers partner links in full.

A coupon browser extension can open a partner link on its own while the buyer is at checkout. If that link loads a page with your script, it looks like any other tagged click. If it does not, the affiliate network may claim an order on which Virling shows no coupon visit.

Compare buyers who arrived with buyers who used it at the end

Sort the orders credited to each coupon partner into two groups:

  • Arrived through it. The coupon visit is the first visit recorded for that buyer.
  • Used it at the end. An earlier visit came from somewhere else, and the coupon visit came later, often minutes before the order and after cart or checkout pages.

In Virling, with last non-direct on:

  1. Read the total. In the Campaign tab of the Channels card, or on Partners, note the partner's revenue and visitors for a few weeks.
  2. Open its payments. Click the campaign to filter the dashboard, then open the Latest payments tab, which lists the 10 newest. Open one to see Credited to, First touch, Time to pay and the visits before it, with the credited one marked.
  3. Sort them. When the credited visit is also the first one, the buyer arrived through the coupon site. When an earlier visit came from another source, they used it at the end.
  4. For more orders, use the API. The payments endpoint of the Read API returns touch, the credited visit with its campaign, and first_touch, the buyer's first visit. Compare their times: the same time means the buyer arrived through the coupon site.
  5. Mark new customers. Look the orders up in your store and note which were the customer's first. A returning customer is not new even when the coupon visit is the first one recorded.

For a quick view of the whole site, switch the model to first touch in Site settings, note the same campaign, and switch back. It rebuilds credit for every payment, history included, and nothing is lost by switching. The first-touch figure also includes buyers who arrived through the coupon site and ordered after another visit, the third journey above.

A worked example

Example only: made-up numbers for an imaginary store, not results from a real business. One coupon partner is credited with 100 orders under last non-direct. The code takes 20% off and the partner earns 10% of what the buyer pays:

GroupOrdersFirst ordersRevenueDiscountCommission
Arrived through it2015$1,000$250$100
Used it at the end8024$4,800$1,200$480
All10039$5,800$1,450$580
  • The report says $5,800. Orders that began with the coupon site are $1,000 of it.
  • 15 of the 100 orders are from new customers who arrived through it. The other 24 first orders came from buyers another channel brought.
  • The second group cost $1,680 in discount and commission. Whether that was wasted depends on how many of those buyers would have left without a code.

Suppose the 80 orders in the second group are $75 each at full price and cost the store $30 to fill. At full price an order makes $45. With the code the buyer pays $60, and after $30 of cost and $6 of commission it makes $24, or $1,920 for all 80. At full price, 43 orders make $1,935. So if 43 or more of the 80 would have bought anyway, the store would have made more without the coupon. For the discount alone, put your own price and cost in the discount break-even calculator.

Visits cannot tell you how many would have left. For that, run a test: pause the partner or the code for a few weeks, and compare total orders and profit with a similar period, not only the partner's row.

What to change on the checkout page

The click at the end often starts with the code box. What you can change depends on your store platform and plan:

  • Make the box quieter. A small “Have a code?” link that opens the field is less of a prompt than an open, empty box.
  • Apply the discount yourself. When an offer is open to everyone, apply it automatically and show it in the cart, so there is nothing to search for.
  • Publish your own offers page. List the current codes, or say there are none, on a page of your site. Someone who searches for a code may find you first.
  • Replace shared codes. Use single-use or expiring codes for email and creators, and retire any that turn up on a coupon site. See discount code attribution.
  • Measure it. Change one thing at a time. Watch completed orders, the number of codes used and the coupon partner's revenue. If orders hold and codes fall, keep the change.

Then look at what you pay. You can agree a lower rate for coupon partners, pay only for a customer's first order, or leave out orders where the cart came before the click. Commission is settled in your affiliate program; Virling does not calculate or pay it.

Set it up with Virling

  1. Add the script to every page of the store. It records each visit with its tags. See add the script.
  2. Connect your store. Shopify needs a token that can only read orders and a snippet that stores the visitor ID on the cart. WooCommerce and the other integrations have their own page.
  3. Tag every coupon link as above, and ask partners to replace the old ones.
  4. Check with a test order. Open your store from a search result in a private window, add a product, then open the coupon link and buy. The order's journey should show both visits.

Two limits apply. A buyer who switches device or browser starts as a new visitor, so a coupon click can look like a first visit when it was not. And a code typed without a click leaves no visit, so compare the partner's row with the code redemptions in your store.

Frequently asked questions

Do coupon sites bring new customers?

Some do, but the share differs for every store, so check your own orders. A coupon site brought a buyer when its click was the first visit you recorded and the order is their first. When the buyer had already filled a cart before the click, the coupon site took part in the order but did not bring the buyer.

Why does a coupon site get credit for sales from other channels?

Because its click comes last. A buyer arrives from search or an ad, reaches checkout, leaves to look for a code and comes back through the coupon site's link. Last touch credits that final visit, and last non-direct does too, since a tagged coupon link is not Direct.

Does first-touch attribution fix coupon site credit?

It stops a last-minute click from taking the credit, but it is not proof of who found you. It credits the earliest recorded visit in the window, so someone who already knew your store and searched for a code first still counts for the coupon site. Use it next to last touch, not instead of it.

How should I tag coupon site links?

Give each coupon partner its own utm_source, utm_medium=affiliate and its own utm_campaign, all lowercase, and point the link at a store page that runs your tracking script. Avoid cpc and email as the medium, since those move the visit into the Paid or Email channel.

Should I remove the coupon code field from checkout?

Not always. An open, empty field can prompt people to leave and search, so try a collapsed link or a discount that applies by itself, where your platform allows it. Test one change at a time and compare completed orders, discount cost and profit.

Can Virling see which coupon code was used?

No. Virling credits each payment to a visit and does not read discount codes. Code redemptions stay in your store. Put the two reports side by side to find codes that were used without a visit from the coupon site.
