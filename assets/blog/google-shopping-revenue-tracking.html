Google Shopping revenue tracking: which free listing clicks pay

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-20.

To see which orders came from Google Shopping clicks, put utm_source=google, a non-paid medium and a campaign such as shopping-free-listings on the link attribute of every product in your Merchant Center feed, then connect your store so each order carries the visitor ID. Left untagged, a free listing click looks like any search click: Virling files it under Organic search on the google.com row. The srsltid code Merchant Center appends is ignored, so your campaign is the only thing that marks a click as Shopping.

Merchant Center counts clicks and impressions per product. It cannot tell you which of those clicks turned into a paid order in your Shopify or WooCommerce store, or what each product page earns per visitor.

Free listings and Shopping ads are two different clicks

A product can reach Google in two ways, and they need different tags:

Free listingsShopping ads
CostNone per clickYou pay per click in Google Ads
Where the URL comes fromThe link (or mobile_link) attribute in your feedThe same feed link, plus any tracking template or final URL suffix set in Google Ads
Google's own parametersrsltid, when Merchant Center auto-tagging is ongclid, when Google Ads auto-tagging is on
Medium to usefree, or anything that is not paid or emailcpc
Channel in VirlingOrganic searchPaid, only if tagged cpc; Organic search if not

The last row is the trap. A paid click with a Google referrer and no paid medium is sorted by its referrer, so untagged ads land in Organic search and inflate what free listings seem to earn. Put the ad tags in the Google Ads final URL suffix, which the Google Ads tracking template builder writes for you, and read how to tag Google Ads for the rest of that setup.

What a product click carries when it lands

Here is a free listing click on a tagged feed link, as it arrives at a store. The srsltid value is a placeholder:

https://yourstore.com/products/linen-apron?utm_source=google&utm_medium=free&utm_campaign=shopping-free-listings&srsltid=EXAMPLE
  • Referrer: www.google.com or a country domain. Virling knows every Google host as a search engine, so the visit is Organic search with google.com as its source. The host alone cannot say Shopping tab, Images or web results.
  • utm_source=google. Only used when the referrer is missing, as it can be from the Google app. Because google is a known search source, the visit still lands in Organic search. A made-up value such as google_shopping is unknown, and with no referrer it becomes Referral.
  • utm_medium=free. Not on the paid list (cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social, paidsocial) or the email list, so it leaves the channel to the referrer.
  • utm_campaign=shopping-free-listings. The value you filter on. Use one fixed string for every product so all free listing clicks add up in one row.
  • srsltid. Not read and not stored. Virling keeps the path without its query string, so /products/linen-apron is one landing page however many srsltid values point at it. What the srsltid parameter is covers canonical tags and whether to strip it.

The complete rule order is on the channels docs page.

Tag the feed

Append the same three tags to every product link. If your feed comes from a Shopify or WooCommerce feed app, look for a setting that adds URL parameters to all products; otherwise edit the link column in a supplemental feed. Google suggests also sending canonical_link when the link has tracking parameters.

https://yourstore.com/products/oak-board?utm_source=google&utm_medium=free&utm_campaign=shopping-free-listings

Keep product names out of the campaign: the landing page already tells you the product. Use utm_content for a feed or country label if you run several feeds. The UTM builder writes the URL, and after Merchant Center re-crawls, click a live listing and paste the address you land on into the UTM checker to confirm a feed app did not drop or duplicate your tags. Naming advice is in which UTM parameters to use.

Install the script and connect the store

The Virling script goes in the store's <head>: in theme.liquid on Shopify, or through the Virling WordPress plugin on WooCommerce (add the script). Then, under Setup and Connect payments, paste a store key and click Connect and backfill.

  • Shopify: a custom app token with read_orders only, plus the theme snippet that writes the visitor ID onto the cart. Steps: Shopify revenue by traffic source.
  • WooCommerce: a read-only REST key, plus the snippet that saves the virling_vid cookie on each order. Steps: WooCommerce revenue by traffic source.

Other platforms are listed on ecommerce integrations. To test, open one of your own listings on Google, click through and place a test order; it should appear under Payments with google.com as its source.

Reading one order back to its product

Virling stores what an order was worth, not its line items. The product comes from the visit: a free listing click lands on that product's page, so the landing page of the credited visit names the listing that was clicked.

  1. Open Payments and pick an order credited to google.com.
  2. Its journey lists every visit before the order. The credited one is marked; check that it carries shopping-free-listings and see which /products/ page it started on.
  3. If the buyer also came back later by typing your address, that Direct visit does not take the credit under the default model, last non-direct, as long as the Shopping click is within the lookback window, 90 days by default (model and lookback window).
  4. If the buyer landed on one product and paid for another, its revenue shows on the page they landed on, since that is the listing that brought them in.

Revenue per product listing

For the totals, open the Channels card, choose the Campaign tab and click shopping-free-listings to filter the dashboard. Then the Landing page tab of the Pages card has one row per product page with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.

  • High clicks, low revenue per visitor: compare the page's price, stock and delivery cost with what the listing promised.
  • Low clicks, high revenue per visitor: a product worth more feed work, such as a clearer title, better image or missing attributes like size and colour.
  • Paid against free: switch the campaign filter to your Shopping ads campaign and compare the same product page. A product that sells as well from free listings may not need the ad spend.

Judge clicks from at least a few weeks back so late buyers are in. The revenue per visitor calculator works the numbers for any row.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which orders came from Google Shopping free listings?

Add utm_source=google, utm_medium=free and utm_campaign=shopping-free-listings to the link attribute of every product in your Merchant Center feed, and connect your store so orders carry the visitor ID. Filter the dashboard to that campaign and the Landing page tab shows revenue and revenue per visitor for each product page.

Does Virling use the srsltid parameter on product links?

No. Merchant Center auto-tagging adds srsltid, but Virling neither reads nor stores it. The channel comes from the referrer and your UTM tags, and the landing page is stored as a path without the query string, so every srsltid version of a product page is one row.

How do I keep free listings and Shopping ads apart?

Give them different mediums and campaigns. Free listing links in the feed get utm_medium=free; Shopping ads get utm_medium=cpc through the Google Ads final URL suffix. Untagged ad clicks with a Google referrer would otherwise count as Organic search next to your free listings.

Can I see which product an order was for?

Virling stores the order amount, not its line items. It shows the product through the visit instead: a free listing click lands on the product page, so the credited visit in the order's journey, and the Landing page tab, tell you which listing brought the buyer.

Why are only some of my products in the Shopping campaign?

The tags reach only part of the feed. A supplemental feed or feed rule may cover some products, or new products and variants were added without the parameters. Apply them to every item and check a live listing after Merchant Center re-crawls.
