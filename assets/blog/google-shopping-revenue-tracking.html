Google Shopping revenue tracking: which free listing clicks pay

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-20.

To see which orders came from Google Shopping clicks, tag the product links in your Merchant Center feed with utm_source=google and a campaign of their own, store the visitor ID on each order, and join each order to the visit before it. Without tags, Virling cannot tell a Shopping click from a search click: a free listing click usually arrives with a Google referrer, so it counts as Organic search on the google.com row. Merchant Center's own click ID, srsltid, is not read, so it changes nothing. Your own utm_campaign is what separates Shopping.

This guide is for Shopify and WooCommerce stores whose products show in Google Shopping free listings. Merchant Center reports clicks and impressions; it does not tell you which clicks became paid orders.

How Google Shopping visits are classified

Virling gives every session one channel, decided by its first pageview. The rules run in order and the first match wins:

  • A paid or email medium. A utm_medium of cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social or paidsocial is Paid, and email, newsletter or e-mail is Email. Any other medium, such as free or organic, does not decide the channel.
  • A Google referrer. Any host that starts with google. or contains .google., such as www.google.com or www.google.de, is a known search host, so the visit is Organic search. Every Google country domain shares one google.com row. A referrer wins over a utm_source, and it only names the host, not the Shopping tab.
  • No referrer, with a tag. When the referrer is missing, Virling reads utm_source, or ref if there is no utm_source. google is a known search source, so the visit is still Organic search. A source Virling does not know, such as google_shopping, counts as Referral.
  • No referrer, no tag. The visit is Direct. If it was opened in the Google app, the Direct row is labelled Google app, but that label covers anything opened in the app, not only Shopping.

So with or without tags, a free listing click lands in Organic search, as long as you use utm_source=google and a medium that is not paid or email. The tag adds a campaign, and the campaign is what you filter on. The full rule order is in channels.

Step 1: add the script

Add the Virling script to the <head> of every page of your store. It records each visit with its referrer and UTM tags, and sets the first-party visitor ID that orders need later. On Shopify, it goes in your theme's theme.liquid; on WooCommerce, the Virling WordPress plugin adds it for you. The app's setup screen shows the exact tag for your site, and add the script covers where it goes.

Step 2: tag your product links with UTM parameters

Free listings use the link attribute of each product in your feed, or mobile_link if you set one. Add the same tags to every product, so all free listing clicks share one campaign:

https://yourstore.com/products/linen-apron?utm_source=google&utm_medium=free&utm_campaign=shopping-free-listings
https://yourstore.com/products/oak-board?utm_source=google&utm_medium=free&utm_campaign=shopping-free-listings
  • utm_source=google, so a click that arrives with no referrer still counts as Organic search, next to the rest of Google.
  • utm_medium=free, or any value that is not a paid or email medium. It keeps the visit out of Paid.
  • utm_campaign=shopping-free-listings, one fixed value for every product. The Campaign tab groups visitors and revenue by it, which is how you see Shopping apart from search.
  • utm_content, if you want it, can name the product type or feed. Virling stores it with each visit; keep what you want to compare in the campaign so it shows up in reports.

If you also run paid Shopping ads, tag their links with utm_medium=cpc and a different campaign. A paid click with a Google referrer and no paid medium counts as Organic search, so untagged ads would mix into your free listing numbers.

The UTM builder makes the tagged URL for you. Feed apps and Merchant Center itself can add their own parameters, so open a live listing, click through to your product page and paste the URL you land on into the UTM checker. It lists the tags on the link and flags a missing source or medium, capitals, spaces and duplicate parameters. More naming rules are in which UTM parameters to use.

Step 3: connect your store

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs the orders and the visitor ID on each one. In the app, open your site, then Setup, then Connect payments, paste the key your store needs and click Connect and backfill.

  • Shopify. Create a custom app with only the read_orders scope and paste its token, then add the short theme snippet that stores the visitor ID on the cart, which Shopify copies onto the order. Track Shopify revenue by traffic source walks through it.
  • WooCommerce. Create a read-only REST API key and paste it, then add the short snippet that stores the virling_vid cookie on each new order. Track WooCommerce revenue by traffic source walks through it.

Other stores and checkouts are on ecommerce integrations. Place a test order through a tagged product link, then check that it appears on the Payments page with google.com as its source.

Shoppers often compare products and come back days later by typing your address. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the order goes to the Google Shopping visit, as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. See Google Shopping revenue

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more.
  2. On the Channels card, open the Campaign tab and find shopping-free-listings. The row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click the row to filter the whole page to free listing visits.
  4. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the order, with the credited one marked.

Compare revenue per visitor by product page

Free listings send people straight to a product page, so the landing page tells you which product they clicked. With the dashboard filtered to your Shopping campaign, open the Landing page tab of the Pages card. Each product page is a row with its visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.

  • Many visitors, little revenue. The listing gets clicks but the page does not sell. Check price, stock and shipping against what the listing showed.
  • Few visitors, high revenue per visitor. The product sells when people see it. Better titles, images or attributes in the feed may get it more clicks.
  • Same page, different channel. Clear the filter and compare the same product page from Organic search, Social or Email. A product that pays from search but not from Shopping, or the other way round, tells you where to spend effort.

Give late buyers time: use a range that runs a few weeks past the clicks you are judging. The revenue per visitor calculator does the sum for any page or channel.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • The Shopping campaign is missing. The tags are not on the URLs Google uses. Check the link attribute in your feed, and paste a live landing URL into the UTM checker.
  • Shopping visits show as Referral. The utm_source is a value Virling does not know, such as google_shopping, and the referrer was missing. Change the source to google.
  • Shopping visits show as Paid. The medium is a paid one such as cpc. Keep paid mediums for ads only.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching the order; check the theme or order snippet. See Unattributed revenue.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so the real visitor number can be higher than any analytics tool shows. Revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which orders came from Google Shopping free listings?

Add utm_source=google, utm_medium=free and utm_campaign=shopping-free-listings to the product links in your Merchant Center feed, store the visitor ID on each order, and use a tool that joins orders to visits. In Virling, the Campaign tab then shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor for your free listings, apart from other Google traffic.

Why does Google Shopping traffic show up as organic search?

A click on a free listing usually arrives with a Google referrer, such as www.google.com. The referrer is a host only, so it cannot say whether the click came from the Shopping tab or from search results. Virling counts any Google referrer as Organic search, on the google.com row, unless the link has a paid or email utm_medium.

What UTM tags should I use on Google Shopping free listings?

Use utm_source=google so the visit stays in Organic search even when the referrer is missing, a medium such as free that is not a paid medium, and a fixed utm_campaign such as shopping-free-listings. Avoid a source like google_shopping: Virling does not know it, so a visit with that tag and no referrer counts as Referral.

Does Virling read the srsltid parameter Google adds to product links?

No. Virling reads the referrer and the utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_content and utm_term tags. The srsltid parameter does not change the channel and does not mark a visit as coming from Shopping, so use your own UTM tags for that.

Are paid Shopping ads counted the same way?

Only if they are tagged the same way. A paid click with a Google referrer and no paid utm_medium counts as Organic search. Tag the ad links with utm_medium=cpc so they land in the Paid channel, and keep free listings on a different medium.
