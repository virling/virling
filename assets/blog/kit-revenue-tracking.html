Kit revenue tracking: see which broadcast brings sales

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-23.

Kit revenue tracking joins each payment for your course, template, membership or paid newsletter to the Kit broadcast or sequence email that sent the buyer. Tag every link to your site with utm_source=kit, utm_medium=email and a utm_campaign named after the email, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and Virling credits each payment to the tagged visit, so the Campaign tab shows revenue per email. Kit, formerly ConvertKit, reports opens and clicks; it cannot see the payment that came after the click.

Why Kit sales land in Direct

Most subscribers read your email in an email app, and most email apps send no referrer when a link is clicked. Without UTM tags, the visit has nothing that says it came from Kit, so it is counted as Direct, and so is the payment that follows. The email did the work, and the report gives the credit to nobody. For more on visits that arrive with no referrer, see dark social revenue tracking.

Tag broadcast links

Use one source and one medium in every email, and change only the campaign. A link in a broadcast looks like this:

https://example.com/course?utm_source=kit&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=launch-week-1

  • utm_source=kit: the same value in every email, so all of your Kit traffic adds up under one source.
  • utm_medium=email: puts the visit in the Email channel. newsletter works too; pick one and keep it.
  • utm_campaign=launch-week-1: one value per broadcast, lowercase, with hyphens, so each one has its own row.
  • utm_content: optional, to tell two links in the same email apart, such as intro and ps.

Tag every link that points to your site, not only the main button. The free UTM builder makes the tagged URLs for you.

Tag sequence links

A sequence sends the same emails to every new subscriber over days or weeks. Give each email in it its own campaign that starts with the sequence name, so you can tell both the sequence and the email apart:

https://example.com/course?utm_source=kit&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=welcome-3

Each email then has its own row on the Campaign tab, and the shared prefix shows which sequence it belongs to. Because a sequence keeps sending, those rows keep growing long after you wrote the emails.

After you send, click a link from the email in your own inbox and check the address bar on your site. The tags that arrive there are the ones Virling reads.

Or use a tracked link

Instead of writing UTM tags by hand, open Links in the app and make a short link. Set the destination, a source such as kit, and the channel Email. A click redirects to your page with utm_source, utm_medium=email and utm_campaign added, and Links shows the clicks, visitors and revenue each link brought. The link’s id becomes the campaign, so give each link a name you will recognise, such as “Welcome sequence, email 3”. See tracked links.

How Virling sorts a Kit visit

Every session gets one channel from its first pageview, and utm_medium is checked first, ignoring case. A medium of email, newsletter or e-mail is the Email channel, whatever the referrer says, so kit does not need to be a known source. The source and campaign are stored with the visit too. See how channels are decided.

When a payment comes in, it is joined to the visits before it. Under the default model the whole payment, after refunds, is credited to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct, and to that visit’s channel, source, campaign and landing page. A subscriber who clicks your broadcast on Monday and comes back on Thursday by typing your address is still credited to the broadcast. See the attribution model.

Sell through Stripe, Polar, LemonSqueezy or Creem

Virling reads payments from your payment provider, not from Kit. Connect the one you sell through, and make sure checkout carries the visitor ID from the virling_vid cookie:

  • Stripe: a restricted read-only key. Payment Links on buy.stripe.com need no code; for Checkout Sessions, pass the visitor ID as client_reference_id. See Stripe.
  • Polar: an organization access token. Checkout links on buy.polar.sh need no code; checkout sessions take virling_vid in metadata. See Polar.
  • LemonSqueezy: an API key. Checkout links on STORE.lemonsqueezy.com need no code; Virling reads the visitor ID through a webhook it registers, which needs a public https address. See LemonSqueezy.
  • Creem: a scoped API key, and virling_vid in checkout metadata. Virling adds one checkout.completed webhook to read it. See Creem.

Link from your email to a page on your site, such as the sales page, not straight to checkout. The script adds the visitor ID to checkout links on pages that run it; a checkout link opened from an email carries none, so that sale is Unattributed. For templates, courses and ebooks, see revenue attribution for digital products, and for all providers, link checkout to visits.

See payments per email

1. Open the Email channel

On the dashboard, the Channels card lists each channel with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. Click Email and the whole page filters to visits from your emails.

2. Switch to the Campaign tab

The Campaign tab on the same card has one row per utm_campaign, so each broadcast and each sequence email shows its own visitors and revenue. Compare them by revenue per visitor, not by visitors: a short email that sends a few buyers can be worth more than a popular one read by many who never pay.

3. Open the payments

Click a campaign to filter the dashboard to it and see its revenue over time. The Payments page lists every payment with its source; open one to see the journey: the sessions before the payment and which one got the credit.

What to check if the numbers look off

  • Kit revenue still shows as Direct. A link was left untagged, or the tags were lost in a redirect before your page. Click a link from a sent email and check the address bar.
  • Emails are split across rows. Values differ in case or spelling, such as Launch-Week-1 and launch-week-1. Pick one format and keep it.
  • Revenue shows no channel at all. The payment was not matched to a visit. The link went straight to checkout, or checkout did not carry the visitor ID; see link checkout to visits.
  • A recent broadcast looks weak. Subscribers often pay days or weeks later. Give it time, and check the date range, since a sale is credited on the day of the payment.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which Kit email brought a paying customer?

Tag every link to your site in the broadcast or sequence email with utm_source=kit, utm_medium=email and a utm_campaign named after the email. Connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Each payment is then credited to the tagged visit, and the Campaign tab shows revenue per email.

Which channel does Kit traffic land in?

A link tagged utm_medium=email lands in Email, because Virling checks utm_medium first and reads email, newsletter and e-mail as the Email channel, ignoring case. An untagged click usually arrives with no referrer and is counted as Direct.

How should I tag a Kit sequence?

Give each email in the sequence its own utm_campaign that starts with the sequence name, such as welcome-1, welcome-2 and welcome-3. Each email then shows its own visitors and revenue on the Campaign tab.

Can I link straight to checkout from a Kit email?

You can, but the sale will be Unattributed. A checkout link opened from an email carries no visitor ID, because the Virling script only adds it on a page that runs the script. Link to a page on your site and let the reader click through to checkout from there.

Does Virling read sales made in Kit itself?

No. Virling reads payments from the providers it connects to, such as Stripe, Polar, LemonSqueezy and Creem. Kit keeps sending your emails; Virling joins the clicks they bring to the payments that follow.
