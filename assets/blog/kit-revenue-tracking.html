Kit revenue tracking: see which broadcast brings sales

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-23.

Kit revenue tracking joins each sale of your course, template, membership or paid newsletter to the Kit broadcast or sequence email that sent the buyer. Kit, formerly ConvertKit, shows opens, clicks and subscribers, but the sale happens in Stripe, Polar or Creem, out of its sight. Virling joins the click to the sale through the visitor on your sales page.

Why Kit sales need tags

Subscribers open broadcasts in email apps that send no referrer, so an untagged click and the sale after it count as Direct. Virling puts a visit tagged utm_medium=email in the Email channel and credits each sale to a visit before it through the visitor ID at checkout; see how channels are decided.

Tag broadcasts and sequences

Add utm_source=kit&utm_medium=email to every link to your site and change only utm_campaign, such as launch-week-1. The free UTM builder makes the tagged URLs for you.

  • Broadcasts go out once to your list or a segment. Give each send one campaign named for the launch or the date, such as launch-week-1 or weekly-2026-11-20.
  • Sequences drip the same emails to every new subscriber. Start each email’s campaign with the sequence name, such as welcome-1 to welcome-5, so the emails sort together. A sequence keeps selling long after you wrote it, so its rows grow every month.

Kit click tracking and Virling tracked links

Kit counts clicks by sending each link through its own redirect before your page. Your tags are part of the destination, so they should arrive; send yourself a test email, click a link and look at the address bar on your site to be sure.

You can also share a Virling tracked link instead of writing tags. On the Campaign tab of the Channels card, click + Tracked link, pick Newsletter and set the page to /course?utm_medium=email: Newsletter alone sets utm_medium=referral, and a medium on the page wins, so the clicks land in Email. The link’s id becomes the campaign, its name labels the row, and the source is newsletter, not kit, so pick one method per list. See tracked links.

Landing pages and forms hosted on Kit

Virling only counts pages on your own domain and its subdomains, so a visit to a landing page or form hosted on Kit is not recorded, and neither is the signup there. What Virling sees is each later click from your emails to your site.

A Kit form embedded on a page of your site is different: that page runs the script, so the visit that brought the subscriber keeps its channel. Send a goal such as newsletter signup when the form is accepted, and the payment journey shows the signup, then the email visits, then the sale, as long as the subscriber reads on the same browser. Sell through Stripe, Polar or Creem

Virling reads payments from your payment provider, not from Kit, and never changes one. Connect the one you sell through:

  • Stripe: a restricted read-only key. Payment Links on buy.stripe.com get the visitor ID from the script with no code; a Checkout Session takes it as client_reference_id. See Stripe.
  • Polar: an organization access token. Checkout links on buy.polar.sh need no code; a checkout session takes virling_vid in metadata. See Polar.
  • Creem: a scoped API key and virling_vid in checkout metadata, read through one checkout.completed webhook that Virling adds. See Creem.

Send readers from the email to your sales page, not to the checkout URL: a checkout link opened from an inbox has no visitor ID, so that sale is Unattributed. For courses, templates and ebooks, see revenue attribution for digital products.

Read sales per email

Click Email on the Channels card and switch to the Campaign tab: every broadcast and sequence email is a row with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. A short broadcast that sends a few buyers can beat a popular one read by many who never pay. Click a campaign to see its revenue over time, and open a sale on the Payments page to see its journey.

When Kit numbers look wrong

  • Sales still show as Direct. A link was left untagged, or the tags were lost before your page. Check a link from a sent broadcast.
  • One sequence email is split in two rows. The values differ in case or spelling, such as Welcome-3 and welcome-3.
  • Subscribers grow but visits do not. They sign up on a landing page hosted on Kit, which Virling does not count; see above.
  • Yesterday’s launch broadcast looks weak. Launch sales often come over several days, and each sale is credited on the day it was paid.
  • A sale has no channel at all. The link went straight to checkout, or checkout did not carry the visitor ID; see link checkout to visits.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which Kit email brought a paying customer?

Tag every link to your site in the broadcast or sequence with utm_source=kit, utm_medium=email and a utm_campaign per email, connect Stripe, Polar or Creem, and link to your sales page rather than checkout. Each sale is then credited to the tagged visit, and the Campaign tab lists revenue per email.

How should I tag a Kit sequence?

Give each email in the sequence its own utm_campaign that starts with the sequence name, such as welcome-1, welcome-2 and welcome-3. Each email then shows its own visitors and revenue on the Campaign tab.

Can I link straight to checkout from a Kit email?

You can, but the sale will be Unattributed. A checkout link opened from an email carries no visitor ID, because the Virling script only adds it on a page that runs the script. Link to a page on your site and let the reader click through to checkout from there.

Does Virling count visits to my Kit landing page?

No. Virling counts pages on your own domain and its subdomains, so a landing page or form hosted on Kit is not recorded. A Kit form embedded on your own site is, and you can send a goal when it is accepted.

Does Virling read sales made in Kit itself?

No. Virling reads payments from the providers it connects to; for creators that is usually Stripe, Polar or Creem. Kit keeps sending your emails, and Virling joins the clicks they bring to the sales that follow.
