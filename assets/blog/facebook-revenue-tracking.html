Facebook revenue tracking: which page posts and group shares bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

To see which Facebook visits turn into paying customers, tag every link you post on your page, in groups and in Messenger with utm_source=facebook and a campaign for that post. Carry the visitor ID into checkout, then join each payment to the visit that came before it. You then see the revenue and revenue per visitor that Facebook, and each post or group share on it, brings. Reach, reactions and link clicks in Facebook's insights do not tell you this.

This post covers organic page posts, groups and Messenger. Paid Meta ads are a separate topic: tag them with utm_medium=paid and they land in the Paid channel.

How Facebook visits get counted

Facebook still sends clicks from pages, groups and shared posts, but without some care most of it lands as Social with no detail, or as Direct. There are three ways a visit gets marked as Facebook, and you want all of them working.

  • The referrer. facebook.com is a known Social host, and subdomains match, so a click that arrives through Facebook's outbound link addresses l.facebook.com or lm.facebook.com is Social too. In the Source tab, l.facebook.com is merged into facebook.com, while lm.facebook.com shows as a row of its own, so add the two when you compare. The referrer is a host only, so it cannot tell you which post or group the click came from.
  • The in-app browser. Most Facebook use happens in the mobile apps, and links opened there often arrive with no referrer. With no referrer and no tag, most tools count that visit as Direct. When the in-app browser names the app in its user agent, Virling credits the visit to facebook.com in Social instead; Messenger counts as Facebook. If the link opens in a plain system browser that does not name the app, the visit stays Direct.
  • A UTM tag. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, and facebook and fb are known Social sources. The tag travels in the link itself, so it survives the app, the system browser and a missing referrer.

A referrer or a utm_source always wins over the in-app browser guess, and a paid or email utm_medium wins over both. The full rule order is in channels, and why Direct is your biggest traffic source covers the other causes of a large Direct row.

Step 1: add the script

Add the Virling script to the <head> of every page. It records each visit with its referrer and UTM tags, and sets the first-party visitor ID that checkout needs later. The app's setup screen shows the exact tag for your site, and add the script covers where it goes, with guides for common frameworks and site builders.

Step 2: tag every Facebook link

Give each page post, group share and Messenger link its own campaign:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=page-post-spring-sale
https://yourdomain.com/pricing?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=group-shopify-owners&utm_content=group
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=messenger-reply&utm_content=messenger
  • utm_source=facebook on every Facebook and Messenger link, so they group under one source and stay out of Direct.
  • utm_medium=social for organic page posts, group shares and messages. Anything except a paid or email medium keeps the visit in Social. Only paid ads get utm_medium=paid.
  • utm_campaign names the post or group, lowercase, with hyphens. The Campaign tab groups visitors and revenue by this value, so this is the tag that gives you revenue per post and per group.
  • utm_content is optional. Use it for the place, such as page, group or messenger, and keep the post or group itself in the campaign so it shows up in reports.

When you share the same link in several groups, give each group its own campaign, such as group-shopify-owners and group-indie-makers, so each one gets its own row. More naming rules are in which UTM parameters to use, and the UTM builder makes the link for you. If the same post also goes out on X, LinkedIn or Instagram, the social link generator makes a link for each; for Facebook, set the source to facebook by hand.

Long tagged URLs look messy in a group post or a chat. Use a tracked link instead: in Virling, open the Campaign tab on the Channels card, click + Tracked link, pick Other, type facebook.com as the source and pick the page it should open. The short link adds the tags on the click, and the Campaign tab lists it with its visitors and revenue. Make one link per post or group. See tracked links.

Step 3: connect your payment provider

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs the payments and the visitor ID in checkout. In the app, open your site, then Setup, then Connect payments, paste the key your provider needs and click Connect and backfill; see payments. Each provider has its own page: Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce, all listed on integrations.

If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the visitor ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Every case is covered in link checkout to visits.

People often click a post, leave, and come back later by typing your URL or searching your name. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the payment goes to the tagged Facebook visit, as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. If they subscribe, every renewal keeps counting toward that post.

See revenue per post and group

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more, so people who clicked a post and paid later have time to show up.
  2. In the Channels card, open Social, or switch to the Source tab, and find facebook.com and lm.facebook.com. Each row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click a row to filter the whole page. The Pages card now shows which landing pages those visitors arrived on.
  4. Switch to the Campaign tab. With one campaign per post or group, each is a row with its own visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

Facebook often introduces a buyer who comes back later through search. Switch the model to first touch in Site settings and compare the Facebook rows under both; history is recomputed, and you can switch back. If you also post on Instagram, Instagram revenue tracking covers bio and story links with the same setup. For posts on Threads, see how Threads clicks are counted, and for links you send in chats, tagging WhatsApp broadcasts and replies.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Facebook looks small but Direct jumped the day a post went out. Some links are untagged and opened in a browser that did not name the app. Check the page post, the group shares and any links you sent in Messenger.
  • Paid ads show up in Social. The ad links are missing utm_medium=paid, or use a medium that is not a paid one.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A buyer says they found you on Facebook but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have seen a post without clicking a link and searched for you later. No analytics tool can count a view that never reached your site.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so Facebook's real visitor number can be higher than what any analytics tool shows. Revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from Facebook?

Tag every link you post on your page, in groups and in Messenger with utm_source=facebook and a utm_campaign for that post or share. Pass the visitor ID into checkout and use a tool that joins payments to visits. The Campaign tab then shows visitors and revenue per post.

What are l.facebook.com and lm.facebook.com in my referrers?

They are the addresses Facebook routes outbound links through, so a click from a post, group or Messenger can arrive with one of them as the referrer. Virling counts both as Social. l.facebook.com is merged into facebook.com, while lm.facebook.com shows as its own row.

Why do Facebook visits show up as Direct?

Links opened in the Facebook or Messenger app often arrive with no referrer. Virling credits them to facebook.com when the in-app browser names itself in the user agent. If the link opens in a plain browser that does not, and the link has no tag, the visit is Direct. A utm_source tag prevents this.

Can I see which Facebook group sent a paying customer?

Not from the referrer, which only says facebook.com, l.facebook.com or lm.facebook.com. Give each group share its own utm_campaign, or its own tracked link, and the Campaign tab shows visitors and revenue per group.

Does this cover Facebook ads?

No. This post covers organic pages, groups and Messenger. Paid Meta ads should be tagged with utm_medium=paid, which puts them in the Paid channel.
