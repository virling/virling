Mastodon analytics: which posts and instances bring customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-21.

To see which Mastodon posts and instances bring paying customers, you need three things: visits counted by the instance they came from, such as mastodon.social or the server your readers use, links on your profile and in your posts tagged with utm_source=mastodon.social and a campaign per post so clicks without a referrer still count, and each visit joined to the payment that followed by a visitor ID carried into checkout. Then you can read the revenue, and the revenue per visitor, that the fediverse brings. Boosts, favourites and follower counts do not tell you this.

Many founders and open source makers share launches and progress on Mastodon. Whether that brings sales comes down to one question: did anyone who clicked through from the fediverse pay, and how much was each of those visitors worth compared with your other channels?

Why fediverse visits are hard to count

There is no single Mastodon website. Each reader uses an instance, a server such as mastodon.social or one run by a community, and a click on your link arrives from that instance. So the same post can send visits from dozens of different domains, or from none at all. A click reaches your site in one of four ways, and Virling handles each one differently.

  • mastodon.social as the referrer. mastodon.social is on Virling's list of known social hosts. Its visits land in the Social channel, named Mastodon. Subdomains of a known host match too.
  • Another instance as the referrer. Most instances are not on that list. A click from one of them counts as Referral, listed under that instance's own domain. Virling stores the referrer host, so each instance gets its own row.
  • No referrer. Links opened from an app often arrive with no referrer at all. Virling can credit some apps from the user agent of their in-app browser, such as Instagram or LinkedIn, but no Mastodon app is one of them. An untagged click with no referrer is counted as Direct.
  • A UTM tag. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, or ref when it is missing. mastodon.social is read as a known social host, so the visit lands in Social, in the same row as referrer clicks from mastodon.social.

A referrer always wins over the tag, so a tagged click from an instance's web page still shows under that instance. The tag matters for the clicks that arrive with nothing else. The full rule order is in channels.

Find your fediverse visits in Referral

Because each instance is its own row, add them up yourself. In the Channels card, open Referral, or switch to the Source tab, and look for the instance domains your followers use. They are usually easy to spot next to blogs and directories. Click a row to filter the page to that instance's visitors, revenue and landing pages.

Tag your profile and post links

Tags pull the clicks that come with no referrer into one place, and tell your profile link apart from a thread or a launch post. Tag each place with its own campaign:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=mastodon.social&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=profile
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=mastodon.social&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=post-launch-v2
  • utm_source=mastodon.social for every Mastodon link, whatever instance you post from. It is a known social host, so tagged clicks with no referrer count as Social. Your own instance's domain would count as Referral unless it is on the known list.
  • utm_medium=social for posts, replies, your profile and direct mentions. Anything except a paid or email medium keeps the visit in Social.
  • utm_campaign names the place: one per post you want to measure, and a fixed one for links that stay put, such as the link in your profile fields.

The social UTM link generator tags your links for X, Bluesky and other platforms in one go; for Mastodon, set the source to mastodon.social by hand. If a long tagged URL looks messy in a post, use a tracked link instead: in Virling, open Links, pick the page it should open, type mastodon.social as the source and choose Social as the channel. The short link adds utm_source, utm_medium=social and its own id as utm_campaign on the click, and the Campaign tab lists it with its visitors and revenue. See tracked links.

Make sure payments can be credited

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs the visitor ID in checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Every provider is covered in link checkout to visits. When no visitor ID reaches checkout, the payment still counts toward revenue but as Unattributed, not credited to any visit.

Few people buy on the first click from a post. With the default model, last non-direct, someone who clicked your post on Monday and came back on Friday by typing your URL is still credited to Mastodon, as long as the Mastodon visit is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default.

Which Mastodon posts bring paying customers?

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more, so slow buyers have time to pay.
  2. In the Channels card, open Social and find mastodon.social, then open Referral for other instances. Each row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  3. Switch to the Campaign tab to split your tagged links by post and by profile link.
  4. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

Bluesky works the same way with one fixed host; Bluesky traffic analytics covers the steps there, so you can compare the two side by side. If you post the same updates on X, see how clicks from X are counted.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Mastodon looks small but Direct jumped the day a post was boosted. App clicks on untagged links arrived with no referrer. Tag the profile link and the next post. Links passed on in private messages and chats land in Direct the same way, as covered in dark social revenue tracking.
  • Referral is full of domains you do not recognize. Many of them are likely instances. Tag your links so the clicks that come with no referrer gather under one source.
  • A Mastodon campaign shows under Referral. The tag probably names an instance or a word that is not a known source. Use mastodon.social.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. If a large share of revenue is Unattributed, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all. Revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track sales from Mastodon?

Count visits with mastodon.social or another instance as the referrer, tag the links on your profile and in your posts with utm_source=mastodon.social and a campaign per post, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and use a tool that joins payments to visits. Then read Mastodon's revenue and revenue per visitor next to your other channels.

Why does Mastodon traffic show up as Referral?

On the fediverse the referrer is the instance your reader uses, not one Mastodon address. mastodon.social is a known social host, so it counts as Social. Most other instances are not, so a click from one of them counts as Referral under that instance's domain.

Why do clicks from Mastodon show up as Direct?

Links opened from an app often arrive with no referrer. Virling does not recognize Mastodon apps from their user agent, so an untagged click with no referrer is Direct. A utm_source on the link keeps it credited to Mastodon.

What utm_source should I use for Mastodon?

Use mastodon.social, whatever instance you post from. It is a known social host, so tagged clicks land in the Social channel and in the same row as referrer clicks from mastodon.social.

Can I see which Mastodon post sent a paying customer?

Not from the referrer, which is only an instance host. Give each post its own utm_campaign, or its own tracked link, and the Campaign tab shows visitors and revenue per post.
