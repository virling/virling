Revenue by device: mobile vs desktop, and how to read it

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-24.

To compare revenue by device, mobile vs desktop, divide the revenue credited to each device by its visitors. That gives revenue per visitor for phones, tablets and computers on one scale. In Virling this is the Device tab of the Devices card. Before you act on a low mobile number, check two things: whether phones get a different mix of channels, and whether people find you on a phone but pay on a computer. Either can make mobile look worse than it is.

How to read the Devices card

A payment does not tell you which device the buyer came on. The visit does, so each payment has to be joined to a visit before revenue can be split by device. The Devices card on the Virling dashboard then has three tabs:

  • Device: desktop, mobile and tablet. Start here.
  • Browser: which browsers your visitors and buyers use. One browser far below the rest can point to a bug rather than a habit.
  • OS: the operating system, which helps you tell one kind of phone from another.

Each row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares the row with your site average: green above it, red below it. Revenue alone mostly follows traffic, so rank by revenue per visitor. The revenue per visitor calculator does the same division if you have the two numbers from somewhere else.

Revenue counts on the payment date, so some revenue in a range can come from visits before it. Pick a range several times longer than your buyers usually take. A payment that was not matched to a visit has no device to go to; the Matched revenue line under the five numbers shows how much is matched and why the rest is not. The card is described in read your dashboard, and you can click through it on sample data in the demo.

Virling credits each matched payment to the device of one visit, so desktop, mobile and tablet sit side by side with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.

Filter it by channel

Click a row on any breakdown card and the whole page is filtered by it. That lets you read device and channel together:

  • Channel first. Click a channel on the Channels card, such as Email. The Devices card now shows only its visitors, so you compare mobile and desktop for people who came the same way.
  • Device first. Click Mobile on the Devices card. The Channels card now shows which channels pay on phones, and the Pages card shows where those visits land.
  • Keep it. Filters show as chips at the top. With them on, click Save as segment and name it, such as "Email on mobile". It stays as a tab at the top of every view.

  • It explains part of the gap. If mobile had the desktop mix of channels, it would earn 20% × $1.50 + 60% × $0.75 + 20% × $0.125 = about $0.78 per visitor. So about 28 cents of the $1.00 gap comes from the mix, not the phone.

Why a phone visit and a desktop payment look like two people

A visitor is recognised by an ID kept in the browser. A phone and a laptop are two browsers, so they have two IDs. Someone taps your link on a phone, reads, and buys on a laptop that evening by typing your address. The report shows a mobile visitor who never paid, and a desktop visitor who came from Direct and did.

That lowers mobile twice over: the phone visitor is counted and the payment is not. In the example, suppose $900 of the desktop revenue came from people who first found the store on a phone. Moved to mobile, it would make mobile about $0.66 per visitor instead of $0.50, and desktop about $1.28 instead of $1.50. The gap shrinks, with nothing changed on the site. You cannot read that $900 in any report, because nothing connects the two visits.

Virling does not guess that two devices are one person. What can connect them is something the buyer gives you: an account they sign in to on both devices, with the first visitor ID saved and passed into checkout, or the same email sent as an identify event and used to pay. The cross-device attribution post covers the steps and their limits, and unattributed payments lists the providers where email matching works. Credit also follows your attribution model: a payment goes to the device of the one visit it is credited to.

What to check before you change a mobile page

  • Enough visitors. One payment moves a small row a lot. Use a longer range until each row has at least a few hundred visitors and more than a handful of payments.
  • The same channel. Filter by one channel at a time, as above. If the gap closes inside each channel, the mobile page is not the problem.
  • The landing page. Click Mobile and open the Landing page tab of the Pages card, then do the same for Desktop. A page that pays on desktop and not on mobile is a better target than the whole site.
  • The step where people leave. With Mobile on, the Funnel in the Goals card shows the share that reached Visit, Signup, Checkout started and Paid. Compare it with Desktop. A drop at checkout needs a different fix from a drop at the first page. If mobile visitors leave early and Direct on desktop is strong, they may be finishing on a computer.
  • One browser or all of them. Open the Browser and OS tabs with Mobile on. If one is far below the others, test that one on a real phone.
  • Page speed. The Page speed card shows load time next to the conversion rate and revenue of the visitors who loaded each page, over the last 30 days. It covers every device together and leaves out some browsers, so use it to find slow pages, then test them on a phone.
  • How they say they found you. A short question at signup or checkout catches phone visits that no report can join to the payment.

If the gap is still there after that, change one page, then use Custom range to read the same filtered view for two periods of the same length, one before the change and one after. Country can hide behind device too, when visitors from one country mostly use phones; revenue by country shows how to check that.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see revenue by device?

Join each payment to the visit before it, then group visitors and revenue by the device of that visit. In Virling, the Device tab of the Devices card shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor for each device type, and the Browser and OS tabs do the same for browsers and operating systems.

Why does mobile bring less revenue per visitor than desktop?

There are three common reasons: phones often get a different mix of channels, some people find you on a phone and pay on a computer, and the mobile page or checkout may be harder to use. Check the first two before you change the page.

Does a person who uses a phone and a laptop count twice?

Yes. Each browser has its own visitor ID and Virling does not merge visitors, so the phone and the laptop are two visitors. If the payment carries the laptop's visitor ID, the revenue goes to desktop.

Can I see mobile and desktop revenue for one channel?

Yes. Click the channel on the Channels card to filter the whole page by it, then read the Devices card. You can also click a device first and read the Channels card.

What is a good mobile revenue per visitor compared with desktop?

No single ratio fits every site, because it depends on price, channels and how people buy. Compare mobile with desktop inside the same channel, and with your own earlier periods.
