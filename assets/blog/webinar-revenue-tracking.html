Webinar revenue tracking: which webinars bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

Webinar revenue tracking means seeing which webinar visits turn into paying customers. Host the registration page on your own site, tag every link to it with the webinar's name in utm_campaign, and give the follow-up email and the replay link campaigns of their own. Carry the visitor ID into checkout, then join each payment to the visits before it. You then see the revenue each webinar and each promotion channel brought, weeks after the live session. Registrations and attendance rates in your webinar tool do not tell you this.

For a B2B SaaS founder a webinar is a week of work: a topic, slides, promotion on LinkedIn and in the newsletter, a live hour, a follow-up sequence. Whether it paid comes down to two questions: did anyone who registered or watched become a paying customer, and which promotion brought them?

Why webinar revenue is hard to see

  • Three links, not one. People reach your site from the registration page, from the follow-up email after the session and from the replay link. Each one is a separate visit, often days apart. If they share one tag, you cannot tell which one did the work; if they carry no tag, the email clicks land in Direct.
  • The delay. Few people buy during a webinar. They attend, start a trial, maybe book a call, and pay weeks later. A tool that only looks at the same session, or a short window, never sees the payment.
  • The registration page is often not yours. When people register on the webinar tool's own page, your analytics never sees that visit, and the promotion channel that brought them is lost before they ever reach your site.
  • Work laptop, then phone. The visitor ID lives in one browser. Someone who registers at their desk and pays later from another device starts as a new visitor there. No analytics tool can join those two from the visit alone.

Set it up with Virling

1. Tag the registration, follow-up and replay links

Pick one name per webinar, lowercase with hyphens and the month at the end, such as webinar-pricing-pages-2026-12. Use it as the campaign on every promotion link to the registration page, and change only the source and medium for each place you share it:

https://yourdomain.com/webinar?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=webinar-pricing-pages-2026-12
https://yourdomain.com/webinar?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=webinar-pricing-pages-2026-12
https://yourdomain.com/webinar?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=paidsocial&utm_campaign=webinar-pricing-pages-2026-12

The Campaign tab groups visits and revenue by utm_campaign only, so the follow-up and replay links need campaigns of their own to show up as separate rows. Start them with the webinar's name, so one webinar's rows are easy to find and add up:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=webinar&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=webinar-pricing-pages-2026-12-followup
https://yourdomain.com/replay/pricing-pages?utm_source=webinar&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=webinar-pricing-pages-2026-12-replay

A medium of email always counts as Email, and a paid medium such as cpc or paidsocial always counts as Paid, whatever the referrer. A replay you post on LinkedIn keeps the -replay campaign with linkedin and social. Links you drop into the webinar chat or onto a slide also deserve a tag, or a tracked link: in Virling, open Links, pick the source and channel and the page it should open, and Links shows the clicks, visitors and revenue it brought. A tracked link's id becomes its campaign. See tracked links and the full rule order in channels.

The UTM builder makes each link for you, and the UTM naming template gives your team one table of values to copy from. Which UTM parameters to use has more naming rules.

2. Add the script, and mark the registration

Put the Virling script in the head of every page, including the registration page and the replay page, with your domain in data-site. It reads the tags on the landing page and sets the visitor ID. See add the script.

If the form lives on the webinar tool's page, move it to your own site or embed it in a page there, so the visit with the promotion tag is tracked. Then send a goal when the form is submitted, or from the thank-you page after it:

virling("goal", "webinar_registered");

Goals are stored with the visitor, the session and the page, and show up in each buyer's journey. In the Goals tab, a funnel from Visit to webinar_registered to Paid shows how many registrants went on to pay and the median time it took. See goals.

The registration form is also the moment you learn the visitor's email. Sending it as an identify event lets Virling credit a Stripe payment that arrives with no visitor ID, such as one from a payment link sent in a sales email, to this visitor when the payer email matches. Virling keeps only a salted hash of the email. See recover payments by email.

3. Connect a payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs the visitor ID in checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. The setup is in link checkout to visits, and each provider has its own page: Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce. All of them are listed under integrations.

If attendees start a free trial with a card, the first real charge happens weeks later without them on your site. For Stripe, Polar and Dodo Payments, Virling reads the trial start from the $0 checkout and credits that later charge from the visits before the trial began. See renewals and free trials.

Allow for the delay between attending and paying

Two settings decide whether a payment weeks after the webinar is credited to it, both in Site settings. See model and lookback window.

  • The lookback window. Only visits that started inside the window before the payment are considered. The default is 90 days; you can choose 30, 60, 90 or 365. If your buyers often take a trial and a sales call before paying, 365 days keeps the registration visit in reach.
  • The model. With the default, last non-direct, the latest tagged visit before the payment gets the credit. Someone who registered from LinkedIn, clicked the follow-up email and then typed your URL to pay is credited to the follow-up email, not to LinkedIn and not to Direct. That tells you which touch closed the deal. First touch credits the earliest visit in the window, the LinkedIn registration, which tells you which promotion brought the buyer in.

Saving either setting rebuilds attribution for all your payments, history included, so you can look at the same webinar both ways and switch back. First-touch or last-touch attribution explains the trade-off, and splitting credit across several visits covers when a small team needs more than that. The Time to pay card on the dashboard shows the median time from first visit to first payment for each channel, so you know how long to wait before judging a webinar. How long a visitor takes to become a customer covers it in more detail.

See which webinar and channel brought paying customers

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range that starts before the webinar's promotion and runs long enough to cover your time to pay.
  2. In the Channels card, switch to the Campaign tab. Each webinar's registration campaign, follow-up and replay is a row with its own visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click the registration campaign's row to filter the whole page by it. The Channel tab now splits that webinar's registrations by promotion channel: Social, Email, Paid or Referral.
  4. Switch the model to first touch to credit each payment to the promotion that first brought the buyer, then back to last non-direct to see whether the follow-up or the replay closed it.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit and goal before the payment, with the credited visit marked.

Compare webinars by revenue per visitor, not by registrations. A webinar with few registrants who are close to buying can be worth more than one that fills the room with people who will never pay. Find which channel pays walks through reading these rows.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • The webinar has no rows but Direct jumped after the follow-up went out. The email links are untagged, or a redirect on the way drops the query string. Click one from a test send and check the address bar. For readers on Apple Mail, see what Apple documents about tracking information in links.
  • Registrations show as Email, not the channel that promoted them. The first tracked visit was the follow-up click, usually because the registration page is not on your site or has no script.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A buyer says they came from the webinar but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have paid from another device, the registration visit may be outside the lookback window, or a later tagged visit took the credit under the default model. Try first touch and a longer window.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so a webinar's real visitor number can be higher than what any analytics tool shows. Revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

If you also give the talk in person, measuring conferences and meetups covers slides, booths and follow-up emails. If attendees get a checklist or a template, see whether free downloads bring buyers.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from a webinar?

Host the registration page on your own site with an analytics script on it, tag every link to it with the webinar in utm_campaign, and tag the follow-up and replay links too. Pass the visitor ID into checkout so each payment can be joined to the visits before it. The Campaign tab then shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor per webinar.

Should follow-up and replay emails use the same campaign as registration?

Give them their own campaign that starts with the webinar's name, such as webinar-pricing-pages-2026-12-followup. The Campaign tab groups by campaign, so this is the only way to see the three links apart, and the shared name keeps one webinar's rows easy to add up.

Why is my webinar revenue credited to Email instead of LinkedIn?

With the default model, last non-direct, the latest tagged visit before the payment gets the credit. A follow-up email click after a LinkedIn registration is later, so Email wins. Switch to first touch in Site settings to see which promotion channel brought the registration.

What lookback window should I use for webinars?

Long enough to cover the time from registration to the first payment. The default is 90 days. If your buyers often take a trial and a sales call first, choose 365 days so the registration visit is still in the window when they pay.

What if people register on the webinar tool's own page?

That page is not on your site, so its visits are not tracked. The first tracked visit is the first click to your site, often from the follow-up email. Hosting the registration form on your own site, or embedding it there, keeps the promotion channel.
