Discord revenue tracking: which servers and posts bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-19.

Discord revenue tracking means joining each payment on your site to the Discord server and post that sent the buyer. Most Discord clicks show up as Direct, because links opened from the desktop and mobile apps often arrive with no referrer, so nothing says they came from Discord. The fix is a tagged link: put utm_source=discord.com, the server in utm_campaign and the channel or post in utm_content, then pass the visitor ID into checkout. Each payment is then credited to the Discord visit before it, per server.

This is for SaaS founders, developer tool makers and creators who share links in their own server or in other people's. Member counts and reactions say a post was seen. They do not say whether anyone who clicked it paid, or which server is worth your time.

Why Discord clicks show up as Direct

A visit from Discord can be recognised in three ways, and only one of them is under your control.

  • The in-app browser. When the Discord app opens a link in its own browser and names itself in the user agent, Virling credits the visit to discord.com, in the Social channel.
  • The referrer. If a click does arrive with discord.com, discordapp.com or discord.gg as the referrer, it also lands in Social under Discord. A referrer is a host only, so it never says which server or channel the click came from.
  • A UTM tag. Many clicks from the app open in the system browser, which carries no referrer and no sign of Discord. With no tag those visits count as Direct, in Virling and in most other tools. A utm_source on the link travels with the click, so it survives the app, the system browser and a missing referrer.

If your Direct traffic jumps the hour you post an announcement, this is usually why. The same thing happens with WhatsApp, Slack and email; dark social revenue tracking covers those, and the full rule order is in channels.

A UTM naming scheme per server and channel

The channel only says Social. What you want is one row per server, so the server goes in the campaign and the channel or post goes in the content:

TagValueExample
utm_sourceAlways discord.comdiscord.com
utm_mediumAlways socialsocial
utm_campaignThe server, one value per server, kept foreveryour-own-server, saas-founders-server
utm_contentThe channel, and the post when it mattersannouncements-v2-launch, show-your-work

So a launch announcement in your own server and a reply in someone else's look like this:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=discord.com&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=your-own-server&utm_content=announcements-v2-launch
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=discord.com&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=your-own-server&utm_content=welcome
https://yourdomain.com/pricing?utm_source=discord.com&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=saas-founders-server&utm_content=show-your-work
  • utm_source=discord.com in every link. Both discord and discord.com put the visit in Social, but discord.com is the source a Virling tracked link for Discord adds, so hand-tagged links and tracked links report under the same source.
  • utm_medium=social in every link. Any medium except a paid or email one leaves the channel to the source, so never use cpc or email for Discord links.
  • utm_campaign names the server: lowercase, with hyphens, and the same value every time you post there. The Campaign tab groups visitors and revenue by this value, so this is the tag that compares servers over months. If one announcement matters on its own, such as a launch, give it its own campaign for that post.
  • utm_content names the channel or post. Virling stores it with each visit; keep the server in the campaign so it shows in reports.

Write the names down once and reuse them. The UTM naming template gives you a sheet for that, and the UTM builder makes each link. Put the tagged link in the places members click again and again: the welcome channel, pinned messages, the server's links or resources channel, and your bot's replies.

Or use a tracked link per server

A long tagged URL looks out of place in a chat. Instead, make a tracked link: on the dashboard, open the Campaign tab, click + Tracked link, choose Discord, then set the page and a name you will recognise, such as “Own server, welcome channel”. A click redirects to your page with utm_source=discord.com, utm_medium=social and utm_campaign set to the link's id, so the visit lands in Social under Discord, and the Campaign tab shows the link under its name with its clicks, visitors and revenue. Make one link per server, or per channel you care about, and keep sharing that same link. See tracked links.

Set up Virling

  1. Add the script. Put the Virling tag in the <head> of every page, including the pages you link to from Discord. It records the referrer and tags of the first pageview and sets the visitor cookie. See add the script.
  2. Connect a payment provider. Virling reads payments from Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce; setup is in the payments docs.
  3. Pass the visitor ID into checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. See link checkout to visits. Without this step you get visits but no revenue.
  4. Test one link. Post a tagged link in a channel, open it from the Discord app yourself, and check that the visit shows on the dashboard in Social with the right campaign.

Where the visit and the payment show in Virling

Every session gets one channel from its first pageview. A tagged Discord click lands in Social with discord.com as its source. When a payment comes in, it is joined to the visits before it. Under the default model, last non-direct, the payment goes to the latest visit in the lookback window, 90 days by default, that was not Direct. So a member who clicks your announcement, leaves, and comes back a week later by typing your URL is still credited to that Discord visit. If they subscribe, every renewal keeps counting toward it.

  1. Channels card. The Social row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. Click it to filter the page, and Discord shows as its own source.
  2. Campaign tab. Switch the Channels card to the Campaign tab. Each server, or each tracked link, is a row with its own visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. Click a row to filter the whole page: the revenue chart shows when that server's money arrived, and the Pages card shows where its visitors landed.
  3. Payments page. Open a payment to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.
  4. First touch. Switch the model to first touch in Site settings and compare. If a server's revenue grows under first touch, it introduces buyers who later pay through another channel. History is recomputed, and you can switch back. See the model and lookback window.

Judge a server over a few months, not one announcement. For Slack groups and forums too, see community revenue tracking. For Telegram channels and groups, see Telegram revenue tracking. If members pass your link on to friends, give each referrer their own link, and use a survey answer next to the tracked source for recommendations that leave no link.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Direct jumped the day you posted, but Discord looks small. A link went out untagged, or a member reshared your plain URL. Use the tagged link or tracked link every time, including in pinned messages and bot replies.
  • One server is split across rows. The campaign was spelled differently, such as SaaS-Founders and saas-founders-server. Pick one value and keep it.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A buyer says they found you on Discord but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have read without clicking, a later non-direct visit took the credit, or they clicked on their phone and paid on a laptop. The cookie lives in one browser, so no analytics tool can join those. Try first touch.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from Discord?

Tag every link you share in Discord with utm_source=discord.com, utm_medium=social, the server in utm_campaign and the channel or post in utm_content. Add the tracking script to your site, pass the visitor ID into checkout and connect your payment provider. The Campaign tab then shows visitors and revenue for each server.

Why do clicks from Discord show up as Direct?

Links opened from the Discord desktop and mobile apps often arrive with no referrer. When Discord opens the link in its own browser and names itself in the user agent, Virling credits the visit to Discord, but a link opened in the system browser carries no such sign. With no referrer and no tag, the visit is Direct.

Should I use utm_source=discord or discord.com?

Use discord.com. Both put the visit in Social, but discord.com is also the source a Virling tracked link for Discord adds, so links you tag by hand and tracked links report under the same source.

How do I see which Discord server brought a customer?

Give each server its own utm_campaign, or its own tracked link, and use it for every post there. On the dashboard, switch the Channels card to the Campaign tab: each server is a row with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the visits before it.

Does Discord's own server data show which members paid?

No. Discord can show activity inside your server, but it cannot see what happens on your site after a member clicks a link. Joining the click to the payment that followed needs a tagged link, the visitor ID passed into checkout and your payment provider connected.
