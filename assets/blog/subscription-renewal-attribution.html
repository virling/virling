Subscription renewal attribution: which channel gets the MRR

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-20.

A subscription renewal has no visit. The billing provider charges the card in month two, month six and month twenty without anyone landing on your site, so most analytics tools either drop renewals or file them under Direct or Unattributed. The channel that won the customer gets credit for one month and none of the rest. The fix for subscription renewal attribution is one rule: a renewal takes the attribution of the customer's first payment. If someone subscribed after a visit from Reddit, every renewal is credited to that Reddit visit, even years later.

That rule is how Virling credits renewal revenue by channel. This post covers why renewals go missing, how each billing provider's renewals are detected, and what the rule changes in your numbers.

Why renewals go missing from channel reports

  • No visit, no channel. Attribution joins a payment to the visits before it. A renewal is charged by Stripe or Paddle on a schedule, so there is no visit before it and no visitor ID on it.
  • Unattributed grows every month. Each month more of your revenue is renewals, so a report that credits payments to visits shows a shrinking share for every channel and a growing pile of Direct or Unattributed.
  • Channels with loyal customers look worst. A channel whose customers stay for a year has most of its revenue in renewals. If renewals are dropped, it looks no better than a channel whose customers cancel after one month.

The rule: a renewal takes the first payment's attribution

Virling finds the customer's first payment: their earliest payment that did not fail, from the same provider, matched by the provider's customer ID. If that first payment was attributed, the renewal copies its channel, referrer, campaign, landing page, country and device. If it was not, the renewal falls back to its own visitor ID, which renewals rarely carry, so it is usually Unattributed too.

The first payment is credited the usual way, from the visits before checkout, so pass the visitor ID into checkout. See link checkout to visits and renewals in the docs.

How each provider's renewals are detected

ProviderA payment is a renewal when
StripeIts invoice's billing reason is subscription_cycle.
Dodo PaymentsIt belongs to a subscription that already had a paid payment before it.
PolarThe order's billing reason is subscription_cycle.
LemonSqueezyIt is a subscription invoice with billing reason renewal.
CreemThe customer already had a payment before it.
RevenueCatIt arrives as a RENEWAL webhook event.
PaddleIts origin is subscription_recurring, subscription_update or subscription_charge.
WooCommerceIt is a WooCommerce Subscriptions renewal order.
ShopifyIts source is subscription_contract, a Shopify Subscriptions renewal.

Upgrades and plan changes count as renewals too, so they keep the first payment's channel: Stripe and Polar subscription_update, LemonSqueezy updated, and any later Dodo subscription payment. Expansion revenue stays with the channel that brought the customer.

Free trials start with nothing charged. Virling keeps the trial start apart from payments, never counting it as revenue, a payment or a customer, and uses it only for credit: a later payment of that customer with no visitor ID of its own is credited from the visits before the trial started.

What to check if renewals look off

  • Renewals show as Unattributed. The first payment was not attributed. Check that checkout carries the visitor ID. See Unattributed revenue.
  • Old subscriptions are Unattributed. The first payment must be in Virling. Payments older than the 90-day backfill are not imported, so renewals of subscriptions that started before that are Unattributed. Channel numbers fill in as new customers arrive.
  • Payments without a customer ID. They cannot be linked to a first payment.
  • A customer counts twice. Customers are matched per provider, so a customer who moved from one provider to another counts as two.

Frequently asked questions

How do I attribute subscription renewals to a channel?

Credit each renewal to the channel of the customer's first payment. A renewal has no visit of its own, so Virling matches it to the first payment by the provider's customer ID and copies that payment's channel, referrer, campaign, landing page, country and device.

Why do renewals show up as Direct or Unattributed in most tools?

A renewal is charged by the billing provider without anyone visiting your site. A tool that credits each payment to the visit before it finds no visit, so the renewal lands in Direct or Unattributed and the channel that won the customer looks smaller every month.

Which channel gets an upgrade?

The channel of the first payment. Upgrades and other plan changes count as renewals, so expansion revenue stays with the channel that brought the customer.

Why is a renewal still Unattributed in Virling?

The first payment was not attributed, it is older than the 90-day import, or the payment has no customer ID to link it to a first payment. Customers are also matched per provider, so a customer who moved providers starts over.

Does this work for free trials?

Yes. A trial start is never counted as revenue, a payment or a customer. Virling uses it only for credit: a later payment of that customer with no visitor ID of its own is credited from the visits before the trial started.
