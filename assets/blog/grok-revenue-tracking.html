Grok referral traffic: how to track traffic from Grok

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-19.

To see which Grok visits turn into paying customers, find the visits that arrive from grok.com, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and join each payment to the visit before it. You then see the revenue Grok referral traffic brings and what each Grok visitor is worth next to search, social and email. A visit count alone cannot tell you that, because the question is not how many people Grok sends but how many of them pay. One thing to know up front: Virling does not list Grok as an AI assistant yet, so its visits show up in the Referral channel, as grok.com.

This post covers Grok only. For assistants Virling counts under AI, see ChatGPT revenue tracking and DeepSeek revenue tracking; for AI assistants in general, see is AI search traffic worth anything.

How Grok visits show up

The AI channel covers a fixed list of assistant hosts: chatgpt.com, chat.openai.com, perplexity.ai, claude.ai, gemini.google.com, copilot.microsoft.com and chat.deepseek.com. grok.com is not on it. When a Grok answer links to your site and someone clicks, the visit can reach you in three ways.

  • The grok.com referrer. A click in the Grok web chat usually arrives with grok.com as the referrer. Any referrer host that is not a known AI, search, community or social host is Referral, so the visit lands there and shows as grok.com in the Referrer breakdown and in the Referral channel's sources, with the revenue credited to it. The referrer is a host only, so it never says which answer or question the click came from.
  • Another site's referrer. If Grok is used inside another app or site, the click carries that site's host, if any, and is classified by it. A visit referred by x.com, for example, is Social, credited to X.
  • No referrer. Links opened from a mobile app, or anywhere else the referrer is dropped, can arrive with none. Nothing in the visit names Grok, so it counts as Direct, in Virling and in most other tools.

So the grok.com row is a floor, not a full count, and it is not part of your AI total. When you compare assistants, add grok.com from Referral to the AI channel yourself. People who read an answer and later type your name into a browser or a search engine arrive as Direct or Organic search; no analytics tool can tie that visit to the answer. The full rule order is in channels, and the traffic source checker shows how a given landing URL and referrer are credited: enter grok.com as the referrer to see it land in Referral.

Tag the links you control

You cannot put the referrer back on a visit that arrived without one, but a utm_source keeps a visit out of Direct. Wherever you place a link meant for Grok users, such as a prompt you share or a page you expect to be quoted, tag it, for example ?utm_source=grok.com&utm_campaign=grok.

  • The source. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source. Neither grok nor grok.com is a known source, so a tagged visit counts as Referral, not AI, and not Direct.
  • The campaign. The Source tab is keyed by referrer host, so a tagged visit with no referrer does not join the grok.com row. Add utm_campaign=grok and those visits, with their revenue, show by name in the Campaign tab of the Channels card. The UTM builder makes the links.
  • A referrer wins. When the referrer is present it decides the channel, and the tag only names the campaign. A paid medium wins over both: utm_medium=cpc counts as Paid.

A "where did you hear about us" field at signup catches people who read a Grok answer and came back later. Keep it next to the dashboard number rather than mixing the two.

Set up Grok revenue tracking in Virling

  1. Add the tracking script to every page. See the tracking script. Nothing Grok-specific is needed: the referrer and the tags are read on every visit.
  2. Connect your payment provider: Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify or WooCommerce.
  3. Pass the visitor ID into checkout. Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links and Dodo Payments links get it from the script on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. See link checkout to visits. Without this step grok.com shows visitors and no revenue.
  4. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more. Short ranges with few visitors are noisy.
  5. In the Channels card, click Referral, or switch to the Source tab, and find grok.com. The row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average. If you tag links, check the Campaign tab for your Grok campaign too.
  6. Click the grok.com row to filter the whole page. The Pages card now shows which landing pages Grok sends people to, and which of them lead to payments.
  7. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

How Virling credits the payment

People often click a link in a Grok answer, leave, and come back days later by typing your URL. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the payment goes to the Grok visit, as long as it started inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. If the buyer subscribes, every renewal keeps counting toward grok.com. If they come back through a Google search or a newsletter before paying, that later visit takes the credit instead.

To see how often Grok starts journeys that another channel finishes, switch the model to first touch in Site settings. First-touch or last-touch attribution explains the difference. Changing the model recalculates every past payment, so you can switch back.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Grok is missing from the AI channel. That is expected today. Look in Referral, or in the Referrer breakdown, for grok.com.
  • grok.com has visitors but no revenue. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • Your tagged Grok links are not in the grok.com row. Tagged visits with no referrer have no referrer host, so they are not in the Source tab row. Find them by campaign in the Campaign tab.
  • A buyer says they found you in Grok but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have read the answer without clicking, or opened the link from an app that dropped the referrer. That visit has no trace of Grok.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted, so Grok's real visitor number can be higher than any analytics tool shows. Their revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Clicks that carry an x.com referrer belong to revenue from X, which also covers tagging your posts and bio. Two other assistants that land in Referral are Meta AI and Le Chat; add them by hand too when you compare assistants.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which Grok visits turn into paying customers?

Join each payment to the visit before it. In Virling, pass the visitor ID into checkout, then open the Referral channel, or the Referrer breakdown, and find grok.com: it shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. The Payments page shows each buyer's visits, with the credited one marked.

How do I track traffic from Grok?

Look for grok.com in your referrers. Virling does not list Grok as an AI assistant yet, so a visit with a grok.com referrer counts in the Referral channel and shows as grok.com, with the revenue it brought.

Why is Grok not in the AI channel?

The AI channel covers a fixed list of assistant hosts: chatgpt.com, chat.openai.com, perplexity.ai, claude.ai, gemini.google.com, copilot.microsoft.com and chat.deepseek.com. grok.com is not on it, so Grok visits are Referral. Add it up yourself when you compare AI traffic.

Why does some Grok traffic show up as Direct?

A visit with no referrer and no utm_source has nothing that names Grok, so it is Direct in Virling and in most other tools. That can happen when a link is opened from an app that drops the referrer. Treat the grok.com number as a floor.

Does a Grok visit still get credit if the buyer pays a week later?

With the default model, last non-direct, yes. If the buyer comes back by typing your URL, that Direct visit does not take the credit; the payment goes to the Grok visit as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default.
