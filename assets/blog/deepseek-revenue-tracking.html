DeepSeek referral traffic: which DeepSeek visits pay

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-19.

To see which DeepSeek visits turn into paying customers, count every visit that arrives from chat.deepseek.com or carries a DeepSeek utm_source, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and join each payment to the visit before it. You then see the revenue DeepSeek referral traffic brings and what each DeepSeek visitor is worth next to search, social and email. A visit count alone cannot tell you that, because the question is not how many people DeepSeek sends but how many of them pay.

This post covers DeepSeek only. For other assistants, see ChatGPT revenue tracking and Perplexity revenue tracking; for AI assistants in general, see is AI search traffic worth anything.

How DeepSeek visits show up

When a DeepSeek answer links to your site and someone clicks, the visit can reach you in three ways, and each one is classified differently.

  • The chat.deepseek.com referrer. A click in the DeepSeek web chat usually arrives with chat.deepseek.com as the referrer. Virling puts that host in the AI channel and lists it as DeepSeek. Only the chat host counts as AI: a visit referred by the deepseek.com homepage or its docs is a Referral, like any other website. The referrer is a host only, so it never says which answer or question the click came from.
  • A utm_source tag. When the referrer is missing, Virling reads utm_source. A visit tagged utm_source=chat.deepseek.com is credited to DeepSeek, in the AI channel, exactly as if the referrer had been there. A bare utm_source=deepseek is not recognized as an assistant and counts as Referral, so use the full host on any link you tag yourself. Virling does not add these tags.
  • No referrer and no tag. Links opened from the DeepSeek mobile apps, or anywhere else the referrer is dropped, can arrive with neither. Nothing in the visit names DeepSeek, so it counts as Direct, in Virling and in most other tools.

So the DeepSeek row is a floor, not a full count. People who read an answer and later type your name into a browser or a search engine arrive as Direct or Organic search; no analytics tool can tie that visit to the answer. The full rule order is in channels, and the traffic source checker shows how a given landing URL and referrer are credited: paste a URL with utm_source=chat.deepseek.com and an empty referrer to see it land in AI.

One exception to the order: a paid medium wins over everything. A link with utm_medium=cpc counts as Paid even if its source is chat.deepseek.com.

When the referrer is stripped

You cannot put the referrer back on a visit that arrived without one, but you can shrink the share of DeepSeek traffic that hides in Direct.

  • Tag the links you control. Wherever you place a link meant for DeepSeek users, such as a prompt you share or a page you expect to be quoted, add utm_source=chat.deepseek.com. Tagged visits keep their source even when the referrer is gone.
  • Ask buyers. A "where did you hear about us" field at signup catches people who read an answer and came back later. Keep it next to the dashboard number rather than mixing the two.
  • Watch Direct on the pages DeepSeek cites. If a page gets cited and its Direct visits rise with no other cause, some of that is likely untagged DeepSeek traffic. Treat it as a hint, not a number to add to the row.

Let DeepSeek read your pages

An assistant can only link to pages it can read. Virling recognizes the DeepSeekBot user agent as a training crawler: its fetches are stored apart from visits and never counted as visitors, and with the server snippet the AI card shows which of your pages it read. See AI crawlers.

The AI crawler checker reads your robots.txt and tells you which AI crawlers may read a page. DeepSeek's crawler is not in its list, so also check that your User-agent: * group does not block the pages you want cited.

Set up DeepSeek revenue tracking in Virling

  1. Add the tracking script to every page. See the tracking script. Nothing DeepSeek-specific is needed: the referrer and the tag are read on every visit.
  2. Connect your payment provider: Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify or WooCommerce.
  3. Pass the visitor ID into checkout. Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links and Dodo Payments links get it from the script on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. See link checkout to visits. Without this step DeepSeek shows visitors and no revenue.
  4. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more. Short ranges with few AI visitors are noisy.

  6. Look at the AI card. DeepSeek has its own row there, with the visitors and revenue it sent you next to the pages its crawler read. The Answers tab of the same card, which shows how often assistants use and name your site, covers ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Gemini; DeepSeek is not one of them yet. See AI answers.
  7. Click the DeepSeek row to filter the whole page. The Pages card now shows which landing pages DeepSeek sends people to, and which of them lead to payments.
  8. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

How Virling credits the payment

People often click a link in a DeepSeek answer, leave, and come back days later by typing your URL. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the payment goes to the DeepSeek visit, as long as it started inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. If the buyer subscribes, every renewal keeps counting toward DeepSeek. If they come back through a Google search or a newsletter before paying, that later visit takes the credit instead.

To see how often DeepSeek starts journeys that another channel finishes, switch the model to first touch in Site settings. First-touch or last-touch attribution explains the difference. Changing the model recalculates every past payment, so you can switch back.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • DeepSeek has visitors but no revenue. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • Your tagged DeepSeek links show as Referral. They probably use utm_source=deepseek. Change the tag to utm_source=chat.deepseek.com; past visits keep the channel they were given.
  • A buyer says they found you in DeepSeek but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have read the answer without clicking, or opened the link from an app that dropped the referrer. That visit has no trace of DeepSeek.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted, so DeepSeek's real visitor number can be higher than any analytics tool shows. Their revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Some assistants are not in the AI channel, so their visits sit in Referral when you compare them with DeepSeek: Le Chat visits show as chat.mistral.ai, and Grok visits show as grok.com.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which DeepSeek visits turn into paying customers?

Use a tool that groups chat.deepseek.com visits into their own source and joins each payment to the visit before it. In Virling, pass the visitor ID into checkout, then open the AI channel and the DeepSeek row: it shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, and the Payments page shows each buyer's visits.

How do I track DeepSeek referral traffic?

Look for chat.deepseek.com in your referrers. Virling puts every visit with a chat.deepseek.com referrer in the AI channel and lists it as DeepSeek. A visit with no referrer but utm_source=chat.deepseek.com counts the same way.

Why does some DeepSeek traffic show up as Direct?

A visit with no referrer and no utm_source has nothing that names DeepSeek, so it is Direct in Virling and in most other tools. That can happen when a link is opened from an app or browser that drops the referrer. Treat the DeepSeek number as a floor.

Why is utm_source=deepseek counted as Referral?

Virling only counts a bare source name as AI for the assistants it lists by name, and deepseek is not one of them. Tag your own links with utm_source=chat.deepseek.com instead; that matches the DeepSeek host and lands in the AI channel.

Does a DeepSeek visit still get credit if the buyer pays a week later?

With the default model, last non-direct, yes. If the buyer comes back by typing your URL, that Direct visit does not take the credit; the payment goes to the DeepSeek visit as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default.
