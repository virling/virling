Substack revenue tracking: which posts bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-19.

Substack revenue tracking means joining each payment on your own site to the Substack post or note that sent the buyer. Substack’s help center describes no tagging for links to other sites, so add the tags yourself: give every link in a post utm_source=substack.com, utm_medium=email and a utm_campaign named after the post, then pass the visitor ID into your checkout. Virling credits each payment to the tagged visit, and the Campaign tab shows revenue per post. Paid Substack subscriptions are a separate matter: they are taken on Substack, not on your checkout.

What Substack adds to your outbound links, and what it cannot

Substack counts clicks: its metrics guide reports how many openers clicked a link, with a count for each link. Its help center does not describe adding UTM tags to links that point to other sites, so plan as if a link reaches your page carrying only what you typed into it. Check it once: open a sent post in your inbox, click through, and read the address bar.

Some things Substack cannot add for you:

  • Your own script in a post. The editor accepts no custom HTML or CSS (Substack: custom CSS or HTML), so a post cannot carry a script of yours.
  • Tracking on your site. The Analytics section of your publication settings takes a Google Analytics, Tag Manager, Meta pixel or X pixel ID and loads it on your publication’s pages (Substack: the Analytics section). That measures Substack pages, not the checkout on your domain.
  • A view of the sale. Substack’s per-link counts end at the click.

Where a Substack reader comes from

  • Your publication page or a note on substack.com. The browser usually passes the Substack host as the referrer. substack.com and its subdomains are on Virling’s social list, so the visit counts as Social; substack.com shows as Substack in the Source tab, and yourname.substack.com gets its own row. A publication on a custom domain, which Substack connects for a one-time $50 fee (Substack: custom domains), sends that domain instead, and it lands in Referral.
  • The email. Mail apps rarely pass a referrer. An untagged click from the newsletter is therefore Direct, and so is any sale it leads to. The Substack app can behave the same way.
  • Any of them. A referrer names a site, never a post, so all your posts share one row until their links carry a campaign.

Background on visits with no referrer: dark social revenue tracking.

Linking to your site from posts

A post is one piece of content sent by email and published on the web, so one tagged link covers both. Keep source and medium fixed and vary the campaign:

https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=substack.com&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=pricing-teardown

  • utm_source=substack.com in every post. When a click has no referrer, a source naming a known host takes its place, so email clicks join web clicks under Substack.
  • utm_medium=email files the visit under Email whatever the referrer says, so web and inbox readers of a post count together.
  • utm_campaign set to the post’s slug, lowercase with hyphens, so you can match a row to a post months later.
  • utm_content if a post links twice to the same page, such as top-button and signoff.

Buttons count too, and so do the links on your About page and in your welcome email, which Substack sends outside any post. The free UTM builder writes the tagged URLs.

Linking to your site from notes

Notes live in the Substack feed and app, not in the inbox, so do not call them email. Drop utm_medium and give each note a campaign such as note-launch-day: a click from substack.com then counts as Social and the campaign is kept. When you write a post and a note about the same launch, give them different campaigns, so you can see which one sold.

Paid Substack subscriptions happen on Substack

A reader who pays for your publication pays on Substack. You connect a Stripe account under Settings, Payments (Substack: setting up Stripe), and Substack keeps 10% of subscription revenue on top of Stripe’s fees (Substack: how much it costs). That checkout never runs on your domain and carries no Virling visitor ID.

  • What Virling attributes is money taken on your own checkout: a product, a course, a SaaS plan or a store order that a Substack reader bought after clicking through.
  • If you connect Substack’s Stripe account to Virling, the subscription payments are imported but show as Unattributed. Substack suggests keeping that Stripe account separate from your other businesses, and keeping it out of Virling keeps your reports about your own sales.
  • For where paid subscribers came from, use Substack’s own stats, which list subscriber sources such as email clicks and the Substack network.

How Virling classifies a Substack visit

The first pageview of a session sets its channel. An email medium (email, newsletter, e-mail) wins and makes it Email. Failing that, a substack.com or subdomain referrer makes it Social, any other referrer makes it Referral, and with no referrer the utm_source decides. The campaign is saved in every case. Reference: how channels are decided.

A payment is then matched to the sessions that came before it. The default model gives the full amount, less refunds, to the last session in the 90 days before it that was not Direct. A reader who opens your post on Sunday and returns on Wednesday by bookmark is still credited to Sunday’s post. Reference: the attribution model.

See payments per post

1. Pick the channel

The Channels card shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor for each channel. Email holds tagged posts; Social holds notes and untagged clicks from substack.com. Clicking one filters the whole dashboard.

2. Open the Campaign tab

Each utm_campaign is a row, so every post and note has its own visitors and revenue. Sort by revenue per visitor: a post read by a few buyers can outearn one with a large audience.

3. Follow a payment

Click a post’s row to see its revenue over time. On the Payments page, open any payment to see each session before it and the one that took the credit.

Or use a tracked link per post

Instead of writing UTM tags by hand, make a tracked link for the post. On the dashboard, open the Campaign tab, click + Tracked link, choose Other and type substack.com as the source, then set the page and a name you will recognise, such as “Pricing teardown post”. A click redirects to your page with utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign added, so the visit lands in Social under Substack, and the Campaign tab shows the link under its name with its clicks, visitors and revenue. Frequently asked questions

How do I see which Substack post brought a paying customer?

Tag the links to your site in each post with utm_source=substack.com, utm_medium=email and a utm_campaign named after the post. Connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into your checkout. Virling then credits each payment to the tagged visit, and the Campaign tab lists revenue per post.

Does Substack add UTM tags to links in my posts?

Substack counts clicks on each link, but its help center does not describe adding UTM tags to links that point to other sites. Plan as if a link arrives with only the tags you typed, and check by clicking a link in a sent post and reading the address bar.

Can Virling attribute my paid Substack subscriptions?

Not to a visit. Readers pay for your publication on Substack, through the Stripe account you connect there, and that checkout carries no Virling visitor ID. If you connect that Stripe account to Virling the payments show as Unattributed. Virling attributes sales taken on your own checkout.

How should I tag links in Substack notes?

Leave out utm_medium, since notes are read in the Substack feed and app rather than the inbox, and give each note its own campaign such as note-launch-day. A click from substack.com then counts as Social with the campaign kept.

Why do some Substack clicks show as Social and others as Email?

A tagged link with utm_medium=email puts the visit in Email, whatever the referrer. Without a medium, a click from substack.com or your substack.com subdomain counts as Social, and a click from a custom domain counts as Referral. Notes, which you tag without a medium, show in Social.
