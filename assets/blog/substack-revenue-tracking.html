Substack revenue tracking: which posts bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-19.

Substack revenue tracking means joining each payment on your own site to the Substack post or note that sent the buyer. To see which post brought a paying customer, tag every link to your site in that post with utm_source=substack.com, utm_medium=email and a utm_campaign named after the post, then pass the visitor ID into checkout. Virling credits each payment to the tagged visit, so the Campaign tab shows revenue per post. Substack’s own stats count clicks on each link; they stop at the click and cannot see the payment that came after.

How Substack traffic reaches your site

The same post reaches readers in two ways, and they arrive differently:

  • From the web. A reader who clicks a link on your publication’s page or in a note on substack.com usually arrives with that host as the referrer. Virling has substack.com and its subdomains on its list of social hosts, so these clicks land in the Social channel. substack.com shows as Substack in the Source tab, and a subdomain such as yourname.substack.com is its own row under that host. A publication on a custom domain, which Substack supports, is not on the list, so its clicks land in Referral under that domain.
  • From the email. Most subscribers read the post in an email app, and most email apps send no referrer when a link is clicked. With no referrer and no tag, the visit has nothing that says it came from Substack, so it is counted as Direct, and so is the payment that follows. Links opened inside a mobile app can arrive the same way.
  • Only the host. Even when the referrer is present, it is a host, not a post. Every post on your publication shares one row. Tags on the links are how you tell posts apart.

For more on visits that arrive with no referrer, see dark social revenue tracking.

Tag the links in each post

Use one source and one medium in every post, and change only the campaign:

https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=substack.com&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=pricing-teardown

  • utm_source=substack.com: the same value in every post. A source that names a known host stands in for a missing referrer, so email clicks join the same Substack row as web clicks. The short name substack still puts the visit in Social, but not in that row.
  • utm_medium=email: puts the visit in the Email channel, whatever the referrer says. A link in a post is the same link in the email and on the web, so readers from both count as Email.
  • utm_campaign=pricing-teardown: one value per post, lowercase, with hyphens. Use the post’s slug so you can find it later.
  • utm_content: optional, to tell two links in the same post apart, such as intro and footer.

For notes, leave out utm_medium and use a campaign such as note-launch-day. A click from substack.com then lands in Social, and its campaign is still recorded. Tag every link to your site, not only the main one, including the one in your about page and your welcome email. The free UTM builder makes the tagged URLs for you.

After you publish, open a link from the email in your own inbox and check the address bar on your site. The tags that arrive there are the ones Virling reads.

How Virling sorts a Substack visit

Every session gets one channel from its first pageview, and utm_medium is checked first. A medium of email, newsletter or e-mail is the Email channel. Without one, a referrer from substack.com or a subdomain is Social, any other referrer is Referral, and a visit with no referrer is sorted by its utm_source. The campaign is stored with the visit either way. See how channels are decided.

When a payment comes in, it is joined to the visits before it. Under the default model the whole payment, after refunds, is credited to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct, and to that visit’s channel, source, campaign and landing page. A reader who clicks your post on Monday and comes back on Thursday by typing your address is still credited to the post. See the attribution model.

See payments per post

1. Open the channel

On the dashboard, the Channels card lists each channel with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor. Click Email for tagged posts, or Social for notes and untagged web clicks, and the whole page filters to those visits.

2. Switch to the Campaign tab

The Campaign tab on the same card has one row per utm_campaign, so each post and note shows its own visitors and revenue. Compare posts by revenue per visitor, not by visitors: a niche post read by a few buyers can be worth more than a popular one read by many who never pay.

3. Open the payments

Click a post to filter the dashboard to it and see its revenue over time. The Payments page lists every payment with its source; open one to see the journey: the sessions before the payment and which one got the credit.

Or use a tracked link per post

Instead of writing UTM tags by hand, make a tracked link for the post. On the dashboard, open the Campaign tab, click + Tracked link, choose Other and type substack.com as the source, then set the page and a name you will recognise, such as “Pricing teardown post”. A click redirects to your page with utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign added, so the visit lands in Social under Substack, and the Campaign tab shows the link under its name with its clicks, visitors and revenue. What to check if the numbers look off

  • Substack revenue still shows as Direct. A link was left untagged, or the tags were lost in a redirect before your page. Click a link from a sent post and check the address bar.
  • Posts are split across rows. Values differ in case or spelling, such as Pricing-Teardown and pricing-teardown. Pick one format and keep it.
  • Revenue shows no channel at all. The payment was not matched to a visit. Pass the visitor ID into checkout; see link checkout to visits.
  • A recent post looks weak. Readers often pay days or weeks later. Give it time, and check the date range, since a sale is credited on the day of the payment.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which Substack post brought a paying customer?

Tag the links to your site in each post with utm_source=substack.com, utm_medium=email and a utm_campaign named after the post. Connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Each payment is then credited to the tagged visit, and the Campaign tab shows revenue per post.

Why do clicks from my Substack emails show as Direct?

Most email apps send no referrer when a reader clicks a link. Without UTM tags the visit has nothing that says it came from Substack, so it is counted as Direct, and so is the payment that follows.

Which channel does Substack traffic land in?

A click from a Substack web page carries the substack.com host, or your publication's subdomain, as the referrer, and lands in Social. A publication on a custom domain is not on the known list, so its clicks land in Referral under that domain. A utm_medium of email or newsletter puts the visit in Email, whatever the referrer says.

Should I use utm_source=substack or substack.com?

Use substack.com. When a click arrives with no referrer, a source that names a known host stands in for the referrer, so the visit joins the same Substack row as clicks that kept their referrer. The short name substack still puts the visit in Social, but not in that row.

Do Substack's own stats show which post brought a sale on my site?

No. Substack's Posts page shows how many readers clicked each link, but it cannot see what happened on your site after the click. Joining the click to the payment that followed is what Virling does.
