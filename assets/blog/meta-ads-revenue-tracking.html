Meta Ads revenue tracking: see which campaign brings paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-24.

Meta Ads revenue tracking means joining each payment on your site to the Facebook or Instagram ad click that brought the buyer. Add utm_source=facebook, utm_medium=paid_social and a utm_campaign to the URL parameters of your ads, add the Virling script to your site, and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Virling counts the click as Paid and credits the payment to it, so the Campaign tab shows payments and revenue per campaign. Ads Manager shows the purchases Meta attributes to its ads; your payment provider shows the money you received.

Step 1: tag the ad

Put this in the URL parameters field of each ad:

utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=paid_social&utm_campaign={{campaign.id}}

Meta fills in the part in double braces when the ad is clicked. Its developer docs list {{campaign.id}}, {{adset.id}}, {{ad.id}}, {{campaign.name}}, {{adset.name}} and {{ad.name}} (source: Meta, Use URL Tags for Tracking). The names come with a catch: Meta saves them when you first publish and keeps using the saved values, so renaming a campaign later does not change the tag. The ID stays the same when you rename the campaign, which makes it the steadier choice.

A plain name that you type, such as spring-sale, works too and is easier to read in a report. Pick one style and keep it, because a campaign tagged two ways becomes two rows. You can add utm_content={{ad.id}}, which Virling stores with the pageview, but reports group by campaign. The free UTM builder makes tagged URLs for you.

One ad can run on both Facebook and Instagram and carries the same tag in both places. If you want to compare the two, give each its own campaign with its own utm_campaign.

What about the fbclid?

Meta’s docs describe the click ID as a value Meta generates and passes with your site’s URL when someone clicks an ad on Facebook or Instagram (source: Meta, fbp and fbc parameters). It arrives as the fbclid parameter and is useful to Meta. Virling does not store it or use it to pick the channel. It only checks whether it is there, so a Direct visit can be labelled a shared link. It does not name your campaign, so leave it on and add UTM tags. Attribution that depends on a click ID surviving in the URL is the kind a browser privacy feature can break; see iOS Link Tracking Protection and UTM parameters.

How Virling sorts a Meta ad visit

Every session gets one channel from its first pageview, and the first matching rule wins:

  • A paid medium. cpc, ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social or paidsocial, in any case, is Paid. It is checked first, so a tagged click from instagram.com is Paid, not Social.
  • No paid medium, with a referrer. facebook.com and instagram.com and their subdomains are Social, so the ad is mixed in with your posts.
  • No referrer, with a source tag. facebook, fb, instagram and ig are Social. A value that is not a known source is Referral.
  • No referrer and no tag. The visit is Social when the in-app browser names the app, and Direct when it does not.

Spelling counts. paid-social with a hyphen and cpm are not on the list, so those clicks are not Paid. See how channels are decided.

In-app browsers

Many ad clicks open inside the Facebook or Instagram app, where links often arrive with no referrer. Tags travel in the URL, so a tagged ad is Paid either way. Cookies are the other half: an in-app browser can keep its cookies apart from the phone’s main browser. A buyer who taps the ad in the app and pays later in another browser, or on a laptop, can have two visitor IDs, and the payment is credited to the visits of the one that reached checkout. See cross-device attribution for what can be joined.

Step 2: add the script

Add the Virling script to the <head> of every page the ad can land on. It reads the tags on the landing page and sets the first-party visitor ID. Redirects on your site must keep the query string; see UTM parameters lost after redirect. The setup is in the script docs.

Step 3: link checkout

Connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout, so each payment can be matched to a visit. Payment links from Stripe, Polar, Dodo Payments and LemonSqueezy get the ID from the script when they are on a page that runs it; a checkout you create on your server needs the virling_vid cookie. See link checkout to visits.

Under the default model, the whole payment, after refunds, is credited to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct. Someone who clicks your ad on Monday and types your address on Thursday is still credited to the ad. See the attribution model.

Step 4: read payments per campaign

  • In the Channels card, click Paid. The page filters to visits with a paid medium, from Meta and any other platform you tag the same way.
  • Open the Campaign tab. Each utm_campaign is a row with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  • Open a payment from the Payments page to see the visits before it.
  • Check Time to pay before you pause a campaign that looks weak.

To see profit, type what you spend on ads each month in Site settings, under Monthly spend per channel, such as Paid 1200. The Profit by channel card then shows spend, revenue and profit for the Paid channel as a whole, so include every platform you tag as Paid. Nothing connects to an ad platform, and the result is only as good as the amount you type.

A made-up example

These numbers are invented to show the arithmetic. Two campaigns each cost $600, as read in Ads Manager. Campaign A has 400 visitors and 12 payments worth $1,200, so $3.00 per visitor and $50 of spend per payment. Campaign B has 900 visitors and 9 payments worth $900, so $1.00 per visitor and about $66.67 per payment. At a 60% margin with one $100 order per customer, the break-even cost per customer is $60, so A is above water and B is not, even though B brought more visitors. Work out your own limit with the break-even CPA calculator.

Why ad-reported purchases differ from payments

  • Different event. Meta’s pixel reference describes Purchase as fired when a purchase is made or a checkout flow is completed, with a value and currency your site sends (source: Meta Pixel reference). It is an event your site reports. A payment is recorded by your provider, after the charge, and Virling takes refunds off.
  • Views count too. Meta’s docs list attribution windows such as 7 days after clicking an ad, 1 day after viewing it and 1 day after an engaged view (source: Meta, Ads Action Stats). A view with no click never reaches your site, so it cannot be credited to a visit.
  • Different window and credit. Virling looks back 90 days by default and gives a payment to one visit. If the buyer clicked an ad and later came from your newsletter, the default model credits the newsletter, while Meta can still count the ad.
  • Different visitors. A buyer who changed browser, or blocked the script, may be in one count and missing from the other.

What to check if the numbers look off

  • Ad revenue shows as Social. The ad has no paid utm_medium. Click your own ad preview and check the address bar.
  • Ad revenue shows as Direct. The tags were dropped by a redirect, or the ad has none and the app did not name itself.
  • A campaign row shows braces. If {{campaign.id}} appears as typed, check the spelling and that it sits in the ad’s URL parameters.
  • Revenue shows as Unattributed. The visitor ID is not reaching checkout.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which Meta ad campaign brought a paying customer?

Add utm_source=facebook, utm_medium=paid_social and utm_campaign={{campaign.id}} to the URL parameters of your ads. Connect your payment provider and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Each payment is credited to the tagged visit, and the Campaign tab shows revenue per campaign.

Does Virling read the fbclid?

Only to see whether it is there: a Direct visit with an fbclid can be labelled a shared link. Virling does not store its value or use it to pick the channel. It reads utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_content, utm_term and ref from the landing URL. The fbclid can stay on the link for Meta.

Which channel does a Facebook or Instagram ad click land in?

Paid, if the link carries utm_medium=paid_social, paidsocial, paid, cpc, ppc or paidsearch. The medium is checked first, so it wins over a Facebook or Instagram referrer. Without a paid medium, the click is usually Social, or Direct if nothing names the app.

Why does Meta report more purchases than my payment provider?

Meta counts Purchase events within its attribution windows, which can include people who saw an ad without clicking it. A payment is money your provider recorded. The two count different things, so they will rarely match.

Does Virling connect to my Meta ad account?

No. The setup on this page uses only the tags on your ads, the Paid channel and your payments. You can type what you spend on Paid each month in Site settings, and the Profit by channel card uses that amount.
