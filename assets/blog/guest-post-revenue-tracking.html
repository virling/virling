Guest post revenue tracking: which articles bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

To track revenue from guest posts, give each publishing site its own utm_source and each article its own utm_campaign, send the bio link and the in-body link to different pages, and ask the editor in writing to keep your tags. With a payment provider connected and the visitor ID passed into checkout, each article becomes a row you can rank by the customers it brought. Page views, shares and domain authority do not tell you which posts paid.

A guest post costs a pitch, a draft, a round of edits and some follow-up. Most founders write several before they know which sites and topics are worth repeating. The aim here is a table where those posts sit side by side, judged by payments.

Author bio links and in-body links

A guest post usually gives you two kinds of link, and readers use them differently.

  • The author bio. One line about you at the top or bottom of the post, read by people who finished the article or wondered who wrote it. Point it at your homepage or signup page.
  • Links in the body. A link inside a paragraph, next to the problem the article is about. Point it at the page that matches that paragraph, such as a feature page or a free tool, not the homepage. Many editors allow one or two, and some allow none.

Both links carry the same source and campaign, so the article is one row in the Campaign tab. Because they open different pages, you can still tell them apart: click the article's campaign to filter the page, then open the Landing page tab on the Pages card. The homepage row is the bio, the feature page row is the body link.

Bio:  https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=exampleblog&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=exampleblog-churn-guide
Body: https://yourdomain.com/features/churn?utm_source=exampleblog&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=exampleblog-churn-guide

Keep utm_medium=referral even when the site also sends the post in its newsletter. A medium of email or newsletter would move every web click to the Email channel. The UTM builder makes both links.

Ask the editor to keep the UTM tags

Editors tidy links. A house style may ban query strings, a CMS may strip them, or someone may swap your link for a clean URL during the edit. Say what you need before it happens:

  • In the submission email, write one line such as: "The two links to our site carry tracking tags so we can see which readers become customers. Please keep them as they are."
  • Paste the links in the draft exactly as they should go live, not as plain text you expect them to rewrite.
  • If the site will not publish query strings, offer a tracked link instead: in Virling, open Links, set the source to the site's name, the channel to Referral, and the page it should open. It is a short URL with no visible tags, which adds them on the click. Its id becomes the campaign, so make one per article. See tracked links.
  • After the post goes live, click both links yourself and check that each visit shows with the right campaign and landing page.

rel attributes on your links

Publishers often add a rel attribute to outbound links. Most of them are for search engines and do not change what Virling sees:

  • nofollow, sponsored and ugc tell search engines how to treat the link. The click still arrives with the referrer and your tags, and is counted the same way.
  • noopener on its own does not hide the referrer.
  • noreferrer does. The click arrives with no referrer, so without a tag it is Direct. With a tag, Virling reads utm_source instead and the article keeps its row. A site-wide referrer policy of no-referrer has the same effect.

That is the practical reason to fight for tags: on a site that uses noreferrer, an untagged guest post is invisible. When the referrer does survive, most blogs land in Referral under their own host, dev.to and Hashnode land in Communities, and Medium and Substack land in Social; channels lists every host. Paste the live link into the traffic source checker to confirm.

Compare several posts by payments

Readers of a guest post often meet you there for the first time and pay weeks later, after a trial or after coming back through a search. Two settings matter. The range: Last 90 days, or a custom range starting at the oldest post you are comparing. The model: the default, last non-direct, lets a later search take the credit, while first touch keeps it on the article that introduced the buyer. Switch it in Site settings, read the table both ways, and switch back; history is recomputed each time. First touch or last touch for a SaaS helps pick one.

In the Campaign tab each article is a row with visitors, revenue, revenue per visitor and a chip against your site average. Click a row and the New customers number at the top counts the buyers that article brought. Line the posts up by new customers and revenue per visitor, not by visitors.

A made-up example: three guest posts

Example only: made-up numbers for an imaginary SaaS on a $49 monthly plan, not results from a real business. Last 90 days, first touch, first payments only, site average $1.10 per visitor:

  • exampleblog-churn-guide: 640 visitors, 6 new customers, $294, so $0.46 per visitor. Landing pages show 4 of the 6 came through the body link to the feature page.
  • examplemag-pricing-essay: 2,300 visitors, 2 new customers, $98, so $0.04 per visitor. A popular essay whose readers were not shopping.
  • exampledev-api-tutorial: 180 visitors, 3 new customers, $147, so $0.82 per visitor, the closest to the site average.

The post with the most readers earned the least per visitor, and the small tutorial earned the most. The next pitch goes to the tutorial's site, on a topic close to it, with a body link to a matching feature page. For a placement you paid for, put its visitors, revenue and price into the revenue per visitor calculator.

When an article's numbers look off

  • The site's host shows in Referral but the campaign does not. The editor removed your tags or replaced the link. Ask for the tagged link on the next post, and read this one by host.
  • Two articles on one site share a row. Untagged clicks group by host only, so they cannot be split. Tag the next one.
  • Direct jumps on the day a post goes live. The site likely uses noreferrer; why Direct is your biggest source covers how to read it.
  • Visits but no revenue for every article. Check Matched revenue under the Overview numbers; a low share means the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.

For stories and mentions you did not write, see whether press coverage brings customers. If your bio links to something free, measure what it leads to: lead magnet ROI covers downloads and revenue by free tool covers calculators and generators.

Frequently asked questions

Should the author bio and the in-body link use different UTM tags?

Keep the same utm_source and utm_campaign on both, so the article is one row. Point them at different pages, and the Landing page tab, filtered to that campaign, shows which link brought each visitor.

How do I ask an editor to keep my UTM tags?

Say it in the submission email in one line, paste the links in the draft exactly as they should go live, and offer a tracked short link from Virling if the site does not publish query strings.

Does a nofollow or sponsored link still get tracked?

Yes. nofollow, sponsored and ugc are instructions for search engines; the click still carries the referrer and your tags. Only noreferrer, or a no-referrer policy on the site, hides the referrer, and then the UTM tag is what keeps the visit credited to the article.

How do I compare several guest posts?

Give each article its own utm_campaign, pick the last 90 days, and read the Campaign tab: visitors, new customers, revenue and revenue per visitor per article, with a chip against your site average. Try first touch too, since articles often introduce buyers who pay later.

What if a site links to me without tags?

The click is still counted under the site's host in the Source tab, usually in Referral, as long as the referrer survives. Every untagged article on that site shares the row, and a click with no referrer is Direct.
