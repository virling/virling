Guest post revenue tracking: which articles bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

To track revenue from guest posts, give each site that publishes you its own utm_source, give each article its own utm_campaign, carry the visitor ID into checkout, and join each payment to the visit that came before it. Mentions you cannot tag still show up under the site's host as a referrer. Then compare paying customers and revenue per visitor for each article over 90 days. Page views, shares and domain authority do not tell you this.

Guest posts, roundup listings and press mentions take real time: pitching, writing, editing and follow-up. Some bring a steady trickle of buyers for months; others bring a spike of readers who never sign up. The question is the same for each one: did anyone who clicked through from it become a customer, and how does that compare with your other articles and your site average?

How a click from an article is credited

Virling gives every session one channel, from its first pageview. A click from an article can be credited in two ways.

  • The referrer. A click in a web browser usually arrives with the publishing site's host as the referrer. Most blogs, magazines and news sites land in the Referral channel and show under their own host in the Source tab. Articles on dev.to, hashnode.com and hashnode.dev are known community hosts, so they land in Communities, and articles on medium.com or substack.com land in Social. The referrer is a host only, so two articles on the same site share one row.
  • A UTM tag. Some sites add rel="noreferrer" to outbound links or set a policy that hides the referrer, and links opened from apps or PDFs often carry none. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, and when that is missing it reads ref. Any value that is not a known search, social, community or AI source lands in Referral. With no referrer and no tag, the visit is Direct.

A utm_medium of email or newsletter moves the visit to Email, and cpc or paid moves it to Paid, before the referrer is checked. The full rule order is in channels, and you can paste any link into the traffic source checker to see where it lands.

Step by step setup

1. One UTM source per site, one campaign per article

Give each publishing site one utm_source and keep it for every article you place there. Give each article its own utm_campaign, so two posts on the same site are two rows in the Campaign tab:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=exampleblog&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=exampleblog-onboarding-guide
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=exampleblog&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=exampleblog-pricing-teardown
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=examplenews&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=examplenews-launch-coverage
  • utm_source names the site, lowercase, one word. Keep the same value for every article on that site.
  • utm_medium=referral on all of them. It keeps the channel decided by the referrer or source. Do not use email or newsletter here, even when the article also goes out in the site's newsletter, or every click from the web page counts as Email.
  • utm_campaign starts with the site's name and adds a short name for the article. Keep it stable once the article is live.

The UTM builder makes each link, and which UTM parameters to use covers the naming rules.

2. Ask for tagged links

In a guest post you write the links yourself, so put the tagged link in the author bio and in any link to your product in the body. For roundups and press, the editor or journalist copies whatever link you hand them, so make that link the tagged one:

  • Put the tagged link in the pitch email, the press kit and the boilerplate paragraph about your company.
  • When a roundup asks for a product URL in a form, paste the tagged link there.
  • If a site strips query strings or the editor wants a clean URL, use a tracked link instead: in Virling, open Links, set the source to the site's name, the channel to Referral, and the page it should open. The short link adds the tags on the click, and its id becomes the campaign, so make one link per article. See tracked links.

3. Referrer-only mentions you cannot tag

Many mentions happen without you: a writer adds you to a list, or a news piece links your homepage. You cannot tag those links, but you can still measure them. As long as the click carries a referrer, it shows under the site's host in Referral, or in Communities for the community hosts above. Open the Source tab, expand Referral, and each host is a row with visitors and revenue.

Two limits apply. Every untagged article on one site shares that host's row, so you cannot split them. And a mention that sends no referrer, because of rel="noreferrer", a privacy setting or an app, arrives as Direct. A sudden rise in Direct on the day an article goes live is often that article; why Direct is your biggest source covers how to read it.

4. First-touch versus last-touch credit

An article is often the first time a buyer hears of you. They read it, leave, and come back days later through a search or a newsletter before they pay. Which visit gets the payment depends on the model:

  • Last non-direct, the default, credits the latest visit that was not Direct inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. A buyer who clicked your guest post and later typed your URL is credited to the guest post. One who came back through a search is credited to search.
  • First touch credits the earliest visit in the window. It shows which articles introduce buyers, which is usually the point of a guest post.
  • Last touch credits the visit right before the payment, Direct included.

Change the model in Site settings; history is recomputed right away, and you can switch back. First touch or last touch for a SaaS explains which one fits your sales cycle, and the model and lookback window has the details.

5. Set up Virling

  1. Add the script. Put the Virling tag in the <head> of every page, including the pages your articles link to. See add the script.
  2. Connect a payment provider. Virling reads payments from Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce. Revenue attribution for SaaS covers how trials and renewals are credited.
  3. Pass the visitor ID into checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. See link checkout to visits. Without this step you get visits but no revenue.
  4. Check one link. Click a tagged link yourself and check that the visit shows on the dashboard with the right source and campaign.

Compare articles by paying customers over 90 days

Guest post traffic comes in a spike on the day an article goes live, then a long tail from search and from people sharing it. SaaS buyers often sign up, try the product and pay weeks later. Judge each article over a long range.

  1. Pick Last 90 days in the date picker, or a custom range that starts when the article went live.
  2. On the Channels card, switch to the Campaign tab. Each article you tagged is a row with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, and a chip that compares it with your site average: green above it, red below it.
  3. Click an article's row to filter the whole page. The New customers number at the top is now the paying customers that article brought in the range, and the revenue chart shows when its money arrived.
  4. Write down visitors, new customers, revenue and revenue per visitor for each article, then clear the filter and note your site's revenue per visitor for the same range.
  5. Switch to the Source tab and open Referral and Communities to see untagged mentions by host.
  6. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

Then read the table. An article with paying customers and revenue per visitor near or above your average tells you which sites and topics to pitch again. One with many visitors and no customers reached the wrong readers. If you paid for a placement, put its visitors, revenue and price into the revenue per visitor calculator to see its return. What is revenue per visitor explains why it is the number to compare.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • A site shows visitors in the Source tab but not in the Campaign tab. The article links to your plain URL, or the site removed the query string. Ask for the tagged link next time, or use a tracked link.
  • One article is split across rows. The campaign was spelled differently on different links. Pick one value and keep it.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A customer says they found you in an article but the payment is credited elsewhere. A later non-direct visit took the credit, or they paid from another device. Switch to first touch to see which articles introduce buyers.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so an article's real visitor number can be higher than what any analytics tool shows. Their revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

For stories and mentions you did not write, see whether press coverage brings customers. If your bio links to something free instead of your homepage, measure what it leads to: lead magnet ROI covers downloads and revenue by free tool covers calculators and generators.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from a guest post?

Link to your site with utm_source set to the publishing site's name, utm_medium=referral and a utm_campaign for the article. Add the tracking script to your site and pass the visitor ID into checkout. Each article is then a row in the Campaign tab with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.

What if a roundup or press article links to me without UTM tags?

The click still arrives with the site's host as the referrer, so it shows under that host in the Referral channel of the Source tab. Two articles on the same site share that row, because the referrer is a host only. If the link carries no referrer at all, the visit is Direct.

How do I ask a journalist or editor for a tagged link?

Put the tagged link in the pitch, the press kit and the bio you send, so it is the link they copy. Keep it short, or use a tracked short link from Virling, which adds the tags on the click. Do not ask them to change a link after publishing unless it is broken.

Should I use first touch or last touch for guest posts?

Articles often introduce buyers who pay later after coming back another way. The default, last non-direct, credits the latest visit that was not Direct. Switch the model to first touch in Site settings to see which articles introduce buyers; history is recomputed, and you can switch back.

How long should I wait before judging a guest post?

About 90 days. An article keeps sending a trickle of visitors for months, and SaaS buyers often pay weeks after their first visit, when a trial ends. Keep the lookback window, 90 days by default, longer than your trial plus decision time.
