Bluesky traffic analytics: track sales from Bluesky posts

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-18.

To see which Bluesky posts bring paying customers, you need three things: visits that arrive from bsky.app or go.bsky.app counted as Bluesky, links in your posts and profile tagged with utm_source=bsky.app and a campaign per post so clicks without a referrer still count, and each visit joined to the payment that followed by a visitor ID carried into checkout. Then you can read the revenue, and the revenue per visitor, that Bluesky brings. Likes, reposts and follower counts do not tell you this.

Plenty of founders and indie makers now post launches and progress on Bluesky. Whether that brings sales comes down to one question: did anyone who clicked through from Bluesky pay, and how much was each Bluesky visitor worth compared with your other channels?

How Bluesky visits arrive

A click from Bluesky reaches your site in one of four ways, and Virling handles each one differently.

  • bsky.app as the referrer. Clicks from Bluesky in a web browser usually arrive with bsky.app as the referrer. Virling puts bsky.app in the Social channel and names it Bluesky.
  • go.bsky.app as the referrer. Some clicks pass through go.bsky.app, a Bluesky link address, on their way to you. Virling treats it as Bluesky too and merges it into the same row as bsky.app, so you see one Bluesky source, not two.
  • No referrer. Links opened from an app often arrive with no referrer at all. Virling can credit some apps from the user agent of their in-app browser, such as Instagram or LinkedIn, but Bluesky is not one of them. An untagged click with no referrer is counted as Direct. The same goes for a Bluesky client that sends no referrer, while one that sends its own web address as the referrer is counted as Referral under that address.
  • A UTM tag. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, or ref when it is missing. bsky.app is read as the Bluesky host, so the visit lands in Social and in the same Bluesky row as referrer clicks.

A referrer always wins over the tag, so a tagged click from the web still shows as bsky.app. The tag matters for the clicks that arrive with nothing else. The full rule order is in channels.

Virling adds up visitors and revenue from both Bluesky hosts in one row of its Source tab, and compares that row's revenue per visitor with your site average.

Tag your profile and post links

The referrer only says bsky.app. It cannot tell your profile link from a thread or a launch post, because Virling stores the referrer host, not the full URL. Tag each place with its own campaign:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=bsky.app&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=profile
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=bsky.app&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=post-launch-v2
  • utm_source=bsky.app for every Bluesky link. bluesky also counts as Social, but it gets no source row of its own next to bsky.app. bsky is not a known source, so it would count as Referral.
  • utm_medium=social for posts, replies, your profile and DMs. Anything except a paid or email medium keeps the visit in Social.
  • utm_campaign names the place: one per post you want to measure, and a fixed one for links that stay put, such as the link on your profile.

More naming rules are in which UTM parameters to use, and the UTM builder makes the link for you. If you post the same link on X or LinkedIn, the social UTM generator makes one per platform; change its Bluesky source from bluesky to bsky.app. If a long tagged URL looks messy in a post, use a tracked link instead: in Virling, open Links, pick Other as the place, type bsky.app as the source and choose the page it should open. The short link adds the source, a social medium and its own campaign on the click, and the Campaign tab lists it with its visitors and revenue. See tracked links.

Make sure payments can be credited

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs the visitor ID in checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Every provider is covered in link checkout to visits.

Few people buy on the first click from a post. With the default model, last non-direct, someone who clicked your post on Monday and came back on Friday by typing your URL is still credited to Bluesky, as long as the Bluesky visit is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. If they subscribe, every renewal keeps counting toward Bluesky.

Which Bluesky posts bring paying customers?

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more, so slow buyers have time to pay.
  2. In the Channels card, open Social, or switch to the Source tab, and find bsky.app. The row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click the row to filter the whole page. The Pages card now shows which landing pages Bluesky visitors arrived on.
  4. Switch to the Campaign tab to split Bluesky by post and by profile link, if you tagged them.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.
  6. Check the Retention page after a month or two. It groups customers by the channel and referrer of their first payment, so you can see whether Bluesky customers keep paying.

A post often introduces a buyer who later comes back through search or a typed URL. Last-touch models can undercount that. Switch the model to first touch in Site settings and compare the Bluesky row under both; history is recomputed, and you can switch back. First-touch or last-touch attribution for a SaaS explains when each one fits. If you also post on X, X (Twitter) revenue tracking covers the same steps there, so you can compare the two side by side. If you are on the fediverse as well, Mastodon traffic by instance explains why those clicks come from many domains.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Bluesky looks small but Direct jumped the day a post took off. App clicks on untagged links arrived with no referrer. Tag the profile link and the next post. Links passed on in private messages and chats land in Direct the same way, as covered in dark social revenue tracking.
  • A Bluesky campaign shows under Referral. The tag probably says bsky or another unknown source. Use bsky.app.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A buyer says they found you on Bluesky but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have read your posts without clicking. No analytics tool can count a view that never reached your site.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so Bluesky's real visitor number can be higher than what any analytics tool shows. Revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track sales from Bluesky?

Count visits with bsky.app or go.bsky.app as the referrer, tag the links you post with utm_source=bsky.app and a campaign per post, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and use a tool that joins payments to visits. Then read Bluesky's revenue and revenue per visitor next to your other channels.

Why do clicks from Bluesky show up as Direct?

Links opened from an app often arrive with no referrer. Virling does not recognize the Bluesky app from its user agent, so an untagged click with no referrer is Direct. A utm_source on the link keeps it credited to Bluesky.

What utm_source should I use for Bluesky?

Use bsky.app. Virling reads it as the Bluesky host, so tagged clicks land in the Social channel and in the same Bluesky row as referrer clicks. bluesky also counts as Social but gets no source row of its own, and bsky is not a known source, so it counts as Referral.

What is go.bsky.app in my referrers?

It is a Bluesky link address that some clicks pass through on the way to your site. Virling treats it as Bluesky and merges it into one row with bsky.app.

Can I see which Bluesky post sent a paying customer?

Not from the referrer, which is only a host such as bsky.app. Give each post its own utm_campaign, or its own tracked link, and the Campaign tab shows visitors and revenue per post.
