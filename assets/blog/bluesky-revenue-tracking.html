Bluesky traffic analytics: track sales from Bluesky posts

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-18, updated 2026-11-26.

Bluesky can bring buyers, but the platform's own numbers will not show it. Likes and follows stop at the edge of Bluesky. To see revenue, Virling needs to recognise a visit as Bluesky, from a bsky.app or go.bsky.app referrer or from a utm_source=bsky.app tag on the link, and then join that visit to a payment through the visitor ID your checkout receives. Once both work, Bluesky gets a row with its own revenue and revenue per visitor.

Reach on Bluesky works differently from an algorithmic timeline. A post spreads through people's Following feed, through custom feeds that anyone can build around a topic, and through starter packs that recommend accounts to new users. Each of these can put your link in front of people who would never have found it otherwise, and each looks the same once the click reaches your site.

bsky.app and go.bsky.app in your referrers

Someone reading Bluesky in a desktop or mobile browser who clicks your link usually arrives with bsky.app as the referrer. Virling files bsky.app under Social and shows it as Bluesky in the Source tab.

go.bsky.app is Bluesky's own short-link host, best known from shared starter pack links. When it does show up as a referrer, Virling merges it into the bsky.app row, so Bluesky stays a single line rather than two partial ones.

What the referrer cannot tell you is where on Bluesky the click happened. Virling keeps the host only, so a click from your profile, from the Following feed, from a custom feed or from a thread several replies deep all read as bsky.app.

Custom feeds and starter packs

Custom feeds pick up posts by topic, keyword or whatever rule their creator sets, so a post about your product can be read by people who do not follow you. Clicks from those readers are real Bluesky traffic and land in the bsky.app row, but nothing in the referrer says which feed carried the post. If a post does unusually well, the honest reading is "this post reached further than usual", not "feed X sent these buyers".

Starter packs work one step removed. Being added to one brings followers, and those followers then see your posts in their timeline. The pack itself rarely sends clicks to your site; it grows the audience that later does. To see whether it paid off, compare Bluesky revenue and new customers for a few weeks before and after you were added, keeping in mind that anything else you changed in that period muddies the comparison.

Apps and other clients

The official Bluesky app often opens links with no referrer. Virling credits a few apps from their in-app browser's user agent, such as Instagram and LinkedIn, but has no rule for Bluesky, so an untagged tap from the app is counted as Direct.

Bluesky also has independent clients built on the same network. A client that sends no referrer behaves like the app and lands in Direct. One that sends its own web address shows up under Referral with that domain, which is easy to mistake for an unrelated site linking to you. If a domain you do not recognise appears in Referral right after a Bluesky post, open it: it may be a Bluesky client.

A utm_source tag settles all of these, because Virling reads it whenever there is no referrer, and bsky.app resolves to the same row as referred clicks. When a client does send a referrer, the referrer is used instead of the tag.

Tag a pinned post and your other links

A pinned post is the closest Bluesky has to a permanent link besides your profile: it sits at the top of your profile for everyone who visits it, often for weeks. Give it a campaign of its own so its clicks do not blur into those from new posts:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=bsky.app&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=pinned-launch
https://yourdomain.com/pricing?utm_source=bsky.app&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=profile
https://yourdomain.com/changelog?utm_source=bsky.app&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=post-v2-release
  • Use a fresh campaign for each new pin. When you replace pinned-launch with a new pin, use a new value such as pinned-v2, so the Campaign tab shows each pin's revenue separately.
  • Keep bsky.app as the source. bluesky is read as Social but sits in a row apart from bsky.app; bsky is not a known source at all and falls into Referral.
  • Use social as the medium. Only paid mediums and email mediums take a visit out of Social.

The UTM builder makes these, which UTM parameters to use explains the naming, and the social UTM generator makes a set for several networks at once if you edit its Bluesky source from bluesky to bsky.app. Bluesky shows links as cards, but a short link is still tidier in the post text: a tracked link in Virling, made under Links with Other as the place and bsky.app as the source, carries the source, a social medium and its own campaign. See tracked links.

None of this credits a sale unless checkout gets the visitor ID. The script adds it to Stripe Payment Links, Polar and Dodo Payments links on click; for a checkout created on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, as link checkout to visits explains. With the default last non-direct model, a buyer who first clicks from Bluesky and later returns by typing your address is still credited to Bluesky if that first visit falls within the 90-day lookback, and so are their renewals.

Reading Bluesky revenue in Virling

  1. Set the range to cover several weeks after the posts you care about, since many buyers do not pay on the day they click.
  2. Find the bsky.app row in the Source tab and compare its revenue per visitor with the site average chip beside it.
  3. Open the Campaign tab and compare your pinned post with individual posts. A pin that keeps earning after a month is worth keeping; one that has gone quiet is worth replacing.
  4. Filter the page to Bluesky and check the Pages card to see which pages those visitors landed on.
  5. Pick a payment on the Payments page and read the journey to see whether the Bluesky visit came first, last or somewhere between.
  6. After a month or two, look at Retention to see whether customers whose first payment came via Bluesky keep paying.

Feeds and starter packs often introduce people who buy later through search. Switching Site settings to first touch shows how much Bluesky started; Virling recomputes history, and you can switch back. First-touch or last-touch attribution for a SaaS explains which to use. For the other places you post, see X (Twitter) revenue tracking and Mastodon traffic by instance, where clicks come from many domains instead of one.

When the Bluesky numbers look off

  • Direct spiked when a post spread through feeds. App taps on an untagged link brought no referrer. Tag the next post and the pin. Links forwarded in DMs end up in Direct too; see dark social revenue tracking.
  • Tagged Bluesky clicks show up as Referral. The source is probably bsky. Change it to bsky.app.
  • A strange domain appeared in Referral after a post. It may be a third-party Bluesky client sending its own address. Tagged links keep those clicks on Bluesky.
  • The pinned post has visitors but no revenue. Check the Matched revenue line on the Overview; if much revenue is unmatched, checkout is missing the visitor ID. See Unattributed revenue.
  • Someone found you through a starter pack but bought later. Their payment is credited to whatever visit the model picks, which may be search or Direct. Try first touch before concluding the pack did nothing.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track sales from Bluesky?

Let Virling count visits referred by bsky.app or go.bsky.app, add utm_source=bsky.app and a campaign to your pinned post and other links so app clicks still count, and pass the visitor ID into checkout. The bsky.app row then shows Bluesky revenue and revenue per visitor.

Why do clicks from Bluesky show up as Direct?

The Bluesky app and some other clients open links without a referrer, and Virling has no user agent rule for them. An untagged click is therefore Direct. A utm_source of bsky.app on the link credits it to Bluesky.

What utm_source should I use for Bluesky?

bsky.app. It joins the same row as referred clicks. bluesky counts as Social but gets a separate row, and bsky is not recognised and counts as Referral.

What is go.bsky.app in my referrers?

It is Bluesky's short-link host, best known from starter pack links. Virling merges it into the bsky.app row.

Can I tell which custom feed or starter pack sent a buyer?

No. The referrer is only bsky.app, whichever feed showed the post. Per-post and pinned-post campaigns tell you which post earned, and a before-and-after comparison shows whether a starter pack grew Bluesky revenue.
