First-touch or last-touch attribution for a SaaS

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-10-26.

For most small SaaS products, use last non-direct touch to decide where to spend this week, and look at first touch to see what introduces you. Both are rules for deciding which visit gets credit for a payment, the core question of revenue attribution.

The two rules

  • First touch: credit the first source that brought the visitor, inside the lookback window
  • Last touch: credit the last source before they paid. Last non-direct touch skips visits typed straight into the browser, which hide the real source

An attribution model example

Someone finds you through an AI assistant answer on Monday. On Thursday they search your name, then pay.

  • First touch gives the credit to the AI assistant
  • Last touch gives it to organic search

Neither is wrong. They answer different questions.

A worked example with five customers

These five customers are made up to show the effect, with made-up payments. Each pays once, and every visit is inside the lookback window. Each payment is credited in full to one visit.

  • Every column adds up to $300. The model moves credit between channels. It never creates or removes revenue
  • Customer B had one visit, so all three models agree. They can only disagree when the visits come from different channels
  • Customers A and C typed the URL before paying, so last touch gives Direct $210 of the $300. Last non-direct skips that visit and credits the search and the AI answer
  • Customer D first arrived as Direct, so first touch credits Direct even though an email brought them back to buy
  • Customer E only ever came as Direct, so even last non-direct credits Direct. It has no other visit to pick

Communities earn $100 under first touch and nothing under the other two. Judged by last touch alone, the channel that introduced customer A would look worthless.

What each rule tells you

  • First touch shows what introduces you to people. Use it to decide what to start or keep doing for awareness
  • Last touch shows what closes. Use it to decide where the final nudge happens, such as search for your brand name or an email

Which one for a small SaaS

Buying cycles are short for low-priced products, often one to a few visits, so the two rules agree most of the time. To check yours, see how long a visitor takes to become a customer. Start with last non-direct touch inside a lookback window (30 to 90 days is common). Check first touch when a channel looks weak but you suspect it starts the journey.

Choosing for a trial, a sales-led SaaS or a store

These are starting points, the same as in the docs. If you are unsure, keep Virling's defaults: last non-direct with a 90-day window.

A SaaS with a trial

Last non-direct with a 90-day window. A trial user who comes back by typing your URL is still credited to the channel that brought them. Pick a window longer than the time people usually take from first visit to first charge, trial included.

A sales-led SaaS

First touch with a 365-day window, so the visit that introduced the buyer still counts after months of calls and demos. See B2B SaaS attribution for linking invoices to visits.

A store

Last non-direct with a 30-day window when most orders follow within days of the first visit. Use 60 or 90 days for expensive or seasonal products that people compare for weeks.

Only visits inside the window count, so first touch is the earliest visit inside it, not the first visit ever. To choose a length from your own buying cycle, see how long a visit gets credit.

What changes when you switch the model

In Virling, saving a new model recomputes the credit of every payment of the site, history included.

  • Total revenue stays the same. The payments themselves do not change, only which channel gets the credit. Switching back restores the earlier credit for the same visits and payments
  • Direct can grow. Last touch counts Direct visits like any other, so a buyer who typed your URL before paying moves to Direct. First touch does the same when the earliest visit in the window was Direct
  • Renewals follow the first payment. When a customer's first payment moves to another channel, their renewals move with it, so one customer can shift many months of revenue. See subscription renewal attribution
  • Unattributed does not move with the model. A payment is Unattributed when no visit is left to credit, and that depends on the attribution window, not on which visit the model picks

The mistake to avoid

Do not switch rules each time a report looks bad. Pick one, keep it fixed, and compare channels under the same rule over time.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between first-touch and last-touch attribution?

First touch credits the first source that brought the visitor. Last touch credits the last source before the payment.

Which attribution model should a small SaaS use?

Last non-direct touch is a sensible default for deciding where to spend time this week. Check first touch to see which channels start the journey.

What is a lookback window?

The number of days before a payment in which visits can earn credit. Thirty to ninety days is common for SaaS.

What is last non-direct click attribution?

It credits a payment to the last visit before it that came from a real source, skipping visits typed in or opened from a bookmark. Shopify's marketing reports use it by default, GA4 uses it for its traffic source reports, and Virling uses it by default.

What does first touch attribution mean?

It credits a payment to the first source that brought that visitor, which shows what introduces people to you. In Virling that is the earliest visit inside the lookback window.

Does changing the attribution model change total revenue?

No. The payments stay the same and only the channel that gets the credit changes. In Virling, saving a new model recomputes every payment of the site, and renewals follow the new credit of the customer's first payment.

Why did Direct grow after I switched to last touch?

Last touch counts Direct visits like any other, so a buyer who typed your URL before paying moves to Direct. Last non-direct skips that visit when another source is inside the window.
