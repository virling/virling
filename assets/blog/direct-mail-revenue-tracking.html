Direct mail tracking: how to track direct mail revenue

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-22.

To track direct mail revenue, print a short tagged link or a QR code on each mailing, with one campaign per mailing or batch, and point it at a page on your site. Store the visitor ID on each order, then join each order to the visit that came before it. You then see which mailings turn into paying customers, with the visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor of each one. Without a tag, a buyer who types your address from a postcard is just another Direct visit.

This guide is for stores and SaaS teams that send postcards, catalogs, package inserts and printed flyers, and want to know which pieces bring buyers.

Why direct mail visits show up as Direct

Paper has no referrer. A buyer who reads a postcard and types your address, or scans a code with a phone camera, opens your site with no page before it. Analytics tools, Virling included, decide the channel from the referrer and the UTM tags. With neither, the visit is Direct, and so is the order that follows.

A tag fixes this because it travels inside the URL. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source. A source such as direct-mail is not one of the known social or search sources, so a tagged visit lands in the Referral channel, under its own source and campaign, instead of in Direct. If Direct rises the week a mailing lands, untagged mail is a likely cause. Why Direct is your biggest traffic source covers the other causes, and the full rule order is in channels.

Step 1: add the script

Add the Virling script to the <head> of every page of your site. It records each visit with its referrer and UTM tags, and sets the first-party visitor ID that orders need later. On Shopify, it goes in your theme's theme.liquid; on WooCommerce, the Virling WordPress plugin adds it for you. The app's setup screen shows the exact tag for your site, and add the script covers where it goes.

Point every printed link at a page with the script, such as the product or collection the mailing promotes, or a landing page for the offer. A link that opens a Stripe, Polar or Dodo payment link directly carries no visitor ID, so those sales come in as Unattributed.

Step 2: make one tagged link per mailing or batch

Give each mailing, and each batch or version you want to compare, its own campaign:

https://yourstore.com/spring?utm_source=direct-mail&utm_medium=print&utm_campaign=postcard-spring-2026-11
https://yourstore.com/?utm_source=direct-mail&utm_medium=print&utm_campaign=catalog-winter-2026-11
https://yourstore.com/reorder?utm_source=direct-mail&utm_medium=print&utm_campaign=insert-reorder-2026-11
https://yourapp.com/demo?utm_source=direct-mail&utm_medium=print&utm_campaign=flyer-trade-show-2026-11
  • utm_source=direct-mail on every piece, so all mail groups under one source and stays out of Direct.
  • utm_medium=print, or another value you keep fixed. Do not use email or a paid medium such as cpc: those put the visit in the Email or Paid channel.
  • utm_campaign names the mailing: one value per piece or batch, lowercase, with hyphens. The Campaign tab groups visitors and revenue by this value, so this is the tag that gives you revenue per mailing. Add the month for anything you send more than once, so each drop is its own row.
  • utm_content tells two versions of the same mailing apart, such as two postcard designs or two lists. Virling stores it with each visit; keep what you want to compare in the campaign so it shows up in reports.

The UTM builder makes one link at a time. For many batches, regions or versions, the bulk UTM builder tags a whole list at once. Once a piece is printed and mailed, its link cannot change, so check each one before it goes to print.

Step 3: print a short link or a QR code

Nobody types a long URL full of tags from a postcard. Print a short link that redirects to the tagged page, or a QR code that opens it, or both. The QR code generator with UTM tags makes a code from the tagged page URL.

In Virling, open the Campaign tab on the Channels card and click + Tracked link. Pick QR code as the place for a printed code, or Other and type direct-mail as the source for a link people type, then enter the page it should open. You get a short URL on your Virling server, such as https://stats.example.com/l/35b6cac. On each visit it redirects to the page with utm_source, utm_medium=referral and the link's id as utm_campaign added, and keeps any tags the page URL already has, so a page URL that already carries utm_campaign=postcard-spring-2026-11 keeps that readable name. The Campaign tab lists each link with its clicks, visitors and revenue. Make one tracked link per mailing or batch. See tracked links.

Do not delete a tracked link that is already in the mail: deleting it makes the short URL return 404 right away, and every printed copy stops working. For codes, tracking QR code sales covers size, placement and one code per placement in more detail.

Step 4: connect your store or payment provider

Tags only get you visits. Revenue needs the orders and the visitor ID on each one. In the app, open your site, then Setup, then Connect payments, paste the key your provider needs and click Connect and backfill; see payments and revenue attribution for ecommerce.

  • Shopify. Create a custom app with only the read_orders scope and paste its token. Then add the short theme snippet that stores the visitor ID on the cart, which Shopify copies onto the order. See Shopify and the Shopify docs.
  • WooCommerce. Create a read-only REST API key and paste it. Then add the short snippet that stores the virling_vid cookie on each new order. See WooCommerce and the WooCommerce docs.
  • SaaS or another checkout. Virling also reads Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem and RevenueCat, all listed on integrations. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. Every case is covered in link checkout to visits.

Before the mailing goes out, open each printed link or code on your phone, place a test order, then check that it appears on the Payments page with the mailing's campaign.

See revenue per mailing

  1. Open the dashboard and pick a range that covers the life of the mailing: a few weeks for a postcard, months for a catalog that stays on a coffee table.
  2. In the Channels card, open Referral, where tagged mail lands. Each row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click the Referral row to filter the whole page. The Pages card now shows which pages those visitors landed on.
  4. Switch to the Campaign tab. With one campaign per mailing, each piece or batch is a row with its own visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the order, with the credited one marked.

People often visit from a mailer, leave, and buy days later by typing your address. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the order goes to the tagged visit, as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. For catalogs and inserts that people keep for months, choose 365 days in Site settings; see model and lookback window.

Discount codes as a cross-check

Many mailers print a code for the offer. A code only counts buyers who type it at checkout, and once printed it gets shared beyond the people you mailed. Use the tagged link to credit the visit and a mailing-specific code to cross-check it; discount code attribution explains how to read the two together.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Few tagged visits, but Direct jumped after the drop. Buyers typed your plain address instead of the printed link. Make the short link or code the most visible thing on the piece, and print your plain domain smaller or not at all.
  • A link shows clicks but no visits. The page it opens may not have the script, or the visit was blocked by a content blocker. Check that the destination page records a pageview.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching the order. On Shopify and WooCommerce, check that the snippet is in place. See Unattributed revenue.
  • Two mailings share one row. They print the same link. Give the next run its own link; the sales already counted cannot be split.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted at all, so a mailing's real visitor number can be higher than what any analytics tool shows. Revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

If the flyer goes out at a trade show, track that event by placement. For a mailing that announces a holiday sale, the Black Friday UTM plan keeps its campaign name in line with your emails and ads.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from direct mail?

Print a tagged short link or a QR code on each mailing, with one campaign per mailing or batch, and point it at a page on your site with the Virling script. Store the visitor ID on each order. The Campaign tab then shows visitors and revenue for each mailing.

Why does direct mail traffic show up as Direct?

A buyer who types a URL from a postcard, or scans a printed code, arrives with no referrer. With no referrer and no tag, analytics tools count the visit as Direct. A tag inside the printed URL travels with the visit, so it is credited to the mailing.

Should every mailing get its own link?

Yes, one link per mailing or batch. If a postcard, a catalog and a package insert all print the same URL, their visits and sales add up into one row and you cannot tell which piece paid. Give each test version its own link too.

Can I use a discount code instead of a link?

A code only counts buyers who type it, and it gets passed around once it is printed. Use a tagged link or QR code to credit the visit, and keep a mailing-specific code as a cross-check.

What about buyers who type my plain address from the mailer?

Those visits arrive with no referrer and no tag, so they count as Direct. No analytics tool can link them to the mailing. Make the printed short link or QR code the easiest way in, and compare Direct before and after each drop.
