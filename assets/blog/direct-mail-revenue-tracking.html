Direct mail tracking: how to track direct mail revenue

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-22.

To track direct mail revenue, give each mailing, list segment and offer its own tagged link, print it as a short address people can type, and connect your store so every order carries the visitor ID. Then read revenue per mailing in the Campaign tab, and check the revenue chart in the weeks after each drop. Coupon redemptions alone only count the buyers who remembered to type the code.

This guide is for stores and SaaS teams that send postcards, catalogs, package inserts and letters, and want to know which drop, which list and which offer paid back its print and postage.

Why mail visits land in Direct

Someone who types an address from a postcard arrives with no referrer, so without a tag the visit, and the order after it, count as Direct. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source instead, and a source such as direct-mail puts the visit in Referral under its own campaign; the full rule order is in channels. The rest of this guide is about the parts only mail has: lists, offers, printed addresses and slow arrival.

Plan one link per mailing, segment and offer

Decide what you want to compare before the artwork goes to the printer, because a printed link cannot change once the piece is in the post. A drop usually has three things worth splitting: the mailing itself, such as a spring postcard or a winter catalog; the list segment it goes to, such as past buyers, lapsed buyers or a rented list; and the offer printed on it, such as free shipping or a percentage off.

  • utm_source=direct-mail on every piece, so all mail groups under one source. It is not a known social or search source, so a visit with no referrer counts as Referral.
  • utm_medium=print, or any value you keep fixed. Avoid email, newsletter and paid values such as cpc, which move the visit to Email or Paid.
  • utm_campaign holds everything you want to read as its own row: the mailing, the month of the drop and, when lists may respond differently, the segment. The Campaign tab groups visitors and revenue by this value.
  • utm_content can carry a detail you only look up now and then, such as the offer or the card design. Virling stores it with each visit, but the Channels card has no tab for it, so anything you plan to compare belongs in the campaign.

Keep the number of versions small enough that each one reaches a real share of the list; a version mailed to a handful of addresses rarely brings enough orders to tell apart. The bulk UTM builder tags every segment and offer of a drop in one pass, and the UTM builder makes a single link.

Print an address people can type

Most people who answer a mailer type what they see, and nobody types a URL with three tags. Print something short that redirects to the tagged link:

  • A short path on your own domain, such as yourstore.com/spring, set up in your store or web host as a redirect to the tagged URL. It reads well on a card. The redirect must keep the full query string: one that drops the tags turns every visit back into Direct. Even when that happens, the path still shows on the Pages card's Landing page tab, which is a rough count of mail visits.
  • A vanity domain, such as springsale-yourstore.com, that redirects to the tagged URL. Make it a redirect, not a copy of your site: the visitor ID cookie belongs to one domain, so a visit on a separate domain is not linked to an order on your store.
  • A Virling tracked link. In the Campaign tab of the Channels card, click + Tracked link, choose Other, type direct-mail as the source and enter the tagged page. You get a short URL such as https://stats.example.com/l/35b6cac. Tags already on the page are kept, so utm_campaign=postcard-spring-2026-11-lapsed stays as your readable name. It is short, but harder to remember than a word on your own domain. See tracked links.

Whichever you use, print the short address larger than your plain domain, or leave the plain domain off the piece. Every reader who types yourstore.com instead becomes a Direct visit that no analytics tool can tie to the mailing. A QR code next to the address helps readers who reach for a phone; tracking QR code sales covers codes on their own.

Match mailing dates to the revenue curve

Mail does not arrive on one day. A drop reaches homes over several days depending on postal class and distance, responses trail on for weeks, and a catalog can keep selling for months. Log each drop under Launches in the app, with the day it went to the post office; it then shows as a marker on the revenue chart, so the response has a starting line.

  • Campaign tab. Revenue and revenue per visitor for each mailing, segment and offer you tagged.
  • Direct. The Channels card splits Direct into typed or bookmarked, a shared link, and a return from checkout. A rise in typed or bookmarked visits after a drop is roughly the response that skipped your printed address. Compare to other dates in the date picker sets the weeks after the drop against the same weeks before it.
  • Location. If a mailing went only to some regions, the City tab shows whether those places moved more than the rest.

Read the Direct lift as a hint, not a result: anything else you run that week moves it too, and the Launches figures count all traffic on the drop day and the day after, not only mail. With the default model, last non-direct, a buyer who first comes in through the printed link and returns later by typing your address is still credited to the mailing, as long as the tagged visit is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. For catalogs that stay in the house for months, choose 365 days in Site settings; see model and lookback window.

When a discount code is the better signal

A tagged link credits the visit; a printed code credits the order. A mailing-specific code tells you more when:

  • Orders happen without a web visit, such as by phone or in a shop, so there is nothing for a link to tag.
  • Many readers type your plain domain or search your name. The code still names the mailing at checkout, even when the visit landed in Direct or Organic search.
  • Each segment gets a different offer. The code shows which offer was actually redeemed, not only which page was opened.

Codes have their own gaps: they only count buyers who type them, and once printed they get passed on to people you never mailed. Print both, use the link for visitors and revenue per visitor, and set code redemptions from your store beside it for the same dates; discount code attribution shows how to read the two together.

Connect your store

Revenue per mailing needs each order to carry the visitor ID: the theme snippet on Shopify, the order snippet on WooCommerce, and for Stripe, Paddle and the other providers, link checkout to visits. Before the pieces go to print, type each short address on your phone, place a test order and check that it appears on the Payments page with the mailing's campaign.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Few tagged visits, but typed or bookmarked Direct rose after the drop. The plain domain was easier to type than the printed address. Make the short address the biggest thing on the next piece.
  • A short path has landing-page visits but no campaign row. The redirect drops the query string, or the path serves a page instead of redirecting. Redirect straight to the final tagged page.
  • Visits from a vanity domain never turn into revenue. The domain hosts its own pages instead of redirecting, so the visitor ID is set on the wrong domain.
  • Two segments share one row. They got the same printed link. Split the next drop; sales already counted cannot be divided.
  • Orders arrive but stay Unattributed. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers, then the snippet or checkout setup; see Unattributed revenue.

If a flyer goes out at a trade show, track that event by placement. For a mailing that announces a holiday sale, the Black Friday UTM plan keeps its campaign name in line with your emails and ads.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track revenue from direct mail?

Give each mailing, list segment and offer its own tagged link with utm_source=direct-mail, print it as a short address that redirects to that link, and connect your store so each order carries the visitor ID. The Campaign tab then shows visitors and revenue per mailing.

Should each list segment get its own link?

Yes, if you want to compare them. Put the segment in utm_campaign, such as postcard-spring-2026-11-lapsed, and print that version only on the pieces going to that list. Once the cards are mailed, sales from a shared link cannot be split by segment.

Can I print a vanity domain on a postcard?

Yes, as long as it redirects to the tagged URL on your store. A vanity domain that hosts its own copy of your pages sets the visitor cookie on a different domain, so its visits are not linked to orders on your store.

How long after a mailing should I keep measuring?

Mail arrives over several days and responses trail for weeks, longer for catalogs. Log the drop under Launches so it marks the revenue chart, read the Campaign tab over a range that covers the whole response, and use a 365-day lookback window for pieces people keep.

Is a discount code better than a link for direct mail?

A code is better for orders with no web visit and for readers who type your plain domain. A link is better for revenue per visitor and does not get shared on coupon sites. Most mailings print both and compare them.
