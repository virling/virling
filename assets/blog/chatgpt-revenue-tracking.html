ChatGPT revenue tracking: which ChatGPT visits pay

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

To see which ChatGPT visits turn into paying customers, count every visit that arrives from chatgpt.com or carries utm_source=chatgpt.com, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and join each payment to the visit before it. You then see the revenue ChatGPT brings and what each ChatGPT visitor is worth next to search, social and email. A traffic count alone cannot tell you that, because ChatGPT sends few visitors and the question is how many of them pay.

This post covers ChatGPT only. For AI assistants in general, see is AI search traffic worth anything.

How ChatGPT visits show up

A visit from ChatGPT can reach your site in three ways, and each one is classified differently.

  • The chatgpt.com referrer. When someone clicks a link in an answer, the visit usually arrives with chatgpt.com as the referrer. Older links can arrive from chat.openai.com, ChatGPT's earlier address. Virling puts both in the AI channel and shows them as one ChatGPT row. The referrer is a host only, so it never says which answer or question the click came from.
  • The utm_source=chatgpt.com tag. OpenAI's publisher FAQ says ChatGPT adds utm_source=chatgpt.com to referral links so analytics tools can identify the traffic. You do not add it; ChatGPT does. When the referrer is missing, Virling reads this tag and credits the visit to ChatGPT, in the AI channel, exactly as if the referrer had been there. A bare utm_source=chatgpt also counts as AI.
  • No referrer and no tag. If the referrer is dropped and the link carries no utm_source, nothing in the visit names ChatGPT. It counts as Direct, in Virling and in most other tools. Virling recognizes some apps' in-app browsers by their user agent, such as Instagram and LinkedIn, but ChatGPT is not one of them.

So the ChatGPT row is a floor, not a full count. People who read an answer and later type your name into a browser or a search engine arrive as Direct or Organic search; no analytics tool can tie that visit to the answer. The full rule order is in channels, and the traffic source checker shows how a given landing URL and referrer are credited: paste a URL with utm_source=chatgpt.com and an empty referrer to see it land in AI.

One exception to the order: a paid medium wins over everything. A link with utm_medium=cpc counts as Paid even if its source is chatgpt.com, so ads you run stay apart from answers.

Let ChatGPT read your pages

ChatGPT can only link to pages it can read. OpenAI's crawler documentation describes two bots that matter here. OAI-SearchBot is used to show sites in ChatGPT's search features, and sites that block it are not shown in ChatGPT search answers. ChatGPT-User visits a page when someone asks ChatGPT a question. GPTBot, the training crawler, is separate: you can block it and still allow OAI-SearchBot.

The AI crawler checker reads your robots.txt and tells you which of these bots may read a page. To write the rules, use the robots.txt generator for AI crawlers. Bot fetches are never counted as visitors in Virling. With the server snippet, each ChatGPT-User fetch shows on the AI card as an answer that used one of your pages; see AI answers.

Set up ChatGPT revenue tracking in Virling

  1. Add the tracking script to every page. See the tracking script. Nothing ChatGPT-specific is needed: the referrer and the tag are read on every visit.
  2. Connect your payment provider: Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify or WooCommerce.
  3. Pass the visitor ID into checkout. Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links and Dodo Payments links get it from the script on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. See link checkout to visits. Without this step ChatGPT shows visitors and no revenue.
  4. Open the dashboard and pick a range of a few weeks or more. ChatGPT sends fewer visitors than search, so short ranges are noisy.
  5. In the Channels card, click AI, or switch to the Source tab, and find ChatGPT. The row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  6. Click the row to filter the whole page. The Pages card now shows which landing pages ChatGPT sends people to, and which of them lead to payments.
  7. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.

How Virling credits the payment

People often click through from ChatGPT, leave, and come back days later by typing your URL. With the default model, last non-direct, that later Direct visit does not take the credit: the payment goes to the ChatGPT visit, as long as it started inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. If the buyer subscribes, every renewal keeps counting toward ChatGPT. If they come back through a Google search or a newsletter before paying, that later visit takes the credit instead.

To see how often ChatGPT starts journeys that another channel finishes, switch the model to first touch in Site settings. First-touch or last-touch attribution explains the difference. Changing the model recalculates every past payment, so you can switch back.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • ChatGPT has visitors but no revenue. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A buyer says they found you in ChatGPT but the payment is credited elsewhere. They may have read the answer without clicking and searched for you later. That visit has no trace of ChatGPT.
  • ChatGPT shows no visits at all. Check that robots.txt does not block OAI-SearchBot, and look at the Answers tab of the AI card to see whether ChatGPT names you when asked your buyer questions.
  • Visitors who block the script are not counted, so ChatGPT's real visitor number can be higher than any analytics tool shows. Their revenue still counts in your total, as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which ChatGPT visits turn into paying customers?

Use a tool that groups chatgpt.com visits into their own source and joins each payment to the visit before it. In Virling, pass the visitor ID into checkout, then open the AI channel and the ChatGPT row: it shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, and the Payments page shows each buyer's visits.

Does ChatGPT add UTM parameters to links?

Yes, for many links. OpenAI's publisher FAQ says ChatGPT adds utm_source=chatgpt.com to referral links so analytics tools can identify the traffic. Virling reads that tag, so a visit with it counts as ChatGPT even when the referrer is missing.

Why does some ChatGPT traffic show up as Direct?

A visit with no referrer and no tag has nothing that names ChatGPT, so it is Direct in Virling and in most other tools. That happens when a link is opened somewhere the referrer is dropped and carries no utm_source. Treat the ChatGPT number as a floor.

Is chat.openai.com the same as chatgpt.com?

Yes. chat.openai.com is ChatGPT's old address. Virling counts both in the AI channel and shows them as one ChatGPT row.

Does a ChatGPT visit still get credit if the buyer pays a week later?

With the default model, last non-direct, yes. If the buyer comes back by typing your URL, that Direct visit does not take the credit; the payment goes to the ChatGPT visit as long as it is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default.

Do ChatGPT-User and OAI-SearchBot visits count as visitors?

No. They are OpenAI's bots, not people. Virling stores crawler hits apart from visits; with the server snippet, ChatGPT-User fetches show on the AI card as answers that used your pages.
