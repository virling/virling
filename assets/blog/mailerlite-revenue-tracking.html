MailerLite revenue tracking: which emails bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-25.

MailerLite revenue tracking means seeing which newsletter issue, automation email or landing page led to a payment. MailerLite counts opens and clicks, and for a connected store it credits purchases to the last email clicked. Virling credits each payment to a visit on your site instead, so a MailerLite click is weighed against search, social and every other channel, and the Campaign tab shows revenue per issue.

What MailerLite does to your links

Every link in a MailerLite email first goes through an mlsend domain, which MailerLite uses to gather click statistics, or through your own domain if you set up domain alignment (source: MailerLite, What Is Mlsend?). That redirect counts the click but adds nothing Virling can read. The UTM tags are a separate switch, and campaigns and automations each have their own (source: MailerLite, How To Use Google Analytics On Your Campaigns and Websites):

  • Campaigns. On the Campaign details page, find Tracking options and toggle UTM tags.
  • Automations. In the workflow editor, click the email step and, under UTM tags in the sidebar, check Add parameters to your URLs to track newsletter traffic on web analytics platforms. Repeat it on each email step.
  • Link tracking tab. Under Account settings, the Link tracking tab holds the UTM code fields, where you type the values MailerLite adds.

The tracking settings also list E-commerce link tracking next to opens tracking and Google Analytics (source: MailerLite, How to enable and disable tracking in MailerLite campaigns). It feeds the purchase report inside MailerLite; Virling only reads the UTM tags. When we read these pages in November 2026 they did not list the default UTM values, so put mailerlite as the source and email as the medium in the Link tracking tab, and open one sent link in a private window to read the address bar before you trust it.

Revenue per newsletter issue

A newsletter is the same email shape every week, so the only thing that tells issues apart in a report is utm_campaign. If the Link tracking tab gives every issue one value, all issues share a row. For those sends, turn UTM tags off and paste links you tagged with the date of the issue:

https://example.com/pricing?utm_source=mailerlite&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=issue-2026-11-20

The free UTM builder makes these. Then click Email in the Channels card and open the Campaign tab: each issue is a row with visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor.

Automations for ecommerce stores

With a store connected, MailerLite automations can start on Abandoned cart, Abandoned checkout, Buys any product, Buys specific product, Buys from category or Purchase frequency. The help page lists Shopify, BigCommerce, PrestaShop, WooCommerce, Shoper, Wix, Squarespace and the e-commerce API, and the customer must also be a subscriber who accepts marketing (source: MailerLite, How to set up automation triggers).

  • Name each step. Give each email step its own campaign value, such as cart-1, cart-2 and post-purchase-1, and keep it while the workflow runs.
  • Pass the visitor ID with the order. Virling imports Shopify and WooCommerce orders, and a short snippet stores the visitor ID on each order. See set up Virling for an online store.
  • Expect a different count. A cart email click that leads to an order is an Email visit, and Virling credits the order to it unless a later visit that was not Direct came in between. MailerLite credits the same order to the last email clicked within 7, 14 or 30 days, if the buyer uses the same email address (source: MailerLite, How E-Commerce Tracking Works).

Landing pages built in MailerLite

A page MailerLite hosts does not run the Virling script unless you add it. The landing page builder has an Analytics and custom code section for header code (source: MailerLite, New landing page builder), and MailerLite’s blog says adding custom code to a website takes a paid account (source: MailerLite, 14 Tools to Expand Your MailerLite Website Capabilities).

  • On a subdomain of yours. MailerLite landing pages can use a custom subdomain, kept separate from the one used for domain alignment (source: MailerLite, How to align a domain). Virling keeps visits from your domain and its subdomains, so with the script in the header the page is tracked like the rest of your site.
  • On a MailerLite address. The page itself is invisible to Virling, and a click on its button arrives from MailerLite’s host as Referral. Tag the buttons with utm_campaign=lp- and the page name; if most of its visitors come from your emails, use utm_medium=email too, so the visit counts as Email.
  • Stripe products on the page. MailerLite can sell Stripe products from a Products block (source: MailerLite, How to Sell Stripe Products in MailerLite). That help page shows no place for a visitor ID, so expect those payments to be Unattributed unless the payer email matches a visitor you identified earlier.

How the click reaches the payment

Most email apps send no referrer, so an untagged click lands in Direct; a visit counts as Email when utm_medium is email, newsletter or e-mail (see how channels are decided). The Virling script sets a visitor ID that your checkout carries to the payment, which is then credited by default to the latest visit in 90 days that was not Direct. See link checkout to visits.

Test one issue end to end

  1. Make a group with only your own address and send it a campaign whose one link carries utm_campaign=tracking-test.
  2. Copy the link into a private window. It passes through the mlsend redirect first. Expected: your page opens with utm_medium=email and utm_campaign=tracking-test in the address bar.
  3. In the dashboard, click Email. Expected: a tracking-test row on the Campaign tab.
  4. Pay in your provider’s test mode from the same window; see test your setup. Expected: after the next sync, the payment shows Email and starts at your tagged visit.

When MailerLite numbers look off

  • An issue shows as Direct. UTM tags were off for that send, or a redirect on your site dropped them; see UTM parameters lost after redirect.
  • Every issue is one row. The Link tracking tab sets the same campaign value on every send.
  • MailerLite shows far more clicks than Virling shows visitors. Some mail servers click links to check them (source: MailerLite, Why does my campaign show unusually high opens or clicks?), and some browsers block scripts.
  • Store orders are Unattributed. The snippet that stores the visitor ID on the order is missing.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which MailerLite newsletter issue brought paying customers?

Give each issue its own utm_campaign, such as issue-2026-11-20, with utm_medium=email, add the Virling script to your site, and pass the visitor ID into checkout. The Campaign tab then shows one row per issue with its revenue.

Does MailerLite add UTM tags to automation emails?

Yes, if you check the box. Open the email step in the workflow editor and, under UTM tags in the sidebar, check Add parameters to your URLs to track newsletter traffic on web analytics platforms. Do it for every email step.

Can Virling track a landing page built in MailerLite?

Only if you add the Virling script to it, in the Analytics and custom code section, and the page sits on a subdomain of your own domain. On a MailerLite address, tag the buttons that lead to your site instead.

Why does MailerLite show different revenue from Virling?

They use different rules. MailerLite credits store purchases to the last email the subscriber clicked within 7, 14 or 30 days, when they buy with the same email address. Virling credits each payment to one visit, by default the latest in 90 days that was not Direct, from any channel.

Do MailerLite opens count as revenue in Virling?

No. MailerLite counts opens with an invisible pixel, and Virling credits payments only to visits on your site, so an issue counts when someone clicks a link and lands on a page that runs the script.
