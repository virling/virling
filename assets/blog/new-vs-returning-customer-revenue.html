New vs returning customer revenue by channel

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-24.

New vs returning customer revenue splits your sales by who paid. New customer revenue is the first payment of each customer. Returning customer revenue is every payment after it: repeat orders in a store, renewals and upgrades in a subscription. By channel, the split shows which channels find buyers and which sell again to people you already have. A channel can lead on total revenue and bring almost no new customers. Before you compare channels, decide who gets a repeat payment: the channel that brought the customer, or the last one they came through.

For a store, the repeat purchase rate calculator gives the share of customers who ordered again, per first channel.

The two definitions

StoreSubscription
New customer revenueA customer's first paid order.A customer's first paid charge. A free trial is not a payment.
Returning customer revenueEvery later order by the same customer, including subscription orders.Every renewal, upgrade and add-on after the first charge.
  • New means new ever, not new in the date range. If someone first bought in March and ordered twice in June, both June orders are returning revenue.
  • A customer is not a visitor. A returning visitor who has never paid is still a new customer when they buy.
  • The same person needs the same ID. A guest checkout or a second email address makes a returning customer look new.
  • The two add up to all revenue only when every payment is in exactly one of them, so take refunds off both in the same way.

In a subscription, most of the returning side arrives without anyone visiting your site. In a store, the buyer usually comes back through some channel, often an email. That is why the crediting rule matters most for stores.

A worked example

Example only: made-up numbers for an imaginary store in its first year, not results from a real business. Nobody had bought before, and there were no refunds. Here each order is credited to the visit right before it, so a repeat order that followed an email counts for Email:

  • By total revenue, email is first. It looks like the channel to grow.
  • By new customers, email is last. It brought 10 of 220. Email is strong on repeat orders and weak at finding buyers, because most people on the list had already bought through another channel.
  • Paid looks modest, but it found 80 buyers. If you moved its budget to email, there would be fewer new people to email later.

  • The totals did not change. $8,500 of returning revenue moved between channels, and email went from $7,000 to $800.
  • Communities found the most valuable buyers: $50 on the first order, $150 over the year. The first table showed it as the smallest channel.
  • Neither table is wrong. The first says what prompted each order, the second who found the customer. Email still did the work of bringing people back, so the second table is no reason to stop sending it.

A subscription has the same trap. A channel with 30 renewals of $20 and no new customers shows $600 for the month (made-up numbers again) and looks healthy while it has stopped bringing anyone.

How Virling counts and credits each payment

Virling follows the rule of the second table. A customer is one customer ID at one payment provider, and every payment after their first that did not fail is called a renewal, whether it is a subscription charge or a one-off repeat order.

PaymentCounted asCredited to
First payment of a customerNew customerOne visit before it. By default, the latest in the last 90 days that was not Direct.
Subscription renewalRenewalThe channel of the customer's first payment.
Upgrade or plan changeRenewalThe channel of the customer's first payment.
One-off repeat order, same customer IDRenewalThe channel of the customer's first payment, even if they came back through another channel.
Order with no customer ID, such as a WooCommerce guestNew customerIts own visit. A guest who orders again can count as new again.
Free trial startNot a paymentNothing, but a later payment with no visitor ID is credited from the visits before the trial.
  • If the first payment was not attributed, a renewal falls back to its own visitor ID. Subscription renewals rarely carry one, so they are usually Unattributed too.
  • History starts at the import. Virling reads the last 90 days of payments, 60 for Shopify. An old customer whose first order is not in Virling counts as new on the next one, unless the provider marks it as a renewal. Such marked renewals are usually Unattributed.
  • The first charge after a free trial is a renewal when the provider marks it as one. Stripe does, with billing reason subscription_cycle, so that charge adds to renewals and not to new customers. Dodo Payments counts it as the first payment.
  • Two providers, two customers. Someone who moves from one provider to another counts as new again.

How each provider marks a renewal is in renewals in the docs, and why the first payment gets the credit is in subscription renewal attribution. For Shopify and WooCommerce setup, see which channel brings your store orders.

Where to read it in Virling

  1. New customers is one of the five numbers on the Overview. Click a channel on the Channels card and it becomes the new customers credited to that channel. The Revenue next to it is new and returning together.
  2. From new customers and From renewals are at the top of the Latest payments tab: revenue net of refunds and the number of payments on each side, for the whole site. They do not change when you filter by channel.
  3. The Retention page groups customers who first paid in the range by the channel of that first payment, with net LTV, average LTV and refund rate. Every later payment counts toward that first channel.
  4. Lifetime value by first source, lower on the same page, shows renewals and repeat share, the share of customers who paid more than once. It groups by where the first visit came from, which can differ from the credited channel.

What the dashboard does not show:

  • New and returning revenue side by side for each channel. The Channels card has one revenue figure per channel with both in it.
  • Subscription renewals apart from one-off repeat orders. From renewals holds both.
  • The channel a repeat order came back through, as a revenue split. For a customer with an attributed first payment, a campaign that brings them back gets no revenue for that order, so judge it with your email tool's own report.

To build the per-channel split yourself, run virling query payments --range 90d --limit 200 --json, or use the Read API. Each payment has is_renewal, net_usd and the credited channel in touch, so add up net_usd per channel for false and true. A call returns at most 200 payments, so use --offset for the rest. Payments with no touch are Unattributed. Every card is described in read your dashboard.

What to do with the split

  • Many new customers, little returning revenue. Give it time first, since recent buyers have had less time to come back. If older groups are no better, look at the offer and who the channel reaches.
  • Few new customers, much returning revenue. Keep it for the customers it already brought, but do not expect more budget to find buyers until new customers move.
  • Strong on both. Worth more effort, as a test.
  • Compare groups of the same age. Pick one month of first payments on the Retention page, then the next. LTV by acquisition channel has the method.

Frequently asked questions

What is new vs returning customer revenue?

New customer revenue is the first payment of each customer. Returning customer revenue is every payment after it, such as a repeat order, a renewal or an upgrade. Together they are all of your revenue.

Which channel should get the credit for a repeat order?

It depends on the question. Credit the channel of the first order to see which channels find valuable customers. Credit the visit right before the order to see what prompted it. Pick one rule and use it for every channel.

Do subscription renewals count as returning customer revenue?

Yes. A renewal is a later payment by a customer you already have, and so is an upgrade. A renewal usually has no visit of its own, so Virling credits it to the channel of the customer's first payment.

Why does email look so strong on returning revenue?

Most people on a customer list have already bought, so when each order is credited to the visit before it, repeat orders land on Email. Compare its new customers with its total revenue to see how many buyers it found.

Is a returning customer the same as a returning visitor?

No. A returning visitor has been on your site before and may never have paid. A returning customer has paid before. A visitor on their tenth visit is still a new customer when they first buy.

Does Virling show new vs returning revenue by channel?

Not side by side. It shows new customers per channel when you click a channel, and revenue from new customers and from renewals for the whole site. The Read API and the CLI return each payment with its channel and whether it is a renewal, so you can add up the split per channel.
