gbraid, wbraid and gad_source explained: what each Google Ads parameter tells you

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-25.

gbraid, wbraid and gad_source are parameters Google adds to the links of its ads. gbraid and wbraid help Google measure ads on iOS, between the web and apps, and gad_source says where the ad traffic came from, such as Google Search or the Display Network. Like gclid, none of them names your campaign to a tool outside Google. Virling does not read any of them: a visit that has only these is sorted by its referrer, and it is Paid only when the link also carries a paid medium such as utm_medium=cpc.

gbraid and wbraid: two directions

Google Analytics help puts it in one line: for web to app measurement the parameter is GBRAID, and for app to web measurement it is WBRAID (source: Google, Updates to iOS 14+ campaign measurement). Google introduced wbraid in March 2021 and gbraid in May 2021. Three details are easy to get wrong:

  • They did not replace gclid everywhere. Google Ads help says it stopped sending the gclid for iOS 14 traffic from ads on a handful of Google apps, and that other Google traffic still includes it (source: Google, iOS 14 updates). The Analytics page words it more widely, as no longer used without user consent on iOS 14 and later.
  • A webpage can get a gbraid too. A newer page covers websites that measure conversions from web campaigns where the Google Click ID is at risk of being dropped, and it says gbraid helps even when a gclid is there (source: Google, offline conversions using GBRAID). Do not read it as proof of an app.
  • Case matters. Google says gbraid is case sensitive, so do not lowercase the address on the server (source: Google, GBRAID URL parameter).

If you send offline conversions with more than one of these, the Google Ads API uses gclid first, then wbraid, then gbraid (source: Google, offline conversions in the API).

gad_source and gad_campaignid

Google says gad_source is not customizable and that all advertisers and campaigns with the same source have the same value. So it cannot tell two of your Search campaigns apart. Google’s help page does not list the values, so this post does not either.

A related parameter, gad_campaignid, is the one that identifies the campaign that drove the click, as an ID (source: Google, gad_* URL parameters). It is an ID and not a name, and a tool has to read that parameter to use it. Virling does not.

Why none of them names a campaign outside Google Ads

Here is one landing URL. Every value in it is made up:

https://example.com/pricing?gad_source=1&gbraid=EXAMPLE1&gclid=EXAMPLE2

  • gclid is a key to a record Google holds. You can look the click up through the Google Ads API, for an account you have access to, one day at a time and up to 90 days back. The characters themselves say nothing to other tools; see gclid, fbclid and msclkid vs UTM tags.
  • gbraid and wbraid help Google attribute conversions to campaigns. Another tool gets only the characters.
  • gad_source is shared by every advertiser with the same source, so it holds nothing about your campaign.

Even Google Analytics can come up short. Its help page says attributed dimensions may display as “(not set)” if you use WBRAID or GBRAID, and that you can add UTM parameters to your campaigns as a fallback, in addition to auto-tagging (source: Google, Clicks and sessions discrepancy).

The UTM suffix that does name it

Put the tags in the Final URL suffix of your ads. Google attaches that text to the end of the landing page URL (source: Google, Add a Final URL suffix). With its default settings, the Google Ads UTM builder makes this one, where brand-search is an example name:

utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=brand-search&utm_content={creative}&utm_term={keyword}

A typed name belongs on one campaign. For a suffix on the whole account, clear the campaign name in the builder and tick Campaign ID, so Google fills in the ID of each campaign:

utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign={campaignid}&utm_content={creative}&utm_term={keyword}

  • Leave auto-tagging on. The tags and Google’s parameters are separate and sit on the same link.
  • Pick a name or an ID. The same campaign tagged both ways becomes two rows.
  • Keep the whole query string in redirects. Google says not to remove aggregate identifiers, such as gbraid and gad_source, from the landing page URL (source: Google, About aggregate identifiers). A redirect that drops the query string loses your UTM tags too. See UTM parameters lost after redirect.

Which channel a visit with only these parameters gets in Virling

Virling does not read gclid, gbraid, wbraid or gad_source, and it stores the page path without its query string. The channel rules look at utm_medium, then the referrer, then utm_source (or ref). So with no UTM tags, the referrer decides:

Visit with any of the fourChannelWhy
Referrer is google.com, or a country domain such as google.co.in, and no UTM tagsOrganic searchA Google host is on the search list, so the ad is mixed in with free search visits
Referrer is youtube.com, and no UTM tagsSocialYouTube is on the social list
Referrer is a site on none of the lists, and no UTM tagsReferralThis is the rule for every other referrer, which can be a site that shows Display ads
No referrer and no UTM tagsDirectThere is nothing else to sort by. It is named Google app if the browser says so, and Typed or bookmark in most other cases

There is one exception to the Direct row: with no referrer, a visit from an in-app browser Virling knows, such as Instagram, is Social.

It is utm_medium=cpc that makes the visit Paid, not Google’s parameters. ppc, paid, paidsearch, paid_social and paidsocial count too, in any case. utm_source=google alone does not: with a Google referrer, or with none, it is Organic search. See how channels are decided.

What iOS privacy features do to these links

Apple’s Link Tracking Protection is a separate matter. Apple names no parameters, so its pages do not say whether these are removed. WebKit says that Private Browsing removes some parameters granular to users or clicks, and that one used only for campaign attribution passes through. iOS Link Tracking Protection and UTM parameters has the sources and a test to run.

From the tag to the payment

Virling keeps the UTM tags with the visit and joins each payment to the visits before it through a first-party visitor ID, so it needs none of Google’s parameters. Google Ads revenue tracking has the full setup, from the suffix to revenue per campaign.

Before you share a link copied from your address bar, run it through the URL cleaner, which removes all of these parameters.

Frequently asked questions

What is gbraid in a URL?

gbraid is a URL parameter that Google Ads adds to a landing page URL. Google calls it a privacy-preserving identifier that measures conversions in a non-unique way, without linking them to individual users or events. It is not a UTM tag and spells out no campaign name.

What is the difference between gbraid and wbraid?

The direction. Google says gbraid is for web to app measurement, such as an ad on the web that opens your iOS app, and wbraid is for app to web measurement, such as an ad in an iOS app that opens your webpage.

What is gad_source?

gad_source is a parameter Google adds to the final URL of an ad to identify the source of the ad traffic, such as Google Search or the Display Network. Google says all advertisers and campaigns with the same source have the same value, so it cannot tell your campaigns apart.

Is a visit with gbraid, wbraid or gad_source counted as Paid in Virling?

Not because of those parameters. Virling does not read them. With no UTM tags the visit is Organic search from a Google referrer and normally Direct with no referrer. With utm_medium=cpc on the link it is Paid.

Do I need UTM tags if the URL already has gbraid or wbraid?

Yes, if you want a tool outside Google to show the campaign. Put utm_source=google, utm_medium=cpc and a utm_campaign in the final URL suffix, and leave Google's own parameters on.

Should I remove gbraid, wbraid or gad_source from my URLs?

Not from ad clicks. Google says not to remove or block aggregate identifiers such as gbraid and gad_source from landing page URLs, and redirects should keep wbraid and gclid too. Remove them only from a link you copy and share.
