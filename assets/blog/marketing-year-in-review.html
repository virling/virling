Marketing year in review: which channels brought paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-20.

For a marketing year in review that shows which channels turned into paying customers, not only clicks, join every payment of the year to the visit that came before it, then read revenue per channel next to visitors. Count renewals toward the channel that brought the first payment, and keep the revenue no visit explains in its own line. Traffic reports tell you which channels were loud. Revenue per channel tells you which ones paid, and that is the list to plan next year from.

December is when most teams write the annual marketing report. This post is a template for SaaS founders, stores and agencies: the questions to answer, the steps to get the numbers, and a simple way to decide what to keep, cut or test next year.

Why traffic is the wrong headline

A year of traffic reports usually ends with a chart of visitors by source and a line about growth. It cannot answer the question the budget needs answered: which of these channels brought people who paid? A channel can send a large share of your visitors and almost none of your customers, and a small one can do the opposite. Ranking by visits rewards the first and hides the second.

Ranking by revenue per channel fixes that, but only if each payment is credited to a visit. Payment providers know the customer and not the visit; analytics tools know the visit and not the payment. A year in review by revenue needs both joined.

The questions your review should answer

  • Which channels brought paying customers? Not signups or trials, but first payments, and how much revenue each visitor from the channel was worth.
  • Which channels brought renewals? For subscriptions, the channel that brought a customer keeps earning as long as they pay. A channel whose customers stay is worth more than its first month shows. MRR by acquisition channel goes deeper on this.
  • Which channels only brought visits? Many visitors and little or no revenue. These are the channels where time and money went without paying back, or where the page they land on is the problem.
  • How much revenue could not be credited? If a large share of the year's revenue has no channel, the answers above are partial, and fixing that comes before next year's plan.

A step-by-step review in Virling

  1. Pick the date range. Use the date picker at the top right: Last 12 months, or Custom range for the calendar year. If you started tracking during the year, pick Since tracking started and say so in the report. To set this year against last year, use Compare to other dates. The range stays in the address, so you can share the link with your team or client.
  2. Read revenue by channel. The Channels card lists visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor for each channel, with a chip that compares it with your site average. Sort by revenue first, then look at revenue per visitor. Open the Source and Campaign tabs to see which sites, posts and UTM campaigns sit behind each channel, and check the Refunded column so a channel full of refunds does not look better than it is.
  3. Check renewals credited to the first channel. A subscription renewal takes the attribution of the customer's first payment, so a renewal in March of a customer who came from a newsletter last year counts toward that newsletter. Open the Retention page with the same range: it groups customers whose first payment fell in the range by the channel of that payment, with how many still pay, their lifetime value and their renewals. See Renewals for how each provider's renewals are detected.
  4. Note what is unattributed. The payments matched pill at the top of the dashboard gives the share of payments matched to a visit; open it to see why the rest were not. Write that share into the report. The common causes, such as checkout not carrying the visitor ID or visits blocked by an ad blocker, are listed in Unattributed revenue.
  5. Give slow channels their time. The Time to pay card shows, for each channel, the median time from first visit to first payment. A channel whose buyers take weeks to pay may look weak in a short range and fine over a year.
  6. Fill in the scorecard. Copy each channel's visitors, paying customers and revenue into the channel scorecard template, add what you spent on it, and let the template mark each row keep, test or cut.

Two limits to state in the report. Visits only exist from the day the script went on your site, and payments are imported 90 days back when you connect a provider, so a review covers the year only from then. Renewals of subscriptions that started before the import have no first payment in Virling and count as Unattributed.

Decide what to keep, cut or test

Compare each channel with your own average revenue per visitor, not with figures from other businesses, whose prices and buyers differ from yours.

  • Keep channels with revenue per visitor above your average and enough paying customers to trust the number. If they also keep their subscribers on the Retention page, they are the first place to add budget.
  • Cut channels with many visitors and little revenue over the whole year, after checking that their payments are not hiding in Unattributed and that their buyers are not simply slow to pay.
  • Test channels with too few visitors to judge, and channels that pay per visitor but bring few customers. Change one thing, such as the landing page or the offer, and read the result next quarter.

Once you know which channels to keep, the marketing budget calculator helps split next year's spend between them.

Frequently asked questions

What is a marketing year in review?

A look back at twelve months of marketing to decide what to do next year. The useful version ranks channels by the revenue and paying customers they brought, not by visits, and ends with a keep, cut or test decision for each one.

What should an annual marketing report include?

For each channel: visitors, paying customers, revenue, revenue per visitor, renewals from the customers it brought, and spend. Add the share of revenue that could not be credited to any channel, and the decision for next year.

How do I see which channels brought paying customers this year?

Pass a visitor ID into checkout so each payment can be joined to the visit before it, then read revenue by channel for the year. In Virling, set the range to Last 12 months and read the Channels card.

Do renewals count toward the channel that brought the customer?

In Virling, yes. A renewal takes the attribution of the customer's first payment, so a channel keeps the credit for the subscribers it brought for as long as they pay.

Why does my review not cover the whole year?

Visits are only recorded from the day the tracking script was added, and payments are imported 90 days back. Revenue from before that, and renewals of subscriptions that started before the import, cannot be credited to a channel.

Should I cut a channel that brought visits but no revenue?

Not before checking two things: whether its payments are sitting in Unattributed, and whether its buyers take longer to pay than your range allows. If neither explains it, move its time or budget to a channel that pays.
