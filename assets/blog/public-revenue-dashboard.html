Build in public with a revenue dashboard: share revenue by channel

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-17.

To share revenue by channel publicly with Virling, open Site settings, turn on Public dashboard and save. Your site then has a read-only page at /share/yourdomain.com that anyone can open without signing in. It shows your revenue, visitors and revenue per visitor for each channel, source and page, and it never shows emails or customer IDs. If you would rather not post dollar amounts, turn on Hide revenue amounts and the page shows shares and ratios instead.

Building in public usually means a screenshot of a revenue chart. A screenshot shows the total. It does not show where the money came from, it is out of date the next day, and nobody can check it. A live page that splits revenue by channel answers the question your readers ask most: what actually worked.

What the public dashboard shows

The public page is a read-only version of the Overview you see when you sign in. To see what that looks like with real-looking data, open the live demo, a sample invoicing tool. The public page shows:

  • The overview numbers: revenue, new customers, revenue per visitor, visitors and average visit time, each with its change against the previous period.
  • The revenue chart for the range, with its chart types, such as revenue by channel.
  • The breakdown cards: channels with their sources, campaigns and keywords, landing pages, locations, devices, timing and the AI card. Each row has visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip against your site average.
  • Customers who stay: retention by the channel of the first payment.
  • The date picker, so readers can switch between the last 7, 30 or 90 days and the other ranges.

The live globe is public too, at /share/yourdomain.com/globe. It shows visitors and payment pulses for the last 30 days, with animal names only, and it can be embedded in an iframe. The full list of what each card means is in Read your dashboard.

How to turn it on

  1. Add the Virling script to your site and connect your payment provider, so revenue is joined to visits. See link checkout to visits. If your app was made with an AI builder, set up Stripe revenue tracking with a prompt.
  2. Open Site settings and scroll to Public dashboard.
  3. Turn on the Public dashboard switch.
  4. Decide on Hide revenue amounts. Off shows dollar amounts. On shows shares of revenue and ratios against your average instead.
  5. Click Save. The public link appears with a Copy button, and Open shows the page as a visitor sees it.

The public page stays off while a site holds sample data, so a demo fill never ends up in front of your readers. To stop sharing, switch Public dashboard off and save: the link stops working on the next request.

What hiding revenue amounts changes

Many founders are happy to say which channel works but not what they earn. With Hide revenue amounts on, the public page changes like this:

  • Revenue becomes Revenue growth, the percent change against the previous period.
  • Revenue per visitor becomes Conversion, the share of visitors who paid.
  • In every card, a row's revenue is its share of revenue, and its revenue per visitor is a ratio against your site average, such as 1.8x.
  • The revenue chart hides its amounts; hovering shows each day's share of the revenue.
  • The link preview, when someone posts your link, gives visitors only, not revenue.
  • The live globe shows payment pulses without amounts.

Visitor counts stay visible either way.

What to share in a build-in-public post

The link is the proof; the post is the story. The build in public revenue update template writes a monthly post from your numbers. A few things readers can act on:

  • The channel that pays, not the one that is loudest. Sort by revenue per visitor and point at the row with the highest chip, even if it sends few visitors.
  • A channel that sends traffic and no revenue. Saying what did not work is often the most useful part of the post.
  • Revenue by channel as an image. On the Channels card, Share card makes a picture of revenue by channel for the range, with amounts off by default. Post it with the public link under it.
  • Retention by first channel. If customers from one channel keep paying and another churns, that is worth a thread. See retention by channel, and MRR by acquisition channel for new and churned MRR per channel. If your post quotes an MRR number, the MRR calculator works it out from your plans.
  • Tagged links to your own posts. Tag the links in your posts so readers who click through count as your posts, and say so. X (Twitter) revenue tracking shows how, and Indie Hackers revenue tracking covers posts and milestones there.

What stays private

The public page is built from the same numbers, with everything about individual people and your setup left out:

  • Emails, customer IDs and customer names.
  • The Payments and Visitors pages, each visitor's journey, and the Recent customers card.
  • Filters and saved segments. Rows on the public page do not filter it.
  • Profit by channel, your monthly spend and payment fees.
  • AI answers, reading, page speed, almost paid and channel verdicts.
  • Site settings, payment connections, launches, partners and API keys.

Your traffic sources are public by design, including referrer hosts, campaign names and landing pages. Pick campaign names you are happy for readers to see.

Frequently asked questions

How do I share my revenue publicly?

In Virling, open Site settings, turn on Public dashboard and save. Anyone with the link, /share/yourdomain.com, can see your revenue, visitors and channels without signing in. Turn it off and the link stops working right away.

Can I share my dashboard without showing revenue amounts?

Yes. Turn on Hide revenue amounts next to the Public dashboard switch. Revenue becomes revenue growth against the previous period, revenue per visitor becomes conversion, and each channel shows its share of revenue and a ratio against your average instead of dollars.

Does a public dashboard show my customers?

No. It never shows emails or customer IDs, and it leaves out the payment list, visitor journeys and the Recent customers card.

Can people filter my public dashboard?

They can change the date range. Rows do not filter the page, and saved segments, filters and Site settings stay private.

Can I turn the public dashboard off later?

Yes. Switch Public dashboard off and save. The next request to the link gets a not-found page, and the page is served with no-cache so nothing keeps a copy.
