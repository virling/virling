Indie Hackers revenue tracking: which posts and milestones bring paying customers

By Devendra Variya, co-founder of Virling. Published 2026-11-18.

Indie Hackers revenue tracking means following the money paid by customers whose credited visit came from indiehackers.com or from a link tagged as Indie Hackers. To see which posts, milestones and product pages bring paying customers, add a tracking script that keeps the referrer, pass the visitor ID into checkout, connect your payment provider, and tag the links you want to tell apart. Upvotes and comment counts do not tell you this.

Indie Hackers is a slow channel compared with a launch day. A post can bring visitors for a few days, a product page keeps sending a trickle for months, and readers who become customers often come back on their own later. Measuring it means following those people over weeks, not counting the clicks on the day you post.

How each kind of Indie Hackers link shows up

Posts, milestones, your product page and comments all live on indiehackers.com. A click from any of them in a web browser usually arrives with indiehackers.com as the referrer. In Virling that host is in the Communities channel and shows as Indie Hackers. The referrer is a host only, so on its own it cannot say which of these the click came from.

  • Posts. A launch story, a revenue update or a question in a group. This is where most of the clicks on the day come from, and where a tag pays off, because you will write more than one post and want to compare them.
  • Milestones. Short updates attached to your product. They bring fewer clicks than a post, but they are worth tagging with their own campaign if you share a link in them.
  • Product pages. Your product's page on Indie Hackers links to your site and keeps sending visitors long after any single post. Give its website link a tag of its own so this steady traffic does not blur into your posts.
  • Comments. Links you leave in replies, yours or other people's threads. These are hard to tag one by one; untagged, they still count as Indie Hackers through the referrer.

Some clicks arrive with no referrer at all, for example from reader apps, email or browsers that strip it. With no referrer, Virling reads utm_source, and when that is missing it reads ref. indiehackers is a known Communities source and lands on the same Indie Hackers row as the referrer. A click with no referrer and no tag is Direct. When a referrer is present, it wins over the tag. The full rule order is in channels.

When to add UTM tags

You do not need tags to see Indie Hackers as a source. Add them when you want to know which post, milestone or page brought the customer, or to catch clicks that arrive with no referrer. Use the same source every time and change only the campaign:

https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=indiehackers&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=ih-launch-post
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=indiehackers&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=ih-milestone-first-customer
https://yourdomain.com/?utm_source=indiehackers&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=ih-product-page
  • Source. Use indiehackers, not a short form such as ih. Virling does not know ih, so those visits would land in Referral as a row of their own.
  • Medium. Use community. A medium such as cpc or email would move the visit to Paid or Email.
  • Campaign. One per post, milestone or page, so the Campaign tab keeps them apart. Add utm_content if you link twice in one post.

The UTM builder makes these links for you. For a short link, open Links in Virling, pick Other as the place, type indiehackers as the source and Communities as the channel; see tracked links. Community revenue tracking covers the same approach for Discord, Slack and other forums.

Set it up before you post

  1. Add the script. Put the Virling tag in the <head> of every page, including the page you link to. It records the referrer and tags of the first pageview and sets the visitor cookie. See add the script, and framework guides for Next.js, Astro, WordPress, Framer and Webflow.
  2. Connect a payment provider. Virling reads payments from Stripe, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Dodo Payments, Creem, RevenueCat, Shopify and WooCommerce; all of them are on the integrations page, with setup in the payments docs.
  3. Pass the visitor ID into checkout. If you use Stripe Payment Links, Polar checkout links or Dodo Payments links, the script adds the ID on click. For a checkout you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie, for example as client_reference_id on a Stripe Checkout Session. See link checkout to visits. Without this step you get visits but no revenue.
  4. Tag the links you control. Your product page link, and the links in your posts and milestones, as above.

Check a test visit and a test payment on the dashboard before you post. Visits recorded before the script was on the page are gone.

See which post brought paying customers

  1. Pick a range from the day of the post to a few weeks after it, or a few months to judge your product page.
  2. In the Channels card, switch to the Source tab and find Indie Hackers. The row shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip that compares it with your site average.
  3. Click the row to filter the whole page. The revenue chart now shows when Indie Hackers money arrived, and the Pages card shows where those visitors landed.
  4. Switch to the Campaign tab. Each tagged post, milestone and product page has its own row with visitors and revenue.
  5. Open a payment from the Payments page to see the buyer's journey: every visit before the payment, with the credited one marked.
  6. Log a bigger post on the Launches page with its name, link, channel and day. Virling marks it on the revenue chart and shows the visitors, new customers and revenue of that day and the day after. Those two days count all traffic, not only Indie Hackers, so use them as context and the Source row for the credit.

Compare the default model, last non-direct, with first touch over the same range. If Indie Hackers revenue grows under first touch, readers found you there and paid after coming back through another channel such as search. Switch the model in Site settings; history is recomputed, and you can switch back. Only visits inside the lookback window count, 90 days by default; see the model and lookback window. The cookie lives in one browser, so a reader who finds you on a laptop and pays from a phone cannot be tied to Indie Hackers.

If you also launch on Product Hunt, Product Hunt launch revenue covers that day the same way, and both sites stay separate rows in Communities. Which Hacker News visits pay does the same for a Show HN post. If you share your numbers in milestones, a public dashboard with revenue by channel gives readers a live page to check, and the build in public revenue post template writes the update.

What to check when the numbers look wrong

  • Indie Hackers looks small but Direct jumped the same day. Clicks with no referrer and no tag. Tag the links you control next time.
  • An Indie Hackers campaign shows up in Referral. The source is not one Virling knows, such as ih. Use indiehackers.
  • Visits but no revenue at all. Check the Matched revenue line under the Overview numbers. If a large share of revenue is unmatched, the visitor ID is not reaching checkout. See Unattributed revenue.
  • A buyer says they found you on Indie Hackers but the payment is credited elsewhere. A later non-direct visit took the credit under the default model, the visit is outside the lookback window, or they paid from another device. Try first touch.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see revenue from Indie Hackers?

Add the tracking script, pass the visitor ID into checkout and connect your payment provider. Visits from indiehackers.com land in the Communities channel as Indie Hackers, and each payment is credited with your attribution model, so the Indie Hackers row shows the visitors and revenue it brought over any range you pick.

Can I tell which Indie Hackers post brought a customer?

Not from the referrer alone. Posts, milestones, product pages and comments all share the indiehackers.com host, and the referrer carries only the host. Give each link its own utm_campaign, and utm_content if you want the place inside the post, and the Campaign tab shows each one separately.

Which utm_source should I use for Indie Hackers?

Use indiehackers. Virling knows it as a Communities source and puts it on the same Indie Hackers row as the indiehackers.com referrer. A short tag such as ih is not known, so it would land in Referral as its own row. When a referrer is present it wins over the tag.

Why does Direct go up after I post on Indie Hackers?

Readers often close the tab and come back later by typing your URL or from a bookmark, and some clicks arrive with no referrer at all. Both count as Direct. With the default model, last non-direct, a later Direct visit does not take the credit from the Indie Hackers visit.

Does Indie Hackers get credit if the visitor pays weeks later?

Yes, if the Indie Hackers visit is inside the lookback window, 90 days by default, and no later non-direct visit such as a search took the credit. Switch the model to first touch to credit the visit that introduced the buyer, whatever came after.
