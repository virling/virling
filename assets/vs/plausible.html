Virling vs Plausible

Plausible is a privacy-friendly analytics tool for traffic. Virling focuses on revenue: it reads your payments and joins each one to the visit before it.

When Plausible is the better fit

You mainly need clean, privacy-friendly traffic stats and do not sell online, or your team already relies on Plausible's reports.

When Virling is the better fit

You sell a product or subscription and want payments, renewals and refunds read for you and credited by channel, from Stripe, Paddle, Shopify, WooCommerce, Dodo Payments, Polar, LemonSqueezy, Creem or RevenueCat, on your own server.

Which one fits a SaaS, a store or an agency

SaaS

Plausible is the better fit if you want to see which sources bring signups and first purchases, with no cookies. A revenue goal sent from your success page credits the first payment to the source of that visit. Its docs suggest your billing tool for MRR, churn and refunds.

Virling is the better fit if your revenue is mostly renewals. It reads them from your billing provider, keeps them on the channel of the first payment, takes refunds off, and shows retention by first channel.

Online store

Plausible is the better fit if you want simple traffic and purchase numbers. Its WordPress plugin tracks WooCommerce carts, purchases and revenue without code, and a Shopify store uses a custom pixel. Virling does not report products or carts.

Virling is the better fit if you want the orders themselves, with refunds, read from Shopify or WooCommerce with a read-only key, also when you self-host.

Agency

Plausible is the better fit if clients want plain traffic reports. Its agency guide covers a team per client, guest logins, shared links with a password and a view across all client sites, and email reports can go to as many people as you want. Virling has no roles other than owner and member.

Virling is the better fit if clients ask which channel paid. An Agency account sends white-label weekly reports on paying customers by channel and gives each client a report link.

Switching from Plausible, or running both

What to install

You can install Virling beside Plausible first and remove Plausible later, or keep it.

  • Add your site in the app, or with add-site on your own server, and put the Virling script in your page head.
  • Connect your payment provider with a key. Virling reads the last 90 days of payments (60 for Shopify, one-time purchases only for RevenueCat), then new ones every minute, or by webhook for RevenueCat.
  • Pass the visitor ID into checkout. Stripe Payment Links, Polar, Dodo Payments and LemonSqueezy links get it automatically; a checkout you create on your server needs one field.
  • Shopify and WooCommerce each need a short snippet that stores the visitor ID on the order, in the store guide.
  • Change the events you still want to virling("goal", "signup"). Goals keep their name only, without properties. See goals. The compatibility script does not read Plausible calls.

What history carries over

  • Daily traffic, if you self-host Virling. Make the full export in your Plausible site settings, unzip it, stop the server and run virling import-plausible example.com ./plausible-export. See the import guide. Hosted accounts cannot use it.
  • The import brings visitors, visits, the daily chart, sources, pages, countries and devices. Only days before the first event Virling tracked are kept, and imported visitors are added up day by day.
  • Payments from the backfill. They count toward revenue, but they are usually Unattributed, because Virling was not on your site when those visits happened.

What does not carry over

  • Goals, custom events, revenue and custom properties. Journeys, funnels and filters in Virling use tracked visits only.
  • Data you imported into Plausible from elsewhere, because the full export leaves it out. Virling can import GA4 exports, but a new import replaces the earlier one, so choose one of the two.
  • Attribution of old payments. Virling only credits a payment to a visit it tracked. Renewals of a subscription that started before you connected are usually Unattributed too.
  • The cookieless setup. Virling credits payments through a first-party cookie. Its cookieless mode exists, but new payments become Unattributed.

Running both

The two scripts are independent. Plausible uses no cookies and Virling sets one, so a site that runs both needs to treat Virling as the script that uses a cookie. With a consent banner, Virling can start cookieless and switch on consent.

Expect different totals. Plausible counts the purchase events sent to it, and Virling counts payments at the provider after refunds. If you import Plausible history, do it after the Virling script is live, so the days line up.

Frequently asked questions

Does Plausible show revenue by channel?

Yes, from goals you send with a value. Revenue goals are a cloud feature, not part of the Community Edition, and renewals and refunds are not connected to a visit. Virling reads your payment provider and shows revenue per visitor by channel.

What does it take to self-host Plausible and Virling?

Plausible Community Edition needs PostgreSQL and ClickHouse. Virling is one Rust binary with embedded storage, so there is no database to run.

When is Plausible the better fit?

When you mainly need clean, privacy-friendly traffic stats and do not sell online, or your team already relies on its reports.

Can I import Plausible history into Virling?

Yes, when self-hosting. The import-plausible command reads the CSV export and adds daily traffic from before your first tracked event. Goals and revenue are not imported, and hosted accounts cannot use the command.

Does Virling work without cookies, like Plausible?

It has a cookieless mode, but without a visitor ID new payments cannot be linked to a visit and count as Unattributed. Plausible does not use cookies.
