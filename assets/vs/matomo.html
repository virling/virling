Virling vs Matomo: a Matomo alternative for revenue

Matomo is a full open-source web analytics suite. Virling does one job: it reads your payments and joins each one to the visit before it, so you see which channels bring customers who pay and stay.

When Matomo is the better fit

You want a broad, GPL-licensed web analytics suite on a PHP and MySQL stack you already run, with AI assistant reports and an MCP plugin, and you are happy to send ecommerce orders from a store integration or your own tracking code.

When Virling is the better fit

You sell a product or subscription and want payments read straight from your provider, joined to the visit before them, with retention by first-payment channel and a weekly check of whether AI assistants name you. All in one binary with no database server.

Which one fits a SaaS, a store or an agency

SaaS

Matomo is the better fit if you want one suite for product and marketing pages. Its feature list includes funnels, cohorts, A/B testing, heatmaps and a tag manager, some as paid plugins when you host it yourself.

Virling is the better fit if the question is which channel brings subscribers who stay. It reads your billing provider, keeps renewals on the channel of the first payment and shows retention by that channel. Matomo cohorts start from the first visit.

Online store

Matomo is the better fit if you want product reports, cart abandonment and sales by referrer. It lists a WooCommerce plugin and a Shopify pixel guide, so a store on those platforms does not start from scratch. Virling does not report products or carts.

Virling is the better fit if you want the orders themselves, with refunds, read from Shopify or WooCommerce with a read-only key and credited to the visit before them, on a server with no PHP or MySQL.

Agency

Matomo is the better fit if clients want full web analytics under your name. White Label changes the logo and colours, and On-Premise can change the product name. Scheduled reports go out daily, weekly or monthly as HTML, PDF or CSV.

Virling is the better fit if clients only ask what paid. An Agency account has white-label weekly emails and a report link per client, and self-hosting is free with every feature, with up to 3 users.

Switching from Matomo, or running both

What to install

Virling does not replace every Matomo report, so start by adding it beside Matomo.

  • Add your site in the app, or with add-site on your own server, and put the Virling script in your page head. Self-hosting needs one binary or the Docker image.
  • Connect your payment provider with a key. Virling reads the last 90 days of payments (60 for Shopify, one-time purchases only for RevenueCat), then new ones every minute, or by webhook for RevenueCat.
  • Pass the visitor ID into checkout. Stripe Payment Links, Polar, Dodo Payments and LemonSqueezy links get it automatically; a checkout you create on your server needs one field. Shopify and WooCommerce each need a short snippet that stores the visitor ID on the order, in the store guide.
  • Send the actions you care about as goals. Ecommerce tracking calls are not needed for revenue, because Virling reads the orders.

What history carries over

  • Payments from the backfill. They count toward revenue, but they are usually Unattributed, because Virling was not on your site when those visits happened.
  • Google Analytics 4 history, if you still have access to the property. Self-hosted Virling can import GA4 CSV exports as daily traffic. Hosted accounts cannot.

What does not carry over

  • Matomo visits, goals and ecommerce orders. Virling has no import for them, so keep Matomo readable for as long as you need its reports.
  • Attribution of old payments. Virling only credits a payment to a visit it tracked. Renewals of a subscription that started before you connected are usually Unattributed too.
  • Heatmaps and product reports. Virling has neither.

Running both

The scripts are independent, so both can be on every page. Matomo credits conversions to the last non-direct source by default, and so does Virling, which makes the two easy to compare for new orders.

Expect differences in totals: Matomo counts the orders it is sent, and Virling counts payments at the provider after refunds.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an open-source Matomo alternative for revenue attribution?

Yes. Virling is open source under AGPLv3. It reads payments from Stripe, Paddle, Shopify, WooCommerce, Dodo Payments, Polar, LemonSqueezy, Creem or RevenueCat and credits each one to the visit before it, with no ecommerce tracking code.

What does it take to self-host Matomo and Virling?

Matomo 6 needs a web server, PHP 8.1 or newer and MySQL 8.0 or MariaDB 10.6 or newer. Virling is one Rust binary with embedded storage.

When is Matomo the better fit?

When you want a broad web analytics suite on a PHP and MySQL stack you already run, and you are happy to send ecommerce orders from a store integration or your own code.

Can I import Matomo data into Virling?

No. Virling imports only Plausible and Google Analytics 4 exports, and only when self-hosting. Payments from the last 90 days, 60 for Shopify, are read from your provider.

Can I run Matomo and Virling on the same site?

Yes. The scripts are independent. Matomo reports on the whole site, and Virling reads your payments and credits each one to the visit before it.
