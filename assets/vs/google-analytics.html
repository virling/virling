Virling vs Google Analytics

Google Analytics is free, but revenue by channel comes from purchase events you send, and credit only reaches visits inside its lookback window. Virling reads the payments themselves.

When Google Analytics is the better fit

You run Google Ads and want to bid on your key events, measure a website and an app together, or want a free tool and have time for tag setup.

When Virling is the better fit

You want revenue by channel from your payment provider in minutes, renewals credited to the channel that won the customer, and your data on your own server.

Which one fits a SaaS, a store or an agency

SaaS

Google Analytics is the better fit if you have a website and an app and want one journey across both, or if a developer can send purchase events for every plan and renewal.

Virling is the better fit if your revenue is subscriptions. It reads them from Stripe, Paddle and the other providers, keeps renewals on the channel of the first payment, and shows retention by first channel.

Online store

Google Analytics is the better fit if you spend on Google Ads: linking the two lets you create Google Ads conversions from your key events for bidding. It also measures item views, carts and promotions, which Virling does not report.

Virling is the better fit if you want order revenue by channel without tagging each step. It reads Shopify or WooCommerce orders and refunds with a read-only key, and the store needs one script and one short snippet.

Agency

Google Analytics is the better fit if clients already have it, run Google Ads, or want raw events in BigQuery.

Virling is the better fit if clients ask which channel brought paying customers. An Agency account sends white-label weekly reports and gives each client a report link, with no login to manage.

Switching from Google Analytics, or running both

What to install

You can add Virling without touching your tags.

  • Add your site in the app, or with add-site on your own server, and put the Virling script in your page head.
  • Connect your payment provider with a key. Virling reads the last 90 days of payments (60 for Shopify, one-time purchases only for RevenueCat), then new ones every minute, or by webhook for RevenueCat.
  • Pass the visitor ID into checkout. Stripe Payment Links, Polar, Dodo Payments and LemonSqueezy links get it automatically; a checkout you create on your server needs one field.
  • Shopify and WooCommerce each need a short snippet that stores the visitor ID on the order, in the store guide.

What history carries over

  • Daily traffic. Self-hosted Virling can import GA4 CSV exports: visitors, visits and views by day, and sources, pages, countries and devices. The import runs on the command line, so hosted accounts cannot use it.
  • Only days before the first event Virling tracked, so a day is never counted twice. Imported visitors are added up day by day.
  • Payments from the backfill. They count toward revenue, but they are usually Unattributed, because Virling was not on your site when those visits happened.

What does not carry over

  • Revenue, purchase events and conversions recorded in Google Analytics. Imported days have no visitors to match, so payments are only credited to tracked visits.
  • Journeys, goals and funnels. Filters show tracked visits only.
  • Attribution of old payments. Virling only credits a payment to a visit it tracked. Renewals of a subscription that started before you connected are usually Unattributed too.
  • Explorations older than your retention setting. Google limits explorations, not standard reports to that period, so export what you need before it goes.

Running both

Running both is a sensible default. Keep Google Analytics for ad bidding and app data, and use Virling for paid revenue by channel. Each keeps its own visitor ID, and neither needs the other.

Totals will differ. Google Analytics counts the purchase events it receives and Virling counts payments at the provider, after refunds. The attribution settings are separate too: see Virling's model and lookback.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see revenue by channel without Google Analytics ecommerce setup?

Use a tool that reads the payments themselves. Virling reads Stripe, Paddle, Shopify, WooCommerce, Dodo Payments, Polar, LemonSqueezy, Creem or RevenueCat with a key and needs no event code.

Does Google Analytics credit a renewal to the channel that won the customer?

Not if that visit is older than its lookback window, which is 90 days by default and can be 30 or 60. User-scoped dimensions such as First user medium are separate. Virling keeps renewals on the channel of the first payment.

When is Google Analytics the better fit?

When you run Google Ads and want to bid on your key events, measure a website and an app together, or want a free tool and have time for tag setup.

Can I import Google Analytics history into Virling?

Yes, when self-hosting. The import-ga command reads GA4 CSV exports with a Date column and adds daily traffic from before your first tracked event. Revenue is not imported, and hosted accounts cannot use the command.

Can I run Virling and Google Analytics together?

Yes. The scripts are independent. Keep Google Analytics for ad bidding and app data, and use Virling to read payments and credit them to visits.
