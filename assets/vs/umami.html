Virling vs Umami

Umami is a free, open-source tool for traffic and events. Virling focuses on revenue: it reads your payments and joins each one to the visit before it.

When Umami is the better fit

You want a free, MIT-licensed tool for traffic and custom events, on a database you already run, and you are happy to send revenue events from your own code.

When Virling is the better fit

You sell a product or subscription and want payments read automatically and joined to the visit before them, with revenue per visitor by channel.

Frequently asked questions

Does Umami track revenue?

Umami has a built-in revenue report fed by the events you send; see its docs for how amounts arrive. Virling reads Stripe, Paddle, Shopify, WooCommerce, Dodo Payments, Polar, LemonSqueezy, Creem or RevenueCat directly with no event code.

What does it take to self-host Umami and Virling?

Umami needs a PostgreSQL or MySQL database. Virling is one Rust binary with embedded storage.

When is Umami the better fit?

When you want a free, MIT-licensed tool for traffic and custom events on a database you already run, and you are happy to send revenue events from your own code.
