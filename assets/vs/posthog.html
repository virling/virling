When PostHog is the better fit

You want product analytics, session replay, experiments and feature flags in one tool, and revenue is one of many questions you ask.

When Virling is the better fit

Your main question is which channel brings paying customers. You want payments read for you, revenue per visitor by channel, and one small server to run.

Which one fits a SaaS, a store or an agency

SaaS

PostHog is the better fit if one team wants product analytics, session replay, experiments and feature flags in one place, bills through Stripe and can write queries. Its revenue metrics sit next to product data, and it has an AI channel for visits from assistants.

Virling is the better fit if you bill through Paddle, Polar, LemonSqueezy, Dodo Payments, Creem or RevenueCat, since PostHog's revenue analytics currently supports Stripe only as a payment platform. Virling also keeps renewals on the channel of the first payment without a query.

Online store

PostHog is the better fit if you want orders in a data warehouse to query beside on-site behavior. It has connectors for Shopify and WooCommerce.

Virling is the better fit if you want a ready report of orders and refunds by channel. It reads either store with a read-only key, and the store needs one script and one short snippet.

Agency

PostHog is the better fit if you build custom dashboards for product teams. Dashboards can be shared by link with a password or embedded, and each client can be a separate project on a paid plan.

Virling is the better fit if clients ask which channel paid. An Agency account sends white-label weekly reports and gives each client a report link. Self-hosted accounts include up to 3 users.

Switching from PostHog, or running both

What to install

Virling covers one part of what PostHog does, so most teams will add it rather than switch.

  • Add your site in the app, or with add-site on your own server, and put the Virling script in your page head.
  • Connect your payment provider with a key. Virling reads the last 90 days of payments (60 for Shopify, one-time purchases only for RevenueCat), then new ones every minute, or by webhook for RevenueCat.
  • Pass the visitor ID into checkout. Stripe Payment Links, Polar, Dodo Payments and LemonSqueezy links get it automatically; a checkout you create on your server needs one field.
  • Shopify and WooCommerce each need a short snippet that stores the visitor ID on the order, in the store guide.
  • Change the events you still want to virling("goal", "signup"). Goals keep their name only, without properties. See goals. The compatibility script does not read PostHog calls.

What history carries over

  • Payments from the backfill. They count toward revenue, but they are usually Unattributed, because Virling was not on your site when those visits happened.
  • Google Analytics 4 history, if you still have access to the property. Self-hosted Virling can import GA4 CSV exports as daily traffic. Hosted accounts cannot.

What does not carry over

  • PostHog events, persons, recordings, flags and dashboards. Its API can export events and persons as files, but Virling has no import for them.
  • The customer link you set in Stripe. PostHog matches customers with a person ID in Stripe customer metadata; Virling needs its own visitor ID at checkout. The two can sit side by side.
  • Attribution of old payments. Virling only credits a payment to a visit it tracked. Renewals of a subscription that started before you connected are usually Unattributed too.

Running both

Keep PostHog for the product and use Virling for paid revenue by channel. PostHog stores its ID in local storage and its own cookie by default, and Virling uses virling_vid, so the scripts do not share an ID.

Revenue will not match to the cent. PostHog spreads a Stripe charge across the months it covers, and Virling counts each payment when it happens, after refunds.

Frequently asked questions

Is Virling a simpler alternative to PostHog for revenue?

Virling does one job: it reads your payments and shows which channels bring paying customers. PostHog is a broad product analytics platform whose revenue analytics reads Stripe data through its data warehouse, which its docs call beta.

Can I self-host PostHog and Virling?

Yes. PostHog's hobby deployment runs with Docker Compose on a Linux server, and its docs ask for 4 vCPU and 16 GB of memory. Virling is one Rust binary with embedded storage and no Postgres, ClickHouse or Redis.

When is PostHog the better fit?

When you want product analytics, session replay, experiments and feature flags in one tool, and revenue is one of many questions.

Can I import PostHog data into Virling?

No. PostHog can export events, but Virling imports only Plausible and Google Analytics 4 exports, and only when self-hosting. Payments from the last 90 days, 60 for Shopify, are read from your provider.

Can I run PostHog and Virling together?

Yes. The scripts use separate IDs. Keep PostHog for product analytics, replay and flags, and use Virling to read payments and credit each one to the visit before it.
