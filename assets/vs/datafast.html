When DataFast is the better fit

You want a hosted product with a mobile app, a Shopify connection that installs its own tracking, or Google Search Console keywords next to your traffic.

When Virling is the better fit

You want the code and the data on your own server, you use Creem or RevenueCat and want payments read with a key, or you want one binary with no database to manage.

Which one fits a SaaS, a store or an agency

SaaS

DataFast is the better fit if you want a hosted product with a phone app, a React Native SDK, or Google Search Console keywords beside your visits. It connects natively to Stripe, LemonSqueezy and Polar.

Virling is the better fit if you want to run the code on your own server, bill through Creem or RevenueCat, or want each renewal kept on the channel of the first payment and retention by that channel.

Online store

DataFast is the better fit for a Shopify or WooCommerce store that wants the least setup. Its Shopify connection adds the tracking for you and records add to cart and checkout events, and its WooCommerce plugin does the same.

Virling is the better fit if you want read-only access to orders and the data on your own server. It needs one script and one short snippet per store, and counts refunds.

Agency

DataFast is the better fit if a plan with 30 websites and 30 team members covers you, as its Growth tier does.

Virling is the better fit if clients need branded reports: an Agency account has white-label weekly emails and a report link per client. Self-hosting is free with every feature, with up to 3 users.

Switching from DataFast, or running both

What to install

The two minute switch keeps your goal calls, goal buttons and checkout code.

  • Add your site in the app, or with add-site on your own server, and put the Virling script in your page head. Replace the DataFast tag with it, and load the compatibility script right after it.
  • Connect your payment provider with a key. Virling reads the last 90 days of payments (60 for Shopify, one-time purchases only for RevenueCat), then new ones every minute, or by webhook for RevenueCat.
  • Leave the checkout code that sends datafast_visitor_id. The compatibility script sets that cookie to the Virling visitor ID, and Virling reads the key on Stripe, Dodo Payments and Polar metadata and LemonSqueezy custom data. Stores need their snippet instead.

What history carries over

  • Payments from the backfill. They count toward revenue, but they are usually Unattributed, because Virling was not on your site when those visits happened.
  • Goal names, because your datafast() calls and data-fast-goal buttons keep working.
  • Plausible history, if you still have the export you imported there. Self-hosted Virling can import the same export from the command line. Hosted accounts cannot.

What does not carry over

  • Visits, goals and journeys recorded by DataFast. That history stays with DataFast.
  • Attribution of old payments. Virling only credits a payment to a visit it tracked. Renewals of a subscription that started before you connected are usually Unattributed too.
  • Goal parameters, the DataFast session ID, scroll events and user identification. Virling keeps the goal name only.

Running both

Both scripts can be on the page while you compare, because each sets its own cookie. Do not load the compatibility script yet: it would put the Virling visitor ID in the DataFast cookie.

In checkout, send virling_vid in the metadata next to datafast_visitor_id. Virling reads virling_vid first, and falls back to the other key only when it is absent. See link checkout to visits.

Frequently asked questions

Is Virling an open-source alternative to DataFast?

Yes. Virling is open source under AGPLv3 and can be self-hosted as one Rust binary with embedded storage. DataFast's docs describe a hosted service and do not mention a self-hosted version.

Which payment providers does Virling read?

Stripe, Paddle, Shopify, WooCommerce, Dodo Payments, Polar, LemonSqueezy, Creem and RevenueCat, read with a key and no event code.

When is DataFast the better fit?

When you want a hosted product with a mobile app, a Shopify connection that installs its own tracking, or Google Search Console keywords next to your traffic.

Do I have to rewrite my DataFast goals and checkout code?

No. Virling's compatibility script keeps datafast() goal calls, data-fast-goal buttons and the datafast_visitor_id cookie working. Goal parameters are dropped, so goals keep their name only.

Can I import my DataFast history into Virling?

No. Your visits and goals stay with DataFast. Virling reads the last 90 days from your payment provider, 60 for Shopify, and those payments are usually Unattributed.
