integrations / woocommerce

WooCommerce orders, joined to the visits behind them.

Virling ties each WooCommerce order to the visit that came before it, so your store sees which channel, campaign and landing page bring buyers, not just traffic. Open source, one Rust binary, or hosted.

Start free trial See the demo

Virling reads WooCommerce orders, their refunds and, with WooCommerce Subscriptions, subscriptions from your store's REST API with a read-only key.

Create a key

  1. In WordPress, open WooCommerce, Settings, Advanced, REST API, and add a key. WooCommerce's guide: REST API.
  2. Set Permissions to Read.
  3. In Virling, paste the consumer key and the consumer secret joined by a colon, as ck_...:cs_..., and enter your store's address, such as https://shop.example.com.

The store must be reachable over https, with pretty permalinks on so /wp-json/wc/v3/ answers.

What is imported

  • Orders from the last 90 days on the first run, then new ones every minute, newest first. Processing and completed orders count as paid, failed and cancelled ones as failed. Pending and on-hold orders wait until they are paid.
  • Refunds. Each order's refunds lower its revenue. Orders from the last 60 days are read again every 6 hours, so later refunds show up.
  • Renewals. With WooCommerce Subscriptions, a renewal order is marked as a renewal and keeps the channel of the first payment. See Renewals.
  • Subscription status, once an hour, when WooCommerce Subscriptions is installed. Without it, Retention uses payment recency.

Guest orders have no customer ID, so each counts as its own customer.

How a payment finds its visitor

Virling reads virling_vid from the order's meta data. Add this to your theme's functions.php, or to a snippets plugin, so every new order stores the visitor ID from the virling_vid cookie. It covers the classic and the block checkout:

// WooCommerce: add to your theme's functions.php (or a snippets plugin)
// so every order keeps the visitor ID from the virling_vid cookie.
add_action('woocommerce_checkout_create_order', 'virling_vid_on_order');
add_action('woocommerce_store_api_checkout_update_order_from_request', 'virling_vid_on_order');
function virling_vid_on_order($order) {
  $vid = $_COOKIE['virling_vid'] ?? '';
  if (preg_match('/^[A-Za-z0-9-]{8,64}$/', $vid)) $order->update_meta_data('virling_vid', $vid);
}

How each order type is credited

An account's first paid order is credited on its own. Any later order by the same account keeps the first order's channel when that was credited, whichever way it was placed:

Subscription renewalThe account's customer ID. An order WooCommerce Subscriptions marks as a renewal is marked as one in VirlingKeeps the channel of the account's first paid order. If that order was not credited, is older than the first import or was a $0 trial, usually Unattributed

Every order in the table counts toward revenue once it is Processing or Completed, credited or not. Pending and on-hold orders wait until they are paid, and an order with a total of 0 is not imported.

Check it with a test order

  1. Add the functions.php snippet, and put your payment gateway in its test or sandbox mode, or turn on Cash on delivery.
  2. Open a private window and visit your store through a tagged link, such as https://shop.example.com/?utm_source=test&utm_medium=email.
  3. In the same window, place a test order as a customer would.
  4. In WordPress, open the order and check that its custom fields include virling_vid and that its status is Processing or Completed.
  5. Wait a minute for the next sync, then open the payments list at /app/yourdomain.com/payments. The payment shows the test source and the Email channel of your tagged visit instead of Unattributed. An order paid more than about 10 minutes after it was created can take up to 6 hours to show.
  6. Refund the order so it does not stay in your revenue. The refund shows within 6 hours.

Use a new account for each test, or check out as a guest: a later order by the same account is a renewal and keeps the first order's channel. Then repeat the test with each gateway and express button you offer. If a step fails, see Test your setup.

What you can see

Once orders come in, these cards on the dashboard answer questions a seller asks:

  • Which channels bring revenue, not only visits? The Channels card lists visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor for each channel, with Source and Campaign tabs for the site, post or tagged campaign behind it.
  • Which landing pages do buyers arrive on? The Pages card shows revenue by the first page of the visit, and by the last page before paying.
  • Which channel's orders get refunded? The Channels card also shows Refunded, the share of a row's payments that were refunded in whole or part.
  • Who bought most recently, and from where? The Recent customers card shows the last 10 paying customers with amount, first source, visits before paying and time to convert.
  • How long do buyers take to order? The Time to pay card shows the median time from first visit to first payment for each channel with at least three first payments in the range. Each guest order counts as a first payment.

Who uses this

WooCommerce store owners see which channel brings orders and revenue, and which first source brings repeat buyers (see Virling for ecommerce). Agencies that report revenue by channel to clients who sell through WooCommerce can share it with them (see Virling for agencies).

  • Stores on WooCommerce Subscriptions: first orders and renewals by the channel that brought the subscriber.
  • Membership sites on WooCommerce: first payments and renewals by the channel that brought the member.
  • Stores that sell downloads: ebook, template, preset and course orders by channel, campaign and post.
  • Online courses sold through WooCommerce: course orders by the video, email or post that brought the buyer.

What stays Unattributed

These orders still count toward revenue, but no channel is credited, unless they match by email as below or the same customer has an earlier order that was credited:

  • Orders with no virling_vid in their meta data because the snippet was missing or the visitor had no cookie.
  • Orders created in the admin or by another system, and orders from a gateway or express button that runs outside the two checkout hooks.
  • Orders from before the snippet was on your store, which usually includes the 90-day import.
  • Orders with a visitor ID when no visit of that visitor falls inside the lookback window.
  • Most renewals whose first payment is Unattributed, older than the first import, or a $0 free trial order, which is not imported.

By email: an order with no visitor ID is credited when its billing email exactly matches one your site sent as an identify event before the order synced, and it shows Matched by email. To find the cause for one order, see Unattributed revenue.

Frequently asked questions

How do I see which channel a WooCommerce order came from?

Connect your store to Virling with a read-only REST API key and add a short snippet to functions.php that saves the visitor ID on each order. Virling reads virling_vid from the order meta data and ties the order to the visit before it.

Does it work with the WooCommerce block checkout?

Yes. The snippet covers both the classic checkout and the block checkout.

Does Virling track WooCommerce Subscriptions renewals?

Yes. With WooCommerce Subscriptions installed, renewal orders are marked as renewals and keep the channel of the first payment.

Do express checkout buttons keep the visitor ID?

They do when the order goes through the classic or the block checkout, since the snippet hooks both. A gateway or express button that creates orders outside those two hooks is not covered, so place a test order with each one and check for virling_vid.

Are WooCommerce refunds taken off the channel?

Yes. A full or partial refund lowers the revenue of its order, on that order's date, so the channel credited with the order loses it too. Orders created in the last 60 days are read again every 6 hours, so a refund can take that long to show, and one on an older order is not picked up automatically.

What happens to WooCommerce orders made in the admin?

They have no virling_vid, because the snippet runs at checkout. Once paid they count toward revenue as Unattributed, unless the billing email matches one your site sent as an identify event, or the same account has an earlier order that was credited, whose channel the later order keeps.

Do WooCommerce guest orders link to a visit?

Yes. The snippet saves the visitor ID on the order itself, so no account is needed. Each guest order counts as its own customer, so a guest's next order is credited to its own visit.

Why has a paid WooCommerce order not shown up yet?

New orders are read every minute, but pending and on-hold orders wait until they are paid. An order paid more than about 10 minutes after it was created can take up to 6 hours to show, when orders created in the last 60 days are read again. An order with a total of 0, such as a free trial, is not imported.
