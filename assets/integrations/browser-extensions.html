Revenue attribution for browser extensions

Virling joins each upgrade of your paid Chrome or Firefox extension to the visit that came before it. The path runs from a channel, such as a launch post, a search or a creator's video, to your site, then to your store listing, an install, and later an upgrade on a checkout page on your own domain. Virling shows which channel at the start of that path brought paying users, not just installs. Works with Stripe, LemonSqueezy, Polar, Creem and Dodo Payments. Open source, one Rust binary, or hosted.

An extension store tells you how many people installed. Your payment provider tells you who paid. Neither knows which post or search sent them. Virling sets a visitor ID when someone lands on your site, keeps it in a first-party cookie on your domain for one year, and matches it when that person later pays on a checkout page on your domain.

How the path links up

  • The first visit. Someone lands on your site from a launch post, a search or a tagged link. The visit gets one channel, source and campaign from its first pageview, and the visitor gets a virling_vid cookie.
  • The store listing. A click from your site to your Chrome Web Store or Firefox Add-ons listing is recorded as an exit, with the link's origin and path.
  • The upgrade. The upgrade button in your extension opens a pricing or checkout page on your domain in a new tab. In the same browser the cookie is still there, so the visit carries the same visitor ID.
  • The payment. Checkout carries the visitor ID, and Virling credits the payment to one of that visitor's sessions in the lookback window: 30, 60, 90 or 365 days, 90 by default.

With the default model, last non-direct, a Direct visit right before the payment does not take the credit from the launch post or search that came before it. Leave UTM tags off the link your extension opens: a tagged visit is not Direct, so it would take the credit itself. First touch and last touch are the other models.

What links and what does not

  • Links: visits to your own site, and checkouts opened from pages on your domain that run the script. The visitor ID is first-party, so subdomains such as app.yourdomain.com share it.
  • Does not link: installs straight from a store search. The person never visited your site before installing, so the first visit Virling sees is the upgrade page the extension opened, and the payment is credited to that visit, not to the store search.
  • Does not link: the install itself, and anything inside the extension's own pages. The script keeps events only from pages on your domain and its subdomains, so put it on your site, not in the extension.
  • Does not link: an upgrade in another browser or on another device, which has its own cookie and a new visitor ID.
  • Unattributed: payments whose checkout carried no visitor ID, for example when the script was blocked, the site runs in cookieless mode, or a payment link was opened from an email. They still count toward revenue.

If users sign in on your site, you can send their email once as an identify event. A Stripe payment that arrives with no visitor ID and a matching payer email is then credited to that visitor. See Unattributed revenue.

Connect your payment provider

  • Stripe: Payment Links on buy.stripe.com need no code: the script adds client_reference_id. For Checkout Sessions, pass the visitor ID as client_reference_id. Step by step: extension revenue tracking with Stripe.
  • LemonSqueezy: checkout links need no code: the script adds the visitor ID as checkout custom data. Virling registers a webhook on each store to read it, which needs a public https address.
  • Polar: checkout links on buy.polar.sh need no code; checkout sessions take the visitor ID in metadata.
  • Creem: pass the visitor ID in checkout metadata. Virling adds one checkout.completed webhook to read it.
  • Dodo Payments: static payment links need no code; checkout sessions take the visitor ID in metadata.

Checkout links must be normal links on a page of your site that runs the script. Each provider's payments from the last 90 days are imported on the first run, then new ones every minute. If you sell a subscription, renewals keep the channel of the first payment; see Virling for SaaS. To see how many trial users each channel turns into paying users, try the trial to paid calculator.

Why a sale is Unattributed

  • The extension opens checkout itself. The script does not run in the extension, so nothing adds the visitor ID. Fix: open an upgrade page on your own domain, and put the checkout link there as a normal link.
  • Your server creates the checkout without the visitor ID. Fix: read the virling_vid cookie from the request your upgrade page makes and pass it into checkout.
  • A checkout link was opened from an email. Fix: link the email to your upgrade page instead.
  • The script was missing or blocked. Fix: put it on the landing page, the upgrade page and every other page. A browser that blocks it records no visit to credit. When only checkout cannot carry the visitor ID on Stripe, an identify event sent from your site can match the payer by email.
  • Cookieless mode is on. Without the cookie there is no visitor ID to pass on. Fix: set Visitor cookie to Cookie in site settings and take data-cookieless off the script tag. If you need consent first, use Until consent: visitors who accept can be credited.
  • The webhook is missing. On your own server, LemonSqueezy and Creem need Virling on a public address to return the visitor ID. Fix: set VIRLING_BASE_URL to a public https:// address, restart Virling and connect the key again. The hosted plan already is.
  • It renews an Unattributed first payment. The same goes for a subscription that began before the 90-day import. Fix: none for that customer; new customers and their renewals are credited.

A buyer who upgrades in another browser or on another device is usually not Unattributed: the upgrade page they open is a new visit, so the sale is in Direct. Unattributed sales still count toward revenue, and a fix usually applies only to new ones.

Frequently asked questions

Can Virling track installs from the Chrome Web Store or Firefox Add-ons?

No. Virling tracks visits to your own site and the payments made from it. A click from your site to your store listing is recorded as an exit, but the install itself happens in the store, which Virling does not see.

Can the upgrade button inside my extension be attributed?

Yes, when it opens a checkout page on your own domain that runs the Virling script. The visitor ID cookie belongs to your domain, so a user who visited your site before installing arrives with the same ID, and the payment is credited to their earlier visit within the lookback window.

What happens to users who installed straight from a store search?

They never visited your site before installing, so the first visit Virling sees is the upgrade page the extension opened. The payment is credited to that visit, not to the store search that led to the install.

Which payment providers work?

Stripe, LemonSqueezy, Polar, Creem and Dodo Payments. Checkout links on buy.stripe.com, buy.polar.sh, LemonSqueezy and checkout.dodopayments.com get the visitor ID from the script with no code; Creem checkouts and checkout sessions you create on your server take it in metadata.

Is Virling open source?

Yes. Virling is open source under AGPLv3. You can run it yourself as one binary with embedded storage, or use the hosted version.

Should I add UTM tags to the upgrade link in my extension?

No. Tags would mark the upgrade visit with a source of its own, so it would no longer be Direct and, with the default model, would take the credit from the post or search that came before it. Tag the links that lead to your landing page instead, with one campaign per store listing and one tracked link per post.

How do I check that an upgrade is credited?

Create a new browser profile, load your extension in it and open your landing page from a tagged link with utm_source=test and utm_medium=test. Click the upgrade button and pay in your provider's test mode. After a minute the payment should show the test source instead of Unattributed. A private window would not do: it has its own cookies, and extensions are usually off there.
