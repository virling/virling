See which channel brings your WooCommerce subscribers.

For WooCommerce stores that sell subscription boxes, replenishment products or memberships with WooCommerce Subscriptions. Virling ties each first order and every renewal to the channel that brought the subscriber, so you compare channels by the subscribers who keep paying, not by first orders alone. Open source under AGPL, hosted or self-hosted.

WooCommerce knows who subscribed and when each renewal was paid, but not the ad, post, email or search result that brought the subscriber to your store. Renewal orders are created months later with no visit at all. Virling joins the two: a visitor ID set when someone lands, saved on the first order at checkout, and carried to every renewal after it.

What you get

  • Each first order credited to a channel. The subscriber's first order is joined to the visits before it, with its channel, source, UTM campaign and landing page. By default it goes to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct.
  • Renewals that keep the first channel. With WooCommerce Subscriptions, a renewal order is marked as a renewal and copies the channel, referrer, campaign, landing page, country and device of the customer's first payment, matched by WooCommerce customer ID. If someone subscribed after a visit from Reddit, every renewal is credited to that visit, even years later.
  • Subscription status, once an hour. Virling reads your subscriptions with the same read-only key. A subscriber counts as still paying while their subscription is active, or pending-cancel until its end date.
  • Retention by channel. The Retention page groups subscribers by the channel of their first payment, so a channel that brings many first boxes and few second ones stands out next to a smaller channel whose subscribers stay.
  • Refunds taken off. Each order's refunds lower its revenue. Orders from the last 60 days are read again every 6 hours, so later refunds show up.

Set it up

  1. Add the site. Click + Add a site, enter your store's domain and paste the script before </head> on every page. Leave cookieless mode off: without the virling_vid cookie there is no visitor ID for the order to carry.
  2. Create a read-only key. In WordPress, open WooCommerce, Settings, Advanced, REST API, add a key and set Permissions to Read. In Virling, paste it as ck_...:cs_... with your store's address. Orders from the last 90 days are imported on the first run, then new ones every minute.
  3. Save the visitor ID on each order. Add this to your theme's functions.php, or to a snippets plugin. It covers the classic and the block checkout.
add_action('woocommerce_checkout_create_order', 'virling_vid_on_order');
add_action('woocommerce_store_api_checkout_update_order_from_request', 'virling_vid_on_order');
function virling_vid_on_order($order) {
  $vid = $_COOKIE['virling_vid'] ?? '';
  if (preg_match('/^[A-Za-z0-9-]{8,64}$/', $vid)) $order->update_meta_data('virling_vid', $vid);
}

Renewal orders need nothing extra: they take their channel from the first order. The full walkthrough is in how to track WooCommerce revenue by traffic source, and subscription renewal attribution explains why renewals should keep the first channel. What to know

  • Renewal detection and subscription status need WooCommerce Subscriptions. Without it, orders are still attributed one by one and Retention uses payment recency.
  • The first payment must be in Virling. Orders older than the 90-day backfill are not imported, so renewals of subscriptions that started before that are usually Unattributed. They still count toward revenue.
  • Guest orders have no customer ID, so they cannot be linked to a first payment and each counts as its own customer.
  • Processing and completed orders count as paid, failed and cancelled ones as failed. Pending and on-hold orders wait until they are paid.
  • Customers are matched per provider. A subscriber who moved to your store from another payment provider counts as two customers.

Frequently asked questions

Which channel brings my WooCommerce subscribers?

Connect your store to Virling with a read-only REST API key and add the snippet that saves the virling_vid cookie on each order. The subscriber's first order is credited to the visits before it, and every renewal order keeps that channel, so the Channels card and the Retention page show which channels bring subscribers who keep paying.

How are WooCommerce Subscriptions renewals attributed?

A WooCommerce Subscriptions renewal order is marked as a renewal. It copies the channel, referrer, campaign, landing page, country and device of the customer's first payment, matched by WooCommerce customer ID, even years later and even though the renewal itself has no visit.

How does Virling know if a subscriber is still active?

When WooCommerce Subscriptions is installed, Virling reads subscription status once an hour with the same read-only key. A subscription counts as live when it is active, or pending-cancel until its end date. Without the plugin, Retention falls back to payment recency.

What happens to subscriptions that started before I connected Virling?

The first run imports orders from the last 90 days. A renewal whose first order is older than that has no first payment in Virling to copy, so it is usually Unattributed. It still counts toward revenue.
