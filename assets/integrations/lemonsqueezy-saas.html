See which channel brings your LemonSqueezy subscribers.

For SaaS and software products that sell subscriptions or licenses through LemonSqueezy as merchant of record. Virling ties each order and every renewal to the channel that brought the customer, so you compare channels by the customers who pay, not by signups. Open source under AGPL, hosted or self-hosted.

Start free trial See the demo

LemonSqueezy knows who pays and how much, but not the visit that brought them. Your ads, posts and search results bring the visit, and the renewal in month six never touches your site at all. Virling joins the two through the visitor ID you pass to checkout.

What you get

  • Each order credited to a channel. Orders and subscription invoices are imported and joined to the visits before them. By default a payment goes to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct; B2B buyers who take weeks to decide can be read with first touch or a 365-day window.
  • Renewals that keep the first channel. A subscription invoice with billing reason renewal is a renewal. It copies the channel, referrer, campaign, landing page, country and device of the customer's first payment, matched by LemonSqueezy customer ID, even years later. Plan changes (billing reason updated) keep that channel too.
  • Refunds taken off. Refunds do not change an order's creation time, so Virling rescans the last 60 days for refunds every 6 hours.
  • Retention and MRR by channel. The Retention page groups customers by the channel of their first payment, with how many still pay, net LTV and 30-day churn. Subscription status is read once an hour: on_trial, active, past_due, and cancelled until its ends_at, count as still paying. The same read gives each subscription's price, so MRR shows too.
  • Revenue per visitor and time to pay. The Channels card shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor per channel, and Time to pay shows the median time from first visit to first payment, so you do not cut a channel before its customers have had time to pay.

Set it up

  1. Add the site. Click + Add a site and enter your domain, such as yourproduct.com, and its timezone.
  2. Install the script. Paste the tag before </head> on your marketing site and your app. The virling_vid cookie is set on your registrable domain, so yourproduct.com and app.yourproduct.com share one visitor ID. Leave cookieless mode off.
  3. Connect LemonSqueezy. In LemonSqueezy, open Settings, API, create a key and paste it under payments. LemonSqueezy keys cannot be made read-only and cover every store on your account; Virling only reads orders, subscriptions and subscription invoices, adds one webhook to each store, and stores the key encrypted. The last 90 days are imported, then new orders and invoices every minute.
  4. Pass the visitor ID in checkout custom data. LemonSqueezy returns custom data only in webhooks, so Virling registers one for order_created and subscription_payment_success when you connect. Checkout links on STORE.lemonsqueezy.com need no code: the script adds checkout[custom][virling_vid] to them. For a checkout you create on your server with POST /v1/checkouts, set data.attributes.checkout_data.custom to { "virling_vid": "<the visitor's virling_vid cookie>" }. For a checkout URL you build in the browser:
const url = new URL("https://STORE.lemonsqueezy.com/checkout/buy/VARIANT_ID");
const vid = window.virling?.vid?.();
if (vid) url.searchParams.set("checkout[custom][virling_vid]", vid);
location.href = url.href;

The full walkthrough is in how to track LemonSqueezy revenue by traffic source, and LemonSqueezy UTM tracking covers tagging links so each order shows its campaign. To see what selling through a merchant of record costs, use the merchant of record fee calculator. Every setting is on the LemonSqueezy integration page.

Read your first month

  1. Open Channels first. Compare revenue per visitor, not visitors. The revenue includes renewals, so check New customers for what the month brought, and read the matched revenue line under the five numbers for how much is tied to a visit. Imported payments are mostly from before the script, so expect a lot of Unattributed at first.
  2. Then Retention, grouped by the channel of the first payment. Look at who still pays and at the refund rate. 30-day churn reads customers whose first payment was more than 30 and at most 60 days ago, so it says nothing yet about the ones you won this month. LemonSqueezy trial starts are not read as trials, so if you offer one, send a trial goal and judge trial customers by Channels and Payments.

What not to conclude from a handful of payments:

  • That a channel does not pay. Buyers still in a trial, or still deciding, have paid nothing yet. Check the median on Time to pay before you cut a channel.
  • That the top channel is the best one. One annual plan can put a channel first. Open its payments and count them before you move effort there.
  • That customers from a channel stay or leave. With fewer than 20 customers or fewer than 5 still paying, the Retention page writes no headline, and neither should you.

What to know

  • Orders older than the 90-day backfill are not imported, so renewals of subscriptions that started before that are Unattributed. Channel numbers fill in as new customers arrive.
  • Renewals carry no custom data of their own; they keep the channel of the subscription's first payment.
  • If you self-host, the webhook needs VIRLING_BASE_URL to be a public https:// address that LemonSqueezy can reach. Without one, no webhook is registered and LemonSqueezy payments show as Unattributed.
  • Payments without a customer ID cannot be linked to a first payment, and payments without a visitor ID still count toward revenue as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

Which channel brings my LemonSqueezy subscribers?

Connect LemonSqueezy to Virling and pass the visitor ID as checkout custom data; LemonSqueezy checkout links get it from the script automatically. Each customer's first payment is credited to the visits before it, and every renewal keeps that channel, so the Channels card and the Retention page show which channels bring subscribers who pay.

How are LemonSqueezy renewals attributed?

A subscription invoice with billing reason renewal is a renewal. It takes the channel, referrer, campaign and landing page of the customer's first payment, matched by LemonSqueezy customer ID. Plan changes keep that channel too.

Does the LemonSqueezy API key have to be read-only?

LemonSqueezy API keys cannot be made read-only and cover every store on your account. Virling reads orders, subscriptions and subscription invoices, adds a webhook to each store, and stores the key encrypted. Delete the key in LemonSqueezy at any time to revoke access.

Why does Virling need a webhook for LemonSqueezy?

LemonSqueezy only returns checkout custom data in webhooks, and the visitor ID travels in custom data. Virling registers a webhook for order_created and subscription_payment_success on each store when you connect, and checks each one against its X-Signature before attaching the visitor ID.

What about LemonSqueezy subscriptions that began before Virling was connected?

Virling imports orders and subscription invoices from the last 90 days. When a subscription's first order is older than that, its earliest payment in Virling stands in, and it rarely has a visit, so its renewals are usually Unattributed. An order inside the 90 days but from before the webhook is Unattributed too. All of them count toward revenue, but a customer whose earliest payment in Virling is a renewal is left out of Retention.
