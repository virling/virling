See which post, search or AI answer brings your paying users on Paddle.

For founders of AI apps and GPT wrappers who sell subscriptions or credits through Paddle. Virling ties each paying user to the channel that brought them, including referrals from ChatGPT, Perplexity and other AI answers, so you see which channel brings the users who pay, not only the ones who sign up and spend free credits. Open source under AGPL, hosted or self-hosted.

An AI app often gives new users free credits, then charges for a plan or a credit pack. A launch post can bring hundreds of signups who spend the free credits and leave, while an answer in ChatGPT may send a few users who keep paying. Paddle knows who paid, but not the visit that brought them. Virling joins the two with a read-only Paddle API key and the visitor ID in checkout custom data.

What you get

  • Each paying user credited to a channel. The customer's first payment is joined to the visits before it. By default it goes to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct, so a user who found you on Reddit and came back later by typing your address is credited to Reddit.
  • AI answers on their own rows. Visits from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, Copilot and DeepSeek get the AI channel, search engines get Organic search, and X, Reddit, Product Hunt and YouTube keep their own referrer rows.
  • Renewals that keep the first channel. A transaction whose origin is subscription_recurring, subscription_update or subscription_charge is a renewal. It copies the channel, referrer, campaign and landing page of the customer's first payment, matched by Paddle customer ID. Plan changes count as renewals too.
  • Credit packs that keep the first channel. A customer's first credit pack is credited to the visits before it when its checkout carries virling_vid. A later one is a renewal, like any payment after the first, and keeps that channel.
  • Free credits and trials kept out of revenue. Virling counts completed transactions, not signups or usage. A subscription's first transaction with nothing to pay starts a trial, is never counted as revenue, a payment or a customer, and the customer's later payments are credited to it.
  • Refunds and chargebacks taken off. Approved refund and chargeback adjustments lower the transaction's revenue, and transactions from the last 60 days are read again every 6 hours, so later refunds show up.
  • Retention by channel. The Retention page groups customers by the channel of their first payment. With subscription.read, subscription status is read once an hour; without it, retention falls back to payment recency.

Set it up

  1. Add the site. Click + Add a site and enter your domain, such as yourapp.com, and its timezone.
  2. Install the script. Paste the tag before </head> on your landing page and your app. The virling_vid cookie is set on your registrable domain, so yourapp.com and app.yourapp.com share one visitor ID. Leave cookieless mode off.
  3. Connect Paddle. In Paddle, open Developer tools, then Authentication, and create an API key with transaction.read and adjustment.read, plus subscription.read for retention. Paste it under payments; live keys start with pdl_live_ and sandbox keys with pdl_sdbx_. The last 90 days are imported, then new transactions every minute.
  4. Link checkout to visits. Virling reads virling_vid from the transaction's custom_data. With Paddle.js, pass it as customData when you open checkout. For transactions you create on your server, set custom_data: { virling_vid: req.cookies.virling_vid }.
Paddle.Checkout.open({
  items: [{ priceId: "pri_...", quantity: 1 }],
  customData: { virling_vid: window.virling?.vid?.() },
});

window.virling?.vid?.() returns nothing when the script is blocked or not loaded yet, so check the value before using it. The Next.js walkthrough is in Next.js Paddle UTM tracking, and ChatGPT revenue tracking shows how to read which ChatGPT visits pay. Every setting is on the Paddle integration page, and Virling for AI apps covers other payment providers.

Visits from AI assistants

An answer in an assistant can bring a user who buys credits that day or subscribes later. Where the visit lands depends on what the click carries:

  • Lands in AI. The referrer is chatgpt.com, chat.openai.com, perplexity.ai, claude.ai, gemini.google.com, copilot.microsoft.com or chat.deepseek.com. With no referrer, a utm_source such as chatgpt, perplexity or claude counts too.
  • Lands in another channel. An assistant whose site is not on that list is Referral. A Google or Bing results page is Organic search, AI answers on it included. A paid or email utm_medium wins over the referrer, so a link tagged cpc is Paid.
  • Arrives as Direct. A visit with no referrer and no tag, as when an assistant's app drops the referrer or someone copies the address from an answer. It cannot be told apart from a typed address, so read the AI channel as a minimum.

To read revenue per visitor for them:

  1. Split AI by assistant. On the Channels card, click AI, then open the Source tab. Each row has visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor, with a chip against your site average.
  2. Check New customers. Revenue counts on the payment date, so later credit packs, upgrades and renewals from users an assistant brought earlier are in it too. New customers leaves renewals out.
  3. Do not read the Direct row as assistants. It mixes them with typed addresses and bookmarks. By default a buyer who came from ChatGPT and returned by typing your address is credited to AI; one whose only visits were Direct is credited to Direct.
  4. Wait for enough visits. With few visitors, one purchase moves the number a lot, so use Last 90 days. Is AI search traffic worth anything? has a worked example.

What to know

  • Transactions older than the 90-day backfill are not imported, so renewals of subscriptions that started before that are Unattributed. Channel numbers fill in as new customers arrive.
  • Renewals carry no custom data of their own; they keep the channel of the subscription's first payment.
  • Payments without a customer ID cannot be linked to a first payment, and payments without a visitor ID still count toward revenue as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

Which channel brings paying users to my AI app on Paddle?

Connect Paddle to Virling with a read-only API key and pass the visitor ID as customData when you open Paddle checkout. Each customer's first payment is credited to the visits before it, so the Channels card shows whether a post, a search or an AI answer brought the users who pay.

Which channel gets credit for a credit pack bought later?

The channel of the customer's first payment, matched by Paddle customer ID. Any transaction after the first is a renewal, so a later credit pack keeps that channel like a subscription renewal or a plan change. Pass virling_vid in its custom data too: if the first payment is Unattributed, the pack falls back to its own visitor ID.

Do free credits count as revenue?

No. Virling counts only completed transactions from Paddle. A user who only spends free credits is a visitor, not a customer. A subscription's first transaction with nothing to pay starts a trial, which is never counted as revenue, a payment or a customer.

Are visits from ChatGPT and Perplexity tracked?

Yes. Visits referred by chatgpt.com, chat.openai.com, perplexity.ai, claude.ai, gemini.google.com, copilot.microsoft.com and chat.deepseek.com get the AI channel, so revenue from AI answers shows on its own rows.

Does a customer who cancels and buys again get a new channel?

Not while they return with the same Paddle customer ID: the new payment keeps the channel of their first one, however long they were away. If that first payment is Unattributed and the new one has its own visitor ID, the new visit is credited. With a new customer ID they are a new customer, credited from their own visits.

Can I count assistant visits that arrive as Direct?

Not separately. A visit with no referrer and no utm_source lands in Direct, next to typed addresses and bookmarks, so the AI channel is a minimum. A link you place yourself can carry a utm_source such as chatgpt or perplexity, which puts the visit in AI when there is no referrer.
