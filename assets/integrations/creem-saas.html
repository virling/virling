See which channel brings your Creem subscribers.

For SaaS and software founders who sell subscriptions through Creem as their merchant of record. Virling ties each payment, the first payment after a trial and every renewal to the channel that brought the customer, so you compare channels by the customers who pay, not by signups. Open source under AGPL, hosted or self-hosted.

Creem knows who pays and how much, but not the visit that brought them. Your ads, posts and search results bring the visit, and the renewal in month six never touches your site at all. Virling joins the two with a scoped Creem API key and one webhook.

What you get

  • Each subscription credited to a channel. The customer's first payment is joined to the visits before it. By default it goes to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct; B2B buyers who take weeks to decide can be read with first touch or a 365-day window.
  • Trials that convert, credited to the visit before the trial. A completed checkout with nothing to pay starts a trial. Virling never counts it as revenue, a payment or a customer, and credits the customer's first paid transaction to the visits before the trial started.
  • Renewals that keep the first channel. Every payment after a customer's first is a renewal. It copies the channel, referrer, campaign, landing page, country and device of the customer's first payment, matched by Creem customer ID, even years later. Plan changes keep that channel too.
  • Refunds and chargebacks taken off. Transactions from the last 60 days are read again every 6 hours, so later refunds and chargebacks update them.
  • Retention by channel. The Retention page groups customers by the channel of their first payment, with how many still pay, net LTV and 30-day churn. With subscriptions:read, subscription status is read once an hour; without it, retention falls back to payment recency.
  • Revenue per visitor and time to pay. The Channels card shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor per channel, and Time to pay shows the median time from first visit to first payment, so you do not cut a channel before its customers have had time to pay.

Set it up

  1. Add the site. Click + Add a site and enter your domain, such as yourproduct.com, and its timezone.
  2. Install the script. Paste the tag before </head> on your marketing site and your app. The virling_vid cookie is set on your registrable domain, so yourproduct.com and app.yourproduct.com share one visitor ID. Leave cookieless mode off.
  3. Connect Creem. In Creem, open Developers, then API keys, and create a key with transactions:read, plus subscriptions:read for retention and webhooks:write so Virling can add its webhook. Paste it under payments; live and test mode keys both work. The last 90 days are imported, then new transactions every minute.
  4. Pass the visitor ID at checkout. Creem returns checkout metadata only in webhooks, so Virling adds one checkout.completed webhook when you connect. It reads virling_vid from the checkout's metadata, then the subscription's, then the customer's. Set it in metadata when you create the checkout on your server.
const res = await fetch("https://api.creem.io/v1/checkouts", {
  method: "POST",
  headers: { "x-api-key": process.env.CREEM_API_KEY, "content-type": "application/json" },
  body: JSON.stringify({
    product_id: "prod_...",
    success_url: "https://yourproduct.com/thanks",
    metadata: { virling_vid: req.cookies.virling_vid },
  }),
});
// Send the customer to (await res.json()).checkout_url

The full walkthrough is in how to track Creem revenue by traffic source, and Creem UTM tracking covers carrying source data through checkout. Every setting is on the Creem integration page. To see what selling through a merchant of record costs, use the merchant of record fee calculator.

Trials, renewals, upgrades and refunds

Creem lists payments and refunds as transactions, and sends the checkout's visitor ID and trial starts only by webhook. Linking a payment to a visit therefore needs the webhook, and the webhook needs Virling on a public https address. The hosted service has one; on your own server, set VIRLING_BASE_URL to yours before you connect the key.

Your first week

  1. Day one: connect in test mode. With the script on your marketing site and app, paste a key that starts with creem_test_. Virling adds its webhook in test mode. A site has one Creem connection, and test payments stay in your reports after you replace the key. To keep them out, connect the live key and test with a small live payment that you refund.
  2. Day one: place a test order. Open a private window and visit a page that runs the script through a tagged link, such as https://yourproduct.com/?utm_source=test&utm_medium=email. In the same window, go to a checkout your server creates on https://test-api.creem.io with virling_vid in metadata, and pay with a test card. Use a plan without a trial, since a trial start is not a payment.
  3. A minute later: read the payment. At /app/yourproduct.com/payments it shows the test source and the Email channel instead of Unattributed. If it is Unattributed, the checkout had no virling_vid or the webhook is missing: check the https address, then use Replace key to add the webhook. Use a new email address for each test, since a later payment by the same customer keeps the first channel.
  4. Day two: go live. Replace the test key with the live one, which adds the webhook in live mode, and point your server back at https://api.creem.io.
  5. After the first few sales: open Payments, then Channels. Payments splits revenue into new customers and renewals at the top; open a new payment to see the visits before it, with the credited one marked. Then compare revenue per visitor on the Channels card. Most payments in the first week can be renewals of customers from before the webhook, so expect a lot of Unattributed. Customers in a trial appear once they are charged.

What to know

  • Transactions older than the 90-day backfill are not imported, so renewals of subscriptions that started before that are Unattributed. Channel numbers fill in as new customers arrive.
  • The webhook needs Virling on a public https address. A self-hosted server on plain http still imports payments, but they stay Unattributed.
  • Payments without a customer ID cannot be linked to a first payment, and payments without a visitor ID still count toward revenue as Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

Which channel brings my Creem subscribers?

Connect Creem to Virling with a scoped API key and set virling_vid in the metadata of the checkout you create. Virling adds a checkout.completed webhook to read that metadata, credits each customer's first payment to the visits before it, and every renewal keeps that channel, so the Channels card and the Retention page show which channels bring subscribers who pay.

How are Creem renewals attributed?

Every payment after a customer's first is a renewal. It takes the channel, referrer, campaign and landing page of the customer's first payment, matched by Creem customer ID. Plan changes keep that channel too.

What about free trials on Creem?

A completed checkout with nothing to pay starts a trial. Virling never counts it as revenue, a payment or a customer. The customer's first paid transaction is credited to the visits before the trial started.

Which scopes does the Creem API key need?

transactions:read, plus subscriptions:read so Retention can tell who still pays, and webhooks:write so Virling can add its one checkout.completed webhook. No other scopes are needed.
