See which channel brings your Polar subscribers.

For SaaS products and developer tools that sell subscriptions, licenses or sponsorships through Polar. Virling ties each order, the first payment after a trial and every renewal to the channel that brought the customer, so you compare channels by the customers who pay, not by signups. Open source under AGPL, hosted or self-hosted.

Polar records every order, refund and subscription, but not the README link, post or search that sent the buyer. A renewal months later happens without any visit to your site. Virling connects the two using an organization access token that can only read.

What you get

  • A channel on every order. Virling joins a Polar customer's first paid order to that visitor's earlier visits. The default model picks the most recent visit within 90 days that was not Direct; switch to first touch or a 365-day window when buyers evaluate for weeks.
  • Trials counted once they pay. A $0 subscription_create order marks the start of a trial and adds nothing to revenue, payments or customers. When the first charge comes, it inherits the visits from before the trial.
  • Renewals tied to the first order. Orders billed as subscription_cycle or subscription_update are renewals. They inherit channel, referrer, campaign, landing page, country and device from the customer's first paid order, found by Polar customer ID.
  • Refunds and late payments picked up. A new refund lowers its order, and one still pending is checked again once it settles. Orders not yet paid are rechecked every 10 minutes for up to 30 days.
  • Who still pays, by channel. Retention groups customers by the channel of their first order, with still paying, net LTV and 30-day churn. The subscriptions:read scope lets Virling read status hourly, counting active, trialing and past_due as paying; without it, payment recency stands in.
  • Revenue per visitor and time to pay. Rank channels by revenue per visitor on the Channels card, and read the median days from first visit to first payment on Time to pay before you judge a slow one.

Set it up

  1. Add the site. Click + Add a site and enter your domain, such as yourproduct.com, and its timezone.
  2. Install the script. Paste the tag before </head> on your marketing site and your app. The virling_vid cookie is set on your registrable domain, so yourproduct.com and app.yourproduct.com share one visitor ID. Leave cookieless mode off.
  3. Connect Polar. In your Polar organization's settings, create an Organization Access Token with orders:read and refunds:read, plus subscriptions:read for retention. Paste it under payments; it starts with polar_oat_, and production and sandbox tokens both work. The last 90 days are imported, then new orders every minute.
  4. Pass the visitor ID at checkout. Checkout links on buy.polar.sh need no code: keep the link as a normal <a href> on a page that runs the script, and the script adds reference_id=<visitor ID>, which Polar stores in the order's metadata. For a checkout session you create on your server, set virling_vid in metadata. Virling reads it from the order's metadata, then the order's custom field data, then the customer's metadata.
const checkout = await polar.checkouts.create({
  products: ["PRODUCT_ID"],
  success_url: "https://yourproduct.com/thanks",
  metadata: { virling_vid: req.cookies.virling_vid },
});
// Send the customer to checkout.url

The full walkthrough, from tagged links to revenue per channel, is in Polar UTM tracking. Every setting is on the Polar integration page, and Virling for developer tools covers the wider setup for products sold to developers.

Checkout links, metadata and benefits

  • Checkout links. On a page that runs the script, a buy.polar.sh link gets reference_id=<visitor ID> added, and Polar keeps it in the order's metadata. A reference_id you already set on the link is left alone, so that order carries no visitor ID, and sandbox links are not touched.
  • Where Virling looks. virling_vid in the order's metadata wins, then the order's custom field data, then the customer's metadata, then the link's reference_id. Setting it on the customer covers orders you create for them later.
  • The amount. Virling records the order's net amount: after discounts, before tax. The sales tax or VAT Polar adds as merchant of record is not in your Virling revenue, and neither is the refunded tax.
  • Benefits. License keys, repository access, Discord roles and downloads are granted by Polar after the order. Virling reads the order that paid for them, not the benefits, so a tier is credited by what the customer paid.
  • The open-source maintainer case. If you sell a paid tier, support plan or sponsorship for an open-source project, many buyers come from your repository. A visit referred by github.com shows as the GitHub source in the Referral channel, next to posts and search. Point the README at a page on your own site, not straight at the checkout link: a link clicked on GitHub never passes the script, so its order is Unattributed.

Read your first month

  1. Start on Channels, with renewals in mind. Revenue there counts renewals, so New customers is the number for what this month won. The matched revenue line shows how much of it has a visit behind it; orders imported from before the script are Unattributed, so early months look thin.
  2. Move to Retention once you have customers. 30-day churn covers only customers whose first payment is older than 30 days and no older than 60. When Polar bills a trial's first paid order as subscription_cycle, that customer is left out of the Retention customers; read trial signups on Payments instead.

Three readings to hold off on:

  • A channel with no orders yet. Buyers still in a trial have paid nothing. Wait until Time to pay says its median has passed.
  • A channel on top because of one buyer. A yearly license or a large sponsorship can lead the table alone. Count its orders on Payments.
  • A retention verdict on a few customers. Under 20 customers, or under 5 still paying, Retention shows no headline, and that is the right call.

What to know

  • The first import covers 90 days of orders. A subscription that began earlier has its renewals mostly Unattributed until new customers replace it.
  • Renewal orders need no metadata, because the customer ID links them to the first order.
  • An order without a visitor ID still counts as revenue, under Unattributed.

Frequently asked questions

Which channel brings my Polar subscribers?

Connect Polar to Virling with a read-only organization access token and pass the visitor ID in the checkout metadata; Polar checkout links get it from the script automatically. Each customer's first payment is credited to the visits before it, and every renewal keeps that channel, so the Channels card and the Retention page show which channels bring subscribers who pay.

How are Polar renewals attributed?

An order with billing reason subscription_cycle is a renewal. It takes the channel, referrer, campaign and landing page of the customer's first payment, matched by Polar customer ID. Plan changes (subscription_update) keep that channel too.

What about free trials on Polar?

A $0 order with billing reason subscription_create starts a trial. Virling never counts it as revenue, a payment or a customer. The customer's later payment, which has no visitor ID of its own, is credited to the visits before the trial started.

Which scopes does the Polar token need?

orders:read and refunds:read, plus subscriptions:read so Retention can tell who still pays. No other scopes are needed, and Virling never creates, changes or refunds anything.

What about Polar subscriptions that began before Virling was connected?

Virling imports orders from the last 90 days. When a subscription's first order is older than that, its earliest order in Virling stands in, and it rarely has a visit, so its renewals are usually Unattributed. A first order inside the 90 days but from before the script was on your site is Unattributed too. All of them count toward revenue, but a customer whose earliest payment in Virling is a renewal is left out of Retention.
