See which channel brings your Polar subscribers.

For SaaS products and developer tools that sell subscriptions, licenses or sponsorships through Polar. Virling ties each order, the first payment after a trial and every renewal to the channel that brought the customer, so you compare channels by the customers who pay, not by signups. Open source under AGPL, hosted or self-hosted.

Start free trial See the demo

Polar knows who pays and how much, but not the visit that brought them. Your ads, posts and search results bring the visit, and the renewal in month six never touches your site at all. Virling joins the two with a read-only organization access token.

What you get

  • Each order credited to a channel. The customer's first payment is joined to the visits before it. By default it goes to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct; B2B buyers who take weeks to decide can be read with first touch or a 365-day window.
  • Trials that convert, credited to the visit before the trial. A $0 order with billing reason subscription_create starts a trial. Virling never counts it as revenue, a payment or a customer, and credits the customer's later payment to the visits before the trial started.
  • Renewals that keep the first channel. An order with billing reason subscription_cycle is a renewal. It copies the channel, referrer, campaign, landing page, country and device of the customer's first payment, matched by Polar customer ID, even years later. Plan changes (subscription_update) keep that channel too.
  • Refunds taken off. New refunds update the order they belong to, and a pending refund is read again once it settles. Unpaid orders are checked again every 10 minutes for up to 30 days and imported once paid.
  • Retention by channel. The Retention page groups customers by the channel of their first payment, with how many still pay, net LTV and 30-day churn. With subscriptions:read, subscription status is read once an hour and active, trialing and past_due count as still paying; without it, retention falls back to payment recency.
  • Revenue per visitor and time to pay. The Channels card shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor per channel, and Time to pay shows the median time from first visit to first payment, so you do not cut a channel before its customers have had time to pay.

Set it up

  1. Add the site. Click + Add a site and enter your domain, such as yourproduct.com, and its timezone.
  2. Install the script. Paste the tag before </head> on your marketing site and your app. The virling_vid cookie is set on your registrable domain, so yourproduct.com and app.yourproduct.com share one visitor ID. Leave cookieless mode off.
  3. Connect Polar. In your Polar organization's settings, create an Organization Access Token with orders:read and refunds:read, plus subscriptions:read for retention. Paste it under payments; it starts with polar_oat_, and production and sandbox tokens both work. The last 90 days are imported, then new orders every minute.
  4. Pass the visitor ID at checkout. Checkout links on buy.polar.sh need no code: keep the link as a normal <a href> on a page that runs the script, and the script adds reference_id=<visitor ID>, which Polar stores in the order's metadata. For a checkout session you create on your server, set virling_vid in metadata. Virling reads it from the order's metadata, then the order's custom field data, then the customer's metadata.
const checkout = await polar.checkouts.create({
  products: ["PRODUCT_ID"],
  success_url: "https://yourproduct.com/thanks",
  metadata: { virling_vid: req.cookies.virling_vid },
});
// Send the customer to checkout.url

The full walkthrough is in how to track Polar revenue by traffic source, and Polar UTM tracking covers tagging links so each order shows its campaign. Every setting is on the Polar integration page, and Virling for developer tools covers the wider setup for products sold to developers.

What to know

  • Orders older than the 90-day backfill are not imported, so renewals of subscriptions that started before that are Unattributed. Channel numbers fill in as new customers arrive.
  • Renewals carry no metadata of their own; they keep the channel of the subscription's first payment.
  • Payments without a customer ID cannot be linked to a first payment, and payments without a visitor ID still count toward revenue as Unattributed.

Open source

Virling is open source under AGPLv3, and the code is on GitHub. Use the hosted service, or self-host it as one binary with every feature. In a load test on 2026-10-31, in a container limited to one vCPU and 1 GB of RAM, it ingested 100,000 pageviews at 2,829 a second with none failed and a p99 of 34 ms, and used 157 MB of memory after the ingest.

Frequently asked questions

Which channel brings my Polar subscribers?

Connect Polar to Virling with a read-only organization access token and pass the visitor ID in the checkout metadata; Polar checkout links get it from the script automatically. Each customer's first payment is credited to the visits before it, and every renewal keeps that channel, so the Channels card and the Retention page show which channels bring subscribers who pay.

How are Polar renewals attributed?

An order with billing reason subscription_cycle is a renewal. It takes the channel, referrer, campaign and landing page of the customer's first payment, matched by Polar customer ID. Plan changes (subscription_update) keep that channel too.

What about free trials on Polar?

A $0 order with billing reason subscription_create starts a trial. Virling never counts it as revenue, a payment or a customer. The customer's later payment, which has no visitor ID of its own, is credited to the visits before the trial started.

Which scopes does the Polar token need?

orders:read and refunds:read, plus subscriptions:read so Retention can tell who still pays. No other scopes are needed, and Virling never creates, changes or refunds anything.
