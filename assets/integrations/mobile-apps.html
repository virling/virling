Revenue attribution for mobile apps: which channel brings subscribers.

Virling joins RevenueCat purchases from web paywalls, and from app users linked to a web visit, to the visit that came before them. See which ad, post or search brings paying subscribers, and keep that credit for every renewal. Open source, one Rust binary, or hosted.

App stores tell you who paid, not where they came from. If you send people to a marketing site or a web paywall, there is a visit to read before the purchase. Virling records that visit, carries its ID to RevenueCat, and credits the purchase and its renewals to the channel behind it.

What Virling sees

  • Web visits on your marketing site and web paywall, with their referrer, UTM tags, landing page, country and device.
  • Web paywall purchases on a page that runs the script, through RevenueCat.
  • RevenueCat purchases, renewals and refunds through a webhook Virling adds with your key, in the currency the customer paid.
  • Free trials. A trial start is kept apart from payments and never counted as revenue. The first paid renewal is credited to the visit that started the trial.
  • One-time purchases from the last 90 days, imported once when you connect.

What Virling does not see

  • Purchases that never touch the web. Someone who finds your app in the store and buys in the app has no visit to credit. The purchase still counts toward revenue, as Unattributed.
  • Store installs. Virling reads payments from RevenueCat, not installs from the App Store or Google Play.
  • Subscription payments from before you connected. RevenueCat lists them per subscription only for some stores, so they are not imported.
  • Subscription status. It is not read, so Retention uses payment recency for RevenueCat customers.

If most of your subscribers start in the store, the Unattributed share will be large. That is a useful number in itself: it tells you how much of your growth the web funnel explains.

Web-to-app funnels

A RevenueCat purchase is credited to a visit only when it carries a visitor ID. Virling reads it from the customer attribute virling_vid, so the ID has to be on the customer before the purchase. A subscriber who came through your site takes one of two roads:

  • They buy on the web. A web paywall on a page that runs the script sells the subscription. Your server reads the virling_vid cookie and sets it on the RevenueCat customer.
  • They sign up on the web, then buy in the app. Set virling_vid on the customer at signup from your server, or from the app with the RevenueCat SDK's setAttributes, before the purchase.

The cookie is set on your registrable domain, so a site on yourapp.com and a paywall on pay.yourapp.com share the same visitor ID. If you sell on the web through Stripe, Paddle or another provider instead, pass the visitor ID into that checkout and connect that provider too.

Subscriptions and renewals by channel

A renewal takes the attribution of the customer's first payment. RevenueCat renewals arrive as RENEWAL webhook events, and each one copies the first payment's channel, referrer, campaign, landing page, country and device, even years later and even though the renewal itself has no visit. A channel that brings subscribers keeps the credit for the revenue they pay over time.

The first payment must be in Virling. Subscriptions that started before you connected are not imported, so their renewals are Unattributed. Customers are matched per provider: a customer who moved from a web checkout on one provider to RevenueCat counts as two customers.

Setup

  1. Add a site in Virling and paste the script tag before </head> on every page of your marketing site and your web paywall. Leave cookieless mode off: without the virling_vid cookie there is no visitor ID to pass on.
  2. Set the virling_vid attribute on the RevenueCat customer before the purchase, from your server or with the SDK's setAttributes. The server call needs a key with write access to Customer information, separate from the one you give Virling.
  3. In RevenueCat, create a V2 secret key with read access to Project configuration and Customer information, and read and write access to Integrations. Paste it into Virling. One key covers one project.
  4. Make a sandbox purchase after visiting your site from a tagged link. It should show that source in Payments instead of Unattributed.
  5. Read the Channels card for revenue per channel, Payments for each purchase and its journey, and Retention for customers by the channel of their first payment.

The RevenueCat webhook needs Virling to be reachable over https. The hosted plan already is.

Frequently asked questions

Can Virling attribute in-app purchases?

Yes, when the app user's virling_vid attribute was set from a web visit before the purchase. In-app purchases from people who never came through your site have no visit to credit, so they count toward revenue as Unattributed.

Does Virling track app installs?

No. Virling reads payments from RevenueCat, not installs from the App Store or Google Play.

Which channel gets credit for a renewal?

The channel of the customer's first payment. A RevenueCat renewal arrives as a RENEWAL webhook event and copies the first payment's channel, referrer, campaign, landing page, country and device.
