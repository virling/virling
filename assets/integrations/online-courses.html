Revenue attribution for online courses

Course buyers find you on YouTube, in a newsletter, at a webinar, through an affiliate or on social, and often enroll weeks after their first visit. Virling joins each course or cohort sale to the visits before it, so you see which video, issue, webinar or partner brought the money, not just the clicks. Works with Stripe, LemonSqueezy, Polar, Creem and Shopify. Open source, one Rust binary, or hosted.

A course sale rarely follows one click. Someone watches a video, joins your list, sits in on a free webinar, and buys when the cart opens. The payment provider records the sale, the analytics tool records the visits, and neither knows about the other. Virling joins them: a visitor ID set when someone lands, passed into checkout, and matched to that visitor's sessions when the payment comes in.

The questions it answers

  • Which video or post sold the course? Revenue per YouTube video, newsletter issue, webinar or social post, from the campaign on its link.
  • Which affiliate was worth it? Revenue, paying customers and refund rate per partner, not clicks.
  • Which launch email filled the cohort? The open-cart, reminder and last-call emails, each as its own row.
  • How long does a buyer take? The median time from first visit to first payment for each channel.

Sales by channel, source and campaign

Every visit gets one channel from its first pageview: Paid, Email, AI, Organic search, Communities, Social, Referral or Direct. YouTube counts as Social, and a link tagged utm_medium=newsletter or email counts as Email. The dashboard lists visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor for each channel, source, campaign and landing page, so a video that sent many visitors and no sales stands apart from a small one that paid.

Links opened in the YouTube app or an email client often arrive with no referrer, and with no tag those visits are Direct. Tag the links in video descriptions, emails and webinar follow-ups with one utm_campaign per video, issue or webinar, or make a tracked link: a short URL that adds the UTM tags for you, with its own clicks, visitors and revenue. YouTube revenue tracking and webinar revenue tracking walk through the tags.

Launches and cohort deadlines

A launch or a cohort deadline sends most of its sales in a few days, often from one email: the cart opens, a reminder goes out, the doors close. Give each of those emails its own utm_campaign, such as launch-2026-03-open and launch-2026-03-last-call, and the Campaign tab shows the visitors and revenue each one brought.

Each site has one attribution model. Last non-direct is the default: it credits the latest session in the window that was not Direct. A buyer who found you on YouTube, clicked your last-call email and then paid is credited to the email, which tells you which touch closed the sale. First touch credits the earliest session in the window, the video, which tells you which channel brings buyers in. Last touch credits the visit right before the payment, Direct included. Saving the setting rebuilds attribution for every payment, history included, so you can look at one launch both ways and switch back.

Only sessions that started inside the lookback window before the payment are considered. Choose 30, 60, 90 or 365 days; the default is 90. If people follow you for months before a launch, a longer window keeps their first visit in reach. See how long a visitor takes to become a customer.

Affiliates and discount codes

Give each affiliate a link with their own value in utm_source, or ref when utm_source is missing, and the same value in utm_campaign. A partner name is not a known source, so with no referrer the visit counts as Referral, and the Campaign tab shows each partner's visitors and revenue. Or add the partner under Partners in Virling: it makes a short link for them and ranks partners by revenue, with paying customers and refund rate, and Copy report link gives each partner a read-only page with only their own numbers.

Virling credits a sale by the visit before it, not by a discount code, and does not read codes, so code redemptions stay in your payment provider's reports. A code counts only buyers who type it, and a code that leaks to a coupon site credits sales its owner did not drive. Give each affiliate a tagged link as well as a code, and compare the two. Virling does not pay partners or calculate commissions; keep your payout software for that. See affiliate revenue tracking.

Connect your payment provider

  • Stripe: Payment Links need no code, since the script adds the visitor ID on click. For a Checkout Session you create on your server, pass the virling_vid cookie as client_reference_id. Step by step: Stripe course sales by traffic source.
  • LemonSqueezy: an API key, and the visitor ID passed as checkout custom data. Virling registers a webhook on each store to read it, which needs a public https address.
  • Polar: an organization access token. Checkout links need no code beyond a custom field with the slug virling_vid; checkout sessions take it in metadata.
  • Creem: a scoped API key and the visitor ID in checkout metadata. Virling adds one checkout.completed webhook to read it.
  • Shopify: an Admin API token that can only read orders, and a few lines in your theme that store the visitor ID on the cart.

Refunds lower a payment's revenue once Virling reads them, so a channel full of refunds stands out. Payment plans and memberships billed as subscriptions keep the channel of the first payment on every renewal; see Virling for SaaS. If you sell one-to-one sessions or packages next to the course, tracking which channels bring coaching clients covers those, and Stripe for coaches and consultants covers deposits and retainers billed on Stripe.

What to know

  • Sales made before the visitor ID was passed into checkout are Unattributed. They still count toward revenue.
  • A buyer who pays from a link in an email or a DM, without visiting your site first, has no visit to credit and is Unattributed.
  • A visit with no referrer and no UTM tag is Direct. Tag the links you control.
  • If someone watches a video on their phone and enrolls later on a laptop, the two browsers are separate visitors.

Frequently asked questions

Which payment providers work for online courses?

Stripe, LemonSqueezy, Polar, Creem and Shopify, among others. Connect one, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and Virling ties each payment to the visit before it. Stripe Payment Links and Polar checkout links need no code: the script adds the visitor ID when someone clicks.

How do I see which YouTube video or newsletter sold a course?

Tag each link with its own utm_campaign, such as one per video or per newsletter issue, or share a tracked link. Every visit gets a channel, source and campaign from its first pageview, and each sale is credited under your attribution model, so the Campaign tab shows revenue per video, issue or webinar.

Does Virling read discount codes?

No. Virling credits a sale by the visit before it, not by a discount code, so code redemptions stay in your payment provider's reports. Give each affiliate a tagged link as well as a code, and compare the two.

What if buyers take weeks to enroll?

Only visits inside the lookback window before the payment are considered. The default is 90 days, and you can choose 30, 60, 90 or 365. With the default model, last non-direct, a buyer who watched a video and came back weeks later by typing your address is credited to the video, not Direct.

Is Virling open source?

Yes. Virling is open source under AGPLv3. You can run it yourself as one binary with embedded storage, or use the hosted version.
