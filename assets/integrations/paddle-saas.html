See which channel brings your Paddle subscribers.

For SaaS products that sell subscriptions through Paddle as merchant of record. Virling ties each transaction, the first payment after a trial and every renewal to the channel that brought the customer, so you compare channels by the customers who pay, not by signups. Open source under AGPL, hosted or self-hosted.

Start free trial See the demo

Paddle sees the checkout, the tax and every later charge. It does not see the ad, search result or post that sent the buyer to your site, and a renewal never brings them back. A read-only API key lets Virling put the two together.

What you get

  • Subscriptions sorted by channel. The first payment of each Paddle customer is matched to the visits that came before it, by default the last one in 90 days that was not Direct. For long B2B sales, first touch or a 365-day window are a setting away.
  • Trial signups that wait for the money. When a subscription opens with a transaction of nothing to pay, Virling treats it as a trial: no revenue, no payment, no customer. The first real charge is credited to the visits before that signup.
  • Every later charge on the first channel. Transactions of origin subscription_recurring, subscription_update and subscription_charge are renewals, and so is any later transaction of the same Paddle customer. Each one copies its channel, referrer, campaign, landing page, country and device from the first payment.
  • Refunds and chargebacks. Approved adjustments of either kind come off the transaction's revenue. Virling rereads the last 60 days of transactions every 6 hours to catch them.
  • Retention by channel. Customers are grouped by the channel of their first payment. With subscription.read, Virling reads each subscription's status and price hourly for still paying, net LTV, churn and MRR; without it, Retention judges by how recently a customer paid.
  • Revenue per visitor and time to pay. Channels lists visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor for each source, and Time to pay gives the median wait from first visit to first charge.

Set it up

  1. Add the site. Click + Add a site and enter your domain, such as yourproduct.com, and its timezone.
  2. Install the script. Paste the tag before </head> on your marketing site and your app. The virling_vid cookie is set on your registrable domain, so yourproduct.com and app.yourproduct.com share one visitor ID. Leave cookieless mode off.
  3. Connect Paddle. In Paddle, open Developer tools, then Authentication, and create an API key with transaction.read and adjustment.read, plus subscription.read for retention. Paste it under payments; live keys start with pdl_live_ and sandbox keys with pdl_sdbx_. The last 90 days are imported, then new transactions every minute.
  4. Pass the visitor ID in custom data. Virling reads virling_vid from the transaction's custom_data. With Paddle.js, pass it as customData when you open checkout; for transactions you create on your server, set custom_data: { virling_vid: req.cookies.virling_vid }.
Paddle.Checkout.open({
  items: [{ priceId: "pri_...", quantity: 1 }],
  customData: { virling_vid: window.virling?.vid?.() },
});

The full walkthrough is in how to track Paddle revenue by traffic source, and Paddle UTM tracking covers passing UTM and custom data to checkout. If you also sell a desktop app, see Paddle for desktop apps for what links when the purchase happens inside the app. If you sell an API, CLI or SDK, see Paddle for developer tools. To see what selling through a merchant of record costs, use the merchant of record fee calculator.

Paddle.js customData, tax and the IDs Virling reads

  • Paddle.js customData. Virling reads virling_vid only from the transaction's custom_data. Overlay and inline checkout both pass it on when you give customData to Paddle.Checkout.open. Custom data on a customer, product or price is ignored, and the script never adds the visitor ID to a Paddle checkout by itself.
  • Merchant of record tax. Paddle sells to your customer as merchant of record and adds sales tax or VAT. Virling records the transaction total, which is after discounts and includes that tax, before Paddle's fee. A payment in Virling is therefore larger than what reaches your payout; the merchant of record fee calculator shows the gap.
  • Transaction IDs. Each payment in Virling is one completed Paddle transaction, stored under its txn_ ID, so you can look it up in Paddle.
  • Customer IDs. The ctm_ ID is what links a renewal to the first payment. A transaction without one cannot inherit a channel, and a customer with two subscriptions has both credited to their first payment.
  • Subscription IDs. The sub_ ID tells a trial's $0 transaction apart from other free ones. With subscription.read it also joins each subscription's status and price to the customer for Retention and MRR. It is not used to match renewals.

Read your first month

  1. Channels: new customers before revenue. Revenue per visitor is the fair comparison, but renewals and backfilled payments inflate the revenue column, and most of the backfill has no visit. New customers and the matched revenue line tell you what the script has tied to a channel since it went live.
  2. Retention: wait for the second month. Churn there needs customers whose first payment is over 30 days old, so this month's are not in it yet. Paddle bills the charge after a free trial as a renewal, so trial customers are not in the Retention customers at all; follow them on Payments.

Early numbers that mislead:

  • An empty channel. It may only be slow. Compare its age with the median on Time to pay first.
  • A winning channel. Totals include tax, and one annual plan in a high-VAT country can decide the ranking. Look at how many payments it has.
  • Churn by channel. Retention stays silent below 20 customers or 5 still paying, and a few cancellations prove little.

What to know

  • The backfill reads 90 days. Renewals of older subscriptions have no first payment with a visit and show as Unattributed until newer customers take over.
  • A transaction created more than about 10 minutes before it is paid, such as an invoice, can take up to 6 hours to appear.
  • A refund on a transaction older than 60 days is not picked up automatically.

Frequently asked questions

Which channel brings my Paddle subscribers?

Connect Paddle to Virling with a read-only API key and pass the visitor ID as customData when you open Paddle checkout. Virling credits a customer's first transaction to the visits that led to it and gives every later charge the same channel, so Channels and Retention compare sources by subscribers who pay.

How are Paddle renewals attributed?

A transaction whose origin is subscription_recurring, subscription_update or subscription_charge is a renewal. Virling finds the customer's first payment by Paddle customer ID and copies its channel, referrer, campaign and landing page. Plan changes keep that channel too.

What about free trials on Paddle?

A subscription's first transaction with nothing to pay starts a trial. That transaction adds no revenue, payment or customer. When the first charge arrives without custom data, it goes to the visits from before the trial.

Which permissions does the Paddle API key need?

transaction.read and adjustment.read, plus subscription.read so Retention can tell who still pays. No other permissions are needed, and Virling never creates, changes or refunds anything.

What about Paddle subscriptions that began before Virling was connected?

Virling imports transactions from the last 90 days. When a subscription's first payment is older than that, its earliest payment in Virling stands in, and it rarely has a visit, so its renewals are usually Unattributed. A first payment inside the 90 days but from before the script was on your site is Unattributed too. Their revenue still counts, but Retention leaves out any customer whose earliest payment in Virling is a renewal.
