See which channel brings your Paddle subscribers.

For SaaS products that sell subscriptions through Paddle as merchant of record. Virling ties each transaction, the first payment after a trial and every renewal to the channel that brought the customer, so you compare channels by the customers who pay, not by signups. Open source under AGPL, hosted or self-hosted.

Start free trial See the demo

Paddle knows who pays and how much, but not the visit that brought them. Your ads, posts and search results bring the visit, and the renewal in month six never touches your site at all. Virling joins the two with a read-only API key.

What you get

  • Each subscription credited to a channel. The customer's first payment is joined to the visits before it. By default it goes to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct; B2B buyers who take weeks to decide can be read with first touch or a 365-day window.
  • Trials that convert, credited to the visit before the trial. A subscription's first transaction with nothing to pay starts a trial. Virling never counts it as revenue, a payment or a customer, and credits the customer's later payment to the visits before the trial started.
  • Renewals that keep the first channel. A transaction whose origin is subscription_recurring, subscription_update or subscription_charge is a renewal. It copies the channel, referrer, campaign, landing page, country and device of the customer's first payment, matched by Paddle customer ID, even years later. Plan changes count as renewals too.
  • Refunds and chargebacks taken off. Approved refund and chargeback adjustments lower the transaction's revenue, and transactions from the last 60 days are read again every 6 hours, so later refunds show up.
  • Retention by channel. The Retention page groups customers by the channel of their first payment, with how many still pay, net LTV and 30-day churn. With subscription.read, subscription status is read once an hour; without it, retention falls back to payment recency.
  • Revenue per visitor and time to pay. The Channels card shows visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor per channel, and Time to pay shows the median time from first visit to first payment, so you do not cut a channel before its customers have had time to pay.

Set it up

  1. Add the site. Click + Add a site and enter your domain, such as yourproduct.com, and its timezone.
  2. Install the script. Paste the tag before </head> on your marketing site and your app. The virling_vid cookie is set on your registrable domain, so yourproduct.com and app.yourproduct.com share one visitor ID. Leave cookieless mode off.
  3. Connect Paddle. In Paddle, open Developer tools, then Authentication, and create an API key with transaction.read and adjustment.read, plus subscription.read for retention. Paste it under payments; live keys start with pdl_live_ and sandbox keys with pdl_sdbx_. The last 90 days are imported, then new transactions every minute.
  4. Pass the visitor ID in custom data. Virling reads virling_vid from the transaction's custom_data. With Paddle.js, pass it as customData when you open checkout; for transactions you create on your server, set custom_data: { virling_vid: req.cookies.virling_vid }.
Paddle.Checkout.open({
  items: [{ priceId: "pri_...", quantity: 1 }],
  customData: { virling_vid: window.virling?.vid?.() },
});

The full walkthrough is in how to track Paddle revenue by traffic source, and Paddle UTM tracking covers passing UTM and custom data to checkout. If you also sell a desktop app, see Paddle for desktop apps for what links when the purchase happens inside the app. If you sell an API, CLI or SDK, see Paddle for developer tools. Trials, renewals, upgrades and refunds

Paddle reports each of these as a transaction, an adjustment or a subscription status. This is what Virling does with it.

What happensHow Virling credits itWhere it shows
First paymentTo a visit of the visitor ID in the transaction's custom_data, by default the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not DirectChannels, Payments, New customers and Retention, and Time to pay once the channel has three first payments
Payment after a free trialTo the visits before the trial, which starts with a $0 subscription transaction of origin web. The charge has origin subscription_recurring, so it is marked as a renewalChannels and Payments, marked as a renewal. Not in New customers, Time to pay or the Retention customers, and the trial itself is never a payment
RenewalA transaction of origin subscription_recurring, or any later one of the same customer, keeps the channel of the first paymentChannels and Payments, marked as a renewal, and in net LTV on Retention
UpgradeA transaction of origin subscription_update or subscription_charge keeps the channel of the first paymentSame as a renewal
RefundAn approved refund or chargeback lowers its transaction, on that transaction's date. The last 60 days are read again every 6 hours; one on an older transaction is not picked up automaticallyRefunded on the Channels card, the refunded filter on Payments, and refund rate and net LTV on Retention
Cancelled customerCredits nothing. Past payments keep their channel, and a customer who returns with the same customer ID keeps the first oneLeaves Still paying on Retention, and MRR. With subscription.read, status is read once an hour and a scheduled cancellation counts as paying until it takes effect; without it, Retention uses payment recency and there is no Paddle MRR

Read your first month

  1. Open Channels first. Compare revenue per visitor, not visitors. The revenue includes renewals, so check New customers for what the month brought, and read the matched revenue line under the five numbers for how much is tied to a visit. Imported payments are mostly from before the script, so expect a lot of Unattributed at first.
  2. Then Retention, grouped by the channel of the first payment. Look at who still pays and at the refund rate. 30-day churn reads customers whose first payment was more than 30 and at most 60 days ago, so it says nothing yet about the ones you won this month. A customer who started with a free trial is not in the Retention customers, because Paddle bills the first charge as a renewal, so judge trial customers by Channels and Payments.

What not to conclude from a handful of payments:

  • That a channel does not pay. Buyers still in a trial, or still deciding, have paid nothing yet. Check the median on Time to pay before you cut a channel.
  • That the top channel is the best one. One annual plan can put a channel first. Open its payments and count them before you move effort there.
  • That customers from a channel stay or leave. With fewer than 20 customers or fewer than 5 still paying, the Retention page writes no headline, and neither should you.

What to know

  • Transactions older than the 90-day backfill are not imported, so renewals of subscriptions that started before that are Unattributed. Channel numbers fill in as new customers arrive.
  • Renewals carry no custom data of their own; they keep the channel of the subscription's first payment.
  • Payments without a customer ID cannot be linked to a first payment, and payments without a visitor ID still count toward revenue as Unattributed.

Open source

Virling is open source under AGPLv3, and the code is on GitHub. Use the hosted service, or self-host it as one binary with every feature. In a load test on 2026-10-31, in a container limited to one vCPU and 1 GB of RAM, it ingested 100,000 pageviews at 2,829 a second with none failed and a p99 of 34 ms, and used 157 MB of memory after the ingest.

Frequently asked questions

Which channel brings my Paddle subscribers?

Connect Paddle to Virling with a read-only API key and pass the visitor ID as customData when you open Paddle checkout. Each customer's first payment is credited to the visits before it, and every renewal keeps that channel, so the Channels card and the Retention page show which channels bring subscribers who pay.

How are Paddle renewals attributed?

A transaction whose origin is subscription_recurring, subscription_update or subscription_charge is a renewal. It takes the channel, referrer, campaign and landing page of the customer's first payment, matched by Paddle customer ID. Plan changes keep that channel too.

What about free trials on Paddle?

A subscription's first transaction with nothing to pay starts a trial. Virling never counts it as revenue, a payment or a customer. The customer's later payment, which has no visitor ID of its own, is credited to the visits before the trial started.

Which permissions does the Paddle API key need?

transaction.read and adjustment.read, plus subscription.read so Retention can tell who still pays. No other permissions are needed, and Virling never creates, changes or refunds anything.

What about Paddle subscriptions that began before Virling was connected?

Virling imports transactions from the last 90 days. When a subscription's first payment is older than that, its earliest payment in Virling stands in, and it rarely has a visit, so its renewals are usually Unattributed. A first payment inside the 90 days but from before the script was on your site is Unattributed too. All of them count toward revenue, but a customer whose earliest payment in Virling is a renewal is left out of Retention.
