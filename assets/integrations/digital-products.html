Revenue attribution for digital products

Virling joins each sale of a template, course, ebook, license or small app to the visit that came before it, so you see which launch post, newsletter or creator brought the money, not just the clicks. Works with LemonSqueezy, Polar, Creem and Dodo Payments. Open source, one Rust binary, or hosted.

Digital products sell in bursts. A launch post, a newsletter mention or a creator's video sends a wave of visitors, and a few of them buy. The payment provider records the sale, the analytics tool records the visit, and neither knows about the other. Virling joins them: a visitor ID set when someone lands, passed into checkout, and matched to that visitor's sessions when the payment comes in.

The questions it answers

  • Which launch post sold the most? Product Hunt, Hacker News, Reddit, X or your own blog, by revenue rather than upvotes or visits.
  • Which newsletter or creator was worth it? Revenue per sponsor, issue or affiliate post, from the source and campaign on their link.
  • Which page turns visitors into buyers? The landing page and the last page seen before paying.
  • How long does a buyer take? Visits before paying and time to convert for each recent customer.

Sales by channel, source and campaign

Every visit gets one channel from its first pageview: Paid, Email, AI, Organic search, Communities, Social, Referral or Direct. Hacker News, Reddit, Product Hunt and Indie Hackers count as Communities, and a link tagged utm_medium=newsletter counts as Email. The dashboard lists visitors, revenue and revenue per visitor for each channel, source, campaign and landing page, so a post that sent many visitors and no sales stands apart from a small one that paid.

For a link you hand to a newsletter, sponsor or creator, make a tracked link: a short URL that adds the UTM tags for you, with its own clicks, visitors and revenue.

Each site has one attribution model. Last non-direct is the default: a buyer who saw your launch post and came back a week later by typing your address is credited to the post. First touch and last touch are the other choices, and the lookback window can be 30, 60, 90 or 365 days.

Connect your payment provider

  • LemonSqueezy for digital products: an API key, and the visitor ID passed as checkout custom data. Virling registers a webhook on each store to read it, which needs a public https address.
  • Digital products on Polar: an organization access token. Checkout links need no code beyond a custom field with the slug virling_vid; checkout sessions take it in metadata.
  • Digital products on Creem: a scoped API key and the visitor ID in checkout metadata. Virling adds one checkout.completed webhook to read it.
  • Dodo Payments for digital downloads: an API key. Static payment links need no code; checkout sessions take the visitor ID in metadata.

Each provider's payments from the last 90 days are imported on the first run, then new ones every minute. Refunds lower a payment's revenue once Virling reads them, so a channel full of refunds stands out. If you also sell subscriptions, renewals keep the channel of the first payment; see Virling for SaaS.

What to know

  • Sales made before the visitor ID was passed into checkout are Unattributed. They still count toward revenue.
  • A buyer who pays from a link in an email or a DM, without visiting your site first, has no visit to credit and is Unattributed.
  • A visit with no referrer and no UTM tag is Direct. Tag the links you control.

Frequently asked questions

Which payment providers work for digital products?

LemonSqueezy, Polar, Creem and Dodo Payments. Connect one with an API key or token, pass the visitor ID into checkout, and Virling ties each payment to the visit before it. Polar checkout links and Dodo Payments payment links need no code: the script adds the visitor ID when someone clicks.

How do I see which newsletter or creator brought a sale?

Share a tracked link, or tag your link with utm_source and utm_medium=newsletter. Every visit gets a channel, source and campaign from its first pageview, and each sale is credited to that visit, so the Source and Campaign tabs show revenue per newsletter, creator or post.

Is Virling open source?

Yes. Virling is open source under AGPLv3. You can run it yourself as one binary with embedded storage, or use the hosted version.
