integrations / paddle for desktop apps

See which channel brings the buyers of your desktop app.

For makers of desktop and Mac apps who sell licenses or subscriptions through Paddle. Virling ties each Paddle payment to the channel and campaign behind the visit to your download page, so you compare launch posts, searches, ads and newsletters by the buyers they bring, not by downloads. Open source under AGPL, hosted or self-hosted.

Paddle knows who paid and how much, but not which post or search sent them to your site. Virling sets a visitor ID when someone lands on your site, keeps it in a first-party cookie on your domain for one year, and matches it when a Paddle checkout carries it. Paddle is read with a read-only API key.

What you get

  • Each payment credited to a channel. A license or first subscription payment is joined to the buyer's visits before it. By default it goes to the latest visit in the last 90 days that was not Direct, so a buyer who found you through a launch post and came back later by typing your URL is credited to the launch post. First touch, last touch and 30, 60 or 365-day windows are the other options.
  • Campaigns and landing pages. Each visit keeps its channel, referrer, UTM campaign and landing page, so you see whether your download page, pricing page or a comparison page brought the buyer.
  • Renewals that keep the first channel. A subscription renewal, a plan change or any later purchase by the same customer, such as a paid upgrade, keeps the channel of the customer's first payment, matched by Paddle customer ID.
  • Free trials handled. A subscription's first transaction with nothing to pay starts a trial. Virling never counts it as revenue, a payment or a customer, and the customer's later payments are credited to the visits before it. Only a transaction with origin web starts one, so open trial checkouts with Paddle.js.
  • Refunds and chargebacks taken off. Approved refund and chargeback adjustments lower the transaction's revenue, and transactions from the last 60 days are read again every 6 hours, so later refunds show up.

When the purchase happens inside the app

Virling links a payment to a visit only when the Paddle transaction carries virling_vid in its custom_data. The script runs on pages of your domain and its subdomains, not inside your app.

  • Checkout on your website: links. The buyer opens Paddle checkout on a page that runs the script, and your code passes the visitor ID.
  • Buy button that opens your site in the browser: links when the buyer visited your site in that browser before, because the cookie is still there. Leave UTM tags off the link your app opens: a tagged visit is not Direct, so it would take the credit from the channel that first brought the buyer.
  • Checkout shown inside the app: does not link. It has no visitor ID, so the payment counts toward revenue as Unattributed, unless the same customer has an earlier payment or trial that was credited.
  • Purchase in another browser or on another device: does not link, since that browser has its own cookie and a new visitor ID.

The script does not add the visitor ID to Paddle checkouts by itself, so your code has to pass it.

Set it up

  1. Add the site. Click + Add a site and enter your domain, such as yourapp.com, and its timezone.
  2. Install the script. Paste the tag before </head> on every page of your site, including the download and pricing pages. The virling_vid cookie is set on your registrable domain, so subdomains share one visitor ID. Leave cookieless mode off.
  3. Connect Paddle. In Paddle, open Developer tools, then Authentication, and create an API key with transaction.read and adjustment.read, plus subscription.read if you sell subscriptions. Paste it under payments; live keys start with pdl_live_ and sandbox keys with pdl_sdbx_. The last 90 days are imported, then new transactions every minute.
  4. Link checkout to visits. With Paddle.js, pass the visitor ID as customData when you open checkout; for transactions you create on your server, set custom_data: { virling_vid: req.cookies.virling_vid }. window.virling?.vid?.() returns nothing when the script is blocked or not loaded yet, so check the value before using it.
Paddle.Checkout.open({
  items: [{ priceId: "pri_...", quantity: 1 }],
  customData: { virling_vid: window.virling?.vid?.() },
});

The full walkthrough is in how to track Paddle revenue by traffic source, and Paddle UTM tracking covers passing UTM and custom data to checkout. See also Paddle revenue attribution and, for subscription products, Paddle for SaaS.

Make a test purchase

  1. Connect a sandbox key. It starts with pdl_sdbx_. A site has one Paddle connection, and sandbox payments stay in your reports after you replace the key with a live one. To keep them out, connect the live key, make one small live payment and refund it.
  2. Visit through a tagged link. Open a private window and go to your download page through a link such as https://yourapp.com/download?utm_source=test&utm_medium=email. Use your real domain, not localhost, and a browser and IP address that Virling does not ignore.
  3. Pay in the same window. Open a checkout with Paddle.js set to the sandbox environment and virling_vid in customData, and pay with one of Paddle's test cards. Use a price without a trial, since a trial start is not a payment.
  4. Read the payment. Wait a minute for the next sync and open /app/yourapp.com/payments. The payment shows the test source and the Email channel instead of Unattributed. Open it: Visitor under Details is the visitor ID, and the journey starts at your tagged visit, not at checkout.
  5. Then test the button in the app. Make a tagged visit in your default browser, click the buy button in a build whose button opens the page with the sandbox checkout, and pay with a new email address. The payment should show the same source, since the app opened the browser that holds the cookie. Use a new address for each test: a later payment by the same customer is a renewal and keeps the first payment's channel. Then replace the sandbox key with the live one.

Why a Paddle payment is Unattributed

Open the payment and look for Visitor under Details. Without one, the transaction did not carry the visitor ID.

  • Checkout was opened without customData, or your server created the transaction without custom_data. Fix: set virling_vid in it. Custom data on a customer, product or price is not read.
  • Checkout opened before the script loaded. The ID was empty. Fix: wait for it, or create the transaction on your server with the ID from the cookie.
  • The purchase happened inside the app. Fix: have the buy button open your pricing page in the browser.
  • The visitor had no ID. The script was missing or blocked, or the site is in cookieless mode. Fix: put the script on every page where visitors arrive, and set Visitor cookie to Cookie and take data-cookieless off the tag. If you need consent first, use Until consent: visitors who accept can be credited.
  • It has a visitor ID but no visit in the window. Someone who tries the app for months can buy after it closes. Fix: choose a longer lookback window.
  • It is a renewal of an Unattributed first payment, or of one older than the import. Check the first payment, not the renewal. There is nothing to fix at checkout for a customer who already pays.
  • It is from before the script was on your site. This covers most of the first import, and cannot be filled in later.

Paddle payments are not matched by email. A fix applies to new customers; earlier payments usually stay Unattributed. More causes are in Unattributed revenue.

What to know

  • Virling does not see downloads, installs or anything inside your app. It sees visits to your site and the payments that carry a visitor ID.
  • Transactions older than the 90-day backfill are not imported, and payments in the backfill were made before the script was on your site, so they are usually Unattributed. Channel numbers fill in as new buyers arrive.
  • Payments without a visitor ID still count toward revenue as Unattributed, unless the same customer has an earlier payment or trial that was credited.

Frequently asked questions

Which channel brings the buyers of my desktop app?

Put the Virling script on your website, connect Paddle with a read-only API key, and pass the visitor ID as customData when you open Paddle checkout on your site. Each payment is then credited to the visits before it, such as the visit to your download page, so the Channels card shows which channels and campaigns bring buyers.

What happens when the purchase happens inside the app?

A checkout shown inside the app runs outside your website, so it has no virling_vid cookie and carries no visitor ID. The payment still counts toward revenue, as Unattributed, unless the same customer has an earlier payment or trial that was credited. To credit it, have the buy button open a pricing or checkout page on your own domain in the browser: if the buyer visited your site in that browser before, the cookie is still there and the payment is credited to the earlier visit.

Does Virling track downloads or installs?

No. Virling tracks visits to pages on your site and the payments that carry a visitor ID. It does not see the download, the install or anything inside the app.

Which permissions does the Paddle API key need?

transaction.read and adjustment.read, plus subscription.read if you sell subscriptions and want Retention to tell who still pays. No other permissions are needed, and Virling never creates, changes or refunds anything.

How are Paddle renewals and paid upgrades credited?

To the channel of the customer's first payment, matched by Paddle customer ID. That covers subscription renewals, plan changes and any later transaction of the same customer, so an upgrade bought inside the app keeps the first channel even without a visitor ID. If the first payment is Unattributed or older than the 90-day import, these are usually Unattributed too.

How is a purchase after a free trial of the app credited?

A trial that runs inside your app involves no Paddle transaction, so the license payment is a first payment, credited to a visit inside the lookback window, 90 days by default. A Paddle subscription trial starts with a transaction that has nothing to pay; a later payment of that customer with no visitor ID of its own is credited to the visits before the trial started.
