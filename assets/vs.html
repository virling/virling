How Virling compares.

Honest, side by side, with when the other tool is the better choice.

Start from what you need

Find your question and open the one or two pages that answer it.

  • Revenue by channel for a SaaS. Virling fits: it joins each payment to the visit before it, and renewals keep the channel of the first payment. Read Virling vs DataFast and Virling vs Google Analytics. It is not the right choice if your customers pay outside the nine providers it reads.
  • Orders by channel for a store. Virling fits a Shopify or WooCommerce store, once a short snippet stores the visitor ID on each order. Read Virling vs Triple Whale and Virling vs Google Analytics. It is not the right choice if your store takes orders that none of the nine providers record.
  • Reports for clients. Virling fits when clients ask which channel paid: one site per client and a white-label weekly report. Read Virling vs Google Analytics and Virling vs Triple Whale. It is not the right choice for a client who only wants visitor counts.
  • Traffic only. Virling is not the right choice. It is built around payments, and without them you would use only part of it. Read Virling vs Plausible and Virling vs Umami, and the part on when the other tool fits better.
  • Product analytics. Virling is not the right choice. It has no session replay, heatmaps, feature flags or A/B tests; what Virling does not do has the full list. Read Virling vs PostHog and Virling vs Mixpanel.
  • Self-hosting. Virling fits: one binary with embedded storage, free on your own server, with every feature. Read Virling vs Umami and Virling vs Matomo. It is not the right choice if you need a native Windows build; on Windows, use Docker.
  • Virling vs DataFastBoth join payments to the visits before them. Virling is open source and runs on your own server or hosted, and supports Dodo Payments.
  • Virling vs DataBullBoth join payments to visits. Virling is open source and runs on any server; DataBull is a paid licence for your Cloudflare account.
  • Virling vs PlausiblePlausible is privacy-friendly traffic analytics. Virling reads your payment provider and shows which channels bring paying customers, open source and self-hosted.
  • Virling vs Google AnalyticsGoogle Analytics can show revenue by channel after ecommerce event setup. Virling reads Stripe, Paddle, Shopify, WooCommerce, Dodo Payments, Polar, LemonSqueezy, Creem or RevenueCat directly and keeps renewals on their first channel.
  • Virling vs PostHogPostHog is a broad product analytics platform. Virling does one job: revenue by channel, self-hosted in one binary.
  • Virling vs UmamiUmami is free, MIT-licensed traffic and event analytics. Virling reads your payments and joins them to visits.
  • Virling vs MatomoMatomo is open-source web analytics on PHP and MySQL, with AI assistant reports. Virling reads your payments and joins them to visits, in one binary.
  • Virling vs FathomFathom Analytics is hosted, cookieless traffic analytics with revenue as event values. Virling reads your payment provider and can run on your own server.
  • Virling vs MixpanelMixpanel is hosted product analytics on the events you send. Virling reads payments directly and shows revenue and retention by marketing channel.
  • Virling vs Triple WhaleTriple Whale is hosted ecommerce analytics priced by GMV. Virling is open source, works with SaaS payment providers too, and has a flat price.

What to ask any tool

Ask these six before you choose, whichever tool it is. Virling's own answers are next to each.

  1. Which payment providers does it read? Virling reads Stripe, Paddle, Shopify, WooCommerce, Dodo Payments, Polar, LemonSqueezy, Creem and RevenueCat. See payments.
  2. Is its access read-only? Virling uses read-only keys where the provider allows it, and never creates, changes or refunds a payment. LemonSqueezy keys cannot be limited. For LemonSqueezy, Creem and RevenueCat it also adds a webhook, so the Creem and RevenueCat keys need write access for that. See which key to create.
  3. What happens to a refund? Virling shows revenue after refunds: a refund lowers the payment it belongs to, on that payment's date. On Paddle, LemonSqueezy, Creem and WooCommerce it can take up to 6 hours to show, and one on a payment older than 60 days is not picked up automatically. Each provider page says what counts.
  4. Which channel gets a renewal? In Virling a renewal takes the channel of the customer's first payment. If that payment is older than the first import, the renewal is usually Unattributed. See renewals.
  5. Where is the data stored? Self-hosted, in one data folder on your own server. On the hosted plan, in India, on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure in Mumbai. See the privacy policy.
  6. What happens to a payment it cannot match? Virling lists it as Unattributed. It still counts toward revenue but is not credited to any visit. See unattributed revenue.
questions

Questions about choosing.

How do I choose between Virling and another analytics tool?

Start from the question you need answered. If it is which channel brings paying customers, compare Virling with the tool you use now. If it is how many people visit, or what they do inside your product, another tool is the better choice.

When is Virling not the right choice?

When you only need traffic counts, when you need session replay, heatmaps, feature flags or A/B tests, or when your customers pay through a provider Virling does not read.

Can I try Virling before I decide?

Yes. The demo needs no sign-up, every hosted plan starts with a 14-day free trial, and self-hosting is free with every feature. The trial needs a card, and nothing is charged if you cancel before day 15.

Will Virling show revenue from before I install it?

Partly. The first sync imports the last 90 days of payments, or 60 days on Shopify, and from RevenueCat only one-time purchases. Visits exist only from when the script is added, so most of those earlier payments are Unattributed.
